# Monthly Housing Summary — August 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The weekly Altos demand read continues to hold above year-ago levels in aggregate, though the trend has softened at the edges. The 13-week average for new pending sales stands at roughly 89,700 — up about 4.8% year over year and positive for 55 consecutive weeks — while the 4-week average has ticked down to +4.4% YoY, a deceleration from where it sat heading into summer. The most recent spot week came in at -3.0% YoY, the third consecutive week of negative readings after a long positive streak, which is worth watching but not yet alarming given seasonal patterns around late June. Inventory sits near 1.075 million active listings, up about 1.2% YoY — growth that has decelerated meaningfully from the double-digit YoY expansion of a year ago. New listings have pulled back; the spot week came in at -7.1% YoY after several months of positive readings, though the 4-week average is essentially flat at +0.4% and the 13-week average remains slightly positive. Pending days on market are running about 7% above last year at 70.9 days, a signal that buyers are taking more time, not less. NAR's July existing home sales printed at 4.06 million SAAR — meaningfully above the 3.85 million consensus — though the month-over-month read was slightly softer and the NSA YoY gain of +2.8% confirms the beat was real, not seasonal noise. Worth noting: new home sales remain under pressure at 628K SAAR in June, down 5.1% YoY, and new home median prices have fallen to $398,300 — off nearly 3% YoY — as builders continue using price and incentives to clear inventory sitting at 9.3 months supply. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.71% as of August 14, ticking up two basis points after a brief decline, and up 15 basis points year over year. The more consequential number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at approximately 2.05-2.08 points, it remains well above the historical norm of around 1.7 points, meaning the mortgage market itself is adding roughly 35 basis points of cost beyond what underlying rates alone would imply. That spread has compressed 20-22 basis points over the past year — a quiet affordability improvement most buyers and agents haven't noticed. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is nearly flat YoY at -0.2%, essentially unchanged, while the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks — has its 4-week average running at -2.3% YoY despite a recent uptick off lows. That divergence suggests MBA's near-flat read may soften modestly in the next few weeks before stabilizing. Single-family permits held at 872K SAAR in June, the best reading since May 2024, while single-family starts continue to drift lower at 895K — the gap between permits and starts suggests builders have pipeline but are pacing construction carefully. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the Altos spot-week YoY on pending sales recovers from its recent negative readings as we move past the seasonal trough, and whether the spread between MII and MBA closes or widens.

The macro picture this month offered more clarity than the prior few. July payrolls missed expectations by a wide margin — the economy added roughly 23,000 net jobs versus a consensus of 59,000 — but the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating expectations of 4.4%, and the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) declined 8.6% in July after rising through spring. That combination — weak headline hiring, but a lower unemployment rate and a shrinking long-duration unemployment cohort — is more benign for housing than the payroll miss alone suggests. The long-term unemployed count had been the early indicator most worth watching for future mortgage stress; its pullback this month reduces near-term delinquency risk, though one month doesn't reset the trend. On inflation, the data was broadly favorable: Core CPI came in at +0.2% month over month versus +0.3% expected, core PCE printed at +0.1% against a +0.3% consensus, and shelter CPI — the component most stubborn for housing affordability — rose only +0.14% in July. Shelter CPI running at 3.2% YoY is still well above the Fed's target path, but the monthly deceleration is consistent with what private-market rent data (Zillow's ZORI at +3.3% YoY but rising only 72 basis points month over month) has been signaling for months. The mechanism here matters: as shelter CPI continues to decelerate, core inflation trends toward the Fed's comfort zone, and rate expectations can ease without an explicit policy move — that is what would compress the 30Y-10Y spread further and deliver the real affordability improvement.
