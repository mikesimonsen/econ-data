**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through most of the summer but showed meaningful deceleration by late June, which is worth understanding clearly before you walk into a September listing appointment. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales was running +4.81% YoY as of June 26 — positive for 55 consecutive weeks — but the 4-week average had pulled back to +4.37% YoY, and the spot week turned negative at -2.99% YoY for the first time in over a year. One soft week doesn't break a streak, but the direction of the trend matters: the 13-week average has been decelerating for several weeks, and pending days on market are running +6.71% YoY (103 consecutive weeks above prior year), meaning homes are absorbing more slowly even as the headline demand number remains technically positive. Inventory on the Altos side was still up about +1.2% YoY in late June and had been rising for eight consecutive weeks, but new listings flipped negative YoY (-7.1% on the spot week, flat on the 4-week average), which means the inventory build is stalling — not from demand absorbing supply, but from sellers pulling back. NAR's July existing home sales print of 4.06 million SAAR beat expectations of 3.85 million and is up +1.25% YoY, but it slipped sequentially for the second month and the underlying NSA data (+2.83% YoY) confirms the modest positive trend. New home sales (June, 628K SAAR) are still running -5.1% YoY, and builders cut average prices sharply — average new home prices fell nearly 9.5% in a single month, an unusually large move — signaling that builders are using incentives to clear standing inventory rather than pricing to strength. The start/permit divergence this month is one of the more important signals in the data: July housing starts fell 12.4% month-over-month and 12.7% YoY, badly missing expectations, with single-family starts down 15% YoY; but permits rose 5.0% month-over-month and 5.9% YoY, beating expectations and reaching their highest level since early 2024. That combination — permits rising while starts and completions fall — tells you builders are filing for future work while pausing current production. The pipeline implication is tighter resale competition from new homes in 2026, but a less-supplied market in 2027-28. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.72% as of August 19, essentially flat for the month and up only 12bp YoY. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.07 percentage points — still elevated relative to the historical norm of around 1.7pp, which means rates can fall 30-35bp without any move from the Fed if lenders normalize margins. MBA purchase applications are running -1.2% YoY on the 4-week average as of mid-August, a meaningful deterioration from the +0.8% on the 13-week average; and the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA by 2-4 weeks — was at -2.3% YoY on its 4-week average as of late June, diverging negatively from MBA at that time. Watch for whether the MII's late-June softness has now shown up fully in MBA data through August, or whether there's another leg of weakness still in the pipeline for September applications.

The macro backdrop is genuinely mixed in ways that matter differently for housing. July payrolls missed by roughly 82,000 jobs (actual -23K vs. +59K consensus), the weakest print in some time, and average hourly earnings came in at just +0.1% month-over-month against a +0.3% expectation — soft labor income growth directly reduces the pool of qualified buyers and weakens move-up demand. The unemployment rate surprised to the downside at 4.1% (consensus was 4.4%), and the long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) actually fell 8.6% in July to 1.771 million — reversing a recent rise. That's encouraging, because it's the accumulation of long-term unemployed that eventually pressures mortgage delinquencies; a reversal here reduces the near-term distressed-supply risk. On inflation: core PCE ran +0.13% in June and +3.29% YoY, cooler than expected, and July CPI core came in at +0.22% month-over-month with CPI shelter holding at +3.16% YoY. Shelter CPI is a lagging series — it's reflecting lease renewals from roughly 12-18 months ago — and the Zillow ZORI rent index, a forward-looking measure, shows rents up only +3.0% YoY with July ticking down. That convergence suggests shelter CPI will continue drifting lower into year-end, which keeps core PCE on a gradual disinflationary path and maintains the conditions for eventual Fed rate reductions.
