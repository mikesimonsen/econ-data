**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through most of the summer but showed its first cracks in late June: the Altos new pending sales 13-week average was running +4.8% year-over-year as of June 26, extending a 55-consecutive-week streak of positive YoY readings, though the single-week print had slipped to -3.0% YoY and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.4%. That divergence between the smoothed trend and the spot reading is worth watching — the 13-week is still pointing up, but momentum is clearly fading. Inventory continued rising, with Altos total active inventory up about 1.2% YoY as of late June, while new listings decelerated sharply: the spot weekly print turned -7.1% YoY even as the 4-week average barely held positive at +0.4%. That pullback in new listings — if it persists — will cap inventory growth and partially offset the demand softening. The July NAR existing home sales print came in at 4.06 million SAAR, ahead of the 3.85 million consensus, though it represents a modest sequential dip and sits only +1.3% YoY; the streak of meaningful beats is more encouraging than the level itself. Notably, NAR's own inventory measure fell 0.7% YoY in July, flipping negative for the first time in several months — consistent with Altos showing new listings pulling back. New home sales (June, the most recent read) ticked to 628K SAAR, still -5.1% YoY, and builder average prices fell sharply, suggesting builders are working through existing pipeline with incentives rather than repricing the market. July housing starts delivered the clearest negative signal of the month: total starts fell to 1,239K SAAR, -12.4% month-over-month and -12.8% YoY, missing the 1,350K consensus by a wide margin. Single-family starts dropped to 808K. The offsetting data point is permits: total permits rose to 1,443K SAAR, +6.0% YoY and the highest since January 2024, with single-family permits at a two-year high. That starts-permits divergence suggests builders are authorized but not yet breaking ground — likely waiting for rate clarity before committing labor and materials. On rates, the 30-year fixed sat at 6.77% as of August 21, essentially unchanged over the past month. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, which was at 2.03-2.06 points — elevated versus the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points, but compressing: the spread is down about 25 basis points year-over-year and has tightened for three consecutive weeks. That compression is doing quiet affordability work; if the 10-year holds near 4.71% and the spread narrows another 20-25bp toward historical norms, the 30-year could drift toward the low-to-mid 6s without any Fed action. The leading indicators are more cautious: the MBA purchase application 4-week average was -1.2% YoY as of August 14 — the first negative 4-week YoY reading in some time — and the 13-week average has been falling for six consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-inquiry-based leading indicator that tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks) showed a constructive uptick in late June, with its 4-week average reversing a recent decline. If the MII recovery holds into July data, the MBA softness may prove temporary. Watch whether the Altos 13-week pending average holds its 55-week streak through September, and whether new listings recover or continue contracting — that supply signal will drive the fall market more than rates will.

The macro backdrop is genuinely mixed, and the housing-relevant reads cut in different directions. Payrolls were the weakest print of the summer: July nonfarm payrolls came in roughly 80K below the 59K consensus expectation (the actual change was negative against expectations for gains), though the unemployment rate simultaneously fell to 4.1% versus a 4.4% consensus — a divergence that reflects labor force participation dynamics rather than underlying strength. For housing, a labor market that is softening at the margin matters most through consumer confidence and mortgage qualification; the Conference Board Consumer Confidence Index at 90.8 remains well below its year-ago level (-8.0% YoY) and has fallen two consecutive months, which historically precedes some softening in purchase intent. The inflation data was the clearest positive signal of the month: July headline CPI came in at +0.1% month-over-month versus a 0.9% consensus expectation, core CPI at +0.2% versus +0.3% expected, and June Core PCE at +0.1% versus +0.3% expected.
