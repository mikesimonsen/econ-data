# Monthly Housing Summary — August 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive on a four-week average basis through late June — the 4-week average running +4.4% year-over-year — and the 13-week average has been in positive territory for 55 consecutive weeks, ticking up another 0.09% in the most recent reading. The spot week flipped negative (-3.0% YoY) for the first time in that streak, worth watching but not yet alarming given seasonal noise in late June data; the 4-week average is the better signal here and it is still constructive. Altos active inventory sits at roughly 1.07 million units, up 1.2% YoY after eight consecutive weeks of gains, a deceleration from the pace of earlier this year. New listings softened meaningfully — the spot week fell 7.1% YoY and the 4-week average is barely positive at +0.4% — suggesting seller hesitation may be capping the supply build rather than any structural change in household mobility. Pending days on market at 70.9 days is running 6.7% above year-ago levels, a consistent signal (103 weeks of positive YoY readings) that the market is slower to clear than in prior cycles, though not deteriorating further in recent weeks. NAR's July existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million but still down slightly from June — YoY the print is +1.3%, with six consecutive months of positive YoY readings now in view. New home sales (June, 628K SAAR) remain 5.1% below year-ago levels, and with new home median prices down 2.7% YoY and average prices off 6.6%, builders are buying volume through incentives and mix-shift rather than holding price. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.74% as of August 12, down about 5 basis points over the past week. The more important story is the spread: the 30Y-10Y Treasury spread has compressed to approximately 2.06-2.08 basis points — still well above the historical norm of around 1.7 points, but the trend is moving in the right direction, down roughly 20-23 basis points year-over-year. If that spread continues toward 1.80-1.85, the 30-year rate falls meaningfully even without a Fed cut, which is the affordability story most buyers are not pricing in. MBA purchase applications ticked up 2.5% week-over-week in the August 7 reading but remain fractionally negative (-0.2%) on a 4-week average YoY basis, with the 13-week average still running a modest +1.7% YoY after decelerating for five consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA applications by two to four weeks — bounced in its last reading (up 3.5% for the week, 4-week average +2.3%), but the 13-week average has been falling for seven weeks and remains -2.4% YoY. That divergence between the short-term bounce and the longer trend suggests the MII is stabilizing rather than turning. On the supply side, total housing starts delivered a large upside reading in June (+19% month-over-month, beating the 1,350K consensus at 1,427K), but the composition matters: multifamily drove virtually all of the gain, with single-family starts actually falling slightly for a third consecutive month and running 3.2% below year-ago. Meanwhile single-family permits ticked down in June and total permits missed expectations (-1.4% YoY), indicating builders are completing the backlog faster than they are authorizing new pipeline. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the Altos spot-week demand reading recovers its positive YoY footing and whether the MII 13-week average bottoms — those two signals together will tell you whether the modest late-summer slowdown is seasonal or something stickier heading into fall.

The macro picture this month was cleaner than the headlines suggested. July payrolls came in at roughly -23K against a consensus expectation of +59K — a meaningful miss — but the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% against a 4.4% consensus, and the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) actually declined by 8.6% for the month after several months of accumulation. The labor market is not uniformly weakening; it is bifurcating, with headline job creation softening while workers who are employed appear relatively stable. For housing, a 4.1% unemployment rate is not a demand-killer, but the pace of hiring (quits rate flat at 2.0%, hires rate modest at 3.4%) suggests households are not gaining the income confidence that drives discretionary moves. Average hourly earnings grew just 0.05% in July (+3.15% YoY), cooler than the 0.3% expected — which is disinflationary but not the kind of wage acceleration that helps buyers stretch into current price levels. On inflation: July CPI came in at +0.07% headline and +0.22% core, both below expectations, and core PCE for June printed +0.13% against a 0.3% consensus. Core PCE running at +3.
