**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through the four weeks ending late June, though the weekly Altos read softened. New pending sales came in at -3.0% YoY for the spot week of June 26, the first negative weekly print after a 55-week streak of positive YoY readings — a streak worth noting because the 4-week average remained firmly positive at +4.4% YoY, and the 13-week average, now in its 14th consecutive week of gains, sat at +4.8% YoY. That gap between the spot week and the rolling average is the key read: one soft week does not break a trend, but it warrants watching. Inventory rose for an eighth consecutive week to roughly 1.075 million active listings, up just 1.2% YoY — growth that has clearly decelerated from the double-digit YoY expansions of 2024-25. New listings flickered; the spot week turned negative YoY (-7.1%) while the 4-week average held barely positive (+0.4%). Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are taking modestly longer to clear than a year ago, though not alarmingly so. NAR's June existing sales print came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus by a notable margin and running +4.1% YoY on a seasonally adjusted basis; the NSA count of 422,000 was up 7.9% YoY, with the South leading volume and the Northeast returning to positive territory. New home sales told a different story — 628,000 SAAR, still down 5.1% YoY, with the new-home months supply at a elevated 9.3 months and builder average sale prices down sharply. Builders are clearing existing inventory at discount rather than pulling forward new pipeline: single-family starts fell for a third consecutive month in June even as total starts beat expectations on a multifamily pop, and single-family permits at 872,000 remain below the 900,000 threshold that typically sustains resale market supply equilibrium. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.77% as of today, down marginally over the past two days but effectively rangebound. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.11 percentage points against a historical norm of roughly 1.7 points, there is meaningful compression potential if credit risk appetite improves — a 25-35bp spread tightening alone would push the conforming rate below 6.5% without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications for the week of July 24 came in at +2.7% YoY on the spot read, but the 4-week average has now fallen for four consecutive weeks and turned negative YoY at -1.4%. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA by roughly two to four weeks — reversed off recent lows, with both its spot reading and 4-week average ticking higher in late June; if that reversal holds, it should show up in MBA purchase data by mid-August. Watch whether the MII follow-through materializes: a confirmed rebound there, combined with the still-positive 13-week pending average, would suggest the late-June softness was seasonal noise rather than a demand inflection.

The macro backdrop shifted meaningfully in July. June headline CPI fell 0.4% month-over-month against an expected +0.9% gain, and core CPI was essentially flat — both well below consensus. Core PCE for June printed +0.13% MoM, also cooler than the +0.3% expected, leaving the 12-month rate at 3.3%. That matters for housing in two ways: it removes the Fed's justification for holding rates higher longer, and — more directly — it should put downward pressure on the 10-year Treasury, which in turn compresses the mortgage rate absent any spread widening. The 10-year sits at 4.66% today, still elevated YoY by 44 basis points but ticking lower. On labor: June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000, unemployment surprised to the downside at 4.2% versus a 4.4% consensus, and the hires rate firmed to 3.4%. That is the employment picture an active buyer needs — job security without a hot labor market that keeps inflation elevated. The long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) fell in June for the first time after a sustained rise, though the YoY reading remains +17.3%; this group does not affect the current selling season but represents the leading edge of a potential distressed-borrower wave in 2027 if the labor market softens further — worth tracking. Regional price data through May (Case-Shiller, reference period ending May 2026) confirms the two-speed market agents in different geographies should be navigating differently: Chicago was up 6.9% YoY and New York up 4.0%, both accelerating; Boston turned positive month-over-month and is running +2.6% YoY. Meanwhile Denver, Seattle, Tampa, Phoenix, and Las Vegas are all negative YoY, with Las Vegas at its lowest appreciation reading since September
