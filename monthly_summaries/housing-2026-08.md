**August 2026 Housing Summary**

The clearest read on demand right now is the Altos 13-week average, which has held positive YoY for 55 consecutive weeks — a streak that continues to be the most durable signal in the data. The spot week ending June 26 dipped to -3.0% YoY on new pending sales, and the 4-week average has decelerated to +4.4% YoY, so the trend is narrowing even if it hasn't broken. That single negative spot week is worth watching but not overreading; seasonal volatility around late June routinely distorts the weekly print. Inventory rose for an eighth consecutive week at Altos, with total active supply up 1.2% YoY — modest growth that keeps the market from tightening further but isn't the wave of supply that would reset pricing. New listings ticked down in the most recent week and flipped to -7.1% YoY on a spot basis, though the 4-week and 13-week averages remain just slightly positive, suggesting the supply-arrival story is stalling rather than reversing. Pending days on market held at 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY for the 103rd consecutive week — a consistent signal that the market is absorbing homes more slowly than a year ago, even as closings remain solid. NAR's June existing sales print of 4.09M SAAR beat expectations of 3.85M cleanly, with NSA sales running +7.9% YoY — the strongest directional signal in the report. New home sales at 628K SAAR were up modestly from May but remain 5.1% below year-ago levels, with new home median prices down 2.7% YoY as builders continue using price concessions to clear inventory; months supply of new homes ticked down to 9.3, still elevated relative to existing inventory at 4.6 months. The 30-year fixed rate ended July at 6.83%, ticking up 6 basis points on the week after a brief decline — the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.13 percentage points against a historical norm near 1.7 points, meaning roughly 40-45 basis points of rate relief could materialize without any Fed action if credit markets normalize. That spread compression thesis hasn't advanced this month; the spread widened slightly. MBA purchase applications came in at a 4-week average of -1.4% YoY, a meaningful softening from where the 13-week average (+2.9% YoY, 76 consecutive positive weeks) still sits — that divergence between the short and longer horizons is worth flagging. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by roughly two to four weeks, posted a spot reading of -5.6% YoY through June 26 with a 4-week average of -2.3% YoY — both negative, and diverging from where MBA currently sits. If MII is leading accurately, MBA's 4-week average has further room to soften into August. On the supply side, the June housing starts headline of 1,427K SAAR was driven almost entirely by multifamily — 5+ unit starts jumped 76% in the month — while single-family starts fell a third consecutive month to 895K SAAR. Permits told the opposite story: single-family permits ticked to their highest level since May 2024 at 872K SAAR, while 5+ unit permits fell. That divergence — multifamily being completed and delivered while single-family starts slow but permits firm — implies single-family production holds roughly steady over the next six months while the apartment delivery wave continues to wind down. Watch whether the Altos 13-week pending average holds above +4% YoY through mid-August; a break below that level would be the first meaningful demand signal worth bringing to a listing conversation.

The macro picture is genuinely mixed in a way that matters for rates and demand. June headline CPI came in at -0.4% month-over-month against a consensus expectation of +0.9% — a substantial undershoot — and core CPI was essentially flat at -0.02% MoM versus an expected +0.3%. Core PCE for June printed +0.13% MoM, also below the expected +0.3%. Three consecutive inflation undershoots of that magnitude shift the Fed's optionality calculus; the bond market's inflation expectations (5-year breakevens — the market-implied inflation rate embedded in Treasury pricing) were already reflecting some disinflation, and these prints reinforce that. The mechanism for housing: lower inflation expectations compress the 10-year Treasury yield, which is the anchor for the 30-year mortgage rate. The 10-year closed July at 4.70%, up slightly on the week, suggesting markets aren't yet fully repricing for the softer inflation data — which is the tension to watch. On labor, June payrolls came in at 159K, the unemployment rate fell to 4.2% (against a 4.4% consensus), and initial claims for the week ending July 25 printed 197K against a 212K expectation.
