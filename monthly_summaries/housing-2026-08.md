**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive on a trend basis through late June, but the spot data is flashing caution. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sat at +4.81% YoY as of June 26 — 55 consecutive weeks of positive YoY readings — but the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37% and the single-week print turned negative (-2.99% YoY), the first negative week in over a year. That single-week dip may be seasonal noise, but it warrants watching. Inventory continued to build slowly — Altos total active supply was up about 1.2% YoY, and the Realtor.com total listing count sat at 1.59 million in July, up 2.6% YoY — though new listings growth has softened, with the Altos 4-week average barely positive at +0.41% YoY after the single-week print turned negative (-7.1%). Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, which suggests modest but persistent buyer selectivity. NAR's June existing home sales (SAAR) came in at 4.09 million — beating expectations of 3.85 million and up 4.1% YoY on an NSA basis — though that headline SAAR figure dipped slightly from May. New home sales told a different story: the June SAAR of 628,000 was down 5.1% YoY, with average new-home prices off sharply, which partly reflects builders repricing through incentives and mix shift rather than pure market weakness. The 30-year fixed mortgage rate stands at 6.77% as of August 6, effectively unchanged over the past month but up about 20bp YoY. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.13 percentage points — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7pp. That 43bp of excess spread is the affordability story most buyers aren't hearing: if credit markets normalize and the spread compresses toward historical levels, the 30-year rate could fall meaningfully without any Fed action. The MBA purchase index 4-week average was -0.23% YoY through July 31 — just turned negative — while the 13-week average remains positive at +2.3% YoY, so the trend hasn't broken yet, only softened. The Xactus MII (a credit-inquiry-based signal that leads MBA by roughly 2-4 weeks) showed a notable divergence: the spot reading ticked up to 123.3 on June 26, but the 13-week average has been falling for seven consecutive weeks and sits -2.38% YoY. When MII trends lower while MBA is still near flat, it typically precedes MBA weakness by a month or so. Watch for the MBA 4-week average to confirm that gap over the next four weeks.

The macro picture is more constructive than the rate environment implies. June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 — above the consensus of 59,000 change implied in the snapshot — and the unemployment rate surprised to 4.2% against expectations of 4.4%. More importantly, the June JOLTS data showed the hires rate ticking back to 3.4% and quits rate holding at 2.0%, both consistent with a labor market that is cooling but not deteriorating. The mechanism for housing is direct: employed households with stable income are the buyer pool; a softening but non-collapsing labor market sustains transaction volume even when affordability is strained. The inflation data is where the narrative shifted most in July. Headline CPI came in at -0.42% MoM versus an expected +0.9% — a meaningful undershoot — and core CPI was essentially flat MoM at -0.02% versus the +0.3% consensus. Core PCE for June printed +0.13% MoM, also below the +0.3% expected, bringing the YoY reading to 3.29%. The mechanism: a sustained string of sub-consensus inflation prints raises the probability that the Fed moves earlier or more than the market currently prices, which flows through to the 10-year and, with a lag, into mortgage rates — particularly if the mortgage-Treasury spread also compresses. Shelter CPI remains stubborn at +3.26% YoY, but it is a lagged reflection of lease-up dynamics from 2024-2025; the Zillow rent index at +3.34% YoY (its highest since June 2023) suggests rent disinflation is not yet assured. Regionally, the Case-Shiller May data (the most recent available, reflecting closings roughly 60-90 days prior) shows the two-speed market sharpening: Chicago is at +6.91% YoY and New York at +4.03%, while Denver is -1.70%, Phoenix -1.34%, Tampa -1.76%, and Seattle -1.77%. Agents in Midwest and Northeast markets are operating in an appreciating environment; agents in Sun Belt metros should expect continued seller concession pressure.
