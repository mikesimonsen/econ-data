**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but showed the first cracks worth watching. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales stood at +4.81% YoY through June 26 — 55 consecutive weeks above year-ago levels — and the 4-week average remained positive at +4.37% YoY. The spot week, however, printed -2.99% YoY, the third consecutive weekly decline, which pulled the 4-week figure down from its recent peak. That divergence between the smooth trend and the spot read is normal summer seasonality until it isn't; the 13-week average is the right anchor for now. Inventory continued to expand — Altos total supply rose +1.17% YoY and has climbed eight straight weeks — but new listings decelerated sharply, with the spot week printing -7.14% YoY after running positive most of the spring. The 4-week average barely held positive at +0.41% YoY, and the 13-week average has been rising 14 weeks but sits at only +0.71% YoY, suggesting the new supply impulse that characterized the first half is fading. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9, up 6.71% YoY — still elevated, though the pace of deterioration is not accelerating. June existing home sales came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating the 3.85M consensus by a wide margin and running +4.07% YoY; on an NSA basis, closed sales were up 7.93% from June 2025, with the South and West both contributing. New home sales ticked up to 628K SAAR in June but remain -5.14% YoY — builder price concessions are doing real work, with median new-home prices down 2.66% YoY and average prices off 6.55% YoY. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.82% as of August 3, essentially flat on the month after a brief rise. The spread to the 10-year Treasury remains 2.13 percentage points — roughly 45 basis points above the long-run norm of ~1.70pp. That spread has compressed 22-26 basis points YoY; if it closes another 20-25bp without the 10-year moving, the 30-year rate falls to roughly 6.55-6.60% on spread mechanics alone. On leading indicators: the MBA purchase 4-week average sits at -1.35% YoY and has fallen four straight weeks, which is the weaker read in this dataset. Xactus MII, which leads MBA by 2-4 weeks, broke its recent decline with a +3.53% week and the 4-week average reversed upward — a modest divergence that argues the MBA softness may not persist into late August. The more consequential supply signal is in permits: total permits missed expectations and fell -1.36% YoY, driven by a -5.87% YoY drop in multifamily. Single-family permits at 872K are the highest since May 2024 and just turned positive YoY, but starts outpaced permits in June, meaning builders are completing the pipeline faster than they are authorizing new units. That configuration supports near-term inventory from completions but implies less single-family supply reaching the market in 2027. Watch whether the Altos new-listings 4-week average recovers from its July dip — that will determine whether the inventory expansion continues or stalls into fall.

The macro picture shifted in a rate-friendly direction this month, though not cleanly enough to move the market decisively. June CPI came in well below consensus — headline -0.42% month-over-month versus an expected +0.9%, and core essentially flat versus an expected +0.3%. Core PCE for June followed at +0.13% month-over-month against a +0.3% consensus. Together these prints suggest the disinflationary trend that stalled earlier this year has resumed, which matters for housing because Fed rate-cut expectations are the primary driver of the 10-year yield, which in turn anchors the 30-year mortgage rate. The 10-year closed at 4.66% on August 4, down a few basis points on the week; if the next CPI and PCE prints confirm this disinflation trajectory, the 10-year has room to drift toward 4.4-4.5%, which would pull the 30-year mortgage rate lower even before the spread compresses further. The labor market is the counterweight: June payrolls came in at 159K, unemployment fell to 4.2% (beating a 4.4% consensus), and initial jobless claims for the week ending July 25 printed 197K against a 212K expectation — a notably clean labor read that reduces the urgency of near-term Fed action. That said, the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) stands at 1.937 million, up 17.3% YoY even after a small monthly decline.
