# Monthly Housing Summary — August 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has held positive YoY for 55 consecutive weeks, and the 4-week average came in at +4.4% YoY through late June — a durable demand signal even as the spot week dipped to -3.0% YoY on normal seasonal softening. That momentum, however, is now running into rate headwinds: the 30-year fixed ticked back up to 6.76% as of August 20, having reversed a multi-week decline, with the 10-year Treasury holding at 4.71%. The spread between the 30-year and the 10-year stands at 2.06 percentage points — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points, which means meaningful rate relief remains possible without any Fed action if lenders compress margins. Inventory from Altos rose for eight consecutive weeks through late June and sat just above 1.07 million units, up about 1.2% YoY, while new listings on a 4-week average were roughly flat YoY — the inventory growth spurt of early spring has stalled. The July NAR existing sales print came in at 4.06 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million by a notable margin, though the number is down slightly from June. On a YoY basis existing sales are up 1.3% — and the NSA read confirms +2.8% YoY — marking six consecutive months of positive annual comparisons. New home sales tell a softer story: June printed 628K SAAR, down 5.1% YoY, with average prices falling sharply enough to flag as statistically unusual, which likely reflects builders mixing toward lower price points to clear the 9.3-month supply on hand. July housing starts were the headline that will rattle some clients: 1,239K SAAR, down 12.4% from June and 12.8% YoY, missing expectations of 1,350K. Single-family starts fell 9.9% in the month and are down 15% YoY. But read alongside permits, the picture is less alarming than the starts number alone implies — total permits rose 5.0% to 1,443K SAAR, the highest since January 2024, with single-family permits up 2.5% to 894K, the highest since May 2024. Builders appear to be authorizing future supply while pausing groundbreakings on projects already in the pipeline, likely waiting for rate and cost clarity. The MBA purchase index 4-week average was -1.2% YoY through August 14, soft but not deteriorating sharply. The Xactus MII — which typically leads MBA by 2 to 4 weeks — had a spot reading of -5.6% YoY in late June before the 4-week average firmed to -2.3%, a mild divergence worth watching: if the MII does not continue recovering through August, the MBA index is likely to weaken further into September. Pending days on market at 70.9 days are running 6.7% above year-ago levels, a sign that buyer urgency has eased even where demand is still nominally positive. Watch whether inventory growth resumes in August data as new listing supply, which the 13-week Altos average still shows slightly positive, translates into active stock — or whether the late-June new listings dip was the start of a seasonal retreat that keeps supply constrained into fall.

The most consequential macro development this month was the July jobs report. Payrolls came in at -23K against a consensus of +59K — a miss large enough to shift expectations — while the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating the 4.4% forecast. That combination is internally inconsistent in a way that warrants some caution: the payroll survey and household survey are telling different stories, which happens at turning points. Average hourly earnings grew just 0.1% in the month, well below the 0.3% consensus, and are running at 3.15% YoY — still above the Fed's implicit comfort zone but decelerating in a way that matters for shelter inflation's eventual trajectory. Core PCE for June printed at +0.1% month-over-month against an expected +0.3%, and core CPI for July came in at +0.2% versus a +0.3% consensus. Shelter CPI continues to run at 3.2% YoY, still elevated, but the market knows that real-time rent growth — the Zillow ZORI is at +3.0% YoY with a slight softening in July — will pull shelter CPI lower with its usual 12-to-18-month lag, which is a disinflationary force building into 2027. For housing, the inflation and payroll combination matters because it is keeping the Fed on hold while also preventing any bond-market rally that would bring mortgage rates down: the 10-year is at 4.71%, up 38 basis points YoY, and until that moves, the spread compression story — not Fed cuts — is the more plausible near-term path to lower mortgage costs.
