**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive over the four-week period, though the momentum is decelerating at the edges. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sat at roughly +4.8% YoY and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks — a durable streak — but the spot week (ending June 26) dipped to -3.0% YoY, pulling the 4-week average down to +4.4% from higher earlier in the quarter. That spot softness bears watching; one weak week in summer is noise, but the direction of the 4-week average has been easing for several weeks. Inventory continued rising — Altos total active listings were up roughly +1.2% YoY — but new listings decelerated sharply, with the spot week at -7.1% YoY after several months of positive YoY readings. The 4-week average held barely positive at +0.4%, and the 13-week trend remains constructive at +0.7%. Pending days on market have been elevated for 103 consecutive weeks, and remain about 6.7% above year-ago — not a market in distress, but not one snapping homes off the shelf either. NAR's July existing sales print at 4.06 million SAAR beat expectations (3.85M consensus) by a meaningful margin and held +1.25% YoY, though the SAAR level ticked down slightly month-over-month and NSA sales were +2.8% YoY — the sixth consecutive positive YoY month on that basis. Inventory in the NAR data actually flipped negative YoY for the first time in several months, landing at 1.54M units and 4.6 months supply, which partly explains the beat. New home sales (June, 628K SAAR) recovered off recent weakness but remain -5.1% YoY, and average new home prices fell sharply in June — a sign builders are working through higher-cost pipeline at discounts. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.73% today, up slightly off recent lows, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at approximately 2.02 points — still elevated relative to the historical norm of around 1.7 points, but compressing: the spread is down about 23 basis points YoY. If that compression continues toward historical norms without the 10-year moving, the 30-year could fall meaningfully without any Fed action. On leading indicators: the MBA purchase 4-week average was essentially flat YoY (-0.2%), recovering from a few weeks of softness but far from strong. The Xactus MII — which leads MBA applications by two to four weeks by capturing mortgage inquiries earlier in the funnel — turned upward in late June after several weeks of decline, with its 4-week average now at -2.3% YoY but improving. The divergence between the MII's recovery and MBA's still-subdued level suggests purchase application activity may firm modestly in coming weeks. Watch whether that MII upturn translates into MBA data over the next four weeks, and watch whether spot pending sales can recover back above zero YoY.

The macro backdrop delivered a notable split this month: the labor market surprised in opposite directions simultaneously. July payrolls came in at roughly -23K against a +59K consensus — a material miss — while the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% against a 4.4% consensus expectation, and initial claims at 209K remain historically low. The payroll miss matters for purchase confidence, but the unemployment rate and claims data suggest the labor market is cooling in volume without yet generating widespread job loss — a distinction that matters for mortgage performance more than purchase intent. Average hourly earnings grew just +0.1% month-over-month versus a +0.3% expectation, which compounds the income softness story. On inflation: July CPI headline came in at +0.1% MoM (consensus expected +0.9%, a striking miss), with core CPI at +0.2% versus +0.3% expected. Core PCE for June was similarly cool at +0.1% MoM against a +0.3% expectation. Shelter CPI held at +3.2% YoY — still elevated but rising only slowly, consistent with the lagged passthrough of real-time rent data that has been softening for over a year. Consumer confidence at 90.8 beat expectations but is still down 8% YoY, suggesting buyers are present but cautious.
