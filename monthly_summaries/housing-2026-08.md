**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through the summer, though the weekly read softened at the margin. The Altos 13-week average of new pending sales sits +4.81% year-over-year and has been in positive territory for 55 consecutive weeks — the underlying trend remains constructive even as the most recent weekly print dipped 2.99% YoY, which is typical late-summer noise. The 4-week average at +4.37% YoY keeps the signal net-positive. Inventory continues to build slowly: Altos total active supply rose for eight consecutive weeks through late June and is up about 1.2% YoY, while new listings on the 13-week average are running just +0.71% YoY — supply is growing, but not fast enough to shift negotiating power meaningfully toward buyers. Pending days on market at 70.9 days is up roughly 6.7% YoY, a reminder that buyers still have more time than they did in 2022-23 but the market is not accelerating in their direction. NAR's July existing home sales came in at a 4.06 million SAAR, beating consensus by roughly 210,000 units and up 1.25% YoY — the second positive YoY print in a row, though the SAAR ticked down slightly month-over-month. New home sales (June, SAAR 628K) remain 5.1% below year-ago levels, and the median new-home price has fallen to $398,300, down 2.7% YoY, reflecting builders' ongoing use of incentives and mix-shift to smaller product to clear supply. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.78% as of August 24, essentially flat over the past four weeks. The more interesting story is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at roughly 2.07 percentage points on the daily read (2.03 on the weekly average), the spread is still elevated relative to the historical norm of about 1.7 points, but has compressed 23-25 basis points year-over-year. Every 10 basis points of further spread compression is roughly $25/month on a $400K mortgage — this is the affordability lever most buyers aren't watching. On leading indicators, the MBA purchase index 4-week average is running -1.2% YoY and has turned negative in two consecutive readings after being positive; the 13-week average (+0.83% YoY) is decelerating. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead the MBA by 2-4 weeks — bounced in its most recent reading (4-week avg +2.31%), suggesting the MBA weakness may not deepen immediately. Watch whether the MII holds that recovery through September; if it rolls back over, purchase application volume likely follows. July's housing starts report — total starts down 12.4% from June and 12.75% YoY, badly missing expectations — is alarming at the headline, but the permit picture is the one to weight more heavily for forward supply: total permits rose 5.0% in July to 1,443K SAAR, the highest since early 2024, with single-family permits up 2.2% YoY. Builders are pulling back on starting spec homes but still filing permits, consistent with a pipeline clearing rather than a structural retreat. The implication for 2027 inventory is mildly supportive; for the 2026 selling season it changes little.

The macro backdrop shifted modestly in a rate-friendly direction this month, though not enough to move the needle on affordability. July payrolls came in at roughly -23K versus a consensus expectation of +59K — the miss is notable, but the unemployment rate simultaneously fell 0.1 point to 4.1%, well below the 4.4% consensus, which limits the alarm. The two readings point in opposite directions partly because of labor force participation dynamics; what matters for housing is that the consumer's job situation looks stable, not deteriorating. The long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) fell in July to 1.771 million, down 8.6% month-over-month, reversing an earlier build — this is worth watching over a longer horizon as a delinquency leading indicator, but for now it is not a near-term threat. On inflation, both headline CPI (+0.1% in July, well below the +0.9% consensus) and core PCE (+0.1% in June vs. +0.3% expected) came in softer than forecast, with core PCE at 3.29% YoY. Shelter CPI ticked up 0.14% in July and is running +3.16% YoY — still the stickiest component of core inflation, and the reason the Fed cannot declare victory even as other components cool. Core PCE at 3.3% is meaningfully above the 2% target; the Fed's path to easing remains constrained. What is moving rates at the margin is the 10-year Treasury, which fell 5 basis points on August 25 to 4.66% and appears to be reflecting the soft payroll print and cooler inflation rather than any Fed pivot signal.
