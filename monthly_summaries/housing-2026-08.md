**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through most of July, but the most recent weekly read deserves attention. Altos new pending sales came in at roughly 85,800 for the week ending June 26 — down 3.0% year-over-year, snapping what had been a 55-week streak of positive YoY readings. The spot week is one data point; the 4-week average remains up 4.4% YoY, and the 13-week average is up 4.8% YoY and has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks. The underlying trend is still constructive, but watch whether the spot weakness persists into July or was a seasonal blip. Inventory rose for the eighth consecutive week to just over 1.07 million units, up 1.2% YoY — a modest build by historical standards. New listings told a more nuanced story: the spot week fell 7.1% YoY, but the 4-week average held essentially flat (+0.4%) and the 13-week average is up 0.7% on an improving trend, suggesting the brief positive YoY streak in new supply is softening, not collapsing. Pending days on market at 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY for the 103rd consecutive week, confirms a market that is absorbing inventory more slowly than 2024. NAR June existing home sales printed at 4.09 million SAAR — well above the 3.85 million consensus — though they edged down from May on a seasonally adjusted basis; on a raw NSA basis, June transactions were up 7.9% YoY, the fifth consecutive month of year-over-year gains. New home sales also ticked up in June to 628,000 SAAR, but remain down 5.1% YoY, and the average new home price fell sharply — a signal that builders are selling through lower price tiers and incentivizing aggressively rather than pulling sales forward on strength. The 30-year fixed rate closed July at 6.83%, reversing a recent modest decline, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury sitting at 2.13–2.15 percentage points against a historical norm closer to 1.7. That 40–45 basis point excess spread means the affordability story does not require Fed action — normalization of that spread alone, as lender margins compress, could bring the 30-year to the low-to-mid 6s under current Treasury conditions. MBA purchase applications for the week of July 24 were up 2.7% YoY but the 4-week average has turned slightly negative (-1.4% YoY) and has fallen four consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA applications by roughly two to four weeks — bounced in late June (+3.5% on the week, 4-week average reversing upward), which suggests the MBA softness may be transient rather than the start of a new trend. What to watch in August: whether the Altos 4-week pending average holds positive, whether the Xactus MII rebound translates into MBA purchase application recovery, and whether single-family permits — which just turned positive YoY for the first time since May 2024 — can sustain that signal as a leading indicator for 2027 supply.

The macro backdrop for housing is probably better than the headline mood suggests. June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 — solid, and the unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, beating expectations of 4.4%. More relevant for housing: average hourly earnings are running at 3.5% YoY, which holds real income growth in positive territory at current inflation levels. Core PCE came in at +0.13% in June (below the 0.3% consensus), bringing the YoY to 3.3%; headline CPI was essentially flat on the month, also well below expectations. For housing, the inflation print matters on two levels: it keeps the 10-year Treasury in check (the 10-year held near 4.69% this week, roughly where it has been), and it reduces the probability of a Fed that tightens further. The mechanism is indirect but real — if inflation continues to decelerate toward 2.5% on core PCE, the 10-year has room to drift lower, which is the precondition for mortgage rates falling meaningfully without any spread compression at all. The long-term unemployed cohort (those out of work 27+ weeks) dipped in June to 1.937 million but remains 17% above year-ago levels; this is not a current-quarter selling-season story, but it is the early accumulation that historically precedes elevated mortgage delinquency by 6 to 12 months. Regionally, the Case-Shiller May data (reported July 29) confirmed the two-speed dynamic that has defined 2025–2026: Chicago gained 6.9% YoY, New York 4.0%, Boston 2.6%, all accelerating or firm; meanwhile Dallas is down 1.0%, Denver down 1.7%, Seattle down 1.8%, Tampa down 1.8%, and Phoenix down 1.3%, all negative for at least nine months.
