**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive on a trend basis through late June but has since softened at the margin. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales was running +4.81% year-over-year as of June 26, extending a streak of 55 consecutive weeks of positive YoY readings on that smoothed basis — the underlying trend remains constructive. The spot week, however, fell 3.0% below the prior year, and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.4% YoY, flagging some loss of momentum heading into the back half of summer. Inventory continued to expand gradually: Altos total active supply was up roughly 1.2% YoY in late June, while new listings on a 4-week average basis held nearly flat YoY (+0.4%) after a brief stretch of outright gains — new listing growth is stalling, not accelerating, which limits the supply-side pressure on prices. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9, up 6.7% YoY, consistent with a market that is moving but not racing. NAR's July existing home sales print came in at 4.06 million SAAR, +1.25% YoY and well ahead of the 3.85 million consensus, though the month-over-month dip of 0.7% ended a short rising streak. More notable is the divergence between existing and new home sales: new home sales fell 10.5% in July to a 607,000 SAAR, turning negative YoY (-5.0%) while new home months supply jumped to 9.6 — builders are accumulating unsold inventory and cutting median prices (-0.9% YoY). The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.74% as of August 25, ticking down from recent highs. The spread to the 10-year Treasury — currently around 2.08 basis points above the 10-year yield of 4.65% — remains well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 percentage points; that 35-40bp of excess spread represents the hidden affordability lever. If spreads compress toward normal as credit conditions stabilize, the 30-year could fall meaningfully without any Fed action. On leading indicators: the MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 3.3% YoY and falling, and the 13-week average — positive for 80 consecutive weeks — has decelerated sharply to essentially flat (+0.02% YoY). The Xactus MII, which typically leads MBA by 2-4 weeks, posted a bounce in late June (+3.5% spot, 4-week average turning up) after several weeks of decline, suggesting the MBA softness may not deepen further immediately — watch whether MII holds its recovery over the next four weeks. Builder permits rose 5.2% YoY in July and beat expectations, with single-family permits reaching their highest level since May 2024. But starts collapsed 12.4% in July versus a year ago, missing forecasts badly. That permits-up, starts-down configuration signals builders are authorized but not yet breaking ground — the current pipeline is clearing (completions are falling too, down 16.7% YoY) and future supply is not being restocked at pace. The implication for inventory two years out is tighter than the permit headline implies.

The macro backdrop in July was cleaner than it looked on first read. Headline payrolls missed badly — the economy added roughly 23,000 net jobs against a 59,000 consensus — but the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, well below the 4.4% expected, and initial jobless claims for the week of August 15 came in at 206,000, well below forecast and down nearly 12% year-over-year. That combination suggests the payroll miss may reflect measurement noise rather than a genuine deterioration in labor demand; the JOLTS hires rate ticked up to 3.4 and quits held at 2.0, neither consistent with a labor market that is cracking. Long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) fell by more than 8% in July to 1.771 million and is now down modestly YoY — the cohort that would eventually feed delinquency pipelines is not accumulating right now, which is a quiet positive for mortgage credit quality over the next 6-12 months. On inflation, Core PCE came in at +3.34% YoY in July, running above the Fed's target and above core CPI (+2.47% YoY) — the PCE/CPI gap is partly mechanical (shelter CPI, at +3.16% YoY, is still sticky and weighted differently across the two indexes). The Fed is not in a position to cut while core PCE holds above 3%, and the 10-year at 4.65% reflects that: rates are not falling because the Fed is moving; they would need to fall because the spread compresses. Consumer confidence softened to 90.8 in July, down 8% YoY, but beat its own weak forecast — households are cautious, not panicked.
