**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held its footing in July even as the weekly numbers softened at the edges. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% year-over-year and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks — the underlying trend remains constructive even though the most recent weekly read turned slightly negative (-2.99% YoY) after three weeks of declines. The 4-week average, at +4.37% YoY, is a more reliable signal right now, and it held positive. Total active inventory reached roughly 1.07 million homes, up about 1.2% YoY, a pace that is no longer accelerating — new listings ticked negative YoY on the most recent weekly read (-7.1%) after several months of modest gains, and the 4-week average has decelerated to near-flat (+0.4% YoY). Pending days on market at 70.9 days is running 6.7% above last year, which tells you homes are sitting a bit longer, but not alarmingly so. The June NAR print — 4.09 million SAAR, up 4.1% YoY and well above the 3.85 million consensus — confirmed that demand is real, not just a statistical artifact; the NSA count of 422,000 closings was up 7.9% from June 2025. New home sales (628,000 SAAR) ticked up in June but remain 5.1% below last year, and new home median prices fell to $398,300, down 2.7% YoY — builders are clearly buying down rates and trimming prices to clear inventory, which is showing up in the 9.3 months of new supply. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.82% as of today, essentially unchanged over the past month. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.13 percentage points — still roughly 40-45 basis points above the historical norm of around 1.70 points — meaning if credit and volatility conditions normalize, there is room for mortgage rates to fall meaningfully without any Fed action. Watch for that compression. On the leading-indicator side: the MBA purchase index 4-week average is -1.35% YoY and has fallen for four consecutive weeks, a softening worth monitoring. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks by tracking credit-pull activity before a borrower ever calls a lender) showed a reversal upward in late June — its 4-week average improved to -2.3% YoY from deeper negative readings. That MII uptick, if it holds, should show up as a stabilization in MBA applications in mid-to-late August. Single-family permits (872,000 SAAR, +0.7% YoY) turned positive for the first time since May 2024, but total permits missed expectations and fell 1.4% YoY, with the gap driven by a pullback in multifamily. Starts jumped in June on a large multifamily swing; single-family starts fell for a third consecutive month to 895,000 SAAR, -3.2% YoY. The pipeline under construction continues to shrink — down 6.2% YoY — meaning the completion wave that has been pressuring rents and new-home pricing is slowly winding down.

The macro backdrop shifted more favorably for housing during July than the headlines suggested. June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 — solid, not exceptional — but the unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, beating the 4.4% consensus, and initial jobless claims in late July printed at 197,000, well below the 212,000 expectation. An employed household is a potential buyer; the headline labor market is not deteriorating. The more important nuance is the long-term unemployed cohort: 1.937 million people have been out of work 27 weeks or more, up 17.3% year-over-year even as it ticked down this month. That accumulation does not affect the current selling season, but it is the seed of future mortgage stress — watch it. On inflation, June core PCE rose just 0.13% month-over-month (3.29% YoY), cooler than the 0.3% consensus, and June core CPI was essentially flat, also below expectations. Neither the Fed nor the bond market is being pushed toward higher rates by inflation right now. The 10-year Treasury sits at 4.69%, up 47 basis points from a year ago but ticking down at the margin.
