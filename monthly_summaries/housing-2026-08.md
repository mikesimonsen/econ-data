**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through most of the period, though the most recent Altos weekly read showed new pending sales dipping 3.0% below year-ago levels — the first negative YoY week in over a year. The four-week average remains constructive at +4.4% YoY, and the 13-week average has now run positive for 55 consecutive weeks, so one soft week does not break the trend. What it does signal is that the mid-summer seasonal slowdown arrived on schedule and the YoY cushion that has supported demand headlines since mid-2024 is narrowing. Inventory at roughly 1.075 million active listings sits just 1.2% above last year — growth that has been decelerating — and new listings ticked down 7.1% YoY on the spot read, though the four-week average is still slightly positive (+0.4%), suggesting the single-week drop may be noise rather than a listings withdrawal. Pending days on market at 70.9 days are running about 6.7% above last year, a steady reminder that the pace of absorption has softened even as headline sales numbers look decent. NAR existing home sales came in at 4.09 million SAAR for June, beating the 3.85 million consensus by a meaningful margin and running +4.1% YoY on the SAAR basis; the NSA count of 422,000 was up +7.9% YoY, which is the directionally cleaner read given the seasonal noise in this series. New home sales at 628,000 SAAR ticked higher but remain down 5.1% YoY, and the average new home price dropped an unusual 9.5% month-over-month — driven by builders mixing down toward more affordable price points, not a true market price collapse. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.75% as of August 5th, roughly flat on the month. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.13 percentage points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points — and has held flat for four weeks. That spread is the rate story agents should be telling clients: the 10-year has been drifting lower (4.62% as of August 5th, down 23bp year-over-year), but the spread compression that would bring the 30-year meaningfully toward 6.25–6.40% has not materialized yet. MBA purchase applications flipped negative YoY as of July 31st (-2.5% on the spot week, -0.2% on the four-week average), the first YoY negative reading in 77 weeks on the 13-week basis; the 13-week average still shows +2.3% YoY, so the underlying trend has not reversed — but it is decelerating. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks, showed a small recovery in late June after seven weeks of decline, with the four-week average turning up. If that MII uptick holds, the MBA softness may prove transitory rather than the start of a sustained negative run. Watch the next two MBA weekly prints for confirmation. On the supply side, June single-family starts fell 3.2% YoY for a third consecutive month, and single-family permits — though technically +0.7% YoY — rolled over month-over-month. Builders are completing units from the existing pipeline (single-family completions +4.8% YoY) while pulling back on new authorizations in the South, where permits fell 8.7% YoY. That dynamic tightens resale competition with new inventory in 2027, not 2026.

The macro backdrop is doing more to support housing than the headlines suggest. June CPI came in materially cooler than expected — headline -0.4% month-over-month versus the +0.9% consensus, with core essentially flat at -0.02% — and core PCE for June printed +0.13%, below the 0.3% consensus. Three consecutive months of sub-consensus inflation readings have done real work on rate expectations: the 10-year has fallen roughly 40bp since its recent peak, and the mechanism that matters for housing is that lower long-duration yields create the precondition for spread compression. The spread needs to compress, not just the 10-year, and at 2.13pp it remains stubbornly wide; if it narrows 25–30bp alongside a continued Treasury rally, the 30-year could drift toward 6.25–6.40% without any Fed action. The labor market is more complicated. The headline June unemployment rate came in at 4.2%, better than the 4.4% consensus, and payrolls added 159,000 — solid enough. Hires and quits rates both firmed in June, suggesting job mobility has not seized up. The signal worth watching is the long-term unemployed cohort: 1.937 million workers have been unemployed 27 weeks or longer, up 17.3% year-over-year even as the monthly count ticked down slightly. That cohort does not threaten the current selling season, but it is the leading population for mortgage stress and eventual distressed inventory — a slow accumulation worth tracking quarterly.
