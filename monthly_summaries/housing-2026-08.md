**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through most of the summer, though the most recent Altos weekly read showed some softening at the edges. New pending sales came in at -3.0% YoY for the week ending June 26, snapping what had been a 55-week streak of positive YoY readings on the 13-week average — that average still sits at +4.8% YoY and has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks, which is the number that matters for trend. The 4-week average was +4.4% YoY, though decelerating. Inventory rose for eight straight weeks to roughly 1.07 million units, up about 1.2% YoY — growth is present but clearly decelerating relative to the double-digit YoY expansions of 2024-25. New listings on a 4-week basis were barely positive YoY (+0.4%), and the single-week read turned -7.1% YoY, suggesting the supply side is starting to pull back alongside demand rather than outrunning it. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are taking longer to go under contract than a year ago, which is the quiet signal to watch on listing appointments. NAR existing sales for July printed 4.06 million SAAR, +1.25% YoY and notably beat expectations of 3.85 million — though the headline SAAR slipped 0.7% from June, so this is a beat against a low bar. The NSA YoY was +2.8%, the sixth consecutive positive month. New home sales (June, the most recent read) edged up to 628K SAAR but remain down 5.1% YoY, and builder median prices fell again, now -2.7% YoY — builders are cutting price to move standing inventory, and months supply at 9.3 is elevated enough to keep that pressure on. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.69% as of August 13, having drifted down slightly over the past two days after a brief rise. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.07 percentage points — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. If that spread compresses toward 1.8-1.9 points, the 30-year falls meaningfully without any Fed action, and that remains the most underappreciated affordability lever in the market. MBA purchase applications for the week ending August 7 were -0.2% YoY on the 4-week average — essentially flat, and recovering from a recent dip. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-inquiry-based leading indicator that tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks) showed its 4-week average at -2.3% YoY, still negative, which suggests purchase application momentum stays muted into September. Watch whether the MII continues recovering from its June trough; if it does, fall demand could firm. Single-family permits came in at 872K SAAR for June, +0.7% YoY — the first positive YoY read since May 2024 — but the starts data told a more complex story: total starts jumped 19% in June on a massive multifamily surge (5+ unit starts up 76%), while single-family starts fell for a third consecutive month. The implication is builders are completing the multifamily pipeline but not adding meaningfully to future single-family supply.

On the macro side, three prints shaped the housing backdrop this month. First, the July jobs report missed badly — payrolls came in roughly 80,000 below the 59K consensus gain, with the actual change reading as a small decline, while the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% and beat the 4.4% consensus, and average hourly earnings rose only 0.1% versus the 0.3% expected. The combination of a soft payroll number and cooling wages reduces near-term rate pressure, but also raises the question of whether job-market weakness starts feeding through to buyer confidence — consumer confidence already sits at 90.8, down 8% YoY. Second, both July CPI (core +2.47% YoY, headline +3.3%) and June core PCE (+3.29% YoY) came in cooler than expected, with core CPI decelerating and core PCE printing +0.13% month-over-month. That is not at the Fed's 2% target, but it gives the Fed room to hold or signal cuts without losing credibility — and any rate-cut signal will be reflected immediately in the 10-year, which at 4.66% remains the anchor on the 30-year.
