**August 2026 Housing Summary**

New pending sales posted a weekly YoY decline of -3.0% for the week ending June 26, breaking what had been a positive streak, though the four-week average held at +4.4% YoY and the 13-week average — the smoothed read Mike watches most closely — remained at +4.8% YoY, positive for 55 consecutive weeks. That durability matters more than the single-week dip, which looks seasonal. Inventory on the Altos tracker reached 1.07 million active listings, up 1.2% YoY, rising for eight consecutive weeks, though new listings fell 7.1% YoY in that same week — the four-week average held a thin +0.4% YoY — signaling that the inventory build is slowing rather than accelerating as sellers step back from a market where pending days on market have stretched to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY. The NAR July existing home sales print came in at 4.06 million SAAR, down fractionally from June but beating consensus of 3.85 million handily, up 1.3% YoY on a seasonally adjusted basis and +2.8% NSA YoY — six consecutive months of positive YoY comparisons. NAR inventory slipped to 1.54 million units, tipping -0.7% YoY for the first time in many months, which reinforces the Altos new-listings deceleration. New home sales (June, 628K SAAR) remain 5% below year-ago levels, and new home average prices dropped sharply — a signal builders are mixing down toward more affordable product to clear the 9.3 months of supply still on their lots. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.79% as of today, 21 basis points above a year ago, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.11-2.13 points — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That spread has compressed 20-25bp over the past year, meaning rates have fallen more than Treasury yields alone would imply; if it compresses another 20bp toward 1.9, the 30-year could drift meaningfully lower without any Fed action. The leading indicators are the complication: the MBA purchase index four-week average was -0.2% YoY through July 31, barely positive and deteriorating from the +2.3% read on the 13-week average. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which captures credit-pull activity 2-4 weeks before it shows in MBA data — bounced off recent lows in late June (four-week avg +2.3% YoY after a string of negative prints) but the 13-week MII remains -2.4% YoY and falling. When MII and MBA diverge this way, MII tends to be right. Watch whether the late-June MII bounce translates into a recovery in MBA purchase applications through August, or fades — that will tell you whether the second half selling season firms or softens further.

The macro backdrop this month delivered a genuinely unusual combination: a payroll print that missed consensus by roughly 82,000 jobs (-23K vs. +59K expected) alongside an unemployment rate that beat at 4.1% vs. 4.4% expected, falling two months running. The divergence — fewer jobs added, lower unemployment — likely reflects labor force participation dynamics rather than underlying strength, and agents should not read the unemployment headline as a green light. Average hourly earnings rose only 0.1% MoM against a 0.3% expectation, and Core PCE came in at +0.13% MoM (+3.3% YoY) — both softer than expected, which is relevant to housing because lower wage growth moderates the Fed's inflation concern without directly boosting purchasing power. Shelter CPI continues to drift higher (+3.3% YoY), still overstating real-time rent conditions — Zillow's ZORI is running +3.3% YoY but from a much lower base, and the two series are slowly converging. The headline CPI for June printed -0.4% MoM, well below the 0.9% expectation; that softness keeps the door open for eventual Fed cuts but the 10-year Treasury at 4.68% — up 41bp YoY — is the real affordability ceiling right now, not the Fed funds rate. Regional price performance in the May Case-Shiller data (reference period: March-May 2026, reported July 29) remains sharply split: Chicago is running +6.9% YoY and New York +4.0%, Boston +2.6% and recovering; meanwhile Denver is -1.7%, Seattle -1.8%, Tampa -1.8%, Las Vegas -1.9%, and Phoenix -1.3% — all still negative YoY with no clear inflection. Agents working Sun Belt markets need a different price story than agents in the Midwest and Northeast.
