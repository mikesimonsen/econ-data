# Monthly Housing Summary — August 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The four-week demand picture through late June held better than the spot week suggested. Altos new pending sales came in at -3.0% YoY for the week of June 26, but the four-week average remained positive at +4.4% YoY, and the 13-week average — now 55 consecutive weeks of positive YoY readings — sat at +4.8% YoY and is still rising. That streak is the most durable thing in the current data set. Inventory continued to build, with Altos total active supply up 1.2% YoY for the week, and the new listings 13-week average holding a slim +0.7% YoY gain after 14 weeks of growth — though the spot week's -7.1% YoY print is worth watching; new listings are decelerating heading into the back half of summer. Pending days on market reached 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, signaling that buyers are not moving as urgently as they were a year ago. NAR existing home sales for July printed at a 4.06M SAAR — comfortably above the 3.85M consensus — and up 1.25% YoY, now six months of positive YoY NSA readings. The read-across to new home sales is less clean: the June new home print came in at 628K SAAR, down 5.1% YoY, with median new home prices falling 2.7% YoY and average prices down 6.6%, suggesting builders are actively cutting size and incentivizing to move standing inventory. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.69% today, off slightly from recent highs, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.07-2.08 points — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap means meaningful affordability improvement can arrive without any Fed action: if lenders' risk appetite normalizes and the spread compresses 25-30 basis points, the 30-year falls toward the mid-6s without the 10-year moving at all. The MBA purchase index four-week average is essentially flat YoY at -0.2%, a notable deceleration from the 13-week average's +1.7% YoY. The Xactus MII — which leads MBA applications by two to four weeks by capturing mortgage credit inquiries before a formal application is filed — has its 13-week average down 2.4% YoY and falling for seven consecutive weeks. That divergence is the most actionable leading signal in the data right now: MII has been weaker than MBA, which implies the near-term application pipeline may soften further before it firms. June housing starts beat estimates at 1,427K SAAR, but almost all of that came from multifamily (5+ units up 76% month-over-month); single-family starts fell for the third consecutive month and are down 3.2% YoY. Permits for single-family ticked to a post-May 2024 high at 872K SAAR and turned positive YoY — a green shoot — but total permits missed consensus, and South permits fell 8.7% YoY. Watch whether single-family starts follow permits higher in the July read.

The macro backdrop is genuinely mixed in ways that matter to housing. July payrolls missed by roughly 80K relative to consensus (actual near -23K on the revision-implied level), and hourly earnings came in at +0.1% versus the +0.3% expected — but the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating the 4.4% consensus, and the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) dropped to 1.77 million, reversing a recent rise. The mechanism: the payrolls miss and wage softness reduce the urgency for the Fed to hold rates high, while the drop in long-term unemployed removes — at least for now — a building delinquency signal. July CPI printed +0.1% headline and +0.2% core versus expectations of +0.9% and +0.3% respectively, and June core PCE came in at +0.1% against a +0.3% consensus. Shelter CPI at +3.2% YoY continues to run above where real-time rent data would suggest it should be — the BLS series lags observable rent by 12-18 months — which means core inflation readings will likely continue drifting lower through the fall as the shelter component catches down to where Zillow's ZORI already is (up 3.3% YoY but well off its 2022 peak). That trajectory matters for rate expectations: softer-than-expected inflation data with a weakening labor print shifts the Fed calculus toward earlier easing, which — combined with any spread compression — is the path to mortgage rates with a 6-handle closer to 6.2-6.4. Regionally, the Case-Shiller May data (the most recent available, reflecting contracts from February-March) continues to show the two-speed market clearly: Chicago +6.9% YoY, New York +4.0%, Boston +2.6%, Cleveland +3.1% versus Dallas -1.0%, Denver -1.7%, Phoenix -1.
