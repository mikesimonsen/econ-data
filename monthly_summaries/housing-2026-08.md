**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through the four weeks ending late June, though the headline reads require some unpacking. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales is running +4.81% year-over-year and has been in positive territory for 55 consecutive weeks — that streak is the real signal, and it has not broken. The spot week did dip -2.99% YoY, ending a positive run, but the 4-week average remained +4.37% YoY, decelerating from prior readings. Watch the spot number: three consecutive negative weeks is worth attention; one is noise. Inventory rose for an eighth straight week to roughly 1.07 million active listings, up about 1.2% YoY — growth that has slowed materially from the double-digit YoY gains seen earlier in the cycle. New listings tell an interesting story: the single-week read tipped -7.1% YoY, but the 4-week average held barely positive at +0.41% YoY and the 13-week average has been rising for 14 weeks. Supply is not accelerating into the market. Pending days on market, at 70.9 days and +6.7% YoY, continue to edge up — the market is taking slightly longer to clear than a year ago, which is consistent with a balanced rather than pressured environment. NAR's June existing sales came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million by a meaningful margin; on a NSA YoY basis, sales were +7.9%, and the momentum is broad — every region posted positive YoY NSA gains. New home sales at 628,000 SAAR ticked higher but remain -5.1% YoY, with the average new home price falling sharply (-9.5% month-over-month, -6.6% YoY) as builders discount aggressively to clear the 9.3 months of supply still sitting on their lots. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.82% as of August 3, essentially flat over the past month. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.13 percentage points, it remains roughly 40-45 basis points above the historical norm of about 1.70 points. That gap is the hidden affordability lever — it does not require a Fed move to close. On leading indicators: the MBA purchase index 4-week average is -1.35% YoY and has fallen four consecutive weeks, a softening worth monitoring. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA applications by two to four weeks — reversed higher in late June, with its 4-week average turning up after a decline. That divergence, MII recovering while MBA weakens, suggests the MBA softness may be transient rather than trend. Builder permits fell -2.6% month-over-month in June and missed expectations, with multifamily permits (-5.9% YoY) pulling the aggregate lower; single-family permits remain barely positive YoY. The starts-versus-permits gap — starts jumping 19% in June while permits fell — signals builders are finishing their existing pipeline but not yet committing to the next wave. For the next four weeks, watch whether the Altos 13-week pending average holds above +4%, whether the MII uptick translates into a MBA recovery, and whether the 10-year Treasury stays near 4.66% or retraces — any sustained move toward 4.40-4.50% would pull the 30-year rate closer to 6.5% without any Fed action.

The macro backdrop in July was notably better than expected across nearly every relevant release, and the combination matters for housing. June payrolls came in at approximately 159,000, unemployment fell to 4.2% against a 4.4% consensus, and average hourly earnings rose 3.5% YoY — a labor market that is cooling at an orderly pace rather than deteriorating. That keeps household formation intact and prevents the kind of forced-seller dynamic that would require distressed pricing. The inflation picture cooperated: headline CPI actually fell -0.4% in June against an expected +0.9% gain, core CPI was essentially flat month-over-month, and core PCE came in at +0.13% against a +0.3% expectation. On a YoY basis, core PCE at 3.29% and shelter CPI at 3.26% remain above the Fed's target — the shelter component, which lags private-market rents by roughly 12-18 months, will take additional time to deflate regardless of what happens to asking rents. Zillow's rent index (ZORI) accelerated to +3.34% YoY in June, its highest reading since June 2023, which implies the shelter CPI relief the market has been anticipating may arrive more slowly than expected. The 10-year Treasury at 4.66% reflects a market that has repriced for rates staying higher longer, and the Cleveland Fed's near-term inflation model and 5-year breakeven inflation expectations — the bond market's implied forecast of average CPI over the next five years — have not fallen far enough to give the Fed cover to cut in September.
