**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held more resilient than the headline rate environment suggests. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% year over year, positive for 55 consecutive weeks, though the 4-week average has decelerated to +4.37% YoY and the most recent weekly read dipped to -2.99% YoY — the first negative spot print in over a year. That one-week dip is worth monitoring but not yet a trend; seasonal softening is normal in late June, and the streak's underlying momentum remains intact. Inventory at Altos registered 1.07 million active listings, up roughly 1.2% YoY, with new listings decelerating — the 4-week average is barely positive at +0.41% YoY and the spot week turned -7.1% YoY. Supply growth is stalling, not building. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, reflecting a market that is slower to clear than a year ago but not broken. NAR's July existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR, up 1.25% YoY and well ahead of the 3.85 million consensus — the third consecutive YoY gain, though the SAAR itself edged down slightly from June. New home sales (June, 628K SAAR) are running 5% below last year, with builders discounting — median new-home prices fell to $398,300, down 2.7% YoY, while the average dropped sharply, suggesting builders are shifting their mix toward more affordable product to move inventory sitting at 9.3 months supply. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.71% today, up slightly on the week as the 10-year Treasury reversed to 4.66%. The more important number is the spread: at 2.05-2.08 points over the 10-year, it remains roughly 35 basis points above the long-run norm of around 1.70. That gap is the hidden affordability lever — if credit markets normalize even partially, the 30-year could fall meaningfully without the Fed doing anything. MBA purchase applications (4-week average) are nearly flat YoY at -0.21%, improved from deeper negative territory earlier in the summer. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks by capturing early-funnel credit-pull activity — was down 5.6% YoY on its spot read but the 4-week average has turned upward, a tentative positive signal heading into late summer. Watch whether the MII's 4-week improvement translates into a sustained MBA recovery in September; if it doesn't, the demand trajectory becomes more complicated.

The macro backdrop shifted in a direction that is net-positive for housing, though the composition requires attention. July payrolls missed consensus by roughly 82,000 jobs and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating expectations of 4.4% — a pairing that probably reflects measurement noise as much as fundamental strength, but the direction on unemployment is unambiguously helpful for buyer confidence. Average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% month over month versus expectations of 0.3%, and July CPI came in at +0.1% headline and +0.2% core, both below consensus. Core PCE for June printed at +0.13%, also below the Fed's effective threshold. Taken together, the inflation readings reduce the probability that the Fed needs to hold rates higher for longer, which matters for where the 10-year settles and therefore where the mortgage rate spread compresses toward. The mechanism: softer wage growth and cooler core inflation lower the expected path of Fed funds, which anchors the 10-year, which gives lenders room to narrow the spread without taking duration risk — that's how rates fall without a Fed cut. Shelter CPI held at +3.16% YoY, still elevated but decelerating, and the Zillow rent index at +3.34% YoY suggests shelter inflation remains sticky rather than re-accelerating. Regionally, the Case-Shiller May data (released July 29) confirmed the two-speed market: Chicago at +6.9% YoY and New York at +4.0% YoY are appreciating at a pace that changes the listing conversation entirely, while Dallas (-1.0%), Denver (-1.7%), Phoenix (-1.3%), Tampa (-1.8%), and Las Vegas (-1.9%) remain in year-over-year price correction territory — with Tampa's decline now 19 months running. Agents in Sun Belt markets with elevated inventory and builder competition are operating in a fundamentally different pricing environment than their Midwest and Northeast counterparts. The signal to watch in September: whether the long-term unemployed cohort (1.77 million, down this month but still elevated on a YoY basis) continues to shrink, or reverses — it is not a current-season story, but sustained accumulation in that cohort is how delinquency volumes build with a 6-to-12-month lag.
