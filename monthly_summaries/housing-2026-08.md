# Monthly Housing Summary — August 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week pending sales average held positive for the 55th consecutive week through June 26, running at +4.81% year-over-year — a streak that has been the clearest evidence that demand has not broken despite rate levels. The 4-week average sat at +4.37% YoY, though it has been decelerating, and the spot week printed -2.99% YoY, the first negative weekly read in over a year. That single-week dip deserves attention but not panic: spot Altos reads are noisy, and the 4-week average still confirms positive territory. Altos inventory continued rising — up eight consecutive weeks and +1.17% YoY as of June 26 — but new listings growth has flipped. The 4-week new listings average was barely positive at +0.41% YoY and the spot week turned -7.14% YoY, meaning the supply pipeline that had been building since early 2025 is now contracting at the margin. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.71% YoY for the 103rd consecutive week — homes are taking longer to sell than a year ago, full stop. NAR's July existing home sales printed 4.06 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus by a meaningful margin and running +1.25% YoY, though the month-over-month read was -0.73% as the prior two months now show as a short peak. NAR inventory fell to 1.54 million units, flipping year-over-year negative for the first time after months of positive YoY readings — the supply expansion that absorbed demand pressure through early 2026 has quietly reversed. New home sales (628K SAAR in June) remain about 5% below year-ago levels, and average new home prices fell sharply, suggesting builders are cutting to move inventory on 9.3 months of supply. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.76% as of August 10, ticking up two basis points after a brief decline, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.06 points — still about 35 basis points above the long-run norm of roughly 1.70. That gap is the hidden affordability story: if lender risk appetite normalizes even partially, rates could fall 25-30 basis points without any move from the Fed or the Treasury market. MBA purchase applications were -2.53% YoY in the week ending July 31, the first negative print in 77 weeks on the 4-week average basis the trend is still marginally positive at -0.23% YoY but falling for five consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA by two to four weeks — bounced off recent lows in late June, 4-week average +2.31% sequentially, though it remains -2.30% YoY. The divergence between a recovering MII and a softening MBA print is the leading-indicator tension to track through August: if MII's uptick is real, MBA applications should firm by mid-month. On the builder side, June single-family permits held slightly positive YoY at +0.69% while the multifamily permit pipeline contracted sharply; starts data was noisy with a large multi-family jump that likely reflects permit backlog clearing rather than new demand. The number to watch over the next four weeks: whether the Altos 13-week pending average holds above zero and whether MBA applications stabilize as the MII bounce feeds through.

The macro picture this month is genuinely mixed in ways that matter for housing. July payrolls came in at roughly -23,000 against a +59,000 consensus — a miss of that size normally triggers rate-cut speculation, and the 10-year Treasury dipped to 4.68%. But the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating a 4.4% consensus, and initial claims of 199,000 beat by 13,000, so the labor market is not in distress — the payroll number reflects composition and revision noise more than deterioration. For housing, the relevant signal is that the Fed now has modest cover to ease if it chooses; the 10-year at 4.68% and a 2.06-point mortgage spread leaves the 30-year fixed highly sensitive to any further Treasury rally. Average hourly earnings rose only 0.05% month-over-month against a 0.3% expectation, which matters because wage growth — not just rate levels — determines how many households can qualify. At 3.15% YoY earnings growth against a 6.76% mortgage rate, affordability math remains constrained. Core PCE came in at +0.13% for June, below the 0.3% consensus, and sits at 3.29% YoY — still above the Fed's 2% target but decelerating in a way that keeps a September cut on the table. Shelter CPI continues printing above 3% YoY, lagging the real-time rent data by roughly 12-18 months; Zillow's ZORI is now running +3.34% YoY and accelerating, which means shelter inflation will not provide the Fed with relief in the CPI prints ahead.
