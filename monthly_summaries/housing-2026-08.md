**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through most of the summer but is now showing the first credible softening of the year. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has run positive year-over-year for 55 consecutive weeks, and the 4-week average was still up roughly 4.4% YoY as of late June — but the most recent weekly read turned negative (-3.0% YoY), the first such reading in that streak, and the 4-week average is decelerating. That divergence between the smoothed trend and the spot read is worth watching rather than overreacting to; one negative week inside a 55-week positive run is noise until it isn't. Inventory at Altos was up about 1.2% YoY through late June, a meaningful deceleration from the double-digit gains of early 2025, while new listings on a 4-week basis were essentially flat YoY (+0.4%) after briefly turning negative on a spot basis — the new-supply pipeline is not expanding the way it was. Pending days on market at 70.9 days are running about 6.7% above year-ago levels, a gradual but consistent drift toward longer marketing times. NAR existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR for July, beating the 3.85M consensus by a meaningful margin and up 1.25% YoY — the positive YoY trend is now six months old — though NAR inventory flipped negative YoY (-0.65%) for the first time in over two years, which will constrain volume into fall. New home sales (628K SAAR for June) are running about 5% below year-ago levels, and builder median prices have fallen nearly 2.7% YoY; builders are clearly using price and incentives to move existing spec inventory rather than adding new units. July starts fell sharply — down 12.4% from June and 12.8% YoY, missing the 1,350K consensus — but July permits rose 5.0% to 1,443K, beating expectations and reaching their highest since January 2024. The starts-versus-permits divergence suggests builders are clearing their current pipelines while filing authorizations for a future cycle; the supply implication is subdued completions through mid-2027 but a modest recovery in starts thereafter. On rates, the 30-year fixed is at 6.78% as of today, up 24 basis points YoY. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.07 points — still well above the long-run historical norm of roughly 1.7 points, but the 4-week spread average has compressed about 23 basis points YoY. If that compression continues toward 1.85 points, the 30-year falls into the low-to-mid 6% range without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications (4-week average) are down 1.2% YoY through mid-August — a reversal from positive territory — and the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks, was also negative YoY (-2.3% on the 4-week average) as of late June, so the MBA softness is not a surprise. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the Altos weekly pending read recovers above zero YoY or whether the 4-week average follows it lower; that is the cleanest signal on whether the late-summer softness is seasonal noise or something with more durability.

The macro backdrop is mixed in ways that matter differently depending on your market. July payrolls missed badly — the economy added roughly 23,000 jobs against a consensus of 59,000 — but the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating expectations of 4.4%, and initial jobless claims are running nearly 12% below year-ago levels at 206,000. The payroll miss likely reflects seasonal adjustment distortions or late benchmark revisions rather than a genuine demand collapse; the claims data and the unemployment rate are the cleaner reads on labor market health, and both are benign. Long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) ticked down to 1.77 million in July after several months of accumulation — a welcome reversal, though the cohort remains elevated enough to watch as a leading indicator of future distressed-sale pressure if it reverses again. On inflation, core CPI came in at +0.22% month-over-month (+2.47% YoY) and core PCE at +0.13% MoM (+3.29% YoY) through June — both cooler than consensus. Shelter CPI at +3.16% YoY continues its slow deceleration, which is the mechanism through which lagged rent data eventually reduces the reported inflation number and gives the Fed cover to ease. The 10-year Treasury is at 4.71%, up 43 basis points YoY, which is the primary reason mortgage rates are elevated despite the spread compression — the Treasury market, not Fed policy, is doing most of the rate work right now.
