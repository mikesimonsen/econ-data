**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through most of the summer, but the Altos data show it decelerating. The 13-week average for new pending sales is up 4.81% year-over-year — a streak now at 55 consecutive weeks — and the 4-week average remains in positive territory at +4.4% YoY, though both are fading from their spring highs. The spot week ending June 26 actually dipped to -3.0% YoY, the first negative spot read in over a year, so watch whether that weakness is sustained or seasonal noise when July and August weekly reads land. Altos inventory continues to expand — total active listings are up roughly 1.2% YoY and have risen eight straight weeks — but new listings are softening: the spot week fell 7.1% YoY, and while the 4-week average is barely positive (+0.4%), the deceleration is clear. That compression in new supply, if it holds, puts a floor under prices even as the pace of sales cools. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, confirming that buyers are taking longer to commit. NAR's July existing home sales print at 4.06 million SAAR beat expectations (consensus was 3.85M) but came in 0.73% below June — and the NSA read is up just 2.8% YoY, consistent with a market that has stabilized rather than accelerated. NAR inventory flipped to a small YoY decline (-0.65%) for the first time in several months, which is the most meaningful shift in that report. New home sales (June, 628K SAAR) ticked up but remain down 5.1% YoY, with median new home prices falling 2.7% YoY — builders are cutting prices to move standing inventory, not adding to it, which is exactly what the starts-vs.-permits divergence implies: July permits rose to 1,443K SAAR (+6.0% YoY, the highest since January 2024), but starts collapsed to 1,239K SAAR, down 12.4% on the month and 12.7% YoY, missing consensus by over 100K units. Builders are pulling future authorizations while working down what they already have in the ground. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.77% as of August 21, essentially flat over the month. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at roughly 2.06 percentage points, it has compressed 23 basis points YoY and has been narrowing for three straight weeks on the weekly average. The historical norm is around 1.7 percentage points, so there is still meaningful spread to compress — if that gap closes another 20–25 basis points without the 10-year moving, the 30-year rate could ease toward 6.50–6.55% without any Fed action. The leading indicators are mixed heading into September: the MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 1.2% YoY, a reversal after months of positive prints, and the 13-week average is decelerating. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by 2–4 weeks, staged a small reversal upward in late June (4-week avg +2.3% on the period), but its 13-week trend is still falling and the YoY read is -2.3% — meaning the MII is not yet confirming a demand recovery. Watch whether the MII holds its late-June uptick when July readings arrive; if it does, the MBA data should firm by mid-September.

The macro backdrop this month is a study in soft but not deteriorating. July payrolls came in at -23K against a consensus of +59K — a significant miss — though the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% (vs. expectations of 4.4%), partly reflecting labor force contraction rather than broad hiring strength. The mechanism that matters for housing: when payroll growth stalls but unemployment stays low, move-up buyers remain employed but lose income confidence, which tends to extend decision timelines rather than cancel transactions outright. That is consistent with the rising days-on-market trend in the Altos and Realtor.com data. Average hourly earnings grew only 0.1% in July against an expected 0.3%, and core PCE for June printed at +0.13% month-over-month (also cooler than the 0.3% consensus). Core CPI for July was +0.22% MoM, +2.47% YoY — decelerating — while shelter CPI is still running at +3.16% YoY, a reminder that the BLS measure of housing inflation lags actual market rents by 12–18 months; Zillow's ZORI shows rent growth at +3.0% YoY, ticking down, which implies shelter CPI will keep easing into early 2027. The 10-year Treasury is at 4.71% — up 43 basis points YoY — and until that moves, the rate relief story runs entirely through spread compression.
