**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through the four weeks ending late June, though the trend is decelerating in ways worth watching. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales stood at +4.81% year over year — 55 consecutive weeks of positive YoY pending sales — but the spot week printed -2.99% YoY, the first negative week in that streak, and the 4-week average has slowed to +4.37% from stronger readings earlier in the quarter. That gap between the smoothed trend and the spot reading is the tell: demand is not rolling over, but the momentum that carried spring is fading into summer. Inventory rose for an eighth consecutive week, reaching 1.07 million active listings, up 1.2% YoY — growth that is decelerating rather than accelerating, with new listings' 4-week average barely positive at +0.41% YoY after the spot week turned -7.1% YoY. Pending days on market held at 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY for the 103rd consecutive week, a persistent signal that the market is absorbing supply more slowly than a year ago even as overall inventory levels remain historically lean. NAR existing home sales for June printed 4.09 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and running +4.1% YoY on a seasonally adjusted basis — a strong number on its face, though it reversed the prior month's rise; the NSA read of 422,000 units was up 7.9% YoY, confirming the underlying demand is real. New home sales told a quieter story at 628,000 SAAR, down 5.1% YoY, with new home median prices falling 2.7% YoY as builders competed on price — the divergence between existing and new home sales volume reflects builders clearing a thinner pipeline rather than a demand impulse. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.75% today, roughly where it has been for the past month. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.11 percentage points against a historical norm near 1.70, that spread remains elevated but has compressed 24 basis points over the past year. If it normalizes another 20-25 basis points from here — which does not require the Fed to move — the 30-year would fall into the 6.40-6.50 range without a single rate cut. That is the affordability lever most buyers are not focused on. MBA purchase applications for the week of July 24 came in at a 4-week average of -1.35% YoY, decelerating meaningfully from the stronger readings of spring; the 13-week average holds at +2.88% YoY. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which tends to lead MBA applications by two to four weeks, ticked up on its spot read to -5.59% YoY after several weeks of decline — the 4-week MII average also reversed higher to -2.30% YoY. The MII-to-MBA divergence is narrowing, suggesting the late-June softness in purchase intent may be stabilizing. On the supply side, single-family permits at 872,000 SAAR are +0.69% YoY — the highest since May 2024 — while starts jumped on multifamily volume that tends to be volatile; the pipeline under construction continues to shrink, down 7.0% YoY for single-family, which matters for resale competition in 2027. Watch whether the MII 4-week average continues recovering or fades again through August — that will determine whether the fall selling season opens with momentum or runs flat.

The macro backdrop shifted modestly in housing's favor this month, with the most important move coming from inflation. June headline CPI printed -0.4% month over month against an expectation of +0.9% — a meaningful miss in the direction that matters for rates — and core CPI came in flat month over month versus an expected +0.3%. Core PCE for June ran +0.13% month over month and +3.29% year over year, also softer than the +0.3% consensus. These prints do not bring the Fed to the table in September, but they do take some pressure off the long end of the Treasury curve: the 10-year yield has pulled back to 4.66%, which is directly why the mortgage-Treasury spread conversation matters right now. If inflation continues cooling at this pace into August and September data, the conditions for spread compression improve — and that compresses before the Fed moves, not after. On labor, June payrolls of roughly 159,000 were solid, and the unemployment rate fell a tick to 4.2%, beating the 4.4% consensus; the hires rate ticked up to 3.4% and quits held at 2.0%, neither alarming nor exciting. The one signal worth flagging longer-term: the number of workers unemployed 27 weeks or more reached 1.937 million in June, up 17.3% year over year.
