**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held firmer than the spot numbers suggest. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has been positive year-over-year for 55 consecutive weeks, and the 4-week average stood at +4.4% YoY as of June 26 — still constructive even as the single-week read dipped to -3.0% YoY, which is typical seasonal noise in late June. The streak matters more than any one week. Inventory continued to build slowly: total Altos inventory rose for eight straight weeks and was up about 1.2% YoY, but new listings decelerated sharply — the single-week read fell 7.1% YoY and the 4-week average nearly flatlined at +0.4%, a meaningful step down from earlier in the year. That new-listings pullback is keeping supply from running ahead of demand, and pending days on market at 70.9 (up 6.7% YoY over 103 consecutive weeks) confirms the market is slower than a year ago but not deteriorating. July existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million by a notable margin — the YoY gain of +1.25% extends a gradual recovery, though the month-over-month dip off June's level is unremarkable. NAR inventory flipped slightly negative YoY at 1.54 million units, which aligns with the Altos new-listings deceleration: sellers are pulling back faster than buyers are. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.74% today, off slightly from its recent peak, but the spread to the 10-year Treasury remains at 2.11-2.13 percentage points — well above the long-run norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap is the affordability story hiding in plain sight: if lender risk premiums compress even modestly toward historical norms, the 30-year could fall meaningfully without any Fed action. The June housing starts print of 1,427,000 SAAR beat expectations, but the composition matters — the jump was almost entirely multifamily (5+ units up 76% month-over-month), while single-family starts slipped a third consecutive month and permits fell 2.2%. Builders are completing their multifamily pipeline rather than committing new single-family ground. MBA purchase applications bounced last week to -0.21% YoY on the 4-week average, essentially flat — a recovery from a deeper hole. The Xactus MII, which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks, had its 4-week average improve to -2.3% YoY after a recent trough, which is mildly encouraging but not yet a clear turn. Watch whether the MII's 13-week average, still falling after seven straight weeks of decline, stabilizes — that would be the first sign that fall demand is firming.

The macro picture this month is cleaner than it has been in some time, and the data diverged from consensus in ways that matter for housing. July payrolls came in roughly 80,000 below the 59,000 expected gain — actually a net miss that leaves the labor market cooling at the margin, but the unemployment rate dropped to 4.1% against expectations of 4.4%, and the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) fell to 1.77 million, reversing a prior rise. That last number is worth watching: it had been accumulating through the first half, and a downturn here delays the delinquency pressure that was building in the pipeline. Average hourly earnings grew just 0.1% in July versus 0.3% expected — softening wage growth lowers both inflation risk and, if it persists, purchasing power, so it is not a clean positive for housing demand. On inflation, July CPI came in well below expectations: core CPI at +0.22% month-over-month and +2.47% YoY, and June core PCE at +0.13% MoM and +3.29% YoY — both softer than forecast. Shelter CPI at +3.16% YoY continues to grind lower slowly, which is the lagged reflection of rent deceleration already visible in real-time data. The mechanism that connects this to housing: softer inflation readings compress the inflation-risk premium embedded in the 10-year Treasury, which in turn creates room for mortgage rates to fall — but only if the mortgage-Treasury spread also cooperates, and right now it has ticked back up slightly after a brief compression. The regional divergence in Case-Shiller through May remains the most agent-actionable story in the price data: Chicago is up 6.9% YoY and New York up 4.0%, while Phoenix is down 1.3%, Denver down 1.7%, and Tampa down 1.8% — this is not a national price story, it is two different markets wearing the same headline. The signal to watch next month is whether the 10-year holds below 4.7% as the softer inflation and payroll data get priced in — if it does, the conditions for spread compression become more plausible, and the fall selling season could get a late tailwind.
