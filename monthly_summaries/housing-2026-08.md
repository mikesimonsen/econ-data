**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive in aggregate but showed clear deceleration through the four weeks ending mid-August. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales remained above year-ago levels — running roughly +4.8% YoY through late June and positive for 55 consecutive weeks — but the 4-week average decelerated to +4.4% YoY, and the spot weekly reading actually turned negative (-3.0% YoY) for the first time in that streak. That's the number to watch: a single negative week doesn't break a 55-week run, but it flags that the demand tailwind is thinning. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up nearly 7% YoY, consistent with buyers taking their time rather than competing urgently. Inventory continued rising — Altos active supply was up roughly 1.2% YoY through late June — while new listings on both Altos and Realtor.com softened on a YoY basis, which means the inventory build is coming from slower absorption, not fresh supply. That dynamic constrains listing activity heading into fall. NAR existing home sales printed at 4.06 million SAAR for July, beating the 3.85 million consensus by a wide margin and running +1.3% YoY, though the NSA read confirms the seasonal pattern is intact. The headline beat looks reassuring, but the July print also marked the second consecutive monthly decline from the spring peak — and NAR's inventory figure flipped to negative YoY (-0.65%) for the first time in months, a reversal worth noting given how much the inventory recovery story has driven agent confidence this year. Builder data told a more complicated story: July housing starts fell sharply to 1,239K SAAR (-12.4% MoM, -12.8% YoY), missing the 1,350K consensus, with single-family starts down 15% YoY — but total permits rose to 1,443K, the highest since January 2024, with single-family permits at a two-year high. Builders are re-authorizing future supply even as they pull back on current production, a pattern consistent with waiting for rate conditions to improve before breaking ground. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.76% today, up 4 basis points over the past week and 15bp above a year ago. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.06 percentage points — still roughly 35bp above the long-run historical norm of ~1.70pp, though it has been compressing gradually (down 26bp YoY). If that spread closes another 20-25bp without the 10-year moving, rates would fall to roughly 6.50% under current Treasury conditions — that is the affordability lever most buyers aren't watching. MBA purchase applications tell the most cautious story in the leading indicators: the 4-week average is -1.2% YoY as of August 14th, the 13-week average has been falling for six consecutive weeks, and the spot week was -3.4% YoY. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA by roughly 2-4 weeks by capturing credit-pull activity before applications are formally submitted — showed a modest uptick in its 4-week average through late June, which should translate to a slight improvement in MBA reads over the next month. The divergence between the MII's stabilization and MBA's continued softness is the leading indicator to resolve. Watch whether the MII uptick sustains through July data or fades — that determines whether early fall demand recovers or continues to decelerate.

July's macro data was, on balance, more constructive for housing than the headlines suggested — but with one meaningful exception. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, a full 30 basis points below the consensus forecast of 4.4%, and initial jobless claims came in at 206K this week, well below the 212K expected and down nearly 12% YoY. A labor market that is holding tighter than economists expect is the primary reason existing home sales have maintained positive YoY readings: employed households buy houses, and job security shapes the decision to list as much as to purchase. The payroll miss (-23K versus +59K expected) is harder to dismiss, however — one weak print against a resilient unemployment rate can reflect compositional shifts or survey-timing noise, but if payrolls confirm soft again in August, it will matter for mortgage qualification volumes and consumer confidence, which already sits at 90.8, down 8% YoY. On inflation, core CPI came in at +0.22% MoM and +2.47% YoY in July — cooler than the 0.3% consensus — and core PCE for June also printed below expectations at +0.13% MoM. The mechanism here runs directly through mortgage rates: softer inflation prints reduce the pressure on the 10-year Treasury, which is the anchor for the 30-year fixed. The 10-year has firmed to 4.70% this week despite the benign inflation data, suggesting the market is pricing something other than near-term Fed cuts — likely fiscal supply concerns or a re-pricing of the terminal rate.
