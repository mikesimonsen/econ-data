# Monthly Housing Summary — August 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive over the four weeks ending early August, though the most recent weekly data warrants attention. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales stood at roughly 89,700, up 4.8% year-over-year, and the streak of positive YoY pending sales has now run 55 consecutive weeks — a durable signal that underlying demand has not broken. The 4-week average was +4.4% YoY as of the June 26 read. The spot week, however, dipped to -3.0% YoY, the first negative week in that streak's shadow, and pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% from a year ago — a quiet signal that pace is softening at the margin. Inventory continued its gradual build: Altos total active supply rose to just over 1.07 million, up about 1.2% YoY, while the Realtor.com active count reached roughly 1.13 million, up 2.1% YoY through July. New listings are the more interesting story — the Altos 4-week average turned up slightly to +0.4% YoY after a period of negative prints, but the spot week fell 7.1% YoY, and the Realtor.com new listing count hit its lowest level since October 2023. Sellers are still participating, but inconsistently. NAR existing home sales came in at 4.09 million SAAR for June, beating the 3.85 million consensus and running +4.1% YoY on an NSA basis (+7.9%) — a notably strong read against expectations that confirms the demand backdrop wasn't as soft as the spring narrative suggested. The divergence with new home sales (628K SAAR, -5.1% YoY, six consecutive negative YoY months) reflects builder pricing pressure: new home median prices fell 2.7% YoY and average prices dropped 6.6% — builders are buying down rates and cutting prices to clear standing inventory. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.77% as of August 6, having ticked up two basis points in recent days after a mild decline — still 20 basis points above a year ago. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.13 percentage points, versus the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points; that 43-basis-point excess premium means the 30-year has room to fall meaningfully without any Fed action if credit spreads normalize. MBA purchase applications fell 2.5% YoY in the most recent week, with the 4-week average barely negative at -0.2% YoY — the 13-week average holds at +2.3%, so this is a deceleration, not a reversal. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a soft-credit-pull indicator that leads MBA by roughly 2-4 weeks) was at 123.3 as of June 26, up on the week but down 5.6% YoY, with its 4-week average still negative YoY at -2.3%. The MII's persistent YoY weakness diverging from the MBA's still-positive 13-week average is the leading indicator worth watching: if the MII does not recover toward flat YoY over the next two readings, the MBA 13-week average likely rolls negative in late August or September. June single-family permits held at 872K, marginally positive YoY (+0.7%), but 5-plus-unit permits fell 5.9% YoY — the multifamily pipeline is contracting while single-family barely holds. Watch the July MBA 4-week average when it prints mid-August.

This month's macro backdrop is more constructive than the headlines suggest, with one meaningful caveat. July payrolls missed badly — the establishment survey showed a net loss of roughly 23,000 jobs against a +59,000 consensus — but the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating the 4.4% consensus, and initial claims printed 199,000 against a 212,000 expectation. The household and establishment surveys are telling different stories; for housing, what matters is that the layoff signal (claims) remains clean, which supports near-term purchase decisions. Average hourly earnings rose only 0.1% in July against a 0.3% expectation, which — combined with Core PCE running at +0.1% month-over-month in June against a 0.3% expectation and headline CPI printing a rare monthly decline — means the inflation picture cooled more than anticipated. That matters directly: lower inflation expectations compress the 10-year Treasury yield, and a lower 10-year pulls the 30-year mortgage rate down with it, even before the Fed moves. The 10-year sat at 4.63% as of August 7; if it drifts toward 4.4% on soft labor and inflation data, and if the mortgage-Treasury spread holds at current levels, the 30-year could approach the low 6.50s — conditionally.
