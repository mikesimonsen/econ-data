**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but began showing seams heading into late summer. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% YoY and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks — that streak remains the clearest evidence the market is not deteriorating broadly. The four-week average of +4.37% YoY is, however, decelerating, and the most recent weekly read ticked negative at -2.99% YoY, the first negative spot read in over a year. One week does not break a streak, but it warrants watching. Inventory at Altos is up roughly +1.2% YoY, a meaningful deceleration from the double-digit YoY gains that characterized 2024-2025 — new listings flipped to -7.1% YoY on the spot read before the four-week average steadied at +0.4%. That new-listings pullback, if it persists, will cap inventory growth through fall and keep supply tighter than the seasonal norm implies. Pending days on market edged to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, consistent with a buyer population that is selective rather than absent. July existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million but still -0.73% off June's pace and up only +1.25% YoY — improving trend, not acceleration. Notably, the NAR inventory read flipped negative YoY for the first time in several months, aligning with the Altos signal that supply growth is stalling. New home sales (June, 628K SAAR) remain roughly 5% below year-ago levels, and new-home average prices fell an unusual 9.5% month-over-month, suggesting builders are mixing toward lower price points to move product. The 30-year fixed is at 6.71% as of August 14, ticking back up after a brief decline. The 10-year Treasury also firmed to 4.66%, and the mortgage-Treasury spread sits at roughly 2.05-2.08 basis points — still well above the historical norm of ~1.70pp, meaning rates could fall 35bp or more without the Fed touching the funds rate if lenders normalize that spread. MBA purchase applications' four-week average is essentially flat YoY at -0.21%, an improvement from the deeper negatives of early summer. The Xactus MII — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks by tracking mortgage intent earlier in the funnel — printed a spot read of 123.3, up on the week but still -5.59% YoY, and the 13-week average has been falling for seven consecutive weeks. That divergence between a stabilizing MBA and a still-softening MII is the leading indicator most worth watching in September.

The macro backdrop delivered a genuinely mixed signal this month, and for housing the net read is cautiously constructive rather than clearly improving. July payrolls missed expectations by roughly 82,000 jobs, but the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% against a consensus of 4.4% — the two numbers tell different stories about labor-market momentum, and the payroll miss likely reflects some volatility rather than a clean deterioration. More relevant for housing: the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) fell to 1.771 million in July, reversing the prior month's rise. That cohort had been accumulating through much of 2025 and is the one to watch for future mortgage stress; its two-month decline is a modest positive, though the YoY level remains elevated relative to 2023. On inflation, both headline CPI (+3.3% YoY) and core CPI (+2.47% YoY) came in cooler than consensus, and Core PCE for June printed +3.29% YoY — above the Fed's target but decelerating. Shelter CPI is running at +3.16% YoY and has been rising for seven months; because shelter is backward-looking by design (it captures rents already locked in), the Zillow rent index at +3.34% YoY and accelerating suggests shelter CPI will not cool quickly and will keep core readings elevated even as goods disinflation continues. That limits how aggressively the market can price Fed cuts, which is why the 10-year has ticked back up and rates have not broken lower. The regional price picture from Case-Shiller (May data, reported late July) continues to show the two-speed dynamic: Chicago is up +6.9% YoY and New York +4.0%, both rising for seven months; Dallas is -1.0%, Denver -1.7%, Phoenix -1.3%, Tampa -1.8%, and Seattle -1.8%. Agents in those Sun Belt markets are still dealing with a price environment that nationally published headlines consistently understate. The signal to watch next month: whether the Xactus MII four-week average turns positive YoY — if it does, MBA applications and then pending sales should follow within a month. If the MII stays negative while MBA appears steady, the MBA read is the lagging one.
