# Monthly Housing Summary — August 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The most current Altos weekly data in the snapshot is dated 2026-06-26, which is roughly eight weeks before today (2026-08-18). I'll use the available data carefully, noting data lags where relevant, and will not fabricate more recent Altos readings.

---

Demand held in positive territory through late June but has since shown early signs of softening. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales was running +4.81% year-over-year as of the June 26 read — the 55th consecutive week of positive YoY pending sales on that smoothed basis — though the spot week slipped to -2.99% YoY, the first negative weekly print after a run of positive reads, and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37%. That combination — 13-week trend still firmly positive, spot week negative — is worth watching but not yet alarming; seasonal softening into late summer is normal. Inventory held near 1.075 million active listings as of June 26, up just over 1% YoY, with the 13-week new listings average rising for 14 consecutive weeks but decelerating to only +0.71% YoY. New listings actually turned negative YoY on the spot week (-7.1%), which, if it holds, would slow the inventory build that characterized the first half of the year. July's NAR existing home sales print of 4.06 million SAAR came in well above consensus (3.85M expected), confirming that the demand that showed up in spring pending data did transact — though the month-over-month dip of 0.7% and the inventory reversal (NAR active listings fell to 1.54M, turning YoY negative for the first time in months) suggest the supply cushion is thinner than the headlines implied. New home sales for June (628K SAAR) ticked up after several months of decline but remain 5% below year-ago levels, and builders are sending a split signal: July housing starts collapsed — total starts missed badly at 1,239K versus 1,350K expected, down 12.4% on the month and 12.8% YoY, with single-family down 15% YoY — while building permits rose to 1,443K, beating expectations and turning positive YoY for the first time since early 2025. That starts-versus-permits gap is the builder behavior signal worth flagging: permits represent intention, starts represent execution, and right now builders are authorizing future supply while pulling back on current production, likely managing unsold spec inventory and watching their own cancellation rates. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.73% as of August 17, roughly where it has been for much of the summer. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.02 points on the daily read (weekly average 2.08), it remains well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points — meaning there is 30 or more basis points of potential rate relief embedded in spread compression alone, entirely independent of any Fed action. The MBA 4-week purchase application average was essentially flat YoY (-0.21%) through early August, and the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks — was running at -2.30% YoY on its 4-week average as of late June, diverging negatively from what MBA was showing at the time. That divergence has since partially resolved as MBA ticked up, but the MII 13-week trend has been falling for seven consecutive weeks. Watch whether the MII firms in the next two reads; if it does not, MBA purchase applications are likely to soften into September.

The macro picture this month is genuinely mixed in ways that matter differently for housing. July payrolls missed badly — the economy added only around 23,000 net jobs against a 59,000 consensus — but the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating the 4.4% consensus, and the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) declined for a second consecutive month to 1.771 million. That decline matters: the long-term unemployed cohort is the leading indicator for future mortgage delinquency stress, and a pullback — even modest — reduces the tail risk of distressed supply entering the market six to twelve months out. Average hourly earnings rose only 0.1% in July against a 0.3% expectation, which is meaningfully softer on the income side; for housing affordability, slower wage growth and rates still above 6.5% is not a helpful combination. On inflation, both headline CPI (+3.3% YoY) and core CPI (+2.47% YoY) came in cooler than expected in July, as did June's core PCE (+3.29% YoY, printing +0.13% month-over-month versus a 0.3% consensus). Shelter CPI continues to run at +3.16% YoY, elevated but decelerating in real time as market rents soften — Zillow's ZORI ticked down in July to +3.0% YoY, and that trend feeds into owners' equivalent rent with a lag of roughly 12-18 months, meaning shelter's contribution to core inflation will continue to ease.
