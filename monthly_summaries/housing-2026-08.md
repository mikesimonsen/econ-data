**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive on a trend basis through late June, even as the spot reading softened. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales stood at +4.81% year-over-year as of June 26 — a streak that has now run 55 consecutive weeks in positive territory — but the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37% and the single-week read turned negative at -2.99% YoY, the first negative spot week in that streak. That softening in the spot data bears watching but is not yet a trend break; seasonal patterns in late June can distort weekly readings. Altos total inventory held at roughly 1.07 million active listings, up about 1.2% year-over-year — growth that has been running well below the pace of early 2025 and appears to be decelerating. New listings ticked negative on both a spot (-7.1% YoY) and Realtor.com July basis (-2.6% YoY), a meaningful shift after months of positive supply additions. Pending days on market at 70.9 days ran about 6.7% above last year, suggesting buyers are taking incrementally longer to commit, though that gap has been stable. NAR existing home sales for June printed at 4.09 million SAAR — well above the 3.85 million consensus — up 4.1% year-over-year and the NSA count rose 7.9% year-over-year, the strongest YoY comparison in over a year. That beat is notable. New home sales, at 628,000 SAAR, remain about 5% below last year; new home median prices fell 2.7% YoY and average prices dropped sharply, reflecting builder incentive use and mix shift rather than outright market deterioration. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.74% as of August 7, edging down 3 basis points on the week; the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.11 percentage points, roughly 40 basis points above the historical norm of around 1.70 points. That spread has compressed 21-22 basis points year-over-year — the hidden affordability improvement most buyers aren't hearing about — and if it returns toward that historical norm, rates fall meaningfully without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications for the week ending July 31 fell to -2.5% YoY on a spot basis; the 4-week average is essentially flat at -0.2% YoY and the 13-week average remains positive at +2.3%, though decelerating. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA applications by roughly 2-4 weeks — reversed higher to 123.3 on a spot basis as of June 26 after several weeks of decline, with the 4-week average also turning up, but the 13-week MII remains in negative YoY territory at -2.4%. That MII uptick is the most encouraging leading signal right now, suggesting the August MBA reads should stabilize. Watch for whether the 4-week MBA average breaks below zero and whether new listings can recover; if new supply stays contracted heading into fall, the inventory ceiling may arrive earlier than the calendar suggests.

The July jobs report was the macro story of the month, and it cuts in multiple directions for housing. Payrolls came in at -23,000 against a consensus of +59,000 — a meaningful miss — yet the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating the 4.4% expectation, and initial claims printed at 199,000 against a 212,000 forecast. Average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% month-over-month versus an expected 0.3%, and are up 3.2% year-over-year. The payroll miss is real but should be read alongside the household survey strength; the net picture is a labor market that is cooling at the margin without breaking. For housing, the wage read matters more than the headline: slower earnings growth reduces purchasing power gains but also reduces the inflation pressure that keeps the Fed on hold. On that front, June core PCE came in at +0.13% month-over-month — well below the 0.3% expectation — and core CPI was essentially flat. Shelter CPI at +3.26% YoY continues to run above core, keeping headline inflation readings stickier than the real-time rent data (Zillow ZORI is running at +3.34% YoY, its highest since June 2023) would imply. The mechanism: CPI shelter lags market rents by roughly 12 months, so the shelter component will add upward pressure to measured inflation into early 2027 even as market rents have already softened. The 10-year Treasury is at 4.63%, and the 30-year spread compression is doing quiet work that rate headlines miss. Regionally, the Case-Shiller May data (the most recent available, reflecting contracts from roughly March-April) shows Chicago up 6.9% YoY and New York up 4.0%, both still accelerating; meanwhile Denver is down 1.7%, Phoenix down 1.3%, Tampa down 1.8%, and Seattle down 1.8% — the Sun Belt correction continues
