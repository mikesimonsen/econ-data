**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through the four-week period but showed the first cracks worth watching. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales remains up 4.81% YoY and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks — that streak is intact and is the most durable signal in the dashboard right now. The spot week ending June 26, however, turned negative YoY (–2.99%), and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37% from higher readings earlier in the quarter, so the trend is cooling even if the baseline is still constructive. Inventory continues to build — Altos total active supply was up 1.17% YoY with eight straight weeks of gains, while Realtor.com's July active count rose 2.13% YoY. The more telling number is on the supply side: new listings have softened. The Altos 4-week average for new listings was barely positive YoY (+0.41%), the spot week turned negative (–7.14%), and Realtor.com's July new listing count fell to its lowest level since October 2023. Sellers are pulling back into late summer, which tends to keep inventory from running away. Pending days on market at 70.9 are up 6.71% YoY — homes are taking longer to go under contract, a steady drift that has now run 103 consecutive weeks. NAR June existing sales printed 4.09 million SAAR, beating the 3.85M consensus handily, though the month-over-month read was slightly negative; the NSA YoY of +7.93% tells the cleaner story. New home sales at 628K SAAR ticked up in June but remain down 5.1% YoY, and average new home prices fell sharply — the gap between existing and new home demand is real and reflects builder incentive pressure. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.76% today, up two basis points on the week, reversing a modest decline. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.11–2.13 points (the historical norm is closer to 1.7), there is roughly 40 basis points of compression available before rates reach their fair-value level — that is the affordability lever that doesn't require the Fed to do anything. The MBA purchase index's 4-week average was –0.23% YoY as of July 31, its weakest read in months; the 13-week average remains positive at +2.30% YoY but is decelerating. The Xactus MII — which leads MBA applications by roughly two to four weeks — bounced in late June (4-week average up 2.31%), which is a mild positive signal for August purchase application data. Watch whether that MII recovery persists or fades; it will tell you whether the MBA soft patch is seasonal noise or something stickier. Builder permits are the other signal to track: single-family permits held slightly positive YoY in June (+0.69%) while multifamily permits fell, and total permits missed expectations — builders are not accelerating future supply, which is supportive of price stability in 2027.

The macro picture clarified meaningfully this month, mostly in directions that complicate the rate outlook. July payrolls missed by roughly 80,000 jobs (–23K vs. +59K expected), and average hourly earnings came in at +0.1% month-over-month against a +0.3% consensus — together, those readings reduce the near-term inflation pressure that has kept the Fed cautious. Core PCE for June printed +0.13% month-over-month and +3.29% YoY, again softer than the +0.3% consensus; the direction matters more than the level here because it gives the Fed more room to move without abandoning its credibility. The mechanism for housing is straightforward: softer labor and softer inflation lower the probability of another Fed hike, keep Treasury yields in check, and create the conditions under which that 40-basis-point spread compression story can actually play out. The 10-year yield sits at 4.63%, essentially flat on the week. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1% in July, beating the 4.4% consensus, and the long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) declined for a second consecutive month to 1.771 million — that reversal is worth noting because accumulation in that cohort is the early-stage signal for future mortgage stress; its decline reduces near-term delinquency risk. Consumer confidence came in at 90.8, above the 87.9 expectation but still down 8% YoY, which is consistent with buyers who can transact but are not enthusiastic about it. Regionally, the Case-Shiller May data (released late July) reinforces the two-speed market: Chicago was up 6.91% YoY and New York up 4.03%, both still accelerating, while Denver fell 1.70%, Phoenix fell 1.34%, and Tampa extended its decline to 19 consecutive months of negative YoY readings. Agents in the Midwest and Northeast are still defending appreciation; agents in the Sun Belt are managing a different conversation entirely. Next month, watch the July CPI release —
