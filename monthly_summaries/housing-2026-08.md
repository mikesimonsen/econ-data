# Monthly Housing Summary — August 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week pending sales average held positive for the 55th consecutive week through June 26, running at +4.81% year over year — but the trend is decelerating. The spot week came in at -2.99% YoY, and the 4-week average has pulled back to +4.37% from a higher reading a month ago. That deceleration is worth watching without over-reading: one soft spot week in late June is normal seasonal noise, but three consecutive weekly declines in pending sales suggest buyers paused. Inventory continued to build, with Altos total active supply up 1.17% YoY and the 13-week new listings average rising for the 14th consecutive week — though the spot new listings reading turned negative YoY (-7.14%) in the final week of June, and Realtor.com's July new listing count fell to its lowest since October 2023. Pending days on market have now been above year-ago levels for 103 consecutive weeks, ticking to 70.9 days — the market is clearing, but not quickly. NAR existing home sales for June printed at 4.09 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and running +4.07% YoY on an NSA basis (+7.93%), though the SAAR read dipped slightly from May. New home sales told a different story: 628K SAAR in June, off 5.14% YoY for the sixth consecutive negative month, with new home median prices falling and months of supply still elevated at 9.3. The spread between existing and new home performance reflects builder incentive pressure — new home average prices dropped nearly 9.5% in a single month, an unusual move more than two standard deviations below trend, likely reflecting mix shift and concession activity. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.74% as of August 7, having ticked down modestly this week after a brief rise. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, currently 2.11-2.12 percentage points — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7pp, but compressing year over year by about 22bp. That compression is the hidden affordability story: the 10-year has held near 4.63%, but if the spread continues to normalize toward 1.8-1.9pp, the 30-year rate falls meaningfully without any Fed action. On leading indicators, MBA purchase applications turned negative YoY (-2.53% on the most recent week, -0.23% on the 4-week average) for the first time in 77 weeks of YoY positivity in the 13-week average. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA by roughly 2-4 weeks — offered a partial offset, with both the spot and 4-week MII turning up after a multi-week decline, though the 13-week MII remains negative YoY at -2.38%. The divergence between a recovering MII and a softening MBA warrants attention: if the MII uptick is real signal rather than noise, purchase applications should stabilize in the next two to three weeks. What to watch over the next four weeks: whether pending sales recover toward the 4-week average after June's soft finish, whether new listings reaccelerate off July's low, and whether the MBA 4-week average can hold above zero YoY.

The macro backdrop shifted noticeably this month, and the direction matters for housing. July payrolls came in at a net loss of roughly 23,000 jobs against a consensus expectation of +59,000 — a meaningful miss, though the unemployment rate simultaneously fell to 4.1% (beating the 4.4% consensus), and average hourly earnings rose only 0.1% versus an expected 0.3%. The combination — weaker job creation, lower unemployment, cooler wages — looks like a labor market that is cooling in a relatively orderly way rather than deteriorating. For housing, the wage reading is the most direct mechanism: slower earnings growth reduces buyer purchasing power at the margin, but it also reduces the inflation pressure that keeps the Fed on hold. The long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) fell for the second consecutive month to 1.77 million, reversing a prior buildup — that reverses a signal worth monitoring for future distressed-borrower volume, though it is not an active 2026 selling-season issue. On inflation, June core PCE came in at +0.13% month over month (+3.29% YoY), cooler than the expected 0.3%; core CPI was essentially flat on the month versus an expected +0.3%. Shelter CPI, which feeds directly into core inflation measures and keeps the Fed cautious, continued its slow crawl higher at +3.26% YoY — the mechanism being that measured rent lags actual market rents by 12-18 months, and actual rents (Zillow ZORI) are now running +3.34% YoY and accelerating, their highest since June 2023. That means the shelter component is unlikely to fall out of inflation readings anytime soon, which keeps the Fed's path narrow.
