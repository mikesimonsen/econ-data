**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but decelerated through the month. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales is running +4.81% year-over-year — a streak now 55 consecutive weeks in positive territory — though the most recent weekly read slipped to -2.99% YoY, and the 4-week average cooled to +4.37% from stronger readings earlier this summer. That weekly dip is worth watching, but one week does not break a 55-week streak. Inventory growth is decelerating rather than accelerating: total active supply sat at roughly 1.07 million units, up just 1.17% YoY, and the new listings 4-week average is barely positive at +0.41% YoY after the single-week read turned -7.14% YoY for the week ending June 26. The 13-week new listings average has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks, so the seasonal pullback in new supply is normal, not alarming. Pending days on market at 70.9 days are running about 7% above year-ago levels — homes are moving slower than last summer, but not stalling. NAR existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR for July, +1.25% YoY and a meaningful beat against the 3.85 million consensus, even though the SAAR print edged down slightly from June. The NSA YoY of +2.83% confirms the directional read. New home sales (June, 628K SAAR) are running -5.14% YoY with 9.3 months of supply — builders are still working through elevated finished-home inventory and their median price is down nearly 3% YoY, which is the discount mechanism clearing that supply. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.79% as of August 11, up about 21 basis points year-over-year. The 10-year Treasury is at 4.68%, putting the mortgage-Treasury spread at 2.11-2.13 points on various averages — still roughly 40 basis points above the long-run historical norm of around 1.70. That gap is the quiet affordability story: if credit markets normalize, the 30-year could fall meaningfully without the Fed moving at all. MBA purchase applications offered a modest recovery this week, with the 4-week average at -0.21% YoY — essentially flat — after several weeks of softening. More telling: the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (which leads MBA by two to four weeks, measuring credit-pull activity before applications are formally filed) bounced in its most recent reading, with the 4-week average up 2.31%, though the 13-week average remains in a seven-week decline at -2.38% YoY. When MII and MBA diverge this way, the 13-week trend deserves more weight than the weekly bounce. Watch whether MBA purchase applications confirm the MII uptick or fade again through late August.

The macro backdrop this month is genuinely mixed in its housing implications. The July employment report missed badly on payrolls (-23K against a +59K consensus), but the headline unemployment rate fell to 4.1% — a full 30 basis points below expectations — suggesting the payroll miss may reflect sampling noise more than a real labor-market break. For housing, the unemployment rate is the variable that matters most: employed households buy homes, and 4.1% is not a distress level. Average hourly earnings grew just 0.1% in July against a 0.3% consensus, which is softer income growth but also alleviates wage-inflation pressure at the Fed. On inflation, June CPI and Core PCE both printed cooler than expected. Core PCE ran +0.13% month-over-month and +3.29% YoY — still above the Fed's 2% target, but the trend is decelerating. Shelter CPI continues to run at +3.26% YoY, but that series lags real-time rents by roughly 12-18 months; Zillow's ZORI, which is current, is up 3.34% YoY and accelerating, so shelter inflation is not going away quickly. The 10-year Treasury at 4.68% is reflecting some of the cooler inflation prints but remains elevated relative to a year ago, which is the primary ceiling on rate relief. On the regional price picture, the Case-Shiller May data (the most recent available, released July 29) shows Chicago up 6.91% YoY, New York up 4.03%, Boston up 2.64%, and Cleveland up 3.12% — all appreciating on a YoY basis and mostly still accelerating. By contrast, Tampa is -1.76% YoY with 19 consecutive months negative, Dallas -1.02%, Denver -1.70%, Phoenix -1.34%, Las Vegas -1.89%, and Seattle -1.77%. Atlanta has crossed into negative YoY territory at -0.11%. Agents in those Sun Belt markets are navigating real price concessions; agents in the Midwest and Northeast are still justifying appreciation. The signal to watch through September: whether the Xactus MII 13-week trend stops falling
