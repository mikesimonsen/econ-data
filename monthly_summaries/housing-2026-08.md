**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through most of the summer, but the most recent Altos weekly read shows some softening at the edges. New pending sales came in at 85,813 for the week ending June 26 — down 3.0% year-over-year, snapping what had been a 55-week positive YoY streak on the 13-week average, which remains up 4.81% YoY and has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks. The 4-week average is still up 4.37% YoY, so the weekly dip looks more like seasonal noise than a trend break, but it bears watching. Inventory grew 1.17% YoY at 1.07 million units, continuing an 8-week rise, while new listings decelerated: the spot week fell 7.1% YoY after running positive — the 4-week average is barely positive at +0.41% YoY. Pending days on market at 70.9 are running 6.7% above last year, a steady creep that signals buyers are not rushing. NAR existing home sales for July came in at 4.06 million SAAR — ahead of the 3.85 million consensus by a notable margin — but the YoY gain is only +1.25%, and the NSA read confirms the direction: up 2.83% YoY, six months of positive YoY readings. The contrast with new home sales is worth flagging: new home sales fell to 607,000 SAAR in July, down 5.0% YoY, and months supply for new homes jumped to 9.6 — builders are sitting on a lengthening pipeline. Starts fell sharply to 1,239,000 SAAR, missing expectations by over 100,000 units and down 12.75% YoY; but permits rose to 1,433,000 SAAR, up 5.2% YoY, with single-family permits at their highest since May 2024. Builders are authorizing future supply while completing the pipeline more slowly — that matters for 2027 availability, not the current selling season. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.74% today, down fractionally, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at approximately 2.08 percentage points against a historical norm closer to 1.70. That 38-basis-point excess spread remains the understated affordability lever: if credit conditions normalize without any Fed movement, rates can fall to the low-to-mid 6s without a single rate cut. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 1.2% YoY — the first negative YoY print in 79 weeks on the 13-week average — while the Xactus MII, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, rebounded off its recent decline with the 4-week average up 2.31%. That divergence — MII recovering while MBA softens — suggests the application pipeline may stabilize rather than deteriorate further. Watch the next two MBA readings closely.

The macro backdrop is pulling in two directions simultaneously, which is why housing sentiment remains unsettled even as transaction volumes hold. July payrolls came in at a net loss of roughly 23,000 jobs against a consensus expectation of +59,000 — a meaningful miss — while the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating the 4.4% consensus. Those two numbers are not contradictory: the household survey and the payroll survey can diverge, and a declining participation rate can push the rate lower even as job creation slows. The mechanism that matters for housing is the wage side: average hourly earnings rose only 0.1% month-over-month against an expected 0.3%, bringing the YoY rate to 3.15%. Slower wage growth reduces purchasing power but also reduces inflation pressure, and that is where the rate story lives. Core PCE for June came in at +0.13% month-over-month, well below the 0.3% consensus, running 3.29% YoY — still above the Fed's 2% target but decelerating. Core CPI for July printed +0.22%, also cooler than expected, with shelter CPI at +0.14% for the month and +3.16% YoY — shelter is still the stickiest component but is no longer re-accelerating. The Fed's path is data-dependent, and what June and July are telling it is that inflation is fading faster than expected while the labor market is more ambiguous than the headline unemployment rate suggests. That combination keeps long-end yields anchored: the 10-year Treasury is at 4.66%, off its recent high.
