**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but is clearly decelerating. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has been running above year-ago levels for 55 consecutive weeks, and the 4-week average was still up roughly 4.4% YoY as of late June — but the most recent weekly read turned negative YoY for the first time in that streak, a signal worth watching even if one week does not break a trend. Pending days on market have been above year-ago levels for 103 consecutive weeks and ticked another 6.7% higher YoY, a quiet reminder that the pace of commitment is still slower than buyers' urgency would suggest. On the supply side, Altos inventory has risen for eight consecutive weeks and is up just over 1% YoY — growth that has essentially stalled relative to the inventory rebuilding story of early 2026. New listings turned negative YoY on the weekly read, though the 4-week and 13-week averages remain fractionally positive; sellers are not accelerating their entry into the market. NAR's July existing sales print of 4.06 million SAAR beat expectations handily (consensus was 3.85M) and is up 1.25% YoY, but the month-over-month dip and NAR inventory flipping to a YoY decline for the first time in months suggest the supply tailwind may be fading. New home sales told a different story: July came in at 607K SAAR, down 10.5% on the month and 5% below a year ago, with months supply rising to 9.6 — builders are sitting on finished product they cannot move as quickly as they'd like. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.87% today, up about 6 basis points on the week. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.11 percentage points — still roughly 40bp above the historical norm of around 1.7pp — meaning there is rate relief available if credit conditions ease even modestly, independent of Fed action. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 3.3% YoY and the 13-week average has been falling for seven consecutive weeks. The Xactus MII, which tends to lead MBA applications by two to four weeks, showed an uptick in late June after a multi-week decline; if that uptick holds in July data, it would suggest the MBA weakness is flattening rather than accelerating. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the MII signal translates into stabilization in MBA applications, and whether Altos pending sales recover from that one negative YoY week or begin a genuine softening trend.

The macro backdrop is genuinely mixed in ways that matter differently for housing. July payrolls missed badly — the print came in around -23K against a consensus of +59K — yet the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating the 4.4% expected, and initial claims dropped to 203K this past week, well below expectations. The divergence between the headline payroll miss and a tightening unemployment rate creates ambiguity about labor market momentum; for housing, what matters most is that employed households with income can still qualify and commit, and claims data suggest layoff activity remains contained. The long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) fell in July after a run of increases — that is a relief, because that is the population that eventually shows up as mortgage stress; it bears watching every month. On inflation, Core PCE for July printed at 3.34% YoY, running above the 3% threshold that keeps the Fed patient, while headline CPI at 3.3% also came in well above the pre-release expectations. Shelter CPI at 3.16% YoY continues to decelerate slowly, which is the mechanism that will eventually pull Core PCE down — but not quickly enough to change the rate picture in the next 30 days. The 10-year Treasury closed August at 4.76%, up 53bp from a year ago, and at that level the spread math is straightforward: the 30-year is unlikely to fall below 6.5% without either a material Treasury rally or meaningful spread compression. Case-Shiller data through June (remember it lags about two months) sharpens the geographic divide: Chicago is up 6.9% YoY, New York up 4.5%, Cleveland up 4.1%, while Dallas is negative for 15 consecutive months, Las Vegas negative for 10, Phoenix negative for 13, and Seattle negative for 11. Agents in the Midwest and Northeast are working appreciating markets with thin supply; agents in the Sun Belt are working markets where prices have softened and builders have excess inventory. The two narratives require entirely different listing conversations. Next month, watch Core PCE — if the August print comes in at or below 3.0%, it changes the Fed's calculus and gives the Treasury market room to rally, which is the most likely path to meaningful mortgage rate relief before year-end.
