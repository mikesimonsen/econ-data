**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through the first half of summer, though the most recent weekly data introduced a note of caution. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales remained +4.81% year-over-year through the week of June 26 — the 55th consecutive week of positive YoY readings on that smoothed measure — while the 4-week average stood at +4.37% YoY. The spot week, however, slipped to -2.99% YoY, the third consecutive weekly decline, and the streak of positive weekly readings broke. One negative spot week inside a healthy 13-week trend is noise, not signal, but it warrants watching as we move through August's traditionally softer demand window. Inventory at Altos reached 1.07 million active listings, up 1.17% YoY — supply growth is present but has decelerated noticeably from the double-digit YoY gains that characterized much of 2025. New listings ticked lower, with the 4-week average at just +0.41% YoY; the 13-week average has risen for 14 consecutive weeks but is barely above flat. Pending days on market extended to 70.9 days, +6.71% YoY, a slow grind longer that reflects selective buyers rather than a stalled market. NAR's June existing home sales printed at 4.09 million SAAR — well above the 3.85 million consensus — with the NSA count up 7.93% YoY, the fifth consecutive monthly gain on that basis; the YoY pace is accelerating. New home sales, at 628,000 SAAR, ticked up from May but remain 5.1% below a year ago, and the average new-home price fell sharply in June, an unusual two-standard-deviation drop that likely reflects builders concentrating closings in lower price bands to clear supply. The 30-year fixed rate stands at 6.75% as of August 5, essentially flat over the past month. The spread to the 10-year Treasury (currently at 4.64%) is 2.13 percentage points — still roughly 40–45 basis points above the long-run norm of about 1.70 points. The spread has been compressing year-over-year (-0.23pp) but stalled in recent weeks; if lenders continue to normalize that premium, the 30-year could fall meaningfully without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications flipped negative on a YoY basis for the week ending July 31 (-2.53%), and the 4-week average is nearly flat at -0.23% YoY after being solidly positive through spring — the 13-week average remains +2.30% YoY, so this is deceleration, not reversal. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-inquiry-based series that typically leads MBA by two to four weeks) showed a positive reversal in its 4-week average as of June 26 after several weeks of decline, with that average up +2.31% from the prior week — a tentative sign that application softness may stabilize rather than worsen into late August. The most important construction signal: single-family permits came in at 872,000 SAAR in June, their highest YoY reading since May 2024, even as total permits missed expectations and multifamily permits fell. Builders are authorizing more for-sale homes while pulling back on apartments — that matters for 2027 resale competition, not the current listing season. Watch whether the MBA 4-week average turns further negative in the next two releases; that would confirm the demand softness in the spot Altos data rather than dismiss it.

The macro backdrop this month was cleaner than the headlines suggested. June headline CPI came in at -0.42% month-over-month versus a +0.9% consensus expectation — the cooling was real, not a rounding artifact — and core CPI printed essentially flat at -0.02% MoM with a 2.57% YoY rate. Core PCE for June followed at +0.13% MoM and +3.29% YoY, also below the 0.3% consensus. The mechanism for housing: inflation running softer than expected reduces the probability of additional Fed tightening and firms up rate market expectations, which puts mild downward pressure on the 10-year Treasury and, in turn, on mortgage rates — though the transmission is imperfect while the spread remains elevated. Shelter CPI continued its slow drift, rising 3.26% YoY in June; the lag in how shelter enters CPI means this is still catching up to real-time rent data from 12–18 months ago. The Zillow rent index (ZORI) is now up 3.34% YoY, its highest reading since June 2023, so shelter CPI is unlikely to cool quickly and will keep core inflation stickier than the non-shelter components suggest. On labor: June payrolls were solid enough (158,984 nonfarm, +0.32% YoY) and unemployment came in at 4.2% versus a 4.4% consensus, a reassuring beat.
