# Monthly Housing Summary — August 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through most of the four-week window, but the most recent Altos weekly read is worth watching closely. The 13-week average of new pending sales sits at roughly +4.8% YoY and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks — that underlying trend remains intact. The 4-week average is running at +4.4% YoY, though the spot week ending June 26 ticked negative (-3.0% YoY) for the first time in that streak, likely reflecting normal late-June seasonality rather than a trend break. Altos inventory rose for eight consecutive weeks and is up about +1.2% YoY, a meaningful deceleration from the inventory expansion pace seen earlier this year; new listings on a 4-week average basis are essentially flat YoY (+0.4%), and the 13-week average has been rising for 14 weeks, which suggests sellers are still entering but not flooding the market. Pending days on market at 70.9 days is up 6.7% YoY, a sign that buyers are taking longer to commit — worth flagging at listing appointments when sellers ask about pricing speed. NAR July existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and running +1.25% above July 2025 — the beat is real even as the month-over-month reading ticked slightly lower for a second consecutive month. New home sales diverged sharply: July new sales fell to 607K SAAR, down 10.5% on the month and 5.0% below a year ago, with months supply rising to 9.6 — builders are sitting on elevated finished inventory. Starts confirmed the builder pullback, coming in at 1,239K SAAR versus an expected 1,350K, down 12.8% YoY, with single-family starts off 15% YoY. The important counterpoint: total permits rose to 1,433K and beat expectations, with single-family permits at their highest since May 2024. Builders are authorizing future projects even as they throttle current production — a pipeline signal for 2027 supply, not this cycle. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.75% today, essentially flat over the past month. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.08 percentage points, down 19 basis points YoY and off its recent high — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points, meaning roughly 35-40 basis points of affordability improvement is available if credit conditions normalize without the Fed moving at all. MBA purchase applications for the week of August 21 are down 5.7% YoY and the 4-week average is off 3.3%, a softer read than the Altos pending data would suggest. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which captures mortgage credit inquiries 2-4 weeks before they show up in MBA data — reversed higher at the end of June after a seven-week decline in its 13-week average, and the 4-week MII average also turned up. That divergence between a still-soft MBA read and a recovering MII is the leading indicator worth tracking through September: if MII is signaling correctly, purchase application volumes should firm in the next two to four weeks.

The macro backdrop shifted modestly in housing's favor this month, though the signal is mixed enough to warrant caution. July payrolls missed consensus badly — the economy added roughly 23,000 fewer jobs than the 59,000 expected — but the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating the 4.4% consensus, and initial claims for the week of August 22 came in at 203,000, well below the 212,000 expected and down 11% YoY. The divergence between a weak payroll print and a low unemployment rate likely reflects composition effects rather than genuine labor market strength; average hourly earnings rose only 0.1% in July versus 0.3% expected, which removes one upside inflation argument but also slows the income growth that supports purchasing power. The more important inflation print for housing is Core PCE, which came in at +3.34% YoY for July — still running above the Fed's target and slightly above Core CPI's +2.47% YoY read. Shelter CPI at +3.16% YoY continues to decelerate gradually, and as the market-rent measures from Zillow ZORI (+3.0% YoY) work their way into the lagged BLS shelter calculation, that component should continue easing — which matters because shelter is the single largest driver keeping headline CPI elevated. The 10-year Treasury at 4.67% ticked up slightly on the day, and with Core PCE still firm, the Fed has limited room to cut without risking a re-acceleration in services inflation. Consumer confidence at 90.8 beat expectations but remains well below year-ago levels (-8% YoY), a drag on discretionary move-up decisions.
