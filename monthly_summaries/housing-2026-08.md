# Monthly Housing Summary — August 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales held at +4.81% YoY for the week ending June 26 — positive for the 55th consecutive week — but the single-week read dipped to -2.99% YoY, and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37% after sitting higher earlier in the quarter. That 13-week trend is the more reliable signal, and it remains constructive, though the recent softening in the weekly read warrants watching. Inventory on the Altos series reached 1.07M units, up 1.17% YoY, with new listings running slightly below year-ago levels on the most recent weekly print (-7.1% YoY spot) even as the 4-week and 13-week averages are barely positive; sellers pulled back at the end of June, a pattern worth monitoring into late summer. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — still elevated, but not deteriorating further. NAR's June existing home sales came in at 4.09M SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85M and running +4.1% YoY on the seasonally adjusted read; the NSA figure was +7.9% YoY, a cleaner comparison showing the strongest year-over-year gain in several years. New home sales at 628K SAAR edged back up in June but remain down 5.1% YoY, and the 9.3-month supply of new homes — still historically elevated — is visibly compressing builder pricing: new home median prices fell to $398,300, down 2.7% YoY. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.74% as of today, essentially flat over the past month. The more important story is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.11 percentage points versus a historical norm of roughly 1.7pp, there is still meaningful compression potential — if that spread normalizes 20-25bp, the 30-year rate falls toward the low-to-mid 6s without any Fed move. MBA purchase applications turned negative YoY on the most recent week (-2.5%), and the 4-week average is essentially flat year-over-year at -0.2%. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which typically leads MBA by 2-4 weeks — bottomed and reversed in late June, with the 4-week MII average turning higher; that divergence, MII firming while MBA is still soft, is the leading indicator to watch over the next four weeks. Builder permits told a cautionary tale: total permits missed consensus and turned negative YoY, with the divergence between starts (which jumped on multifamily) and permits (which fell) suggesting builders are completing pipeline but not yet committing to new ground. Single-family permits at 872K SAAR are barely positive YoY; that keeps forward single-family supply constrained well into 2027.

The macro backdrop shifted more notably this month than the housing data alone suggests. July payrolls came in at -23K versus a consensus expectation of +59K — the first monthly decline in well over a year — but the unemployment rate simultaneously fell to 4.1%, beating expectations of 4.4%, and initial jobless claims printed 199K, well below the expected 212K. Those three readings are in genuine tension: the payroll miss likely reflects survey noise or sectoral volatility rather than broad deterioration, and the claims data argues against an imminent labor market break. Wage growth also cooled, with average hourly earnings rising just 0.1% in July against an expected 0.3%, which reduces the wage-push inflation concern. On prices, June core PCE came in at +0.1% month-over-month versus the expected +0.3%, bringing the YoY read to 3.29% — still above the Fed's target but decelerating in a way that keeps rate-cut optionality alive without requiring it. Shelter CPI continues to run at 3.26% YoY, elevated but no longer accelerating; as actual rent growth (Zillow ZORI hit 3.34% YoY, its highest since June 2023) gradually feeds into the lagged CPI shelter calculation, the shelter component may begin to firm again modestly before it ultimately falls. The regional price picture in the May Case-Shiller data — which reflects closings from roughly March-April — shows Chicago at +6.9% YoY and New York at +4.0% YoY still leading, while Phoenix (-1.3%), Denver (-1.7%), Tampa (-1.8%), Las Vegas (-1.9%), and Seattle (-1.8%) remain in negative territory; agents in those Sun Belt and Mountain West metros are working a fundamentally different price environment than their Midwest and Northeast counterparts. The signal to watch next month: whether the July payroll print is revised materially upward in the August report, and whether the Xactus MII's late-June uptick translates into a firming MBA purchase application trend — that sequence would confirm the demand recovery is still intact heading into fall.
