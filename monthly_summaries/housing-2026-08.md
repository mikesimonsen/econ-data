**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but is showing early signs of deceleration. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks, running at roughly +4.8% YoY as of the week ending June 26 — a streak that reflects genuine underlying demand. The 4-week average was up 4.4% YoY, though it has been decelerating, and the spot week printed negative YoY (-3.0%) for the first time in that run, which is worth watching without overreacting to a single week. Inventory rose for eight consecutive weeks and sat just above 1.07 million units, up 1.2% YoY — growth that has been gradual rather than accumulating. New listings softened: the spot week fell 7.1% YoY, though the 4-week and 13-week averages remained marginally positive, suggesting the trend is flattening rather than reversing sharply. Pending days on market at 70.9 days are running 6.7% above last year, which tells you homes are taking longer to find their buyers even as overall demand stays positive. NAR existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR for July — well above the 3.85 million consensus — though that beat deserves some skepticism: sales fell for the second consecutive month and the YoY gain of 1.25% is modest. New home sales diverged, running 5.1% below year-ago levels in June with median prices down 2.7% YoY, as builders continued discounting to clear supply. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.75% as of August 18, up slightly on the week, with the 30Y-10Y spread at 2.01 points — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap is the affordability story hiding in plain sight: if the spread compresses toward its long-run average, rates could fall 25-30 basis points without any Fed action. The July starts print was the sharpest negative signal of the month: total starts fell 12.4% month-over-month and 12.8% YoY, badly missing the 1,350K consensus, with single-family down 15% YoY and Southern starts at their lowest since April 2023. Permits told the opposite story — up 5.9% YoY, beating expectations, with single-family permits at their highest since May 2024. Builders are pulling authorizations for future work while completing and pausing current pipelines; the implication for inventory is more supply in 2027-28, not 2026. The MBA purchase index 4-week average was down 1.2% YoY as of August 14, and the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks — was also running negative YoY (-2.3% on the 4-week average) as of late June. That convergence warrants attention: when MII and MBA both soften simultaneously, the leading-indicator value of MII is confirming rather than warning. Watch whether MBA applications stabilize or extend their decline through September.

The macro picture is genuinely mixed in ways that matter for housing. July payrolls missed badly — the economy added roughly 23,000 net jobs against a 59,000 consensus — but the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating expectations of 4.4%, and average hourly earnings rose only 0.1% against an expected 0.3%. Read together: labor demand is cooling at the margin, but workers still in jobs are not losing them, and wage deceleration reduces the Fed's inflation anxiety. The long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) actually fell in July to 1.771 million, reversing a recent build — that's a relief, because that cohort is the leading edge of eventual mortgage stress and its accumulation was worth tracking. On inflation, July headline CPI came in at +0.1% MoM and core CPI at +0.2%, both below consensus, with shelter CPI at +3.16% YoY — still elevated but no longer accelerating at the pace that drove rate expectations earlier in the year. Core PCE for June was similarly cool at +0.1% MoM. The 10-year Treasury fell 9 basis points this week to 4.65%, which puts it 35 basis points above a year ago — the rate environment is higher than last summer, not dramatically so, but persistently so in a way that keeps affordability constrained. The regional picture from May Case-Shiller (the most recent available) continues to show the two-speed market clearly: Chicago up 6.9% YoY, New York up 4.0%, Boston up 2.6%, while Denver is down 1.7%, Phoenix down 1.3%, Tampa down 1.8%, and Seattle down 1.8% YoY — agents in those Sun Belt and Mountain West markets are working through a different price conversation than their Midwest and Northeast peers. The one macro signal to watch in September: whether the 10-year holds below 4.65% or drifts back toward 4.8%.
