**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held firmer than the spot data implies. The Altos new pending sales 4-week average came in at +4.37% YoY for the week of June 26, and the 13-week average has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks — that streak is the more durable signal than the single-week reading, which dipped to -2.99% YoY as seasonal patterns softened late June. Inventory continued to build slowly: Altos total inventory rose for eight consecutive weeks and sat +1.17% YoY, while new listings on a 4-week average basis were essentially flat (+0.41% YoY) after turning briefly negative on a spot basis. The 13-week new listings average has been rising for 14 weeks, which tells you the supply acceleration is real but measured — not a flood. Pending days on market at 70.9 days ran +6.71% YoY for the 103rd consecutive week, confirming that buyers are not rushing. NAR existing home sales printed 4.06 million SAAR for July — up +1.25% YoY, beating expectations of 3.85 million by a notable margin, though the month-over-month dip of 0.7% ended two months of sequential gains. NAR inventory flipped to -0.65% YoY, the first negative YoY reading in some time, which is worth watching: if active supply contracts into fall, price pressure builds even at modest demand levels. New home sales (June, 628K SAAR) recovered modestly from recent softness but remain -5.1% YoY, and the builder price concession story continues — new home average sale prices fell an unusual 9.5% in a single month, now -6.6% YoY, which is builders clearing inventory through incentives rather than list price cuts. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.79% as of August 11, up slightly over two days but down from recent highs. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.11 percentage points — still roughly 40 basis points above the long-run historical norm of about 1.70 points. That excess spread is the affordability lever most buyers aren't seeing: if credit markets normalize even modestly, rates decline without any Fed action. The MBA purchase index 4-week average turned marginally positive week-over-week but sits at -0.21% YoY — essentially flat. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (which tends to lead MBA applications by 2-4 weeks) reversed upward to +3.53% on the spot reading after a multi-week decline, with the 4-week MII average also turning positive. That divergence — MII firming while MBA still lags slightly negative — is the early signal to watch heading into September. Over the next four weeks: track whether the MII recovery translates into MBA applications following, and watch NAR inventory YoY — if it stays negative while pending sales hold positive, that sets up price support into the fall.

The macro picture in July was unusually clean: nearly every major print came in softer than expected, and housing-relevant data read constructively. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1% against a 4.4% consensus — the labor market is not breaking, and employed households are the demand pool. The offset is the payrolls print, which missed badly (-23K versus +59K expected); that single number will keep rate-cut speculation alive and bond yields from running much higher, which matters directly for mortgage pricing. Average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% in July against a 0.3% expectation, and July CPI core came in at +0.22% month-over-month (+2.47% YoY) — also below consensus. Core PCE for June printed +0.13% against +0.3% expected, holding YoY at 3.29%. Taken together, wage growth and inflation are both decelerating, which compresses the argument for further tightening and keeps Treasury yields in check; the 10-year sits at 4.68%, down two basis points from recent highs. The long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) fell to 1.771 million in July, down 8.6% from June — a meaningful reversal after a worrying accumulation trend. This matters because sustained long-term unemployment is the leading precursor to mortgage delinquency stress 6-12 months out; one month of improvement is not a trend, but it removes some tail risk from the near-term outlook. Regionally, the Case-Shiller May data (released July 29, so roughly two months lagged) confirms the two-speed market that agents in different geographies are living every day: Chicago is up +6.9% YoY and New York +4.0%, while Dallas, Denver, Phoenix, Seattle, Tampa, and Las Vegas are all still negative YoY — with Las Vegas at its softest since September 2023. Boston's period change turned positive and its YoY is accelerating, while Tampa reversed lower and its 19-month YoY decline continues to steepen.
