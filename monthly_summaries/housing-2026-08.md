**August 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive on a trend basis through the four weeks ending late August, though the weekly signal softened meaningfully. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales stood at roughly 89,700 units and has been positive year-over-year for 55 consecutive weeks — that streak remains intact — but the 4-week average decelerated to +4.4% YoY from higher readings earlier in the summer, and the most recent weekly print turned negative YoY (-3.0%) for the first time in that run. One negative week does not break a trend, but it warrants attention. Inventory on the Altos weekly read was up about 1.2% YoY, still growing but at a pace well below the double-digit gains of 2024-2025; new listings flipped slightly negative YoY on the spot week (-7.1%) though the 4-week average remained barely positive (+0.4%), suggesting seasonal deceleration more than a structural pullback. Pending days on market at 70.9 days is running 6.7% above last year — homes are taking longer to go under contract than they were in 2025, which is consistent with the modest softening in weekly demand. NAR's July existing home sales print came in at 4.06 million SAAR, up 1.25% YoY and well ahead of the 3.85 million consensus — the beat was real, not a revision artifact. But new home sales told a different story: July new home sales fell to 607,000 SAAR, down 10.5% on the month and 5.0% YoY, with months supply rising to 9.6 — builders have inventory sitting, and they know it. The 30-year fixed rate ended the period at 6.81%, up modestly from earlier in August and 31 basis points above a year ago. The 30Y-10Y spread sits at 2.08 percentage points — still roughly 38 basis points wide of the historical norm near 1.70pp. That gap represents the hidden affordability story: if credit and volatility conditions normalize even partially, rates fall meaningfully without any Fed action. On the leading side, the MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 3.3% YoY and has been falling for two weeks; the 13-week average is barely flat YoY (+0.02%), decelerating from solidly positive readings earlier in the year. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA purchase applications by two to four weeks — ticked up in its most recent reading after declining for seven weeks, with the 4-week average also reversing higher. That MII uptick is worth watching: if it holds, it argues the MBA softness is temporary. Building permits beat expectations at 1,433,000 SAAR (+5.2% YoY), with single-family permits at their highest since May 2024 — but July starts collapsed to 1,239,000 SAAR, missing badly and turning negative YoY (-12.7%). Builders are pulling permits but not breaking ground, which signals they are waiting for demand clarity before committing capital. The spread between rising permits and falling starts is the number to watch heading into September.

The macro backdrop is genuinely mixed in ways that matter for housing. July payrolls missed the consensus by roughly 82,000 jobs — the actual print came in around -23,000 net change against a +59,000 expectation — though the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, two-tenths below consensus, and initial jobless claims are running 11% below year-ago levels, so the labor market is not deteriorating uniformly. The quits rate held at 2.0%, and hires ticked up — workers are not panicking, but neither are they confident enough to move freely, which matters for household formation and listing supply. Average hourly earnings grew just 0.1% in July against a 0.3% expectation, and Core CPI came in at +2.47% YoY with a 0.2% monthly gain — both cooler than forecast. Core PCE, the Fed's preferred gauge, printed 3.34% YoY for July, still running above target and in its seventh consecutive monthly increase. That combination — weakening payrolls, cooler wages, but sticky core PCE — keeps the Fed anchored and the 10-year Treasury elevated at 4.67%, which is why the 30-year rate has not moved lower despite the softer inflation prints. Consumer confidence at 90.8 beat its consensus but is down 8% YoY, and that erosion in sentiment is showing up in buyer hesitation more than in outright cancellations. The regional price picture from Case-Shiller through June reinforces the two-speed market: Chicago is up 6.9% YoY, New York 4.5%, Boston 2.7%, Cleveland 4.1% — Northeast and Midwest markets where supply remains constrained continue to appreciate. Meanwhile Dallas is down 0.6% YoY, Phoenix -0.7%, Las Vegas -1.8%, Seattle -1.8%, and Tampa -1.5% — Sun Belt markets that overbuilt or saw the sharpest pandemic-era appreciation are still working through the correction.
