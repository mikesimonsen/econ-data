# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held firmly positive in October, with the Altos 13-week average running +5.5% year over year — now 56 consecutive weeks of positive YoY readings, a streak that continues to anchor the demand picture even as the spot weekly reading (+5.9% YoY) sits below that trend level. The 4-week average has decelerated to +3.6% YoY, worth watching but not yet alarming. Inventory rose for the ninth consecutive week, with total Altos supply up just over 1.0 million units and +0.02% YoY — effectively flat on a year-ago basis, meaning supply growth has stalled well below where it was running a year ago. New listings ticked up 8.5% YoY in the most recent week, though the 4-week and 13-week averages tell a more modest story at +1.2% and +1.5% YoY respectively, and the 13-week trend has been rising for 14 weeks. Pending days on market edged higher YoY for the 104th consecutive week, sitting at 70.8 days — the market is not tightening on that dimension. NAR September existing home sales came in at 3.99 million SAAR, a beat against a 3.85 million consensus and a fractional uptick from August, though the NSA count is down 3.9% YoY and the YoY trend is decelerating. Inventory at 1.6 million units rose 4.6% YoY with months supply at 4.8 — still below a balanced 5-6 months but no longer pinched. The divergence with new homes is notable: new home sales fell to 676K SAAR in September, down 6.0% YoY, with 8.4 months of supply sitting on builders' lots — that is an elevated level that will keep builder pricing under pressure. The 30-year fixed mortgage rate stands at 7.33% as of October 26, up 114 basis points year over year and essentially unchanged over the past month. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.35 percentage points — historically the spread runs closer to 1.7 points, so there remains roughly 60 basis points of compression available if credit risk appetite normalizes, which would push the 30-year toward the mid-6s without any Fed action. That spread has actually been widening slightly over the past seven weeks, so the compression story is not playing out yet. The leading indicators are less encouraging: the MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 15.7% YoY, at its lowest level since April 2024, and has fallen seven consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA by 2-4 weeks — turned negative YoY on a 4-week basis at -1.6%, after spending several weeks in positive territory. When MII rolls over before MBA, the directional signal is meaningful; here both are pointed the same way. The one offset is that September single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY after running positive — the pipeline of future supply is narrowing — while starts missed estimates badly at 1,221K total against a 1,350K consensus. Builders are completing what is in the pipeline but pulling back on future authorizations. Watch the October MBA purchase applications print and whether the Xactus MII stabilizes; if both remain weak into November, the spring selling season setup looks softer than last year's.

The macro backdrop for housing is genuinely complicated right now. September payrolls came in at +29K against a +59K consensus — a meaningful miss — while the unemployment rate ticked up to 4.2%. That combination is not yet recessionary, but the labor market is clearly decelerating, and the direction matters: fewer hires, softer wage growth (+3.0% YoY in average hourly earnings versus a +0.3% monthly expectation), and a quits rate that fell back in August all point to workers feeling less confident about job mobility. The housing channel here is direct — fewer confident job-changers means fewer voluntary moves, which suppresses transaction volume independent of affordability. The longer-term unemployment cohort, those out of work 27 weeks or more, rose to 1.944 million in September, up 7.1% YoY and rising for two consecutive months. This group does not affect the current selling season but will shape delinquency volume in the first half of 2027 if it keeps accumulating. On inflation, Core PCE sits at +2.95% YoY through August — still above the Fed's 2% target — while shelter CPI ran +3.2% YoY in September. Shelter inflation has been sticky, but the private-market rent data (Zillow ZORI at +1.4% YoY) suggests it will continue to cool with a lag. The 10-year Treasury is at 4.99% — essentially at 5% — and has moved up 98 basis points year over year. That is the single largest constraint on mortgage affordability right now, and it is driven by term premium (the extra yield investors demand to hold long-dated debt) rather than near-term rate expectations, which means the Fed cutting the overnight rate does not directly solve it.
