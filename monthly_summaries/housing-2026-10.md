# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held in positive year-over-year territory through the four weeks ending July 3, with the Altos weekly read at +5.85% YoY and the 4-week average at +3.57% YoY — extending what is now a 56-consecutive-week streak of positive YoY demand readings on the 13-week measure, which sits at +5.48% YoY. That streak is the headline, but the direction within it deserves attention: the 4-week average has decelerated and the period change turned negative, meaning demand is still ahead of last year but losing momentum as we move into the seasonal slowdown. Inventory rose for a ninth consecutive week, with total active supply at roughly 1.09 million units, essentially flat year-over-year (+0.02% YoY), while new listings ticked up +8.46% YoY on the spot reading but only +1.22% on the 4-week average — suggesting the listing acceleration is uneven week to week rather than a clean trend. Pending days on market at 70.8 days sits +6.21% above a year ago, a sign that absorption is gradual; homes are moving, but buyers are taking their time. September existing home sales printed at 3.99 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and snapping a recent decline — but the NSA figure shows -3.94% YoY, which is the more honest read on underlying activity. New home sales at 676K SAAR came in roughly in line with existing, though the new home median price fell -3.93% YoY as builders continued to compete on effective price. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.29% as of today, up 102 basis points year-over-year, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.30-2.34 points — roughly 60-65 basis points above the historical norm of ~1.70. That excess spread is the affordability lever most agents aren't watching: if bond market volatility subsides and lender margins normalize even partially, rates could ease meaningfully without any Fed action. The MBA purchase index bounced +3.79% in the latest week but the 4-week average is -16.79% YoY and the 13-week average is -9.59% YoY — both at multi-year lows — which is the leading demand signal to watch into November. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks, printed at 110.9 with its 4-week average rolling over (-4.69% over the period) and its 13-week average in a falling 8-week trend. The divergence between a still-positive Altos pending streak and a sharply negative MBA trend is the tension in the market right now: Altos is capturing what is happening at the transaction level, MBA is signaling what is forming in the pipeline. Watch whether the MII stabilizes or continues lower in early November — that will tell you whether the MBA weakness is seasonal or something more durable. Single-family permits fell -3.86% YoY after having been positive as recently as last month, and starts missed expectations at 1,221K SAAR, down -8.06% YoY. Builders are still completing the pipeline — single-family completions are running, though down -11% YoY — but pulling back on future authorization. That restraint limits the resale competition from new homes in 2027, not this selling season.

The macro backdrop is doing a reasonable impression of "stable but fragile." September payrolls came in at roughly +29K against a +59K consensus — a notable miss — while the unemployment rate ticked up to 4.2%, beating the 4.4% expectation but still reversing a recent downtrend. The mechanism for housing is direct: a labor market that is softening at the margin makes mortgage underwriting tighter in practice (lenders respond to employment uncertainty before the data fully arrives), and it reduces the urgency that drives move-up buyers. Average hourly earnings grew only +0.13% month-over-month against a +0.3% expectation, which keeps the real wage picture murky. Core PCE for September printed at +3.11% YoY — still above the Fed's 2% target and rising for seven consecutive months — while shelter CPI held at +3.22% YoY. The persistence of shelter inflation reflects the lag between real-time rent deceleration and the BLS measurement methodology; Zillow's rent index is up only +1.42% YoY and falling for three consecutive months, which means shelter's contribution to CPI will mechanically ease over the coming quarters, giving the Fed cover it doesn't yet have. The 10-year Treasury sits at 4.99% — up 91 basis points year-over-year — and that level, more than the Fed funds rate, is what is keeping the 30-year fixed near 7.3%. The two-speed price story in Case-Shiller (data through August) remains the most agent-actionable regional read: Chicago is up +6.61% YoY, New York +6.24%, Cleveland +4.26%, and Miami +4.33% — markets where constrained supply is still winning.
