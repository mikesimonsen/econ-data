# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales ended the four-week window at 84,611, up 5.85% year-over-year, extending a streak that now stands at 56 consecutive weeks of positive YoY readings. The four-week average is running at +3.57% YoY — decelerating from mid-summer pace but still firmly positive — and the 13-week average holds at +5.48% YoY, with its trend still improving. That underlying demand signal is more durable than it looks in the weekly choppiness. Inventory reached 1.09 million active listings as of early July, essentially flat year-over-year (+0.02%), with new listings running +8.46% YoY on the spot week but the four-week average a much quieter +1.22% — suggesting the spot spike was seasonal noise rather than a structural shift in seller behavior. Pending days on market ticked up 6.2% YoY, now at 70.8 days, a gradual lengthening that has persisted for two years and reflects the affordability drag rather than any sudden softening. NAR's September existing home sales printed at 3.99 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and snapping a run of monthly declines — but on a NSA basis, sales were down 3.9% YoY, which is the truer read at this point in the seasonal calendar. New home sales came in at 676K SAAR in September, down 6.0% YoY, with months supply still elevated at 8.4 — builders are sitting on inventory, and single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY after being positive most of the year, a signal that the pipeline beyond the current selling season is thinning. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.29% as of October 29, having eased a few basis points in recent days after a prolonged climb; the 10-year Treasury closed at 4.95%. That puts the mortgage-Treasury spread at approximately 2.34% on a four-week average basis — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 percentage points. The spread has been widening for eight consecutive weeks, which means even if the 10-year holds here, mortgage rates have room to fall 50-60 basis points without any Fed action, simply from spread normalization. Watch that compression — it is the affordability lever most buyers and agents aren't pricing in. The leading indicators are sending a cautionary note: the MBA purchase index four-week average is down 16.8% YoY, at its lowest level since February 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which tends to lead MBA applications by two to four weeks, turned negative on its spot print and its four-week average has reversed lower — meaning the MBA data is unlikely to improve materially in November absent a rate move. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the 10-year Treasury can hold below 5.0% and whether any spread compression translates into a meaningful rate dip; that is the only near-term catalyst that could re-accelerate purchase application volume before year-end.

On the macro side, three data points warrant attention. September payrolls added just 29,000 jobs against a consensus of 59,000, and the unemployment rate ticked to 4.2% — though initial jobless claims for the week of October 24 came in at 194,000, well below the 212,000 expected, which complicates the picture. A soft payroll print alongside lean weekly claims suggests the labor market is still adding jobs but at a decelerating pace; for housing, what matters is that fewer new hires means fewer households ready to take on a purchase, while low claims mean existing homeowners are not being forced to sell. The hires rate moved back to 3.3% in August after declining for months, which is a modest positive — voluntary job-changers are the most likely first-time and move-up buyers. More concerning is the slow accumulation of the long-term unemployed: 1.944 million workers have been out of work 27 weeks or more, up 7.1% year-over-year, with the median unemployment duration now at 11.5 weeks, the highest in over a year. This cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is the leading indicator for delinquency pressure in the 2027-2028 window — worth flagging at listing appointments in markets with high purchase-loan concentrations. On inflation, Core PCE printed at +3.11% YoY in September and has been rising for seven consecutive months; shelter CPI held at +3.22% YoY. With core PCE running above 3%, the Fed has little room to cut, which keeps upward pressure on the 10-year and, through it, on mortgage rates. Regionally, the Case-Shiller August data (released this month, reflecting summer closings) shows the two-speed market intact: Chicago +6.6% YoY and New York +6.2% YoY remain the strongest appreciating metros in the index, while Dallas is now down 0.68% YoY and Las Vegas has fallen in seven of the last twelve months, now -1.1% YoY. Seattle is -2.
