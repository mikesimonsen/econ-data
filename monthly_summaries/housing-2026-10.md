# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive for the 56th consecutive week in the Altos data, with the spot reading up 5.9% year-over-year and the 4-week average up 3.6% — though both decelerated from their midsummer pace and the 4-week average just turned negative on a period basis for the first time in months. The 13-week average, up 5.5% YoY, remains the steadier signal and continues to improve, suggesting the underlying demand trend is intact even as the seasonal slowdown arrives. Inventory stood near 1.09 million active listings, essentially flat year-over-year (+0.02%), with new listings running about 8.5% above last year's depressed levels on a spot basis and roughly 1.5% above on the 13-week average — growth that is real but modest, not the supply flood some anticipated. Pending days on market ticked to 70.8 days, up 6.2% YoY, which tells you the market is not tightening further; it is simply stable. September existing home sales came in at a 3.99 million SAAR, a genuine beat versus the 3.85 million consensus and a slight uptick from August — though the NSA YoY read of -3.9% keeps the broader picture honest: transaction volume is still running below year-ago levels. New home sales at 676,000 SAAR slipped modestly and are down nearly 6% YoY, with 8.4 months of supply — elevated by historical standards, which is why builders are cutting prices; the new home median fell to $400,400, off 3.9% YoY. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.35% today, up 122 basis points from a year ago and essentially unmoved over the past month. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.32 points — well above the long-run norm of roughly 1.7 points — meaning that even without Fed action, meaningful rate relief is possible if credit and volatility conditions normalize. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 16.8% YoY, the weakest reading since February 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus MII, which typically leads MBA by 2-4 weeks, printed its 4-week average down 1.6% YoY after a brief positive stretch, and the 13-week trend is worsening — a warning that the near-term MBA trajectory is unlikely to improve sharply. Single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY in September and turned negative for the first time in several months; starts also missed badly (1,221K vs. 1,350K expected), and are down 8.1% in total though single-family starts remain modestly positive YoY at +2.75%. The divergence between a still-positive starts figure and a now-negative permits number suggests builders are completing what is already in the pipeline but not committing meaningfully to new supply — a dynamic that limits inventory replenishment well into 2027. Watch whether the 10-year Treasury holds near 5% or retraces over the next four weeks; that single variable will determine whether the spread conversation becomes real or remains theoretical.

The macro picture in October is one of resilience on the surface with accumulating stress beneath it. September payrolls came in at roughly +29,000 — a significant miss versus the +59,000 consensus — though the unemployment rate beat expectations at 4.2% versus 4.4% forecast, leaving a somewhat contradictory read that will likely be revised. Average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% month-over-month, cooler than the 0.3% expected, which matters for housing in two directions: lower wage growth reduces affordability pressure on prices but also limits the income gains that help buyers qualify. The more consequential labor signal is in the long-duration unemployed cohort: those out of work 27 weeks or more rose 7.1% YoY to 1.944 million, and median unemployment duration is up nearly 14% from a year ago. This cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is the leading population for mortgage delinquency stress 6-12 months out — worth tracking now so it doesn't surprise later. On inflation, core PCE held at 2.95% YoY through August, still above the Fed's target, while shelter CPI ran at 3.22% YoY in September — decelerating but stickier than the spot rental data (Zillow ZORI at +1.4% YoY) would imply, because CPI shelter measures with a significant lag. The 10-year Treasury at 5.03% today is the rate that sets the floor for mortgages, and it reversed a recent decline to tick higher this week; if it holds at this level, the spread compression story stays dormant.
