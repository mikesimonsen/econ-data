# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive on a year-over-year basis for the 56th consecutive week through the Altos data ending July 3, with the spot reading up 5.9% YoY and the four-week average up 3.6% — though both figures are decelerating from the stronger readings earlier in the quarter. The 13-week average, up 5.5% YoY, tells a more stable story: underlying demand has not rolled over, it has simply softened at the edges as affordability continues to bite. Inventory rose for a ninth straight week, sitting just above 1.086 million active listings and essentially flat YoY (+0.02%), with new listings up 8.5% YoY on the spot week but the four-week and 13-week averages running only +1.2% and +1.5% YoY respectively — meaning the spike in new listings is not yet a trend. Pending days on market ticked up 6.2% YoY, a steady grind that signals buyers are not moving with urgency. September existing home sales came in at 3.99 million SAAR, a narrow beat versus the 3.85 million consensus, and technically broke a string of declines — but the NSA read of 341,000 was down 3.9% YoY, which is the more honest comparison for a series this seasonal. New home sales told a softer story: 676,000 SAAR in September, down 6.0% YoY, with the new-home months supply holding at 8.4, still well above equilibrium. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.29% as of October 29, essentially unchanged over the past month and up about 100 basis points YoY — a rate environment that continues to lock in sellers and dampen move-up activity. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.30 points today and averaging 2.34 for the week, well above the historical norm of roughly 170 basis points; that 60-plus-point excess spread is the number agents should know, because it means rates can fall materially on any shift in credit risk appetite without the Fed moving at all. The leading indicators are sending a cautionary signal: the MBA purchase index four-week average is down 16.8% YoY and has fallen for five consecutive weeks, sitting at its lowest since February 2024. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks — turned sharply lower in early July, with its four-week average off 4.7% in the period and its 13-week average falling for eight straight weeks. When MII and MBA diverge this way, with MII turning first, it tends to confirm rather than contradict the MBA weakness. Single-family permits fell to 846,000 SAAR in September, down 3.9% YoY and turning negative for the first time after a stretch of positive YoY readings, while single-family starts dropped to 859,000 — still positive YoY but reversing after a recent run higher. Builders are completing pipeline but pulling back on new authorizations, a pattern that constrains supply in 2028 more than it affects the current selling season. Watch the MBA four-week average over the next four weeks: if it does not stabilize as rates have edged off their peak, it will confirm that the demand softness is rate-driven and durable, not just seasonal.

The macro backdrop in October is genuinely complicated, and agents working with buyers who ask "when will rates come down?" deserve a more precise answer than "when the Fed cuts." September payrolls came in at only 29,000 — well below the 59,000 consensus — with the unemployment rate rising a tenth to 4.2%, though initial jobless claims through October 24 remain remarkably low at 194,000, their best reading in nearly a year. That divergence matters for housing: a labor market that is adding jobs slowly but not shedding them quickly does not produce the forced-sale wave that would reset prices, but it also does not generate the income-confidence that propels first-time buyer activity. Average hourly earnings grew just 0.1% in September versus an expected 0.3%, which helped on inflation optics but signals that real wage growth is decelerating. Core PCE for September printed at 3.11% YoY — still meaningfully above the Fed's target — while shelter CPI ran at 3.22% YoY and continues to grind higher on a seven-month streak. That shelter persistence matters because it keeps the core PCE above the level where the Fed can credibly ease, and it is the single biggest reason the 10-year Treasury is sitting at 4.99% and the spread-adjusted mortgage rate is 7.29%. On the regional price picture: the August Case-Shiller data (the most recent available, reflecting contracts signed roughly in June and July) shows a clean two-speed market. Chicago is up 6.6% YoY and New York up 6.2%, both accelerating, while Dallas is down 0.7%, Seattle down 2.0%, Las Vegas down 1.1%, and Tampa down 0.3% YoY and falling for 22 consecutive months.
