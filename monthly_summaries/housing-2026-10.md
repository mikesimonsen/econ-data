# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales rose 5.85% year-over-year in the most recent Altos weekly read, and the 13-week average has held positive for 56 consecutive weeks — a streak that tells a more durable demand story than any single week's print. The four-week average is running +3.57% YoY, decelerating from the pace of the spring, which is normal for October, but the 13-week trend is still improving. Pending days on market ticked up 6.2% year-over-year, a sign that buyers have a little more time to decide than they did a year ago but not enough to call it a buyer's market. Inventory stands at roughly 1.09 million units, up just 0.02% YoY — supply growth has effectively stalled near its seasonal peak, and the new listings 13-week average at +1.5% YoY is rising but not fast enough to change the balance. NAR's September existing home sales printed at 3.99 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and reversing two months of softness — but on an NSA basis the picture is more muted, with YoY volume down 3.9%. Inventory at 1.6 million units and 4.8 months' supply remains well below the 5-6 month threshold that typically produces flat or falling prices. New home sales at 676,000 SAAR are running about 6% below last year, with 8.4 months of supply on the new-home side — builders have too much finished product relative to their pace of sales, which is why single-family permits have now turned negative YoY for the first time in a while. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.29% today, up 102 basis points year-over-year, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.30 points on a daily basis — against a historical norm closer to 1.7 points. That 60-basis-point excess spread is the affordability lever most buyers aren't aware of: if credit conditions normalize and the spread compresses toward 1.9-2.0 points without the 10-year moving, the 30-year falls to roughly 6.9-7.0% without a single Fed cut. The MBA purchase index four-week average is down 16.8% year-over-year and has fallen for five consecutive weeks — the weakest reading since February 2024. The Xactus MII, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, turned positive YoY in early July but the four-week average has since rolled back over, suggesting the MBA weakness is not bottoming yet. Watch whether the MII stabilizes in early November — if it doesn't, the purchase application softness will likely extend into the holiday stretch.

The macro picture is complicated in a way that matters directly for listing conversations. September payrolls came in at roughly +29,000 — well below the +59,000 consensus — while the unemployment rate ticked up to 4.2% despite beating a higher consensus expectation of 4.4%, a combination that reflects labor market cooling rather than collapse. The mechanism for housing is straightforward: softer hiring reduces household formation and the confidence new buyers need to commit to a 30-year obligation at 7.29%. The quits rate held at 1.9%, near cycle lows, which tells you workers are staying put rather than trading up — and people who don't change jobs tend not to change houses either. On inflation, Core PCE printed at +3.11% year-over-year in September, still running above the Fed's target, and shelter CPI at +3.22% YoY continues to lag the real-time rent deceleration visible in ZORI (Zillow's rent index, now up only 1.4% YoY). That lag keeps reported inflation elevated longer than the underlying reality warrants, which keeps the Fed cautious — and keeps the 10-year Treasury anchored near 5%, the ceiling that has consistently capped affordability this cycle. The long-term unemployed cohort — workers out of work 27 weeks or more — reached 1.944 million in September, up 7.1% year-over-year and rising for the second consecutive month after a brief decline. This cohort doesn't affect October closings, but if it continues to accumulate through winter, it becomes the seed of a delinquency story in mid-2027. Regionally, the Case-Shiller August data (which reflects contracts signed roughly in June-July) confirms the two-speed market remains intact: Chicago +6.6% and New York +6.2% YoY are compounding gains from already-tight inventory markets, while Dallas is down 0.7%, Seattle down 2.0%, Las Vegas down 1.1%, and Tampa down 0.3% — Sun Belt markets still working through excess supply and the affordability math of 2022 pricing. The number to watch next month is the October jobs report: if payrolls recover toward 100,000-plus and the long-term unemployed level stops rising, the demand picture stabilizes; if payrolls print weak again, the MBA applications weakness becomes a trend rather than noise.
