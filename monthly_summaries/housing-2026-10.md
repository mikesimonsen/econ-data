# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive for the 56th consecutive week on Altos's 13-week average, which is the signal to anchor to — even as the spot week and four-week average both softened. The 13-week average stood at 89,627 contracts, up 5.5% year-over-year, and has been rising for over a year. The four-week average came in at 87,636, up 3.6% YoY, though that reading has decelerated from the mid-single-digit pace of late summer. Inventory continued to build: Altos total active supply reached roughly 1.09 million units in early July, up on both a weekly and year-over-year basis for nine consecutive weeks, while new listings on a 13-week average basis were up 1.5% YoY and have risen for 14 straight weeks. Pending days on market, at 70.8 days, remained elevated — up 6.2% year-over-year — which tells you the market is absorbing supply at a slower pace than a year ago, even if it is still absorbing it. NAR's September existing home sales print of 3.99 million SAAR beat expectations of 3.85 million and broke a multi-month declining streak, though the NSA read was down 3.9% year-over-year, the more honest comparison. New home sales (August, the most recent) ran at 684,000 SAAR, up 6.4% on the month but now down 3.1% year-over-year — builders are moving units by cutting price, with the average new-home sale price down 8.8% YoY. At 7.33% today, the 30-year fixed is up 114 basis points year-over-year and has not meaningfully moved in the past month. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, currently 2.35 percentage points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. If that spread compresses toward 2.0, the 30-year rate falls meaningfully without any Fed action. That compression has not started: the spread has ticked up seven consecutive weeks. The forward read is cautionary. MBA purchase applications fell for seven straight weeks, with the four-week average down 15.7% year-over-year — the weakest reading since April 2024. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, turned negative on its four-week average and its 13-week average has been falling for eight weeks, suggesting the MBA weakness is not yet finished. Single-family permits fell 4.0% YoY in September, turning negative for the first time after a positive streak, while total starts missed expectations by nearly 10% and are down 8.1% YoY. The supply pipeline is being cleared, not restocked. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the Xactus MII stabilizes — if it doesn't, the seasonal demand window for Q4 closes faster than the headline pending figures suggest.

The macro backdrop clarifies why: the September labor report was soft on nearly every dimension that matters for housing. Payrolls came in at roughly 29,000 against a consensus of 59,000, and average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% month-over-month versus the 0.3% expected. The unemployment rate ticked up to 4.2%, beating the 4.4% consensus but still reversing a recent declining trend. On its own, a 4.2% unemployment rate is not recessionary — it is roughly consistent with a functioning labor market — but the composition is worth noting: long-term unemployed (27 weeks or more) reached 1.944 million in September, up 7.1% year-over-year and rising for two consecutive months after a brief dip. This cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is the leading indicator for distressed borrower volume in six to twelve months, and agents in markets that already carry elevated inventory should track it. On inflation, Core PCE printed 2.95% year-over-year through August — still above the Fed's target — and shelter CPI, at 3.22% YoY in September, has been running hotter than the broader index for seven consecutive months, which keeps the Fed cautious even as the labor market softens. The combination of a still-elevated 10-year Treasury yield (4.98% today, up nearly 100bp year-over-year) and a wide mortgage-Treasury spread means the affordability math for buyers has not improved since last year. The Case-Shiller data through July still shows the two-speed market clearly: Chicago (+6.8% YoY), New York (+5.7%), San Francisco (+3.7%), and Cleveland (+4.2%) continue to appreciate at a meaningful pace, while Dallas (−0.3%), Denver (−1.0%), Las Vegas (−1.2%), Seattle (−1.6%), and Tampa (−0.7%) remain in negative or near-flat territory. Phoenix just turned positive for the first time after a negative streak, which is worth watching as a potential inflection.
