# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held above year-ago levels for the 56th consecutive week as of early October, with the Altos 13-week average running +5.5% YoY — a durable positive trend even as the spot reading softened to +5.9% and the 4-week average decelerated to +3.6%. That deceleration is worth watching but not yet alarming; the streak's length and the 13-week average's direction suggest underlying demand is intact. Inventory continues to rebuild: Altos total active supply was up about 1,600 units at 1.09M and rising for nine consecutive weeks, with new listings tracking +1.2% YoY on the 4-week average and +1.5% on the 13-week — a gradual normalization rather than a flood. Pending days on market ticked up 6.2% YoY, a modest slowing of pace consistent with buyers having somewhat more time to decide. NAR's September existing home sales came in at 3.99M SAAR, a beat against the 3.85M consensus and a modest uptick from August, though on an NSA YoY basis sales were still off 3.9% — do not conflate the SAAR uptick with a demand acceleration. New home sales tell a different story: September printed 676K SAAR, down 6% YoY, with months supply still elevated at 8.4. Builders are working through pipeline, not adding to it — single-family starts fell 4.6% in September and single-family permits dropped 3.9% YoY, both now in negative YoY territory for the first time in recent months. That pullback on future authorizations matters more for 2027 supply than anything happening this selling season. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.33% today, up 114 basis points from a year ago, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.34 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. The spread has been rising for seven weeks on the 4-week average. The hidden affordability story here is that if the spread compresses back toward its historical range, the 30-year could fall meaningfully without any Fed action at all — that is the mechanism agents should be able to explain at a listing appointment. Against that backdrop, MBA purchase applications are flashing a clear warning: the 4-week average is down 15.7% YoY and at its lowest reading since April 2024, falling for seven straight weeks. The Xactus MII, which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks, was up just 1.6% YoY in early October on the spot reading but the 4-week average was already down 1.6% YoY and decelerating — the two indicators are roughly aligned, both pointing lower. Watch the MBA 4-week average in the first two weeks of November; if it does not stabilize, the October closing data will be soft.

The macro picture has shifted in a way that matters for housing. September payrolls came in at roughly +29K against a +59K consensus expectation — a genuine miss, not a rounding issue. The unemployment rate rose a tenth to 4.2%, though initial jobless claims have since fallen sharply to 194K, well below the 212K consensus, which partially offsets the payroll concern. The more worrying labor signal is in duration: long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose 7.1% YoY in September, and median unemployment duration is up 13.9% YoY. This cohort does not affect the current selling season — these households are not in the market now. But as that stock accumulates, it becomes the future distressed-borrower pipeline. It is worth tracking every month. On inflation, Core PCE was 2.95% YoY through August, and Core CPI came in at 2.57% in September — both cooler than expected, which matters because it keeps the Fed's path toward easing open even if that path is slower than the market would prefer. Shelter CPI at 3.22% YoY continues to run hot relative to real-time rental indices; the Zillow ZORI fell 0.82% month-over-month in September and is up only 1.4% YoY, so the shelter lag in the CPI will eventually pull that component down. With the 10-year Treasury at 4.99% today — near a 52-week high — and the mortgage-Treasury spread still 60+ basis points above its long-run norm, the rate environment is the primary constraint on demand heading into year-end. The Case-Shiller August data reinforces the two-speed market: Chicago is up 6.6% YoY, New York up 6.2%, Cleveland up 4.3%, Miami up 4.3% — Midwest and Northeast metros where supply is structurally thin are still appreciating at a meaningful pace. Against that, Seattle is down 2.0% YoY, Dallas down 0.7%, Las Vegas down 1.1%, Tampa down 0.3% — Sun Belt markets that added inventory and absorbed speculative demand during the pandemic are still working through the correction. The signal to watch for next month is whether the 10-year Treasury stabilizes or continues
