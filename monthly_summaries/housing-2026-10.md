# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held solidly positive through the four weeks ending around October 7, with the Altos spot reading up 5.9% year-over-year and the 4-week average up 3.6% — extending a streak that has now run 56 consecutive weeks in positive territory on the 13-week average, which itself sits at +5.5% YoY. That consistency in the demand signal is the headline: buyers have not walked away even as rates climbed. Inventory reached roughly 1.09 million active listings, up just 0.02% YoY — inventory growth has essentially stalled against the seasonal norm of continued expansion — while new listings are running about 8.5% above year-ago on the spot week and the 13-week average is up 1.5% YoY, still rising for 14 consecutive weeks. Pending days on market ticked up 6.2% YoY, a subtle sign that absorption is slower than a year ago, though not alarming. NAR September existing home sales came in at a 3.99 million SAAR, a thin beat against a 3.85 million consensus, and YoY sales are down 1.5% — down, but decelerating in the decline. On a NSA basis the YoY gap is -3.9%, which is the right comparison for the direction of the market; the SAAR beat is worth noting but not overstating. New home sales (August, the most recent print) ran at 684,000 SAAR, up 6.4% on the month but turning negative YoY for the first time after a period of outperformance — a sign that builder pricing power is fading, confirmed by median new-home prices off 5.8% YoY. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.33% as of October 23, down modestly over four days but up 115bp YoY and well above the level where rate-locked sellers move voluntarily. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.34 points — well above the long-run norm of roughly 1.7 points — and the 4-week average has been rising for seven weeks. Compression from here to historic norms would bring the 30-year down toward 6.6-6.7% without any Fed action; that is the affordability lever agents should understand. The MBA purchase index is the warning sign: the 4-week average is down 15.7% YoY, at its lowest since April 2024, and falling for four consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA applications by two to four weeks — turned positive YoY in early October at +1.6%, but its 4-week average is down 1.6% YoY and the 13-week trend has been falling for eight weeks. The divergence between a still-positive spot MII reading and a deteriorating moving average is not a clean leading indicator right now; watch whether the MII stabilizes or rolls over further. September single-family permits fell 4.0% on the month and turned negative YoY, while total starts missed expectations by a wide margin (1,221K actual vs. 1,350K expected) and single-family starts reversed lower after a recent run. Builders are completing what is in the pipeline — single-family completions are tracking — but they are pulling back on future commitments, which constrains new supply in 2027-2028, not this selling season.

The macro backdrop this month is best read as a labor market that is cooling more slowly than feared, combined with inflation that remains above target but is no longer reaccelerating. September payrolls came in at roughly +29,000 — a significant miss against the +59,000 consensus — but initial jobless claims as of October 17 printed 194,000, well below the 212,000 expected and the lowest in over a year, which argues the payroll miss was noisy rather than a clean deterioration signal. The unemployment rate rose to 4.2%, but beat the 4.4% consensus, and hires rates firmed slightly in August. For housing, the relevant read is: the labor market is not collapsing in a way that would force distressed selling, but neither is it tight enough to push buyer confidence decisively higher. The long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 weeks or more — rose to 1.944 million in September, up 7.1% YoY, with median unemployment duration at 11.5 weeks and climbing. This group does not threaten the current selling season, but they are the seed corn for delinquency pressure in 6-12 months; worth tracking every month. On inflation, Core PCE through August was running at 2.95% YoY, and shelter CPI in September sat at 3.22% YoY — both still above the Fed's 2% target but neither accelerating. The 10-year Treasury yield is at 4.99% as of October 23, up 98bp YoY; that is the primary force holding the 30-year fixed where it is, and any softening in the long end would flow through to mortgage rates faster than a Fed cut would.
