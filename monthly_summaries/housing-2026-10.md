# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive for the 56th consecutive week on Altos data, with the spot week at +5.9% YoY and the four-week average at +3.6% — though the four-week average has decelerated over the past month and the 13-week average, at +5.5% YoY, is nearly flat week-over-week, which is worth watching as we move into the seasonally slower period. Inventory rose for the ninth consecutive week and stood roughly flat YoY (+0.02%), with new listings up +8.5% YoY on the spot week — the 13-week new-listings average is up +1.5% YoY and has been rising for 14 weeks, meaning supply is rebuilding gradually but not flooding the market. Pending days on market at 70.8 days is running +6.2% YoY, a sign that homes are taking modestly longer to go under contract than a year ago. The September NAR existing home sales print came in at 3.99 million SAAR, a beat against the 3.85 million consensus and a fractional uptick from August — but the NSA read was down 3.9% YoY, and four consecutive months of falling NSA sales in the Midwest underscore that the headline SAAR isn't capturing the regional softness. New home sales told a cooler story: 676,000 SAAR in September, down 6.0% YoY and reversing three months of gains, with new home inventory at 8.4 months supply — elevated and still rising YoY. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.27% as of today, up 94 basis points YoY. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.32 percentage points — still roughly 60 basis points above the long-run norm of about 1.7 points — meaning rates can fall meaningfully even if the 10-year holds near 4.95%, provided lenders and MBS markets normalize. That spread actually ticked back up this week after compressing in September, which is the hidden headwind agents should note. MBA purchase applications delivered a single-week bounce (+3.8%) but the four-week average is down 16.8% YoY, at its lowest since February 2024, and the 13-week average is at its lowest since September 2024 — a sustained softening, not a blip. The Xactus MII, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, turned positive YoY at +1.6% on the spot week but its four-week average fell for the first time in several months; watch whether that MII four-week average firms or continues lower — it will tell you before MBA does whether November applications recover. Single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY in September, turning negative after a run of positive readings, and starts missed consensus badly (1,221K actual vs. 1,350K expected), down 8.1% YoY. Builders are clearing completed pipeline — single-family completions running at 840,000 SAAR — but pulling back on new authorizations, which constrains 2027 resale competition from new product.

The macro backdrop in October presented three housing-relevant data points that cut in different directions. September payrolls came in at roughly +29,000, well below the +59,000 consensus — a soft print that, on its own, would push rate expectations lower and support housing demand; but the unemployment rate simultaneously beat expectations, rising to 4.2% against a 4.4% consensus forecast, and initial claims as of October 24 fell to 194,000, the lowest in months and well below the 212,000 expectation. The picture is a labor market that is generating fewer jobs but not yet shedding workers at scale. The mechanism for housing: fewer new hires means fewer household formation events and fewer move-up buyers who feel confident enough to give up a locked-in mortgage, but low claims mean delinquency pressure remains contained. The long-term unemployed cohort — workers jobless 27 or more weeks — rose 7.1% YoY in September, now at 1.944 million and rising for two consecutive months after a brief decline. This is not a 2026 selling-season story; it is a 6-to-12-month leading indicator for distressed inventory and servicer watch-lists. Core PCE came in at +3.11% YoY in September, still above the Fed's target and running hot enough that rate-cut timing remains uncertain; that keeps the 10-year anchored near 5% and limits how much the spread compression story can play out in the near term. The regional Case-Shiller data through August reinforces the two-speed market: Chicago (+6.6% YoY) and New York (+6.2%) are appreciating at a pace that makes affordability a genuine constraint for buyers in those markets, while Dallas (−0.7%), Denver (−0.8%), Seattle (−2.0%), and Tampa (−0.3%) are still in YoY price contraction — agents in those Sun Belt markets are operating in a fundamentally different pricing conversation than their Midwest and Northeast counterparts.
