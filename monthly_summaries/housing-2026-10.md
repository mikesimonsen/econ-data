# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held in positive year-over-year territory for the 56th consecutive week in the Altos data through early July, with the 13-week average running +5.5% YoY — the most reliable read of underlying demand momentum we have. The four-week average has decelerated to +3.6% YoY and the spot week came in at +5.9% YoY, suggesting demand is still positive but losing some velocity as we move into the autumn cooling period. Pending days on market ticked up 6.2% YoY to 70.8 days, a reminder that the market is not operating at the frenetic pace of 2021-22 even where demand remains positive. Active inventory was essentially flat year-over-year at just over 1.08 million units in the Altos weekly read, with new listings running +8.5% YoY on a spot basis but the 13-week average holding a more modest +1.5% YoY — telling us the new-listing acceleration is real but not large enough to materially reset supply conditions. September existing home sales came in at a 3.99 million SAAR, modestly above the 3.85 million consensus, which reversed a recent declining streak; on an NSA basis the YoY comparison was -3.9%, a more sober read on the underlying pace. New home sales at 676K SAAR are running roughly 170K above existing, an unusually wide divergence that reflects builders' willingness to buy down rates where existing sellers cannot. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.27% as of today, up 94 basis points year-over-year, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury has widened back to 2.32-2.34 points on a 4-week average basis — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That spread is the number to watch: if it compresses 30-40 basis points toward a more normal level, the 30-year falls meaningfully without any Fed action, and that is the most plausible near-term affordability release valve. Against that backdrop, the MBA Purchase Index's 4-week average is running -16.8% YoY and the 13-week average -9.6% YoY, both at multi-year lows — a significant caution flag. The Xactus MII, which typically leads MBA by 2-4 weeks, showed a spot reading of 110.9 YoY +1.6% but the 4-week average has turned down to -1.6% YoY, and the 13-week average has been falling for eight consecutive weeks. When MII rolls over before MBA, it tends to confirm softening rather than predict a reversal. The builder side adds nuance: total starts missed expectations badly at 1,221K SAAR (-8.1% YoY), single-family permits fell to 846K and have now turned negative YoY for the first time in recent months, and units under construction remain in a 38-month YoY contraction. Builders appear to be completing their existing pipeline — single-family completions are running — without committing meaningfully to future supply. Watch November MBA applications and the next Xactus MII print for evidence the demand softening is stabilizing or deepening.

The macro picture is doing most of the work on rates, and not in a helpful direction for housing. September payrolls came in at roughly +29K against a +59K consensus — a meaningful miss — while the unemployment rate rose a tenth to 4.2%, and average hourly earnings cooled to +3.0% YoY. Taken together, the labor market is clearly softening, but not breaking: the mechanism for housing is straightforward — softer payrolls reduce household formation and transaction confidence, particularly among move-up buyers who need employment certainty to take on a larger mortgage. The more concerning labor signal is structural: the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose to 1.944 million, up 7.1% YoY and back in an uptrend after a brief decline. This cohort does not affect the current selling season but represents a future delinquency pipeline that will matter for distressed-inventory supply in 2027. On inflation, Core PCE came in at 3.11% YoY in September — still running above the Fed's target and up for the seventh consecutive month — while shelter CPI held at 3.22% YoY. The persistence of shelter inflation is somewhat circular given where home prices and rents are, but it keeps the Fed cautious and the 10-year Treasury anchored near 4.95%. On the regional price story, the August Case-Shiller data (reference period: August 2026) makes the two-speed market vivid: Chicago is appreciating at +6.6% YoY and New York at +6.2% YoY, both rising seven months running, while Dallas sits at -0.7% YoY, Las Vegas at -1.1% YoY, Seattle at -2.0% YoY, and Tampa at -0.3% YoY, all in negative territory. Agents in Midwest and Northeast markets are working a fundamentally different price conversation than agents in the Sun Belt cities where inventory has been rebuilding for two years
