# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive year-over-year for the 56th consecutive week on the Altos 13-week average, which tracked at +5.48% YoY — a streak worth noting to buyers who think the market has simply stalled. The four-week average came in at +3.57% YoY, though the spot reading of +5.85% was accompanied by four straight weeks of week-over-week declines, a seasonal pattern worth watching as October closes. Inventory continued building: Altos total active supply rose for a ninth consecutive week, up fractionally YoY after running negative for years, and new listings on a 13-week basis have been higher for 14 straight weeks, up about 1.5% YoY. Pending days on market sat at 70.8 days, up 6.2% YoY — still elevated, and a reminder that the pace of absorption has not returned to 2021-era norms. September existing home sales (SAAR) printed at 3.99 million, beating the 3.85 million consensus and reversing a string of declines, though the NSA read of 341,000 was down 3.9% YoY — the seasonally adjusted beat matters more for direction than magnitude here. New home sales ran hotter at 684,000 SAAR in August, up 6.4% on the month but down 3.1% YoY, with builders discounting aggressively: the new-home median fell to $393,700 (-5.8% YoY). The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.33% today, unchanged on the day, up 114 basis points YoY, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.35 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points and, notably, widening again after several weeks of compression. That spread is the hidden affordability story: even without Fed action, a normalization toward 1.8-2.0 points would pull the 30-year into the mid-to-upper 6s. MBA purchase applications are the indicator demanding the most attention right now: the four-week average is down 15.7% YoY, at its lowest since April 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 straight weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which typically leads MBA by 2-4 weeks — told the same story a month ago: the MII four-week average reversed lower in early October after a brief rise, and the 13-week average has been falling for eight weeks. Both signals point to a softer November close rate. September single-family permits fell 4.0% YoY and turned negative after months of positive readings, while total starts missed expectations at 1,221,000 SAAR (-8.1% YoY). The divergence between a still-positive single-family starts trend and a now-negative permit trend implies builders are completing their existing pipelines but pulling back on future authorization — a supply constraint that will matter more in 2027 than in the current selling season. Watch whether the MII stabilizes in the next two to three weeks; if it does not, November pending sales will soften beyond normal seasonal adjustment.

The macro backdrop is doing genuine damage to affordability, and the mechanism is worth being precise about. Payrolls grew by only 29,000 in September against a 59,000 consensus — a miss that, on its own, would signal softening labor demand and potentially lower rates. But the 10-year Treasury is sitting at 4.98% today, up 97 basis points YoY, because inflation has not cooperated: Core PCE ran at 2.95% YoY through August, and headline CPI came in at 3.72% YoY in September (cooler than the 0.9% monthly consensus but still well above target). Shelter CPI was 3.22% YoY in September, which continues to lag the deceleration already visible in asking rents — Zillow's ZORI fell for three straight months and sits at +1.42% YoY. As measured shelter inflation catches up to real-time rent data over the next several months, Core PCE has a credible path toward 2.5%, which would give the Fed more room to act. The labor deterioration worth watching closely is not the headline unemployment rate (4.2%, which beat the 4.4% consensus) but the long-term unemployed: the count of workers unemployed 27 or more weeks rose to 1.944 million in September, up 7.1% YoY, and median unemployment duration ticked up to 11.5 weeks, +13.9% YoY. That cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is the seed of future mortgage stress if it continues accumulating.
