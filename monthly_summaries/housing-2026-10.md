# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through the four weeks ending October 27, but the signals underneath are worth reading carefully. Altos new pending sales came in at +5.85% YoY on the spot read, and the 4-week average is running +3.57% YoY — the 13-week average, now 56 consecutive weeks positive YoY, sits at +5.48% and is still trending higher. That streak is the most durable demand signal in the data right now, and it has survived a rate environment that should, by most conventional models, have broken it. Inventory continued to build — Altos total supply is up roughly 9 straight weeks and sits just above 1.08 million units, though YoY growth has nearly flattened (+0.02%), suggesting the inventory expansion that defined 2025 is running out of momentum. New listings are edging higher: the 4-week average is +1.22% YoY, the 13-week average +1.50% and rising for 14 consecutive weeks, a steady if unspectacular supply pulse. Pending days on market at 70.8 days is running +6.21% YoY — listings are sitting longer than a year ago, which matters for pricing conversations but is not yet signaling a demand breakdown given that pending counts are still positive. NAR September existing home sales printed at 3.99 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus and reversing two months of declines — a meaningful beat, though the NSA read of 341,000 units was down 3.9% YoY, so the seasonally adjusted headline is doing more work than it appears. New home sales at 676,000 SAAR fell about 6% YoY, and with months supply at 8.4, builders are carrying more inventory than the existing side. The 30-year fixed closed today at 7.33%, unchanged on the day but up 114 basis points year-over-year — the headline rate is the story most agents are hearing, but the mechanism that matters more is the spread: the 30Y-to-10Y Treasury spread sits at 2.34 points against a historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. The 10Y itself is at 4.99%. If that spread compresses toward 1.9-2.0 points — not an implausible scenario if the Fed's forward guidance softens — the 30Y rate could fall 35-40 basis points without any change in the underlying Treasury. Watch for that compression; it is the affordability improvement that does not require a Fed cut. MBA purchase applications are the sharpest caution flag: the 4-week average is -15.7% YoY and has fallen for four consecutive weeks to its lowest level since April 2024. The Xactus MII, which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks, is itself softening — the 4-week average reversed lower and the 13-week average has fallen for eight straight weeks — confirming this is not a seasonal blip. Builder permit data reinforces the caution: single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY in September and have now flipped negative after months of positive readings. Starts missed expectations by a wide margin at 1,221,000 versus the 1,350,000 consensus. Builders are completing their pipelines — single-family completions are still running — but pulling back on what they authorize next. That matters less for 2026 inventory and more for what becomes available in 2027-2028.

The macro backdrop going into November is a study in conflicting signals, and the conflict itself is the story. September payrolls came in at roughly +29,000 — a significant miss against the +59,000 consensus — but the unemployment rate surprised in the other direction, printing 4.2% against a 4.4% expectation. Initial jobless claims for the week ending October 17 were 194,000, well below the 212,000 consensus, suggesting the labor market is not falling apart even if monthly payroll counts are erratic. The more concerning labor signal is not in the headlines: the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose to 1.944 million, up 7.1% YoY for the second consecutive month after a brief reversal. This cohort does not affect this selling season — these households are not buying homes in the next 90 days — but their accumulation is the leading edge of future mortgage stress. Average hourly earnings grew just 3.0% YoY and came in cooler than expected, which is rate-favorable but also compresses the affordability math for would-be buyers whose incomes are the denominator. On inflation, Core PCE for August was 2.95% — above the Fed's 2% target and rising for seven consecutive months — while shelter CPI for September ran at 3.22% YoY, a figure that will not mechanically cool quickly given how the index is constructed. The gap between real-time rent data (Zillow ZORI at +1.42% YoY, itself softening three consecutive months) and CPI shelter means the official inflation measure is still absorbing last cycle's rent increases.
