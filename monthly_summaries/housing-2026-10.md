# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through October but is showing clear signs of deceleration heading into year-end. Altos weekly new pending sales came in at +5.85% YoY for the week ending July 3 — the 56th consecutive week of positive year-over-year reads — but the 4-week average has slipped to +3.57% YoY and is falling, and the 13-week average, while still up +5.48% YoY and gradually improving in trend, reflects a market that peaked in midsummer and is now cooling at a normal seasonal pace. The pace of inventory build has similarly stalled near flat: total Altos inventory sat just +0.02% above a year ago at roughly 1.09 million units, rising for nine consecutive weeks but barely registering a YoY gain. New listings are a brighter spot — up +8.46% YoY on the spot read, with the 13-week average running +1.50% YoY and trending higher for 14 weeks — which tells you sellers are showing up, but not fast enough to meaningfully relieve the supply constraint. Pending days on market at 70.8 days are running +6.21% above last year, a sign that homes are taking longer to move even as demand remains technically positive. NAR's September existing home sales print of 3.99 million SAAR beat expectations of 3.85 million and reversed a recent decline, though the NSA read of 341,000 closed units was down 3.94% YoY — the SAAR improvement reflects seasonal adjustment mechanics more than an underlying acceleration. New home sales at 676,000 SAAR slipped 0.59% from August and are down 5.98% YoY, with months supply for new homes at 8.4 — elevated enough that builders are clearly prioritizing clearing existing inventory. Total housing starts missed badly at 1,221,000 SAAR versus a 1,350,000 consensus, and single-family permits fell 3.86% month-over-month and turned negative YoY for the first time in several months — the starts-versus-permits divergence signals builders are delivering on the pipeline they authorized earlier but pulling back on future commitments, a supply story that won't resolve until 2027 at the earliest. On rates: the 30-year fixed sits at 7.35% today, up 122 basis points from a year ago. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.32 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points — meaning a meaningful portion of today's rate pain is spread-driven, not purely a Treasury story. If credit and volatility conditions normalize enough to compress the spread 30-40 basis points, the 30-year could fall to the mid-6s without any Fed action. The MBA purchase index's 4-week average is down 16.79% YoY and at its lowest level since February 2024 — the trend is deteriorating. The Xactus MII, which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks, showed its 4-week average turning lower after a recent rise and the 13-week average now falling for eight consecutive weeks, suggesting no near-term relief in purchase application volumes. Watch the 10-year Treasury — it touched 5.03% today and is moving in the wrong direction. If it holds above 5%, mortgage rates are unlikely to provide any demand tailwind heading into November.

The macro picture is working against housing on two fronts simultaneously: rates are elevated and the labor market, while not broken, is softening at the edges in ways that matter for buyer confidence. September payrolls came in at just +29,000 — a significant miss versus the +59,000 consensus — though the unemployment rate beat expectations at 4.2% versus a 4.4% forecast, and initial jobless claims have been running well below expectations, printing 194,000 against a 212,000 estimate. Those two data points pull in different directions: the claims number says layoffs remain contained, but the quits rate at 1.9 — down 0.1 point YoY — tells you workers feel less confident about leaving jobs voluntarily, which is historically a leading softener of household formation and discretionary moves. The long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) is the number to watch: it rose 7.11% YoY in September and is now rising for two consecutive months after a brief decline, accumulating quietly at 1.944 million people. That cohort doesn't threaten the current selling season, but as it grows it seeds a future distressed-borrower story 6-12 months out. Core PCE sits at 2.95% YoY — the Fed's target is close but not cleared — and shelter CPI is running +3.22% YoY, still embedding last cycle's rent gains into the index with a lag. The Case-Shiller data through August (released this week, so reflecting closings from roughly June-August) continues to show the two-speed market clearly: Chicago is up 6.61% YoY, New York up 6.24%, Cleveland up 4.26% — the Midwest and dense Northeast metros are holding price appreciation that would surprise many national-narrative readers.
