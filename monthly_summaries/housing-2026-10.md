# Monthly Housing Summary — October 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive year-over-year for the 56th consecutive week, with the Altos 13-week average running at +5.5% YoY — a level that suggests underlying demand has been more resilient than the headline environment implies. The spot read for the week of July 3 came in at +5.9% YoY, and the 4-week average sits at +3.6% YoY, though it decelerated from the prior period. Inventory continued rising, up roughly 4.6% YoY on the NAR count and a more modest +0.02% YoY on the Altos active inventory measure, meaning the supply gap from prior years is narrowing but has not flipped to oversupply nationally. New listings on Altos ticked up +8.5% YoY on the spot week, with the 13-week average rising for 14 consecutive weeks at +1.5% YoY — supply is easing in gradually, not flooding in. Pending days on market at 70.8 days are running +6.2% YoY, a steady softening of pace that has persisted for over 100 weeks. NAR existing home sales printed 3.99 million SAAR for September, beating expectations of 3.85 million, though the NSA count is still down 3.9% YoY — use the YoY on the raw count to frame direction. New home sales came in at 676,000 SAAR, down 6.0% YoY and softening after several months of relative strength, with months supply at 8.4 — elevated, which is why builder prices continue to compress (median new home price -3.9% YoY). The 30-year fixed sits at 7.33% as of October 27, up 114 basis points year-over-year and essentially flat over the past two days. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, currently 2.34 percentage points against a historical norm near 1.7pp — that 64bp of excess spread is the hidden affordability lever. With the 10-year at 5.03%, the math on rate relief is constrained, but any spread compression toward historical norms would move the 30-year meaningfully without Fed action. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is running -16.8% YoY, its weakest read since February 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus MII, which leads MBA applications by 2-4 weeks, had its 4-week average turn lower after a period of improvement, and the 13-week average has been falling for 8 weeks — the two measures are now directionally aligned to the downside, which removes the divergence signal that had offered some near-term optimism. Single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY in September and turned negative after several months positive; starts missed consensus by a wide margin at 1,221,000 SAAR. Builders are delivering the pipeline they built but pulling back meaningfully on new authorizations, particularly in the West (-7.8% YoY permits) — the supply implication is a lighter flow of new single-family inventory in 2027-2028. Watch whether the MBA index stabilizes above -15% YoY over the next four weeks; if the 4-week average continues deteriorating, contract volumes will follow into late November.

The macro picture in October carried three signals worth parsing for their housing implications. First, September payrolls came in at +29,000 against a +59,000 consensus — a soft read that would ordinarily support rate expectations softening, but the 10-year Treasury has instead risen to 5.03%, up 104 basis points year-over-year, which suggests the bond market is pricing something other than near-term Fed easing: likely a combination of fiscal supply and stickier-than-expected inflation. Core PCE for August was +2.95% YoY and CPI shelter is running +3.22% YoY — shelter inflation is decelerating but remains the primary remaining driver of above-target core readings, and its persistence keeps the Fed on hold longer than buyers would prefer. The mechanism matters here: with the 10-year elevated and the spread still wide, the 30-year mortgage rate stays structurally high even as the labor market softens at the margin. Second, the long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 weeks or more — rose to 1.944 million in September, up 7.1% YoY and reversing a prior decline. This does not threaten the current selling season; these households are not yet in distress, and the quits rate at 1.9 is still functioning. But a cohort of this size, accumulating at this rate, begins to shape delinquency volumes in 6-12 months, particularly in markets where price appreciation has been thin. Third, on the regional price picture: the Case-Shiller August data (released October 27, referencing August closings) shows the Midwest/Northeast divergence from Sun Belt remains the clearest story in the index. Chicago is up 6.6% YoY, New York up 6.2%, Cleveland up 4.3% — all rising
