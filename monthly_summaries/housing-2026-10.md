*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

New pending sales held in positive territory for the 56th consecutive week on the Altos 13-week average, up 5.5% year over year, which is the more reliable read through seasonal noise — though the spot week at +5.9% YoY and the 4-week average at +3.6% are both decelerating, and the 4-week has turned negative in absolute terms for the first time in months. Inventory continued its steady climb, with Altos total supply rising for nine consecutive weeks and now running about flat year over year at roughly 1.09 million units, while new listings on the 13-week average are up about 1.5% YoY and have been rising for 14 straight weeks — modest growth, not a wave. Pending days on market ticked to 70.8 days, up 6.2% year over year for the 104th consecutive week, which tells you the market is moving but buyer urgency hasn't fully returned. NAR's September existing home sales printed at 3.99 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and snapping a recent declining streak — though on an NSA basis sales were down 3.9% year over year, so the beat is real but not a turning point. Months' supply edged back down to 4.8 after rising, and the national median held positive YoY for the 39th straight month at $417,500 (+1.3% YoY), while median new home prices fell 3.9% YoY to $400,400, keeping builder product priced below resale — a spread that continues to funnel rate-sensitive buyers toward new construction despite new home sales falling 6.0% YoY to 676,000 SAAR. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.33% today, up 114 basis points year over year and holding near its recent range — the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.34 points against a historical norm of roughly 1.7 points, meaning roughly 60 basis points of excess premium remains in the rate. That spread has been widening rather than compressing over the past seven weeks, which is the affordability story most agents are missing: the Fed doesn't need to move for rates to fall meaningfully, but right now the spread is moving in the wrong direction. MBA purchase applications are the warning flag this month — the 4-week average is down 15.7% year over year, at its lowest since April 2024, and has fallen for four consecutive weeks; the 13-week average is off 8.0% YoY. The Xactus MII, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, confirmed the weakness in early October with its 4-week average turning lower after a recent rise and the 13-week average falling for eight straight weeks — the two signals are aligned, not diverging, which removes any ambiguity. Watch for the next two Altos pending reads: if the 4-week average slips further below flat while the MII stays soft, the demand picture for November closings deteriorates meaningfully.

The macro picture for housing this month is dominated by three signals, ranked by near-term relevance. First, the labor market printed softer than expected in September — payrolls came in at +29,000 against a consensus of +59,000, and average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% against an expected 0.3% — but the headline unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, beating the 4.4% consensus, so the report reads as a slowing labor market rather than a deteriorating one. For housing, the wage miss matters more than the payroll miss: slower income growth directly constrains purchasing power at 7.33% rates, and the quits rate held at 1.9%, a level associated with workers who feel less confident about job-switching — which historically correlates with reduced household formation risk-taking. Second, Core PCE came in at 2.95% year over year through August, still running above the Fed's 2% target, and CPI shelter is still rising at 3.2% YoY — shelter inflation's lag to actual rent trends means it will stay elevated into early 2027 even as Zillow's ZORI shows actual rents up only 1.4% YoY. The mechanism: sticky shelter CPI keeps overall core inflation elevated, which keeps rate-cut expectations restrained, which keeps the 10-year Treasury near 5.0% — where it sits today — which keeps mortgage rates where they are. Third, the long-term unemployed cohort rose to 1.944 million in September, up 7.1% year over year with median unemployment duration now 13.9% higher year over year.
