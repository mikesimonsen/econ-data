**July 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive on the 13-week trend but showed a meaningful split at the weekly level heading into July. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% year-over-year — a streak that has now run 55 consecutive weeks — but the most recent weekly read flipped to -2.99% YoY, and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37% from readings closer to +6% earlier in the quarter. That weekly negative is worth watching, not panicking about: July 4th seasonality compresses contract activity every year, and one soft week does not break a 55-week streak. What matters more is whether the 4-week average holds above zero through late July. Inventory at Altos reached roughly 1.075 million active listings, up 1.2% YoY, continuing a gentle eight-week rise — but new listings ticked down 7.1% YoY on the most recent week, and the 4-week average is barely positive at +0.41% YoY. The inventory growth story is decelerating at the source: sellers are pulling back as the selling season peaks, which should keep supply from building aggressively into the fall. Pending days on market held at 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — still elevated, meaning homes are taking longer to go under contract than a year ago, though the rate of increase is not worsening. NAR's June existing home sales came in at 4.09 million SAAR, up 4.1% YoY and well above the 3.85 million consensus — a genuine beat, though the month-over-month dip of roughly 2% off May's pace is a reminder that the headline SAAR is still historically subdued. The NSA read is cleaner here: June closed transactions ran +7.9% YoY, broad-based across all four regions. New home sales tell a different story — May came in at 580,000 SAAR, down 7.5% YoY, with months supply at 10.3, a level that typically pressures builder pricing and incentives. Single-family starts fell to 882,000 in May, down 7.1% YoY, while total starts missed badly against a 1.35 million expectation; single-family permits ticked up to 892,000 but remain negative YoY for the 24th consecutive month, confirming builders are clearing pipeline rather than committing to new supply. The 30-year fixed rate stood at 6.75% as of July 13, edging up 11 basis points over the past month after a period of gradual decline. The spread to the 10-year Treasury — currently 4.57% — sits at approximately 2.18 percentage points, still well above the historical norm of around 1.7 points. The weekly 4-week average spread has been compressing, down 32 basis points YoY, which is the understated affordability lever of the moment: if the spread continues normalizing toward 1.9 points without any move in the 10-year, the 30-year rate falls toward the mid-6s on spread compression alone. MBA purchase applications (4-week average) are essentially flat YoY at +0.50%, decelerating from the +5% pace on the 13-week average — a signal of cooling momentum. More instructive: the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (MII), which tracks credit inquiries 2-4 weeks ahead of MBA applications, turned up in its most recent reading after seven weeks of decline on the 13-week average. The 4-week MII average is also recovering. When the MII leads to the upside, MBA applications tend to follow within a month. Watch whether that recovery in mortgage intent translates into a stabilized purchase index reading through late July and August.

The macro picture this month was shaped by two prints that came in better than feared and one that remained stubborn. June payrolls added approximately 159,000 jobs and the unemployment rate ticked down to 4.2% against a 4.4% consensus expectation — a labor market that is cooling but not cracking, which supports household formation and mortgage qualification at the margin. The long-term unemployed cohort (those out of work 27+ weeks) did pull back month-over-month to 1.937 million, a welcome reversal, but the YoY increase of 17.3% has not resolved. This cohort is not a 2026 selling-season risk; it is a 2027 delinquency signal, particularly in markets where affordability is already stretched and equity cushions are thin. Today's June CPI release was the month's most consequential number for housing: headline CPI fell 0.4% month-over-month against an expectation of +0.9%, and core CPI came in essentially flat at -0.02% against an expected +0.3%. Core PCE for May was still running at 3.41% YoY — above the Fed's target — but the CPI print suggests the May-to-June trend is moving in the right direction.
