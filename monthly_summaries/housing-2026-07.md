# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through June but showed the first cracks worth watching. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% year-over-year — positive for 55 consecutive weeks — and the 4-week average remains at +4.37% YoY, though it has decelerated from higher readings earlier in the quarter. The most recent weekly print flipped negative at -2.99% YoY, the first negative week in that 55-week streak; that single week likely reflects the late-June holiday drag, so read the 4-week average rather than the spot print. Inventory reached 1.07 million active listings nationally as of June 26, up 1.17% YoY — growth is now decelerating sharply after running well ahead of that pace earlier in the year. New listings tell the same story: the 4-week average sits at just +0.41% YoY and the most recent weekly print turned -7.14%, a meaningful pullback from the supply-expanding trend of late 2025 and early 2026. Pending days on market are running 6.7% above a year ago at 70.9 days, confirming that buyers are taking longer even as the headline demand numbers remain modestly positive. NAR existing home sales came in at 4.17 million SAAR for May — a solid beat versus the 3.85 million consensus, up 3.2% YoY and accelerating — though the NSA read shows flat YoY, which is the cleaner comparison given seasonal adjustment quirks this time of year. New home sales tell a different story: 580K SAAR in May, down 7.5% YoY and falling for two consecutive months, while months supply of new homes hit 10.3 — a level that historically pressures builder pricing and incentive behavior. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.65% as of July 1, having ticked up 11 basis points over the past two days. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.19 points currently, against a historical norm near 1.70, the spread remains meaningfully elevated but has compressed 22-32 basis points over the past year depending on the averaging window. That compression — not any Fed action — is the primary mechanism through which mortgage rates can move lower without a policy change; if the spread reverts another 25-30 basis points toward historical norms, the 30-year could approach 6.1-6.2% on an unchanged 10-year. MBA purchase applications reversed higher this week at +3.1% YoY on the 4-week average, though the 13-week trend is worsening. More telling is the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — a credit-pull-based leading indicator that tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks — where the spot reading rebounded to +3.53% on the week but the 13-week average has fallen for seven consecutive weeks and sits -2.38% YoY. The MII's divergence from the MBA 4-week read is the signal to watch in July: if the MII continues recovering, MBA applications should firm; if the MII softens again, the MBA bounce may prove short-lived. On the supply side, single-family starts fell to 882K SAAR in May, down 7.1% YoY, while single-family permits held near 892K — the gap between permits and starts suggests builders have the pipeline authorized but are throttling ground-breakings, likely waiting for rate or demand clarity before committing additional capital. That behavior keeps near-term resale competition in check but means new supply remains constrained into 2027.

The macro backdrop going into July is genuinely two-sided, which is why rate markets are not moving decisively in either direction. May payrolls came in at 172K — a substantial beat against a 59K consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.3%, also beating the 4.4% expected. That combination normally closes the door on near-term rate cuts. But beneath the surface, the labor market shows real softening: the quits rate has fallen 20 basis points YoY to 1.9%, the hires rate is running below year-ago levels, and the cohort of workers unemployed 27 weeks or longer has risen 35.8% year-over-year to nearly 2 million people. The quits rate matters for housing because job mobility — people moving for new positions and triggering a home sale — is a structural source of transaction volume; at this level it is a quiet headwind. The long-term unemployed figure does not show up in this selling season's closed sales, but if it continues accumulating it will feed into delinquency data 6-12 months out. On inflation, core CPI printed +0.21% month-over-month in May, below the 0.3% consensus, with shelter CPI at +0.32% — the slowest shelter print in several years, reflecting the lagged pass-through of 2024's rent deceleration into the index.
