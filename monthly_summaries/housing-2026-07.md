**July 2026 Housing Summary**

New pending sales ticked down 3.0% YoY on the spot read for the week ending June 26 — the first negative weekly print after what had been a long positive streak — but the 4-week average held at +4.4% and the 13-week average sits at +4.8% YoY, now positive for 55 consecutive weeks. The spot softness looks like normal summer seasonality compressing against a strong comparable period last year rather than a trend break; watch the 4-week average over the next two weeks to confirm. Inventory at Altos reached roughly 1.075 million active listings, up 1.2% YoY and rising for eight straight weeks, though growth is decelerating — new listings slipped 7.1% YoY on the spot week, and while the 4-week average is barely positive (+0.4% YoY), the 13-week trend has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks, suggesting the supply pipeline is intact even if July new-listing flow is softening seasonally. Pending days on market held at 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — the market is absorbing homes but not snapping them up the way it did in 2023. NAR's June existing home sales print landed at 4.09 million SAAR, beating consensus of 3.85 million and up 4.1% YoY, with the NSA read up 7.9% YoY — the strongest year-over-year comparison in this cycle. That stands in clear contrast to new home sales, which fell 7.5% YoY in May to 580,000 SAAR with months supply at 10.3 — builders have meaningful unsold inventory to clear, and single-family starts fell a second consecutive month. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.65% as of today, off slightly from its recent high, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.10-2.13 points — still 40 basis points above the long-run norm of roughly 1.7 points. If that spread compresses toward historical levels, the 30-year could fall to the low-to-mid 6s without any Fed action, and that is the affordability lever most agents are not pricing into their conversations. MBA purchase applications turned negative YoY on the most recent weekly read (–6.3%), though the 4-week average is barely positive (+0.5%) and the 13-week average remains +5.1% YoY. More notably, the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA applications by two to four weeks — reversed higher, with the 4-week average up 2.3% from its recent low, suggesting the MBA softness may be short-lived. What to watch over the next four weeks: whether the Altos 4-week pending average holds above zero YoY as July comps get harder, and whether the MII's uptick translates into a corresponding firming in MBA purchase apps by late July.

The macro backdrop improved more than the headlines suggested. June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 net new jobs, and the unemployment rate fell a tenth to 4.2% against a consensus of 4.4% — the labor market is not deteriorating at the pace many expected. For housing, a stable jobs picture matters more than rate moves in any single month; employed households buy homes, and the hires rate holding at 3.3% means the pool of potential buyers is not shrinking. The concern that has not gone away is the long-term unemployed cohort: those out of work 27 weeks or more rose 17.3% YoY even as the headline rate fell, suggesting job-market stress is concentrating rather than spreading broadly. That does not affect the current selling season, but it is the population that will drive delinquency volumes in six to twelve months if it keeps accumulating. On inflation, May Core PCE came in at 3.41% YoY, and CPI core at 2.82% — both running above the Fed's target and both cooling more slowly than breakeven inflation rates (the market's implied forecast for future inflation embedded in Treasury pricing) had anticipated earlier this year. The result is that rate-cut expectations have been pushed out, which is why the 10-year has held near 4.55% and the spread remains wide. Regionally, the Case-Shiller April data (released with the usual two-month lag) sharpens the two-speed story: Chicago is up 6.4% YoY and New York up 3.7%, both rising for seven months; Dallas is down 1.7%, Denver down 1.8%, Phoenix down 1.8%, Seattle down 2.2%, and Las Vegas down 1.7% at its weakest YoY reading since September 2023. Agents in those Sun Belt and Mountain West markets need a different price narrative than their Midwest and Northeast counterparts — appreciation is not the story, and listing-price strategy has to reflect that. The single macro signal to watch in August: whether Core PCE for June (released late July) shows any further deceleration. A print closer to 3.0% reopens the spread-compression story; a re-acceleration closes it.
