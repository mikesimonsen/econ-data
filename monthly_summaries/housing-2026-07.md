# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales have held above year-ago levels for 55 consecutive weeks on the 13-week average, though the near-term read softened noticeably into late June. The spot week of June 26 came in at 85,813, down 3.0% YoY — the first negative YoY spot print in over a year — while the 4-week average at 89,198 remained +4.4% YoY, and the 13-week average at 89,672 held +4.8% YoY. That divergence between spot and trend matters: seasonally, late June often produces a soft week, so one negative print doesn't break the streak, but it is worth watching whether July confirms or reverses. Total active inventory reached 1.07 million at Altos (through June 26), up about 1.2% YoY — growth that has been decelerating for several weeks — and new listings on the 4-week average of 93,757 ticked just +0.4% YoY after running positive for months, with the spot week flipping to -7.1% YoY. Pending days on market at 70.9 days are running about 6.7% above year-ago levels, a signal that the market, while still moving, is absorbing listings more slowly than it was in 2025. NAR existing home sales for June printed at 4.09 million SAAR, beating the consensus of 3.85 million and running +4.1% YoY — the strongest YoY growth rate in recent months — though the SAAR number ticked down 1.9% from May's revised level; using the NSA read, June closings were up 7.9% YoY, which is the cleanest comparison. New home sales at 628,000 SAAR for June were still down 5.1% YoY and have been negative for six months, a meaningful divergence from the existing market that reflects the growing builder price concession story: new home median prices fell to $398,300, off 2.7% YoY, and the average sale price dropped sharply — an unusual move that suggests builders are incentivizing down market heavily to clear standing inventory. The 30-year fixed rate sat at 6.76% as of July 28, roughly where it has been for several weeks, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.13 percentage points — still well above the historical norm of around 1.7 points, meaning rates carry meaningful excess versus where pure Treasury pricing would put them. That spread has compressed 26 basis points over the past year; if it moves another 20-25 basis points toward historical norms, the 30-year would fall to roughly the low-to-mid 6s without any Fed action. The MBA purchase index 4-week average through July 24 came in at 163.1, down 1.3% YoY after running positive — a deceleration worth noting. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks, turned higher in late June after several weeks of decline, with the 4-week MII average recovering to 127.5 from its recent lows — a tentative signal that August purchase application volumes may stabilize. For the next four weeks, the question is whether that MII uptick translates into MBA purchase index recovery, and whether new pending sales in early July re-establish their positive YoY streak.

The macro backdrop shifted meaningfully this month, and most of the moves were constructive for housing. June payrolls came in at 159,000 — solid, consistent with trend — and the unemployment rate fell a tenth to 4.2%, beating a consensus expectation of 4.4%. For housing, this matters primarily through consumer confidence in job security; a buyer who thinks their job is stable is a buyer who can commit to a mortgage. The more important print was June CPI, which came in well below expectations: headline fell 0.4% month-over-month against a consensus of +0.9%, and core fell essentially flat against a +0.3% expectation. The mechanism here runs through the bond market — a cooler inflation print reduces the inflation risk premium embedded in longer-dated Treasuries, which puts downward pressure on the 10-year yield and, with some lag, on the 30-year fixed rate. Core PCE for May was still running at 3.4% YoY, but if June's CPI softness carries through into the PCE read, rate markets will price in an earlier Fed pivot, which would compress spreads further and bring the 30-year meaningfully lower. One note of caution: the long-term unemployed cohort — workers out of work 27 weeks or more — remains elevated at 1.937 million, up 17.3% YoY, even as it ticked down in June. This group does not affect the current selling season, but historically it is a leading indicator of mortgage delinquency stress six to twelve months out, and agents in markets with concentrated layoff exposure should track it.
