# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales finished June in a complicated place: the spot weekly read for the week ending June 26 dipped 3.0% year over year — the first negative YoY print after a 55-week positive streak — but the four-week average held at +4.4% YoY and the 13-week average has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks and is still running at +4.8% YoY. One soft week interrupting a 55-week streak is noise; a persistent softening of the 13-week average is signal. Watch the distinction. Inventory on the Altos side rose to just over 1.07 million active listings, up about 1.2% YoY — still growing, but the growth rate is decelerating from the double-digit YoY readings we saw in 2024 and early 2025. New listings ticked negative YoY on the spot read (-7.1%), though the four-week average barely held positive (+0.4%) and the 13-week average has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks at +0.7% YoY. Sellers are not withdrawing en masse, but the acceleration in new supply has clearly stalled. Pending days on market came in at 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — still elevated relative to the 2021–22 pace, though that comparison is hardly the relevant benchmark anymore. NAR's June existing home sales print came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating consensus of 3.85 million, up 4.1% YoY — the best beat against expectations in several months. The NSA read is cleaner for direction: 422,000 transactions, up 7.9% YoY, with every region positive. New home sales told a quieter story at 628,000 SAAR, down 5.1% YoY, with the average new home price falling an unusual 9.5% in a single month — that is a 2-standard-deviation move, almost certainly a mix shift toward lower-priced product as builders clear spec inventory and price down to clear it. Months supply of new homes fell to 9.3, which remains elevated but is off its recent peak. The 30-year fixed mortgage rate sits at 6.80% as of July 27, essentially flat on the month. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.16 percentage points, it remains roughly 45 basis points above the long-run norm of about 1.70 points. That gap closed meaningfully in the second half of 2024 and compressed further into early 2026; it has since plateaued. If lender capacity and MBS demand recover enough to bring the spread back toward 1.85–1.90 points, the 30-year could fall to the low 6s without any Fed action — that is the affordability unlock most agents are not explaining to buyers yet. MBA purchase applications printed +0.4% YoY on the most recent week (week ending July 17), reversing several weeks of negative YoY readings, but the four-week average is still -1.2% YoY and the 13-week average, while positive at +4.2% YoY, has turned down for two consecutive weeks. The more important divergence is in the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — a credit-inquiry-based measure that leads MBA applications by roughly two to four weeks. The MII spot read ticked up 3.5% week over week on June 26 after several weeks of decline, and the four-week average reversed upward as well, though both remain negative YoY (-5.6% and -2.3% respectively). The MII and MBA are telling a similar story: demand is present but fragile, with the YoY tailwind narrowing. What to watch over the next four weeks: whether the 13-week pending average sustains its 55-week positive run, and whether the MII's uptick translates into a firmer MBA read in late July and early August.

The macro backdrop heading into August is genuinely mixed, though the housing-relevant reads lean more supportive than the headlines suggested. June headline CPI fell 0.4% month over month — cooler than the +0.9% consensus expected — and core CPI was essentially flat at -0.02%, against a +0.3% expectation. That is a notable miss in the disinflationary direction. Core PCE for May ran hotter, at +0.32% MoM and +3.41% YoY, and shelter CPI remains sticky at +3.3% YoY, which is ironic given that real-time rent data (Zillow's ZORI is now at +3.3% YoY, a three-year high) suggests the shelter index will stay elevated for several more months before the lag fully unwinds. The mechanism for housing: if core inflation prints consistently below expectation, the Fed's optionality to cut expands, which compresses the 10-year yield and, with it, the mortgage rate — even before the spread moves.
