**July 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through most of July, though the weekly Altos data showed some softening at the edges. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales is running +4.81% year-over-year and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks — that streak is the clearest evidence that underlying buyer engagement has not broken. The 4-week average came in at +4.37% YoY, though it is decelerating, and the spot week ending June 26 ticked negative at -2.99% YoY — the first negative spot read after a positive run, worth watching but not yet a trend reversal on its own. Inventory at Altos stood near 1.075 million units, up about 1.2% YoY, with new listings decelerating: the spot week turned -7.1% YoY even as the 4-week average held barely positive at +0.41%. The 13-week new listings average is still rising — up 14 consecutive weeks and +0.71% YoY — suggesting sellers are still entering, just more slowly than they were in spring. Pending days on market reached 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, which confirms homes are taking longer to clear even as the YoY demand streak continues. NAR existing home sales for June came in at 4.09 million SAAR, a notable beat against the 3.85 million consensus, and up 4.1% YoY — the YoY pace is accelerating. The NSA read tells the same story: +7.9% YoY for June. New home sales (628K SAAR) edged up in June but remain -5.1% YoY, a divergence that reflects builders working through elevated finished inventory — months supply of new homes ticked down to 9.3, still historically elevated. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.77% as of July 30, essentially flat on the month. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, currently 2.11 percentage points — down 5 basis points on the day and 26 basis points below year-ago levels. The historical norm for that spread is roughly 1.7 points, meaning there is still approximately 40 basis points of compression potential before rates reach their "fair value" relative to Treasuries, entirely independent of Fed action. If the 10-year holds near 4.70% and the spread normalizes even partially, the 30-year could drift toward 6.3–6.4% without a single Fed cut. MBA purchase applications for the week ending July 24 came in at 159.8, down 3.6% on the week, and the 4-week average is now -1.35% YoY after decelerating for four consecutive weeks — a softening that is real but modest against the still-positive 13-week average of +2.88% YoY. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (which leads MBA by roughly 2–4 weeks, measuring credit-pull activity before a formal application is filed) reversed off a seven-week decline with a +3.5% week, and the 4-week MII average is also turning up. That divergence — MII firming while MBA 4-week average softens — suggests the August application data may improve. Watch whether that MII recovery persists into July readings.

The macro backdrop shifted in ways that matter for housing. June nonfarm payrolls came in at roughly 159K, unemployment fell to 4.2% against a 4.4% consensus expectation, and average hourly earnings rose 3.5% YoY — a labor market that is decelerating but not deteriorating, which keeps household formation intact and distressed-sale risk contained for now. The more important inflation prints arrived in July: June headline CPI fell 0.4% month-over-month against an expected +0.9%, core CPI was essentially flat month-over-month against a +0.3% consensus, and June core PCE came in at +0.13% MoM against a +0.3% expectation. Those readings matter for housing through two channels: first, they remove the Fed's inflation constraint and open the door to rate cuts sooner than the market had priced; second, shelter CPI is still running +3.26% YoY and rising, which means the Fed's inflation models will keep registering housing cost pressure even as actual home prices in Sun Belt markets are negative YoY. On that regional split — the Case-Shiller data through May is unambiguous: Chicago +6.9% YoY, New York +4.0%, Boston +2.6% are the appreciation story, while Dallas -1.0%, Denver -1.7%, Phoenix -1.3%, Tampa -1.8%, Las Vegas -1.9%, and Seattle -1.8% are all still negative YoY. Agents working those Sun Belt corridors are in a different market from agents in the Midwest and Northeast, and median price statistics that blend them obscure both realities.
