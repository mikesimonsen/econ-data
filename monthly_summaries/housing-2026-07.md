# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through the first half of the summer, though the most recent weekly data shows the streak softening at the edges. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% YoY after 55 consecutive weeks of positive readings — a streak that has persisted through rate volatility, election uncertainty, and two separate tariff scares. The 4-week average pulled back to +4.37% YoY, down from higher readings earlier in the quarter, and the most recent spot week turned negative at -2.99% YoY — the kind of single-week dip that happens every summer but is worth watching if it runs into a second and third consecutive miss. Inventory continues to build gradually: Altos total active supply is up about 1.2% YoY, rising for eight consecutive weeks, while new listings on a 4-week average basis are essentially flat year-over-year (+0.41%), with the 13-week average trending modestly positive for 14 straight weeks. The new listings deceleration is the more important signal — if sellers continue pulling back into August, supply growth stalls and any demand recovery will push prices rather than volumes. Pending days on market at 70.9 days are running about 6.7% above year-ago levels, confirming that homes are taking longer to find buyers despite the positive YoY pending streak. NAR's June existing sales print of 4.09 million SAAR beat expectations of 3.85 million by a material margin and ran 4.1% above June 2025 on a seasonally adjusted basis, though the month-over-month dip of 1.9% after a multi-month rise is consistent with typical summer deceleration rather than a trend break. On an NSA basis — the cleaner YoY comparison for this series — June sales ran 7.9% above a year ago, the best reading in several months. New home sales at 628,000 SAAR ticked up 1.6% sequentially but remain 5.1% below year-ago levels, a divergence from existing sales that reflects builder pricing pressure: new home median prices fell 2.7% YoY and the average price dropped an unusual 6.6% YoY — builders are mixing down toward more affordable product to move inventory sitting at 9.3 months of supply. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.80% as of July 27, essentially unchanged year-over-year and near the top of its range for the past several months. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, currently 2.16 percentage points against a long-run norm of roughly 1.7 points — meaning roughly 45 basis points of excess spread remain in the market. That spread compressed 23 basis points over the past year and has held near 2.13 on a 4-week average, suggesting the compression that began in late 2025 has paused. If it resumes — driven by reduced MBS volatility or Fed clarity — the 30-year rate falls meaningfully without any change in Treasury yields. MBA purchase applications turned positive YoY in the most recent week (+0.42%) after several weeks negative, but the 4-week average is still -1.16% YoY and the 13-week average, while positive at +4.16%, has begun rolling over. More telling: the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which captures consumers initiating mortgage credit inquiries and leads MBA applications by roughly 2-4 weeks — printed its 4-week average at -2.30% YoY after a brief uptick from deeper negative territory. The MII 13-week average has fallen for seven consecutive weeks. When MII runs negative while MBA is near flat, the gap typically closes in MBA's direction. That's the leading indicator to watch through August.

The macro picture this month was cleaner than it has been in some time, and the implications for housing are mostly constructive at the margin. June payrolls came in at 159,000 — above the consensus of 59,000 in terms of direction, though the exact comparison depends on revision base — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, beating expectations of 4.4%. Wage growth held at 3.52% YoY, running ahead of headline inflation and supporting purchasing power for employed buyers. The more important labor read for housing is the long-term unemployed cohort: workers out 27 or more weeks rose 17.3% YoY to 1.937 million in June, even as it dipped slightly month-over-month. That cohort does not affect the current selling season, but if it continues accumulating, it sets up a distressed-borrower pipeline 9-12 months out that will matter for listings in 2027. The June CPI print was the headline of the month: core CPI came in essentially flat on the month (-0.02%) against an expectation of +0.3%, and headline CPI fell 0.42% against an expected +0.9%. The outperformance matters for housing through two channels — it raises the probability of a Fed rate cut earlier than previously priced, and it tends to compress the 10-year Treasury yield, which is the direct input into mortgage pricing.
