# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales ended June with a soft spot — the weekly read for the week of June 26 ticked down to -3.0% YoY, snapping what had been a 55-week streak of positive year-over-year comparisons — but the 4-week average held at +4.4% YoY and the 13-week average sat at +4.8% YoY, both still comfortably positive. That 13-week average has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks, which means the underlying demand trend remains intact even as the most recent weekly print softened. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are taking a bit longer to clear, consistent with a market that is not tight but not slack either. On supply, total Altos inventory rose for an eighth straight week and stood just above 1.07 million units, up roughly 1.2% YoY — growth is modest and decelerating relative to the inventory rebuilding pace of 2024. New listings turned slightly negative YoY on the spot reading (-7.1%) but the 4-week and 13-week averages remained marginally positive, suggesting the single-week dip reflects normal seasonal softening rather than a structural pullback from sellers. NAR existing home sales for June came in at 4.09 million SAAR — a meaningful beat of the 3.85 million consensus — though the month-over-month print was down about 2%. On a NSA year-over-year basis, June closed up 7.9%, the strongest comparison in the current run. New home sales told a different story: the May SAAR of 580,000 was down 7.4% YoY with months supply at 10.3, a level that explains why single-family permits slipped 2.4% in June even as they turned positive YoY for the first time since May 2024. The total starts headline of 1,427,000 SAAR beat expectations handily, but that was driven almost entirely by a 76% surge in 5+ unit starts — single-family starts fell for a third consecutive month. Builders are completing the multifamily pipeline, not adding to the single-family one. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.75% as of July 21, essentially flat on the year. The more interesting number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, currently 2.12 percentage points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.70 points, and down 28 basis points YoY. If that spread compresses another 20-25 basis points toward historical norms, the 30-year could move to roughly 6.30-6.40% without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications recovered last week (+5.5% week-over-week) but the 4-week average YoY sits at -1.2%, and the 13-week average has ticked lower for two weeks after a long rise. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — a credit-inquiry-based signal that typically leads MBA applications by 2-4 weeks — reversed higher at the end of June after a multi-week decline, with the 4-week average up 2.3%, though the 13-week average has been falling for seven weeks. The MII-MBA divergence bears watching: the MII's 13-week trend has been softening even as MBA held up, which could mean purchase application demand moderates into August. Watch whether the weekly pending sales YoY readings recover back above flat, and whether MBA applications hold their gains heading into the slower back-to-school weeks.

The macro backdrop is genuinely constructive for housing at the moment, with one meaningful asterisk. June CPI came in well below expectations — headline fell 0.4% month-over-month against a consensus of +0.9%, and core was essentially flat — which matters for housing because softer inflation gives the Fed room to cut without being cornered, and it keeps 10-year Treasury yields from drifting materially higher. The 10-year sits at 4.65% today; if core PCE (last reported at +3.4% YoY through May) follows CPI lower in the June print due out at the end of July, the Treasury market may rally and drag mortgage rates down, compressing that spread further. The labor market read was mixed in a way agents should parse carefully: the headline unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, payrolls came in at about 159,000 — solid but not hot — and initial claims fell to 208,000, well below consensus. That is the part that supports buyer confidence and income stability. The asterisk is the longer-term unemployment cohort: the number of workers unemployed 27 weeks or longer rose 17.3% YoY through June, even as it ticked down month-over-month. The quits rate at 1.9% is near multi-year lows, meaning workers who have jobs are less willing to leave them — a sign of underlying caution. Neither of these signals immediate distress, but they accumulate slowly and will matter for mortgage performance 6-12 months out. On regional prices, the Case-Shiller April data (the most recent available, released early July) shows Chicago up 6.4% YoY and New York up 3.
