# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week pending average has now run positive year-over-year for 55 consecutive weeks, and the four-week average sits at +4.4% YoY — healthy, though it has decelerated from highs earlier in the spring. The most recent weekly read ticked down 3.0% YoY, worth watching but not yet a signal given the Independence Day week distortions that reliably compress contract volumes. Total active inventory reached 1.075 million homes as of June 26, up 1.2% YoY — growth that has essentially stalled after a stronger run earlier in the year. New listings are telling a similar story: the 4-week average is barely positive YoY (+0.4%), and the single-week reading turned negative (-7.1% YoY) for the first time in months, though holiday-week seasonality is a significant confound there. Pending days on market stand at 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are taking longer to clear than a year ago, but the pace has been stable. NAR's May existing sales print came in at 4.17 million SAAR, beating consensus of 3.85 million by a meaningful margin and running +3.2% YoY — the strongest read in over a year. The NSA comparison tells a more sober story: actual closings in May were flat YoY at 390,000, which is the honest number to use for direction. New home sales moved the opposite way, falling to 580,000 SAAR in May (-7.5% YoY) with months' supply at 10.3 — builders are sitting on finished inventory while starts fell to 1,177,000 in May, missing expectations of 1,350,000 by a wide margin. Single-family permits have now been negative YoY for 24 consecutive months, which matters more for 2027 supply than for this selling season. The 30-year fixed rate closed at 6.60% on July 2, down about 5 basis points on the month; the spread to the 10-year Treasury compressed to 2.13 percentage points, still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points but down 30 basis points YoY — that compression is the quiet affordability story most buyers aren't hearing. The MBA purchase index four-week average is +3.1% YoY, decelerating but still positive. The Xactus MII — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks by capturing credit-pull activity before borrowers file applications — reversed higher on the week to 123.3 but its 13-week average has fallen for seven straight weeks and sits -2.4% YoY. That divergence between the spot MII bounce and the deteriorating trend average is the indicator to watch through July; if the 4-week MII average (currently +2.3% period-over-period but -2.3% YoY) doesn't firm, the MBA purchase index likely follows lower by late July.

The macro backdrop shifted in a meaningful direction this month. June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 jobs added, and the unemployment rate fell to 4.2% — beating consensus of 4.4% — which removes some of the recession anxiety that had been building in rate markets. Average hourly earnings grew 3.5% YoY, which keeps real income positive but not so hot that it forces the Fed's hand. For housing, steady employment means qualified buyers remain in the pool; the unemployment beat is the primary reason the pending sales trend held as well as it did in June despite elevated rates. The inflation picture is more complicated. Core PCE ran at 3.4% YoY in May — above the Fed's 2% target and above the core CPI reading of 2.8%, which itself came in below expectations. Shelter CPI contributed 3.4% YoY and remains sticky, a known lag effect from lease renewals cycling through the index. The gap between realized shelter inflation and the real-time rent signals — Zillow's ZORI at +3.0% YoY — suggests shelter CPI will keep the overall core elevated for several more months even as actual rental market conditions ease. That dynamic constrains the Fed from cutting rates as quickly as the bond market would like, which is why the 10-year has held near 4.47% despite the jobs beat. The one regional divergence worth flagging: May existing sales in the South were +6.0% YoY by NAR's SAAR measure while the Northeast was -8.0% YoY, a gap that reflects both affordability at the margin and the continued relocation tailwind into lower-cost Sun Belt markets. The signal to track in July is Core PCE's July print alongside any Fed communication about the September meeting — if core PCE decelerates toward 3.0% or below, the spread compression story gets real traction and the 30-year rate has room to move into the mid-6s without any Fed action.
