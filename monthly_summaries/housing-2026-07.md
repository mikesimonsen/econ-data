# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through the bulk of the four weeks but showed the first meaningful wobble of the cycle. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% year over year and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks — that streak remains intact and is the right anchor for your listing conversations. The 4-week average pulled back to +4.37% YoY, decelerating from the mid-single-digit pace of recent months, and the most recent weekly read flipped to -2.99% YoY, the first negative spot reading in over a year. One week does not break a trend, but it warrants watching. Inventory continues to build slowly: Altos total active supply is up roughly 1.2% YoY, not the rapid accumulation of 2024, and new listings slipped to -7.1% YoY on the most recent weekly read — though the 4-week average is still marginally positive at +0.41%, suggesting the dip is partly seasonal noise. Pending days on market are at 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, a quiet signal that urgency has softened from its peak. The June NAR existing home sales print came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million by a wide margin and running +4.1% YoY — the YoY pace is accelerating — though the month-over-month reading ticked down from May's level. On an NSA basis, June closings were +7.9% YoY, consistent with the demand signal Altos was showing in the April-May contract window. New home sales diverged sharply: the May SAAR of 580,000 fell 7.4% MoM and 7.5% YoY, and months supply for new homes rose to 10.3 — builders are sitting on elevated finished inventory even as June starts beat expectations at 1,427,000 SAAR (up 19% MoM, though almost entirely multifamily; single-family starts fell 0.2% MoM and are down 3.2% YoY). Total permits missed at 1,367,000 and turned negative YoY, with South permits down 8.8% YoY — the forward pipeline for single-family is not expanding. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.77% as of today, essentially flat year over year. The spread to the 10-year Treasury — currently 4.65% — is 2.12 percentage points, still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points; that 40-plus-basis-point excess premium is the hidden affordability lever. If credit volatility and MBS spreads continue to normalize, the 30-year could fall 30-40 basis points without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications bounced +5.5% in the most recent week but the 4-week average is -1.2% YoY and decelerating — the single-week pop looks like noise against a softening trend. More telling: the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (which leads MBA by 2-4 weeks and measures early-funnel credit pulls before a formal application) bounced to +3.5% on the week, but the 4-week average is -2.3% YoY and the 13-week average has fallen for seven straight weeks. When MII leads MBA lower, applications tend to follow within a month. Watch whether the MII 4-week average confirms a sustained turn or continues recovering over the next four weeks — that will set the tone for August contract activity.

The macro picture shifted notably in July. June headline CPI came in at -0.4% month over month against an expectation of +0.9%, and core CPI was essentially flat at -0.02% MoM versus an expected +0.3% — both meaningful downside surprises. Shelter CPI continues to grind higher, up 3.3% YoY, but the gap between shelter's persistence and collapsing goods prices is now doing most of the work holding core inflation above target. The May Core PCE reading, at +3.4% YoY, remains elevated — that is the Fed's preferred gauge, and it has not cooperated with the disinflation narrative as cleanly as headline CPI has. Still, the June CPI surprise meaningfully shifted rate expectations; the 10-year yield is at 4.65% today, and if Core PCE follows CPI lower in the June print (due late July), the rate environment could ease modestly heading into fall. On labor: June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 — solid, with the unemployment rate surprising lower at 4.2% against a 4.4% consensus. The headline is encouraging, but underneath it, the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) stand at 1.937 million, up 17.3% YoY. That cohort is not shopping for homes today — they are the forward delinquency signal to watch over the next 6-12 months as their savings deplete. The quits rate at 1.9% (down 0.2 points YoY) and the hires rate at 3.3% (down 0.1 point YoY)
