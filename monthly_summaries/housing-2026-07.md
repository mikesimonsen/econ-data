# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The four weeks ending July 17 produced a genuinely mixed demand signal that deserves a close read. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% YoY — positive for the 55th consecutive week — which is the durable trend. But the spot week of June 26 came in at -2.99% YoY, the first negative weekly print after an extended positive streak, and the 4-week average has decelerated to +4.37% from higher levels earlier in the quarter. That softening at the margin is consistent with a seasonal air pocket rather than a reversal, but it warrants watching. Inventory continues to build gradually: Altos total active supply is up about +1.2% YoY over eight consecutive rising weeks, while new listings flipped negative YoY on the spot week (-7.1%) even as the 4-week average holds slightly positive (+0.4%). Pending days on market have extended to 70.9 days, +6.7% YoY, which tells you the pace of absorption has eased. NAR existing home sales for June printed at 4.09 million SAAR — well above the 3.85 million consensus — with NSA transactions up +7.9% YoY, the strongest year-over-year gain in this cycle. The SAAR number ticked down month-over-month as seasonal adjustment penalized a strong June, but the underlying NSA comparison is what matters here; demand was real. New home sales tell a different story: the May print came in at 580,000 SAAR, down 7.4% YoY with months of supply at 10.3 — builders are sitting on a growing finished-lot inventory and reacting accordingly, as single-family starts fell for the third consecutive month even while total starts beat expectations on a multifamily pop. On rates, the 30-year fixed ticked back up to 6.68% after a brief softening — a reversal of what looked like downward momentum. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, currently 2.09–2.13 percentage points against a long-run historical norm of roughly 1.7 points; that gap represents the rate relief that does not require a Fed move, and with the 10-year at 4.52%, a spread normalization alone would carry the 30-year into the low-to-mid 6s. MBA purchase applications are running -0.6% YoY on the 4-week average — the first negative YoY reading after a 74-week positive streak in the 13-week average — which is a yellow flag. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, turned upward on its 4-week average in late June after a prolonged slide, suggesting the MBA softness may be brief. Watch whether MBA recovers in the July 24 and August 7 prints; if MII's upturn is real, purchase applications should follow. The June housing starts beat (1,427 vs. 1,350 expected) was driven almost entirely by the 5+ unit segment, which jumped 76% month-over-month — single-family starts were essentially flat and down 3.2% YoY. Single-family permits at 871,000 are still barely positive YoY (+0.6%), the highest since May 2024, but total permits missed and fell 1.9% YoY. The divergence between what builders are starting (multifamily catch-up) and permitting (single-family caution) implies the for-sale supply pipeline stays constrained well into 2027.

The macro backdrop hardened in housing's favor this month in two meaningful ways. June CPI came in sharply below expectations — headline -0.4% month-over-month versus a +0.9% consensus, core essentially flat — and while shelter CPI is still running +3.3% YoY (a lagging measure of leases signed a year ago), the broader disinflation signal matters because it reduces the pressure on the 10-year Treasury. If the bond market prices in a less-sticky inflation path, the 10-year has room to move lower, and given the elevated spread, even modest Treasury yield compression translates to meaningful mortgage rate relief without any Fed action. The labor market remained firmer than expected: June payrolls printed solidly, unemployment fell to 4.2% against a 4.4% consensus expectation, and initial claims at 208,000 continue to track well below recessionary thresholds. The combination of a healthy job market and cooling inflation is the best possible environment for housing demand — it sustains purchase intent without requiring a rate shock to restart the market. The one accumulating concern is the long-term unemployed cohort: workers unemployed 27 or more weeks ticked down in June but remain +17.3% YoY. This group does not affect the current selling season — they are not buyers — but they represent the future delinquency pool, and if the cohort continues to expand, it will show up in distressed-borrower volume in six to twelve months.
