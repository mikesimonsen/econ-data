**July 2026 Housing Summary**

The most important read on current demand is the Altos 13-week average for new pending sales, which held at +4.81% year-over-year through the week of June 26 — now 55 consecutive weeks of positive YoY readings on that smoothed basis. The spot week dipped to -2.99% YoY, and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37%, so the streak is narrowing rather than broadening, but the trend line has not broken. That context matters when you see the headline: NAR existing home sales came in at a 4.17 million SAAR for May, up 3.2% year-over-year and well ahead of the 3.85 million consensus. The NSA read was flat year-over-year, which is the more honest comparison for a month with an unusual seasonal swing — still, the directional story is real. New home sales told the opposite story: 580,000 SAAR in May, down 7.5% year-over-year, with months of supply jumping to 10.3. Builders have inventory they cannot move at current prices, which is why single-family starts fell for a second consecutive month and missed expectations badly at 1,177,000 total starts versus a 1,350,000 consensus. Single-family permits edged up but remain negative year-over-year for the 24th consecutive month — builders are not restoring future pipeline. Altos total inventory rose for an eighth consecutive week to just over 1.07 million, up 1.2% year-over-year, though the YoY growth rate is decelerating. New listings turned negative year-over-year on the spot week (-7.1%) even as the 4-week average held barely positive at +0.4%. The 13-week average for new listings is +0.71% YoY after 14 weeks of gains — still growing, but losing momentum. Pending days on market at 70.9 days is running nearly 7% above last year, confirming homes are not moving faster. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.59% today, down 20 basis points year-over-year. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.11 points — still roughly 40 basis points above the long-run norm of about 1.7 points, which means meaningful rate relief could come without any Fed action if credit conditions normalize. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is +3.1% year-over-year, decelerating from the 13-week average of +5.1%. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks by capturing early-stage credit inquiries before a full application is filed — reversed off recent lows, with its 4-week average bouncing to -2.3% YoY after trending lower for seven weeks on the 13-week basis. The MII and MBA are not yet diverging sharply, but the MII is softer on a sustained basis, which suggests the MBA's recent resilience may not hold into August. Watch whether the MII 4-week average crosses back above flat YoY — that would be the early green light for the fall selling season.

The macro picture this month is a study in contradictions that resolve in a specific direction for housing. June payrolls came in at 159,000 — the number itself was fine, though the 10-year Treasury rose 11 basis points on the day to 4.48%, suggesting the bond market read the report as removing urgency for Fed cuts. Unemployment fell to 4.2%, beating the 4.4% consensus, which is good for household formation and purchase confidence — but the long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) stands 17.3% above last year even after a slight monthly dip. That cohort does not affect the current selling season; it is the input to watch for distressed-borrower volume six to twelve months out. The labor market is generating enough jobs to support demand but not enough wage growth to offset affordability drag — average hourly earnings at +3.5% year-over-year are running below core PCE at +3.4% in real terms, meaning purchasing power is roughly flat. Core PCE came in at +3.4% year-over-year in May, above the Fed's 2% target, which combined with the stronger employment print narrows the near-term window for rate cuts. Shelter CPI at +3.4% year-over-year continues to run above overall core inflation, keeping measured inflation elevated even as spot rent indices like Zillow's ZORI (now at +2.95% YoY) suggest the pipeline pressure will ease over the next six to twelve months as leases reprice. On the regional price story: the Case-Shiller data through March (the most recent available) shows Chicago at +6.0% YoY and Cleveland at +3.2%, while Dallas is -1.7%, Phoenix -1.8%, Seattle -2.4%, and Tampa -1.8% — the Midwest-versus-Sun-Belt split that has defined this cycle is not fading. Agents in appreciating markets can still defend list prices with comps; agents in markets with negative YoY readings need to frame seller expectations carefully
