**July 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive over the four weeks ending June 26, but the read is more nuanced than the headline suggests. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% YoY and has been in positive territory for 55 consecutive weeks — a durable trend. The 4-week average is running at +4.37% YoY, though it decelerated from last month. The spot week itself slipped to -2.99% YoY, snapping a positive streak; one week doesn't break the trend, but it warrants watching. Pending days on market are running 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are moving, just a bit more deliberately than a year ago. On supply, Altos total inventory reached 1.07 million units, up 1.2% YoY, continuing a slow grind higher, though the pace of YoY growth is modest compared to earlier in the year. New listings told a more complicated story: the spot week printed -7.1% YoY, the first negative YoY reading after a stretch of positive prints, though the 4-week average remains barely positive at +0.41% and the 13-week average at +0.71% YoY has been rising 14 straight weeks — so the new listings pipeline hasn't closed, it just softened into the seasonal July lull. NAR existing home sales for June came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating consensus of 3.85 million by a notable margin, and up 4.1% YoY — the YoY pace is actually accelerating. On an NSA basis, that's 422,000 units, +7.9% YoY, the strongest YoY print in several months. NAR inventory held at 1.56 million units, 4.6 months of supply, essentially flat YoY — still a sellers' market in most price ranges. New home sales told a different story: the May print was 580,000 SAAR, down 7.5% YoY, with months of supply for new homes at 10.3 — builders have product they're working through, and starts in May fell to 1,177,000 SAAR, missing consensus by nearly 15% and down 8.2% YoY, led by a sharp drop in multifamily. Single-family permits ticked up to 892,000 but remain negative YoY for the 24th consecutive month, which continues to cap the resale-equivalent pipeline for 2027. The 30-year fixed rate stands at 6.70% as of July 14, off about 5 basis points over the past month. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.13 percentage points — down 27 basis points YoY and the 4-week average is now ticking lower. The historical norm on that spread is roughly 1.7 percentage points; if it compresses another 30-40 basis points toward that norm without a corresponding rise in the 10-year, the 30-year rate can ease toward 6.3-6.4% without any Fed action. That is still the most underappreciated affordability lever in the market. On leading indicators: the MBA purchase index 4-week average flipped to -0.6% YoY — the first negative reading after a positive streak — and the 13-week average, while still up 5.2% YoY, is rolling over. The Xactus MII (a credit-inquiry-based demand signal that leads MBA by 2-4 weeks) told a more constructive story through June 26: the 4-week average reversed higher to +2.31% from recent lows, though still -2.3% YoY. The MII upturn suggests MBA applications may firm modestly in the next few weeks rather than extend the recent softness. Watch whether that MII signal holds through late July.

The macro backdrop shifted in one important way this month: the June CPI print was meaningfully cooler than expected, with headline down 0.4% month-over-month versus the +0.9% consensus, and core CPI essentially flat (-0.02%) versus +0.3% expected. Core PCE for May was running at +3.4% YoY — still above target — but the June CPI data, if it carries into the PCE calculation, implies inflation is losing momentum faster than the market had priced. That matters for housing because it gives the Fed more room without forcing their hand, and more directly, it reduces the inflation premium embedded in long-term Treasury yields — which is what drives the 10-year, which drives the mortgage rate.
