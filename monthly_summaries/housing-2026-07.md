**July 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through the four weeks ending June 26, though the read has grown more complicated at the surface. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% year-over-year and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks — that streak is intact and the underlying trend remains constructive. The spot week, however, slipped to -2.99% YoY, pulling the 4-week average down to +4.37% from higher levels a month ago, so the deceleration bears watching even if it doesn't break the streak. Inventory continued its measured rise: total active listings at roughly 1.07 million were up about 1.2% YoY, and new listings on the 4-week average turned slightly positive at +0.41% YoY after running negative. The 13-week new-listings average has now risen for 14 consecutive weeks, which points to a supply pipeline that is slowly refilling — but not fast enough to shift leverage toward buyers. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, a quiet signal that absorption is incrementally slower than a year ago. NAR's June existing home sales print came in at 4.09 million SAAR, up 4.1% YoY and well above the 3.85 million consensus — the strongest beat in recent months and a reminder that seasonally adjusted reads were understating activity. On an NSA basis, the directional confirmation is clean: June closings rose 7.9% YoY. New home sales told a different story, falling to 580,000 SAAR in May (-7.5% YoY) while months of new-home supply climbed to 10.3 — that overhang will keep builder pricing cautious. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.70% today, down about 5 basis points over the past month. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.13 percentage points, it has compressed 27 basis points year-over-year and ticked lower again this week. The long-run norm is roughly 1.7 points; at current Treasury levels, a return to that norm would put the 30-year in the low-to-mid 6% range without any Fed action — that is the affordability lever agents should understand and communicate. MBA purchase applications' 4-week average is running just +0.50% YoY and has decelerated sharply; the spot week was -6.3% YoY. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks — bounced off recent lows, with its 4-week average turning upward to +2.31% from a declining trend. That divergence is worth flagging: the MII inflection in late June suggests MBA applications may stabilize or recover modestly in July. Watch whether that recovery materializes in the next two weekly MBA prints.

The macro backdrop shifted in one important way this month: June CPI came in sharply below expectations, with headline prices falling 0.4% month-over-month against a consensus of +0.9%, and core CPI essentially flat at -0.02% against an expected +0.3%. That is a significant undershoot, and if it holds in the PCE revision — May core PCE was still running at 3.4% YoY — it accelerates the timeline for the market to price in Fed easing, which feeds directly into Treasury yields and, through spread compression, into mortgage rates. The 10-year held at 4.57% today, but a softer inflation print of this magnitude typically pressures yields lower in the weeks that follow; watch the 10-year closely heading into the July PCE release. The labor market gave agents a more ambiguous read. June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000, unemployment fell to 4.2% against a 4.4% consensus, and average hourly earnings rose 3.5% YoY — conditions that ordinarily support purchase demand. The counterweight is the long-term unemployed cohort: workers unemployed 27 weeks or longer numbered 1.937 million in June, up 17.3% YoY. That cohort pulled back slightly from its recent peak, but the level remains elevated. It doesn't affect this selling season, but it is accumulating the conditions for higher delinquency volume in 2027. JOLTS hires fell 3.0% YoY and the quits rate held at 1.9%, both suggesting a labor market that is cooling at the margins even if it isn't breaking. On pricing geography, the April Case-Shiller data (the most recent available, with a roughly two-month lag) continued to show the two-speed market clearly: Chicago +6.4% YoY and New York +3.7% YoY are still appreciating with momentum, while Dallas (-1.7%), Denver (-1.8%), Phoenix (-1.8%), Seattle (-2.2%), and Las Vegas (-1.7%) remain in contraction. That split is durable and should frame how agents in those markets are presenting comparable data to sellers.
