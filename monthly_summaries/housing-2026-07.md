# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos weekly demand read through June 26 shows new pending sales running 4.4% above year-ago levels on the four-week average and the 13-week average at +4.8% YoY — now 55 consecutive weeks of positive year-over-year readings on that longer trend. The single-week print dipped to -3.0% YoY, which is the first negative weekly reading in that streak, and worth watching but not yet a signal given seasonal noise in late June. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — still elevated, meaning the market is clearing but not quickly. Inventory rose for an eighth consecutive week, reaching 1.07 million active listings and up about 1.2% YoY. New listings have firmed on the four-week average (+0.4% YoY) and the 13-week average (+0.7% YoY) after slipping to -7.1% on the most recent single week — a normal seasonal pullback heading into July. NAR's June existing home sales print came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus by a notable margin, and up 4.1% YoY on the SAAR basis; the NSA read confirms the direction at +7.9% YoY. That is the strongest YoY comparison in over a year, and the beat-versus-expectations gap suggests the prior-month weakness was partly noise. New home sales, by contrast, fell to 580,000 SAAR in May, down 7.5% YoY, with months supply of new homes at 10.3 — a builders' market in the opposite direction from resale. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.64% as of July 15, down about 21 basis points year-over-year, but the more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.09–2.13 points, it remains roughly 40 basis points above the historical norm of about 1.70 points. That gap is the hidden affordability lever — if spreads compress toward normal as volatility recedes, the 30-year rate can fall to the low-to-mid 6s without any Fed action. The MBA purchase index four-week average turned slightly negative YoY (-0.6%) through July 10 after running positive for 74 weeks on the 13-week read, a deceleration to watch. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA applications by two to four weeks — reversed higher through June 26 on both the spot (+3.5%) and four-week average (+2.3%), though the 13-week average remains in seven-week decline and -2.4% YoY. That divergence between the shorter and longer Xactus averages suggests the demand signal is stabilizing rather than recovering. On the supply side, single-family starts fell to 882,000 SAAR in May and single-family permits have been negative YoY for 24 consecutive months — builders are not adding to the forward pipeline even as they carry 10.3 months of completed or under-construction inventory. Over the next four weeks, the single-week Altos pending number needs to recover to confirm the 13-week trend is intact, and any movement in the 30-year below 6.50 — plausible if the spread compresses even modestly on the June CPI tailwind — would be the clearest demand catalyst available.

The macro backdrop shifted meaningfully in the past four weeks, and the direction is more housing-friendly than the consensus expected. June CPI printed well below forecast: headline fell 0.4% month-over-month against an expectation of +0.9%, and core was essentially flat at -0.02% against a +0.3% consensus. Core PCE for May was still running at 3.4% YoY, so the Fed is not declaring victory, but the June CPI data — if it holds through the PCE read — puts the trajectory of inflation back on a path that allows the Fed to cut later this year without losing credibility. The mechanism for housing is direct: lower expected short-term rates reduce the incentive for lenders to maintain elevated spreads, and the 10-year yield, sitting at 4.59%, would need to fall only modestly for the 30-year to test the mid-6s. June payrolls added about 159,000 jobs and the unemployment rate came in at 4.2%, beating the 4.4% consensus — labor is not falling apart, which matters because job security is the proximate driver of purchase decisions at the household level. However, the long-term unemployed cohort (those out of work 27 weeks or more) stands at 1.937 million, up 17.3% YoY even as it ticked down from May's peak. That accumulation does not affect the current selling season, but it is the population most likely to produce mortgage stress in 6 to 12 months, and agents in markets with concentrations of tariff-exposed or federal-employment-adjacent workers should be aware it is building.
