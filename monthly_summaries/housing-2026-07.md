# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through the month but showed the first signs of deceleration worth watching. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% YoY — positive for 55 consecutive weeks — and the 4-week average is still up 4.37% YoY, though it has decelerated from prior readings. The spot week ending June 26 ticked down 3.0% YoY, ending a positive streak at the weekly level, so the trend is softening at the edges even as the longer averages remain constructive. Inventory is rising but not accelerating: total active supply was up 1.2% YoY at Altos, with the 4-week new listings average barely positive at +0.41% YoY after the spot week turned negative (-7.1% YoY). That pullback in new listings — if it persists — will keep supply from building meaningfully into late summer. Pending days on market at 70.9 days are running +6.7% YoY for the 103rd consecutive week, a reminder that absorption is slower than it was in 2023-24 even if volume is growing. NAR's June existing sales print came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and up 4.1% YoY — the acceleration in the NSA count (+7.9% YoY) is more telling than the SAAR wobble. New home sales also firmed to 628K SAAR in June, though they remain down 5.1% YoY and new home median prices fell 2.7% YoY, reflecting builders cutting to move inventory. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.78% as of July 29, essentially unchanged on the month after a brief dip, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.16 percentage points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap is the affordability story that isn't in the headlines: if lender risk appetite normalizes even partially, rates fall to the mid-to-low 6s without the Fed moving a basis point. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 1.35% YoY and has fallen four consecutive weeks — a meaningful softening from the positive trend earlier this year. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks, ticked up to 123.3 on the spot read and the 4-week average reversed higher to 127.5, but the 13-week average remains down 2.4% YoY and has fallen seven consecutive weeks. The divergence between a recovering MII spot and a deteriorating MBA 4-week average is worth watching closely: if MII's uptick is real, purchase applications should stabilize in August; if it stalls again, the MBA trend will confirm a broader demand softening. Housing starts jumped to 1,427K SAAR in June — beating expectations and turning positive YoY — but nearly all of the gain was multifamily (5+ units up 76% on the month). Single-family starts fell for the third consecutive month to 895K SAAR and are down 3.2% YoY. Single-family permits came in at 872K, positive YoY for the first time in months but down on the month. Builders are completing existing pipeline — single-family completions rose to 964K SAAR, up 4.8% YoY — while pulling back on new single-family authorizations. That dynamic keeps near-term resale competition from builders contained, but it also means the supply pipeline for 2027 is thinning.

The macro backdrop improved more than the consensus expected in June, but the improvement is uneven in ways that matter for housing. Headline CPI fell 0.4% in June — well below the +0.9% consensus — driving the YoY rate to 3.46%, and core CPI was essentially flat on the month at 2.57% YoY, both cooler than expected. Shelter CPI continues to run at 3.26% YoY despite real-time rent data suggesting rents are only modestly higher, which means the measured inflation overhang from housing lags will continue to fade through the second half of the year and give the Fed room to move if labor weakens. Core PCE through May was still at 3.41% YoY — above target — so the Fed is not done, but the directional signal from CPI is that the last mile is getting shorter. Labor was the genuine positive surprise: payrolls came in at 159K for June (well above the 59K consensus print in the data), the unemployment rate fell to 4.2% against a 4.4% expectation, and initial jobless claims for the week of July 18 came in at 187K versus the 212K consensus. The hires rate held at 3.3% but total hires are down 3.0% YoY, and the quits rate at 1.9% is down 0.2 points YoY — workers are staying put, which suppresses the job-change-driven relocations that generate housing transactions. The long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) ticked down in June to 1.
