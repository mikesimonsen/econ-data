**July 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through June but is showing the first cracks worth watching. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% year-over-year, now 55 consecutive weeks in positive territory — a streak that has anchored the bull case for transaction volume all year. The 4-week average remains at +4.37% YoY, but the spot weekly read turned negative last week at -2.99%, the third consecutive weekly decline. One soft week is noise; three is a pattern to monitor. Inventory grew to roughly 1.07 million active listings in the Altos data, up 1.17% YoY — still modest growth, and notably slower than the YoY gains we were tracking earlier in the year. New listings ticked down 7.1% YoY in the most recent week, though the 4-week and 13-week averages remain essentially flat YoY (+0.41% and +0.71% respectively), suggesting the spot week was partly seasonal. Pending days on market have been running about 7% above year-ago levels for 103 consecutive weeks, a persistent signal that homes are taking longer to clear even as sales volumes stay positive. NAR's May existing home sales came in at 4.17 million SAAR — up 3.2% YoY and well above the 3.85 million consensus — with the Midwest and South leading the regional gains. On a non-seasonally adjusted basis, the YoY change was flat at 0.0%, which is the more honest read in May; the SAAR beat largely reflects favorable seasonal adjustment. New home sales diverged sharply, falling to 580,000 SAAR in May, down 7.5% YoY, with months of supply at 10.3 — a level that historically pressures builder pricing. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.60% as of July 2, down slightly on the week after briefly firming. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.13 percentage points, down 30 basis points year-over-year from its recent peak but still well above the long-run norm of around 1.7 points. That gap remains the hidden affordability lever: if the spread compresses another 25-30 basis points toward historical norms — without any move from the Fed — the 30-year could fall to the mid-6s without a single rate cut. MBA purchase applications for the week ending June 26 rose 3.2% YoY, with the 4-week average at +3.1% — but the 13-week average, at +5.1%, is drifting lower, reflecting gradual deceleration. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which captures early-stage credit inquiries and typically leads MBA by 2 to 4 weeks — bounced off recent lows to +3.5% on the week, but its 13-week average has now fallen for seven consecutive weeks and sits at -2.38% YoY, diverging meaningfully from MBA's still-positive trend. That divergence is the leading indicator to watch in July: if MII doesn't recover, MBA applications are likely to soften in 3 to 4 weeks. On the supply side, single-family completions fell to their lowest level since June 2011, down 17.4% YoY — a structural constraint on resale-adjacent supply that is quietly tightening available inventory faster than the headline active listings number suggests.

The macro backdrop shifted modestly in a more constructive direction this month, but not without complications. June payrolls rose by roughly 159,000 — solid, and above the 59,000 consensus — while the unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, beating expectations of 4.4%. That jobs number reduces near-term recession risk and supports purchase intent among households with stable employment. Wage growth at +3.5% YoY continues to outpace home-price appreciation running near +2%, which is quietly improving real affordability at the margin. The complication is inflation: core PCE ran at +3.41% YoY through May, above the Fed's 2% target and still sticky enough to keep the Fed on hold. Shelter CPI rose 3.35% YoY, decelerating slowly — the private-market rent data from Zillow ZORI (+2.95% YoY) and the broader new-lease trackers suggest official shelter inflation will continue drifting lower over the next two to three quarters as leases roll over, which is the main mechanism by which core PCE could eventually give the Fed room to cut. For now, the 10-year Treasury has held near 4.47%, and rate markets are not pricing aggressive easing. On the regional price picture, Case-Shiller's April data (which reflects contracts signed roughly in February and March) shows the national index up just 0.79% YoY and decelerating — but the metro-level story is more differentiated than the headline implies. Dallas remains the clearest laggard at -1.65% YoY for 13 consecutive negative months, and Charlotte is essentially flat at +0.93%. Meanwhile, Chicago has posted seven consecutive months of acceleration, Cleveland is running +3.0%, and Detroit +2.5%
