# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held in positive territory over the four weeks ending June 26, with the Altos 4-week average up 4.4% year-over-year and the 13-week average running at +4.8% — now 55 consecutive weeks of positive YoY readings on that longer trend. The spot week did slip to -3.0% YoY, the first negative weekly print in that streak, worth watching but not yet alarming given the 13-week trend's steadiness. Inventory rose for an eighth consecutive week to roughly 1.075 million units, up 1.2% YoY — growth that has decelerated noticeably from the 10-15% YoY clips of late 2025. New listings tell a slightly different story: the 4-week average sits just barely positive at +0.4% YoY and the single-week reading flipped to -7.1% YoY, suggesting the spring listing impulse is fading earlier than normal. Pending days on market are running 6.7% above last year at 70.9 days, a level that has now been elevated for 103 consecutive weeks — pace is not tightening. June existing home sales printed at 4.09 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and up 4.1% YoY, though the month-over-month read was negative; on an NSA basis, the YoY gain was a cleaner +7.9%, and the trend has been improving for several months. The new home market diverges sharply: May new home sales fell to 580,000 SAAR, off 7.4% YoY, with months supply rising to 10.3 — builders are sitting on inventory they cannot move at current prices, even as median new home prices firmed to $424,900. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.63% as of July 17, down modestly from its recent range, but the 30Y-10Y spread has ticked back up to 2.13 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points — meaning the compression story that could deliver meaningful affordability relief without any Fed action has stalled for now. MBA purchase applications were down 1.5% YoY on the most recent weekly read, with the 4-week average turning slightly negative (-0.6% YoY) after months of positive readings; the 13-week average remains up 5.2%. The Xactus MII, which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks, showed a reversal upward in late June after a multi-week decline, with its 4-week average recovering to -2.3% YoY — the MII and MBA are currently pointing in different directions, which suggests the next few weeks of purchase application data will be the tell. June housing starts beat expectations at 1,427,000 SAAR, but the composition matters: single-family starts fell a third consecutive month to 895,000 while the gain was driven entirely by multifamily (5+ units up 76% on the month). Single-family permits dipped to 871,000 but remain barely positive YoY for the first time since last year, and total permits missed at 1,367,000 with South permits down sharply. Watch whether the MBA applications 4-week average holds negative or recovers toward the 13-week trend — that divergence will tell you how much of June's existing sales beat was pull-forward versus genuine improvement.

The macro backdrop improved in ways that matter directly to housing demand. June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 — above the consensus of 59,000 (the expectation appears to be a monthly change figure, suggesting actual job gains were solid), and the unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, beating expectations of 4.4%. Wage growth held at +3.5% YoY, which at current price levels keeps the employed buyer's purchasing power roughly stable. The more important development was the June CPI print: headline fell 0.4% month-over-month against an expectation of +0.9%, and core was essentially flat against a +0.3% expectation. Core CPI is now running at 2.6% YoY. That matters for housing through two channels: first, it reduces the near-term risk of the Fed tightening further, removing one ceiling on rate expectations; second, it puts downward pressure on Treasury yields, which are the floor under mortgage rates. The 10-year yield ticked back up to 4.56% on July 20, partially offsetting the CPI tailwind, and that uptick is why the mortgage-Treasury spread reversal bears watching — if Treasuries firm from here, the spread compression needed to bring the 30-year below 6.25% becomes harder to achieve.
