# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales held at +4.81% year-over-year for the week ending June 26 — now 55 consecutive weeks of positive YoY readings on that smoothed basis — but the spot week turned negative (-2.99% YoY) for the first time in that streak, and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37%. That spot softness deserves watching but does not yet reverse the trend; seasonal patterns typically pull weekly pending counts lower in late June and July. Inventory rose for an eighth consecutive week, with Altos total active supply at roughly 1.07 million, up 1.2% YoY — still tight in absolute terms but growing consistently enough to give buyers more options than they had a year ago. New listings, however, flipped negative on a YoY basis in the most recent week (-7.1%), though the 4-week average remains modestly positive (+0.4%) and the 13-week average is in a 14-week rising trend at +0.7% YoY, suggesting the weekly print is noise rather than a shift. Pending days on market extended to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are taking longer to go under contract than a year ago, which is consistent with the Sun Belt inventory overhang visible in the Case-Shiller data (Dallas -1.7% YoY, Phoenix -1.8%, Denver -1.8%, Seattle -2.2% through April). The NAR existing home sales print for June came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating consensus of 3.85 million and running +4.1% YoY on a seasonally adjusted basis; the NSA count of 422,000 was up 7.9% year-over-year, the strongest YoY comparison in recent months. New home sales, by contrast, fell to 580,000 SAAR in May (-7.4% YoY) with months supply rising to 10.3 — builders are sitting on elevated finished inventory in the same Sun Belt markets where resale supply has already been weighing on prices, and single-family permits fell 2.4% in June even as total starts jumped 19% on a multifamily wave. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.63% as of July 17, down about 5 basis points over the past month. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.13 points — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points, meaning roughly 40 basis points of rate relief are embedded in lender risk premiums rather than in the underlying benchmark. If credit conditions normalize and the spread compresses toward 1.85-1.90, rates could fall to the mid-to-low 6% range without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications turned negative YoY on a 4-week average basis (-0.6%) for the first time in 74 weeks, and the spot week fell 7.3%. The Xactus MII — which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks — ticked up 3.5% in the most recent reading and the 4-week average reversed higher after a declining stretch, a modest divergence that implies the MBA softness may not persist into late July. The signal to watch over the next four weeks is whether the MII follow-through materializes in MBA purchase applications or fades, and whether the Altos spot pending count recovers its YoY footing.

The macro backdrop shifted meaningfully this month in ways that matter for housing. June headline CPI fell 0.4% month-over-month — well below the +0.9% consensus — and core CPI was essentially flat, bringing the YoY core reading to 2.57%. That is the most significant single data point for housing in months: lower realized inflation reduces the pressure on long-end Treasury yields, which is the direct input to mortgage rates. The 10-year Treasury has already responded, falling 7 basis points to 4.52%; if it holds or continues lower, the arithmetic on spread compression becomes even more powerful for affordability. Core PCE for May was still running at 3.41% YoY — the Fed's preferred measure remains above target — but the June CPI print will feed into the June PCE release and likely pull it lower. The labor market printed better than expected: June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 (consensus was 59,000, which appears to reflect a survey-period anomaly in the consensus figure; the actual print is solidly positive), unemployment fell to 4.2% against a 4.4% expectation, and initial claims for the week ending July 11 hit 208,000, a 5.9% YoY decline. The quits rate at 1.9% is 20 basis points below a year ago, signaling that workers feel less confident about voluntary job changes — historically a leading indicator of softening wage growth 6-9 months out, which could temper the income side of affordability. The long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) remains 17% above year-ago levels even after a slight June decline; that accumulation does not affect the current selling season but is the variable to watch for any future distressed-inventory story.
