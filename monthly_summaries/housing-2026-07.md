# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive territory on the 13-week average — up 4.81% year-over-year for the 55th consecutive week — but the spot reading slipped to -2.99% YoY for the week ending June 26, and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37% after running higher through spring. That deceleration is worth watching but not yet alarming; the 13-week trend is still rising. Inventory continued its slow climb, with Altos total active supply up 1.17% year-over-year and Realtor.com active listings up 1.86%, though new listings growth stalled — the single-week Altos new listings reading turned -7.14% YoY even as the 4-week average held slightly positive at +0.41%. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.71% YoY, confirming that homes are taking modestly longer to absorb into contract. NAR June existing sales came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating consensus by roughly 240,000 units and up 4.07% YoY — the fastest YoY pace in over two years — though the month-over-month SAAR reading edged down. On a non-seasonally-adjusted basis the YoY gain was a cleaner +7.93%, with all four regions positive. New home sales (628,000 SAAR) ticked up after a string of declines but remain 5.1% below a year ago; builders are cutting prices — new home median fell 2.66% YoY — which is pulling buyers toward new construction and slightly away from existing. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.78% today, essentially unchanged on the month and up just 1 basis point year-over-year, after a modest mid-month retreat that reversed heading into month-end. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.16 percentage points — well above the long-run norm of roughly 1.7 points. The four-week average spread has held flat at 2.13 for three weeks, which means rate relief is not coming from spread compression right now; if the spread were to normalize, the 30-year could fall 40-45 basis points without the Fed moving a single time. MBA purchase applications for the week of July 24 were up 2.70% YoY on the spot read, but the 4-week average has slipped to -1.35% YoY and has been falling for four consecutive weeks — a mild caution flag. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, reversed higher in the June 26 reading (+3.53% on the week), and the 4-week MII average also turned up; that bounce suggests the MBA softness may be transient rather than the start of a trend. What to watch in August: whether the MII follow-through materializes in MBA purchase data in the next two to three weeks, and whether new listings can sustain any YoY growth as sellers who sat out spring reassess.

The macro backdrop shifted in ways that matter more than the headlines suggested. June headline CPI fell 0.42% month-over-month — substantially cooler than the +0.9% consensus — and core CPI was essentially flat on the month at -0.02%, bringing the YoY core reading to 2.57%. Shelter CPI continued its slow drift, up 0.12% for the month and 3.26% YoY; that gap between shelter CPI and real-time rent data (Zillow ZORI at +3.34% YoY, Redfin rent implied by ZORI) continues to compress, which means shelter's drag on overall inflation is fading rather than adding. Core PCE for May held at +3.41% YoY, still above the Fed's target, but the June CPI surprise shifts the probability distribution for the July PCE release. For housing, the more immediate mechanism is the 10-year Treasury: at 4.62%, it has barely moved even as inflation data improved, which explains why mortgage rates are stuck. The labor market delivered a genuine beat: the June unemployment rate fell to 4.2% against a 4.4% consensus, payrolls rose 159,000, and initial claims for the week of July 18 came in at 187,000 — 25,000 below consensus. A labor market this firm supports purchase demand but also limits the Fed's urgency to cut, keeping the 10-year anchored. The one data point that deserves an agent's attention is the long-term unemployed cohort: those out of work 27 weeks or more stood at 1.937 million in June, up 17.3% year-over-year. It ticked down from May's high, but the YoY accumulation is real. This group doesn't affect the current selling season, but it is the leading population for mortgage delinquencies 6 to 12 months out — worth a quiet watch as it compounds.
