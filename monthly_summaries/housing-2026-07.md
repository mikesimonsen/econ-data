**July 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through most of June but showed visible deceleration into the back half of the month. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% year-over-year — 55 consecutive weeks of positive YoY readings — but the spot week ending June 26 turned negative at -2.99% YoY, and the 4-week average has decelerated to +4.37% from higher levels earlier in the quarter. That spot dip warrants watching but does not yet break the trend; seasonal softness in late June is normal, and the 13-week average remains the signal, not the noise. Inventory rose for an eighth consecutive week to roughly 1.075 million active listings, up about 1.2% YoY — growth that has stalled well below the pace of a year ago. More notable: new listings turned negative on a YoY basis in the spot week (-7.1%), though the 4-week average is barely positive (+0.4%) and the 13-week average has been trending up for 14 weeks at +0.7% YoY, suggesting the spot read is partly seasonal. Pending days on market held at 70.9, up 6.7% YoY — sellers are still waiting longer than last year, but that metric has been consistently elevated for over two years and is not accelerating. NAR existing home sales came in at 4.09 million SAAR for June, beating expectations of 3.85 million by a wide margin; on an NSA basis, June transactions ran +7.9% YoY — an acceleration. The divergence with new home sales (580K SAAR for May, -7.5% YoY, with months supply at 10.3) is real and worth flagging to clients asking about builder competition: new home supply is elevated and builders are not absorbing it quickly. The June starts headline of 1,427K SAAR beat expectations and looked strong, but the composition matters — single-family starts fell 0.2% and are down 3.2% YoY for a third consecutive month; the headline was entirely driven by a statistically unusual 76% jump in 5+ unit starts. Single-family permits ticked up to 871K, the highest since May 2024, which is modestly constructive for 2027 supply but not 2026. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.75% as of July 21, essentially flat year-over-year and up a few basis points on the week as the 10-year Treasury edged back to 4.63%. The mortgage-Treasury spread (the difference between the 30-year rate and the 10-year yield, historically around 1.7 percentage points) has compressed to 2.12 from 2.40 a year ago — that 28-basis-point compression has delivered roughly a quarter-point of rate relief independent of the Fed, and is the main reason affordability has not deteriorated further even as the 10-year has drifted higher. If the spread returns to its long-run norm, the 30-year falls toward 6.3–6.4% with no Fed action required. On leading indicators: the MBA purchase index 4-week average is running -1.2% YoY — a mild softening from earlier in the year — and the 13-week average, though still positive at +4.2%, has rolled over for two weeks. More important, the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-inquiry-based signal that leads MBA applications by roughly 2–4 weeks) shows the 4-week average up from its recent trough but the 13-week average still falling at -2.4% YoY. The MII and MBA are not diverging sharply, but neither is giving a clean positive signal. Watch whether the MBA 4-week average stabilizes or continues to soften as the primary demand indicator over the next four weeks.

The macro backdrop is genuinely mixed in ways that matter for housing. June payrolls came in at roughly 159K — a moderate pace, above the expectations implied by the consensus — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, well below the 4.4% that had been forecast. That outcome reduces near-term recession concern and supports buyer confidence. But the labor market internals are softer than the headline: the hires rate sits at 3.3%, down 0.1 point YoY, and the quits rate at 1.9% is down 0.2 point YoY — both suggesting workers feel less able to voluntarily change jobs, which historically correlates with reduced household formation and move-up demand. The long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) ticked down in June to 1.937 million, a welcome reversal, but the cohort is still up 17% year-over-year; this is the slow-building risk to the mortgage market, not the current selling season. June CPI was the headline economic surprise of the month — both headline (-0.4% MoM vs. +0.9% expected) and core (-0.0% MoM vs. +0.3% expected) came in sharply below consensus, pulling core CPI YoY to 2.57%.
