**July 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive in June, but the composition of the signal deserves attention. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% YoY — that streak has now run 55 consecutive weeks — and the 4-week average is +4.37% YoY, though it decelerated from the prior read. The spot week ending June 26 flipped negative at -2.99% YoY, which on its own would not be alarming given holiday-week noise, but it follows three consecutive weeks of falling spot readings. Inventory continued its measured climb: total active supply reached 1.07 million units, up roughly 1.2% YoY, with the Realtor.com active count confirming the direction at +1.9% YoY for June. New listings, however, softened — the Altos spot read fell 7.1% YoY that week, though the 4-week average held just barely positive at +0.4% YoY and the 13-week average remained constructive at +0.7% YoY. The supply expansion that characterized early 2026 appears to be decelerating into summer, not accelerating. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, +6.7% YoY, now 103 consecutive weeks above year-ago levels — a persistent if mild signal of a buyer-paced market. May existing home sales came in at 4.17 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus by a wide margin and up 3.2% YoY, with the South and West leading. New home sales moved in the opposite direction, falling to 580,000 SAAR in May (-7.5% YoY) while the months supply of new homes rose to 10.3 — builders are sitting on finished inventory at historically uncomfortable levels, which explains why single-family starts fell again in May (-7.1% YoY) even as single-family permits ticked up. The starts-to-permits gap confirms builders are still working off existing pipeline rather than breaking new ground aggressively; single-family starts are now at their lowest YoY reading in seven months. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.59% as of July 6, essentially flat on the month. The more interesting number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.11 points Friday versus a long-run norm near 1.70, the spread remains elevated — and it has compressed 28 basis points YoY. If it returns toward 1.85-1.90 without the 10-year moving, the 30-year falls to roughly 6.30-6.35% without the Fed doing anything. MBA purchase applications were up 3.1% YoY on the 4-week average, decelerating from the 5.1% on the 13-week. The Xactus MII tells a more cautious story: the spot read reversed higher to 123.3, but the 13-week average has fallen seven consecutive weeks and sits -2.4% YoY — a meaningful divergence from MBA that, given the MII's 2-4 week lead, suggests purchase application strength may soften into mid-July. Watch whether the MII 4-week average, which turned up this past week, confirms a stabilization or fades again.

The macro backdrop is the most important frame for the second half. June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 — above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, also beating expectations. That combination removes the near-term catalyst for the Fed to cut rates, which keeps the 10-year anchored near 4.49% and limits how much rate relief can come from the Treasury side of the spread equation. The remaining relief, if it arrives, must come from spread compression — which is why agents should watch credit markets as closely as Fed meeting minutes. The more complicated signal is in prices: headline CPI for May printed +4.2% YoY but came in cooler than the feared +0.9% month-over-month, and core CPI at +2.8% YoY was also below consensus. Core PCE for May, however, held at +3.4% YoY — still well above the Fed's 2% target — and shelter CPI, which lags real-time rent by 12-18 months, continues running at +3.4% YoY and will keep measured inflation elevated well into 2027 even as actual rents cool. The Zillow ZORI rose 1.1% in May, its largest monthly gain in years, which is the opposite of what the shelter CPI will need to see to fall. On the regional price picture, the Case-Shiller April data (which reflects closings from roughly February-March) continues to show a clean two-speed market: Chicago is up 6.4% YoY and New York up 3.7%, while Dallas, Denver, Phoenix, Las Vegas, and Seattle are all negative on a 12-month basis — Las Vegas at its weakest YoY reading since September 2023.
