**July 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive for the 55th consecutive week on the Altos 13-week pending sales average, which came in at +4.81% YoY as of June 26 — though the most recent weekly read slipped to -2.99% YoY, and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37%. That single-week negative print bears watching but doesn't reverse the trend; summer seasonality always introduces noise here. Total active inventory rose for an eighth consecutive week to roughly 1.075 million units, up about 1.2% YoY — growth that has clearly decelerated from the double-digit YoY gains of 2024-25. New listings tell a similar story: the 4-week average is barely positive at +0.41% YoY and the most recent weekly read turned negative at -7.1%, suggesting the supply-rebuild that characterized the past two years is stalling. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are moving, but not as briskly as a year ago. The June NAR existing home sales print came in at 4.09 million SAAR, up 4.1% YoY and well ahead of the 3.85 million consensus — the strongest beat in several months. The NSA read is cleaner here: +7.9% YoY. New home sales, by contrast, fell to 580,000 SAAR in May, down 7.5% YoY, with months of supply at 10.3 — a divergence that reflects builders working through a bloated pipeline rather than any consumer pullback on existing homes. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.85% today, up about 8 basis points over the past four days and up slightly YoY. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, which is holding at approximately 2.13-2.14 percentage points — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap represents a meaningful affordability lever that does not require Fed action to close; if spreads compress toward historical norms, the 30-year rate falls toward the mid-to-upper 6s even with the 10-year where it is. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 1.2% YoY and has fallen three consecutive weeks, a mild caution signal. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks — rebounded on its most recent weekly read (+3.5% on the spot, 4-week average +2.3% after reversing lower), suggesting the MBA softness may be brief. Watch whether MII sustains its uptick through late July; if it does, August purchase applications should firm. The June starts headline of 1,427,000 SAAR beat expectations, but essentially all of the beat came from multifamily — single-family starts fell for a third consecutive month to 895,000 SAAR, down 3.2% YoY. Single-family permits also pulled back to 871,000, suggesting builders are cautious about new single-family commitments heading into fall.

The macro backdrop shifted in one important direction this month: June CPI came in well below expectations, with headline falling -0.4% month-over-month against a +0.9% consensus and core essentially flat against a +0.3% forecast. That brought core CPI to +2.57% YoY — the lowest reading in some time — and it opened the door for the 10-year to ease if it holds. The problem is that core PCE, the Fed's preferred gauge, printed at +3.41% YoY through May, and shelter CPI continues to run at +3.26% YoY. The Fed is not going to declare victory on inflation based on one month's CPI miss, and breakevens (market-implied inflation expectations embedded in TIPS) will determine whether the June print is the start of a disinflationary move or an outlier. That is the single most important thing to watch heading into August — specifically the 5-year breakeven. On labor, June payrolls of roughly 159,000 and an unemployment rate of 4.2% (beating the 4.4% consensus) describe an economy that is softening but not breaking. The number that warrants attention over the next six to twelve months is the long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 or more weeks — which rose 17.3% YoY through June even as it pulled back slightly month-over-month. This cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is the leading indicator for future mortgage delinquencies; as it accumulates, distressed-seller volume eventually follows. On regional prices, the April Case-Shiller data (the most recent available) continued to show Chicago +6.4% YoY and New York +3.7% YoY while Dallas, Denver, Phoenix, Seattle, and Las Vegas all registered negative YoY readings — Las Vegas at -1.7% and flagged as an unusual move. Agents working Sun Belt metros should expect sellers to need more pricing patience heading into fall; Midwest and Northeast agents have a different conversation.
