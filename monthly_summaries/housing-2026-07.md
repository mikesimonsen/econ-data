**July 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through the first half of 2026, but the most recent Altos weekly read introduced a note of caution: new pending sales fell 3.0% year-over-year in the week ending June 26, ending what had been a positive streak. The four-week average is still running +4.4% YoY, and the 13-week average — now 55 consecutive weeks in positive territory — sits at +4.8% YoY, which means the single-week dip looks more like seasonal noise than a turn, but it warrants watching. Altos inventory reached 1.07 million active listings as of June 26, up 1.2% YoY, with the 13-week new-listings average holding a thin +0.7% YoY gain after a brief stretch of positive growth reversed in the most recent week (down 7.1% YoY spot). New listings are essentially flat on a trend basis — supply is not building the way it was a year ago. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, a slow but persistent lengthening now 103 weeks running. The May NAR existing home sales print came in at 4.17 million SAAR, beating consensus by roughly 320,000 units and accelerating to +3.2% YoY — a meaningful positive surprise. The NSA YoY read was flat, which is more honest given the seasonal calendar, but the directional beat is real. New home sales told a different story: 580,000 SAAR in May, down 7.5% YoY, with months supply rising to 10.3 — builders are carrying elevated finished inventory as starts fell sharply (-8.2% YoY) and single-family completions hit their lowest reading since June 2011. The starts-permits divergence is worth noting: single-family permits edged up 1.25% in May but remain negative YoY for the 24th consecutive month, confirming builders are finishing pipeline rather than committing to future supply. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.68% today, having ticked up two basis points over the past two days after a modest decline earlier in the month. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.09 points, it is compressing — down 30 basis points YoY — but still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. If that spread normalizes even partially, rates fall meaningfully without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications flipped negative YoY in the most recent week (-6.3%), though the four-week average is still up 0.5% YoY — the streak of 73 weeks of positive 13-week YoY readings is decelerating. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, reversed higher in the week ending June 26 after several weeks of declines, with its four-week average also turning up. MII is still negative YoY (-2.3% on the four-week average) while MBA remains barely positive, so the two series are not yet diverging in a way that signals alarm — but if MII's 13-week average, falling for seven straight weeks, does not stabilize, expect MBA to follow lower into late July and August.

The macro picture this month is genuinely mixed in ways that matter for housing. June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 with the unemployment rate falling to 4.2% against a consensus of 4.4% — a clear beat that removes near-term recession anxiety and supports buyer confidence. Average hourly earnings are running at +3.5% YoY, which at current price levels keeps real wage growth modestly positive. The problem is that these headline numbers coexist with a labor market that is quietly softening underneath: the hires rate has been running below pre-pandemic norms, the quits rate at 1.9% signals workers are less confident about job mobility, and the long-term unemployed (27-plus weeks) stand at 1.937 million, up 17.3% YoY. That last figure does not affect this selling season, but it is the leading indicator for future mortgage stress — watch it accumulate. On inflation, May core PCE printed at +3.41% YoY and core CPI at +2.82%, both above the Fed's target but the monthly prints came in cooler than expected. Shelter CPI is still running at +3.35% YoY, which means official inflation measures are being propped up by housing costs that private-sector rent indices like ZORI (+2.95% YoY) suggest are decelerating. That shelter-CPI lag will continue to pull core PCE higher than underlying conditions warrant, keeping the Fed cautious. The 10-year Treasury finished today at 4.59%, up 10 basis points over three days on the jobs beat — that is the number to track this month. If the 10-year firms toward 4.7%-4.8% on continued strong data, the 30-year mortgage rate follows, and the affordability window that opened in the spring narrows again.
