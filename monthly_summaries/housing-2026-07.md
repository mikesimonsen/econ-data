# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through June, though the underlying trend is more nuanced than the headline reads suggest. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% YoY and has risen for 55 consecutive weeks — that streak is the most important number in this report. The most recent weekly read, however, dipped to -2.99% YoY, pulling the 4-week average to +4.37% and marking three straight weeks of week-over-week declines; the streak is intact but it is being tested at the margin. Inventory continued to build — Altos total active listings reached 1.07 million, up 1.17% YoY, with the 4-week new listings average running +0.41% YoY, though the most recent weekly reading flipped to -7.14% YoY, a meaningful deceleration worth watching. Pending days on market extended to 70.9 days, up 6.71% YoY, confirming that homes are taking modestly longer to clear even as sales volumes hold. NAR existing home sales came in at 4.17 million SAAR for May, up 3.22% YoY and beating the consensus of 3.85 million by a wide margin — the strongest beat in recent memory. NSA, May closed flat YoY at 390,000 units, which is the honest read on actual transaction volume. New home sales told a different story: 580,000 SAAR, down 7.5% YoY with months supply climbing to 10.3 — builders are sitting on finished inventory they cannot move at current prices and rates. The 30-year fixed rate closed July 1 at 6.65%, ticking up slightly over the past two days. The spread to the 10-year Treasury sits at 2.19 points against a historical norm near 1.7 points — that 50-basis-point excess premium is still the most underappreciated affordability lever in this market. If lender risk appetite normalizes, the 30-year can fall meaningfully without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications rose 0.53% week-over-week for the week ending June 26, with the 4-week average up 3.12% YoY, though decelerating from the 13-week average of +5.09%. The Xactus MII — a credit-inquiry-based index that typically leads MBA applications by two to four weeks — bounced off recent lows with a +3.53% weekly gain, though its 13-week average has now fallen for seven consecutive weeks and sits -2.38% YoY. That divergence between the improving 4-week MII and the still-declining 13-week trend suggests the demand picture stabilized in late June but has not yet convincingly turned. What to watch in July: whether the 13-week Altos pending average holds above +4% YoY as the holiday-week distortions clear, and whether the MII 4-week recovery follows through into MBA purchase volume.

The macro backdrop shifted modestly in housing's favor this month, but the improvement is conditional. June payrolls came in at 159,000 — above the 59,000 consensus and enough to keep the unemployment rate at 4.2%, a tenth below expectations. That labor resilience matters directly: households with stable employment are the pool of potential buyers, and the hires rate holding at 3.3% means the job market is not yet deteriorating in a way that would suppress purchase demand. The concern sits one layer beneath the headline. The long-term unemployed — those out of work 27 weeks or more — number 1.937 million, up 17.3% YoY. That cohort is not buying homes this year; they are the seeds of a future delinquency wave if labor conditions soften further. On inflation, May Core PCE printed at +3.41% YoY, above the Fed's 2% target and above the +0.3% monthly consensus. Shelter CPI ran at +3.35% YoY, still elevated but decelerating — and because shelter CPI lags actual rent trends by 12-18 months, the Zillow ZORI reading of +2.95% YoY on typical rents suggests the shelter component will continue drifting lower through the back half of the year, which is the most direct path to Core PCE moving closer to target without additional rate hikes. The 10-year Treasury closed at 4.47%, holding a range that keeps the 30-year fixed anchored near 6.65%. On the regional divergence: March Case-Shiller (the most recent available, lagged roughly two months) shows Chicago up nearly 6% YoY and New York up 4%, while Dallas, Denver, Phoenix, Seattle, and Tampa all posted negative YoY readings, with Tampa now 17 consecutive months in the red. The two-speed market is not a narrative — it is in the data. Agents in supply-constrained Midwest and Northeast markets are working a different price environment than those in Sun Belt metros still absorbing the 2021-2022 overshoot.
