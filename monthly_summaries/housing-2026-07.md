# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% YoY after 55 consecutive weeks above the prior-year level — the underlying demand signal remains constructive even as the spot reading dipped to -2.99% YoY for the week of June 26, a single-week softness that the four-week average at +4.37% largely absorbs. That four-week average is decelerating, however, and pending days on market have run above the prior year for 103 consecutive weeks, now at 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are taking longer to go under contract. Inventory at Altos reached ~1.07 million active listings, up 1.2% YoY, but new listings flipped negative on a YoY basis (-7.1% for the week), though the four-week average held fractionally positive (+0.4%). The 13-week new-listings average has risen for 14 straight weeks on a trend basis, suggesting the supply pipeline hasn't broken — it may simply be reflecting early-summer seasonal pullback. NAR's June existing home sales came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating consensus by a wide margin (expected 3.85M), and the NSA YoY print of +7.9% is the cleanest read on actual transaction volume; the SAAR month-over-month dip of -1.9% is noise against that beat. New home sales told a different story: the May print was 580,000 SAAR, down 7.4% YoY, with months of supply hitting 10.3 — builders are sitting on elevated finished inventory even as single-family completions ticked up 6.6% in June. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.77% as of July 22, essentially flat year-over-year, but the spread to the 10-year Treasury remains at 2.12–2.13 percentage points — well above the long-run norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap is the affordability story hiding in plain sight: if spread normalization delivers even 25–30 basis points of compression without the 10-year moving, the 30-year drops toward the low-to-mid 6s without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications rebounded 5.5% week-over-week for the July 17 week, but the four-week average remains -1.2% YoY, and the 13-week average just turned lower after a 75-week positive streak. More importantly, the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA applications by two to four weeks — bounced from its recent trough (spot +3.5%, four-week average +2.3% week-over-week) but the YoY four-week average is still -2.3%, meaning the rebound is real but from a soft base. The divergence between MII and MBA has narrowed; watch whether MII's four-week YoY turns positive over the next two weeks as the clearest early read on August application volume. On supply, June housing starts beat expectations at 1,427,000 SAAR (+19% month-over-month), but that headline was driven almost entirely by a 76% jump in 5+ unit multifamily — single-family starts actually fell 0.2% and are down 3.2% YoY for the third consecutive month. Total permits missed at 1,367,000, with South permits down 8.8% YoY. Builders are completing their existing pipelines and pulling back on new single-family authorizations, which matters for 2027 resale competition more than today's selling season.

The macro backdrop shifted meaningfully this month, and agents should understand why it both helps and complicates the picture. June payrolls at roughly 159,000 and unemployment at 4.2% — a beat versus the 4.4% consensus — signal a labor market that remains functional without running hot, which keeps Fed rate-cut pressure muted but also keeps qualified buyers employed. The JOLTS quits rate at 1.9% (down 0.2 points YoY) and hires rate at 3.3% suggest workers are less confident about job-switching, which historically correlates with reduced household formation and move-up buying. The more consequential number: long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) at 1.937 million, up 17.3% YoY. This cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is the leading edge of future mortgage stress — worth tracking monthly. On inflation, June CPI was the headline of the month: -0.4% month-over-month on headline and essentially flat on core, both well below expectations. Shelter CPI continues to run at +3.3% YoY, which keeps measured inflation stickier than the goods side suggests, but the direction of travel matters for Fed policy and bond markets more than the absolute level. Core PCE for May was still at 3.4% YoY — the Fed's preferred gauge has not given them the cover to cut. The 10-year Treasury at 4.65% reflects a market that has absorbed the CPI surprise without dramatically repricing rate cuts, which is why the 30-year mortgage hasn't moved.
