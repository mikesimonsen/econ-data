# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held above year-ago levels through mid-June, but the streak is showing its first real cracks. The Altos 13-week average remains positive at +4.81% YoY — running positive for 55 consecutive weeks — but the spot reading for the week ending June 26 slipped to -2.99% YoY, marking the third consecutive weekly decline and the first YoY negative print in that run. The 4-week average held at +4.37% YoY, so the trend hasn't broken cleanly, but the direction of travel has clearly softened. Inventory continued to build — total active supply reached roughly 1.07 million, up about 1.2% YoY — though new listings decelerated sharply, with the spot week printing -7.1% YoY after running positive earlier in the spring. The 4-week new listings average held fractionally positive at +0.41% YoY, and the 13-week average has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks, suggesting the pullback is seasonal noise more than a structural reversal. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — still extending the 103-week streak of above-year-ago contract times. On the closed-sales side, NAR's June existing home sales came in at a 4.09 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million by a meaningful margin, and running +4.1% YoY on the seasonally adjusted basis; on the NSA count that's +7.9% YoY, which is the more reliable directional read for June. New home sales told a different story — the May print came in at 580,000 SAAR, down 7.5% YoY and falling for two consecutive months, with months of supply at 10.3, a level that historically pressures builder pricing. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.64% today, down about 21 basis points from a year ago, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.09 points — still roughly 40 basis points above the long-run norm of about 1.70. That gap matters: spread compression alone, without any Fed move or change in the 10-year, could bring the 30-year toward 6.20–6.30% if lender margins normalize. MBA purchase applications weakened into the July 4th period, with the 4-week average now -0.6% YoY after running positive — the first YoY negative in that average in over a year. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks, offered a modest counterpunch: the MII 4-week average reversed higher to +2.31% period-over-period even as its YoY sits at -2.3%, suggesting some stabilization in early-stage purchase intent. Builder starts fell sharply in May — total starts came in at 1,177,000 SAAR, roughly 15% below the prior month and well below the 1,350,000 consensus — driven by a two-standard-deviation drop in multifamily. Single-family starts fell only modestly, but single-family completions are now down 17.4% YoY and at their lowest since June 2011, which will keep resale competition from new supply lower than the permit pipeline implied. Watch whether the MII continues to recover over the next four weeks — if it firms, the MBA softness is a holiday-week artifact; if it falls further, the demand deceleration is real.

June's macro backdrop was the most consequential in months. Headline CPI printed -0.42% month-over-month against an expectation of +0.9%, and core CPI was essentially flat at -0.02% MoM against an expected +0.3% — both meaningful misses that reframe the rate conversation. The mechanism for housing is direct: cooler inflation reduces the probability that the Fed holds rates higher for longer, which puts modest downward pressure on the 10-year Treasury and, with it, mortgage rates. The 10-year is at 4.55% today; if it drifts toward 4.30–4.40% on continued disinflation, and the spread compresses even modestly, the 30-year could fall 30–40 basis points without a single Fed action. That is the affordability lever most buyers are not watching. Core PCE for May was still running at +3.41% YoY, above the Fed's 2% target, so the easing case isn't clean — but the direction shifted in June. Labor held better than expected: June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 and the unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, beating the 4.4% consensus, with average hourly earnings up 3.5% YoY. That combination — jobs growing, wages above inflation — supports purchase capacity. The long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) is worth flagging separately: it rose 17.3% YoY to just under 1.94 million, even as the headline rate improved.
