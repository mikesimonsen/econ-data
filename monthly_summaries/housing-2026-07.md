The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales remains positive at +4.81% year-over-year for the 55th consecutive week, confirming that the underlying demand trend is intact — but the most recent weekly reading slipped to -2.99% YoY, and the 4-week average has decelerated to +4.37%, down from higher levels earlier in the spring. That spot-week softness isn't alarming on its own, but it bears watching as we move through the traditionally slower weeks of mid-summer. Active inventory reached 1.07 million units on the Altos weekly read, up 1.17% year-over-year and rising for eight consecutive weeks — not a flood, but steady accumulation. New listings tell a more mixed story: the 4-week average is just barely positive at +0.41% YoY, while the most recent week printed -7.14% YoY, suggesting sellers pulled back around the holiday period. The 13-week average has been rising for 14 weeks, which is the better signal of the underlying trend. Pending days on market at 70.9 days are running 6.7% above last year — homes are taking longer to go under contract, a consistent softening signal now in its 103rd week of positive YoY readings. NAR's June existing home sales came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating consensus of 3.85 million by a wide margin and running 4.1% above June 2025; on an NSA basis that's 422,000 units, up 7.9% year-over-year. The beat is real, though the SAAR ticked down slightly from May — the YoY acceleration is the more durable read. New home sales, by contrast, fell to 580,000 SAAR in May, off 7.5% year-over-year, with months supply for new homes rising to 10.3 — builders are sitting on elevated unsold inventory even as existing inventory remains lean. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.64% as of July 15, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at approximately 2.09 basis points above the 10-year at 4.55% — still about 40 basis points wide of the historical norm near 1.70%. That gap matters: if credit conditions normalize even partially, rates could fall meaningfully without any Fed action. On leading indicators, the MBA purchase index 4-week average turned negative year-over-year (-0.60%) for the first time in several months, a shift worth flagging. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA applications by two to four weeks — has turned back up: the 4-week MII average reversed higher after several weeks of decline, but the 13-week average remains down 2.4% YoY after seven consecutive weeks of falling. The MII's recent uptick offers a tentative sign that the MBA softness may be brief, but the divergence between the short-term bounce and the still-declining 13-week trend means the signal isn't clean. On the supply side, May starts came in at 1,177,000 SAAR — well below the 1,350,000 consensus and down 8.2% year-over-year — with single-family completions falling to their lowest level since June 2011. Builders are finishing what's in the pipeline but pulling back sharply on new ground-breaking. That's a 2027 supply story, not a 2026 one, but worth holding onto. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the Altos weekly pending sales YoY stabilizes back into positive territory and whether the Xactus MII follow-through confirms a brief seasonal dip or the start of a broader softening.

The macro backdrop shifted in housing's favor this month in two meaningful ways. June CPI came in substantially cooler than expected — headline fell 0.42% month-over-month against a consensus of +0.9%, and core was essentially flat at -0.02% against an expected +0.3%. That reading directly affects rate expectations: lower inflation prints give the Fed room to move and compress the mortgage-Treasury spread faster than the market had anticipated. The 10-year Treasury held near 4.55%, so the immediate effect on mortgage rates was modest — 6.64% — but the inflation trajectory matters more than any single print. Shelter CPI continues running at +3.26% year-over-year, still elevated but decelerating from its pandemic peak; the gap between observed rent indexes (Zillow's ZORI rose to $1,951, up 2.95% YoY) and shelter CPI is closing slowly, which should continue to pull core inflation lower over the coming months. On labor, June payrolls printed 159,000, unemployment fell to 4.2% against a consensus of 4.4%, and initial jobless claims remain low at 215,000. The headline labor picture looks solid.
