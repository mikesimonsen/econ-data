**July 2026 Housing Summary**

The demand picture looks better than the headline number suggests. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% YoY and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks — that streak, not the single-week dip, is the right frame. The most recent weekly read came in at -2.99% YoY, but single-week July figures around the holiday are notoriously soft, and the 4-week average held at +4.37% YoY, ticking up from the prior week. What shifted this month is new listings: after running modestly positive YoY through spring, the 4-week average fell back to nearly flat (+0.41%) and the most recent week printed -7.14% YoY — sellers pulled back into the summer lull faster than usual. Inventory continued rising, up 1.17% YoY on Altos, but that growth is clearly decelerating as fresh supply thins. Pending days on market are running +6.71% YoY, now positive for 103 consecutive weeks — homes are taking longer to absorb than they did in 2024, though at 70.9 days the market is not stalled. The NAR May existing sales print — 4.17 million SAAR, up 3.2% YoY and beating consensus by roughly 320,000 units — confirmed the spring demand that the pending data had telegraphed. The NSA read was flat YoY, a more tempered signal. New home sales told a different story: 580,000 SAAR in May, down 7.5% YoY and falling for a second consecutive month, with months supply hitting 10.3 — builders are carrying elevated finished inventory precisely when starts are pulling back sharply (-8.2% YoY in May, missing expectations by a wide margin). Single-family permits are negative YoY for the 24th consecutive month, so the supply pipeline builders are clearing now will not be replenished quickly; that matters for 2027 resale competition more than the current selling season. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.59% today, down 20bp YoY, but the more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.11-2.16pp, it remains roughly 40-45 basis points above the historical norm of ~1.70pp. If that spread compresses toward normal as rate volatility subsides, the 30-year could fall meaningfully without any Fed action — that is the affordability lever most buyers and agents are not pricing in. MBA purchase applications came in at +3.1% YoY on the 4-week average, decelerating from the 13-week trend of +5.1% — worth watching. The Xactus MII, which leads MBA by 2-4 weeks, bounced back to -2.3% YoY on its 4-week average after a steeper decline, and the weekly read reversed higher; that recovery, if it holds, should stabilize or lift MBA applications into late July.

On the macro side, three prints matter most for housing right now. June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 net new jobs, and the unemployment rate fell to 4.2% against a consensus expectation of 4.4% — a genuinely clean report that reduces the probability of a near-term demand shock from labor market deterioration. Wage growth at +3.52% YoY continues to outpace home price appreciation at the national level, slowly rebuilding affordability math for qualified buyers at the margin. The complication is inflation: Core PCE printed at +3.41% YoY in May, and while May CPI came in cooler than feared (core +2.82% YoY), both measures remain above the Fed's 2% target, which keeps the 10-year yield elevated — it rose 11bp to 4.48% just in the last two days as the strong jobs data pushed rate-cut expectations further out. Shelter CPI at +3.35% YoY is still running hot but continues to decelerate, and as private-market rent data (Zillow ZORI +2.95% YoY) feeds through the lagged BLS methodology over the next 6-12 months, shelter's contribution to core inflation should diminish — which is the transmission path to lower long rates and eventually lower mortgage rates. The regional Case-Shiller data through March (two-month lag) captures the geographic split cleanly: Chicago +6.0% YoY, Cleveland +3.2% YoY, New York +4.0% YoY — the Midwest and Northeast are appreciating; Dallas -1.7%, Phoenix -1.8%, Tampa -1.8%, Seattle -2.4% YoY — the Sun Belt markets that led the pandemic run-up are still correcting. If you are listing in Charlotte or Detroit, the price narrative is stable to positive; if you are in Phoenix or Portland, sellers still need to be counseled on where the market actually is versus where their neighbor sold in 2022.
