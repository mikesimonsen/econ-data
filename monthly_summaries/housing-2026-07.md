# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The four weeks ending July 23 present a market that is healthier than the headline rate environment suggests, though the underlying demand data is sending a more cautious signal. Altos weekly new pending sales came in at -3.0% YoY for the most recent week, which broke a streak that had run 55 consecutive weeks positive — but the 4-week average held at +4.4% YoY and the 13-week average sits at +4.8% YoY, still positive, still rising slowly. The single-week negative read looks more like seasonal noise given where the 4-week sits, but it is worth watching for confirmation. Active inventory reached roughly 1.07 million units, up about 1.2% YoY — growth is decelerating relative to the pace earlier in the year, and new listings ticked negative YoY on the spot read (-7.1%) while the 4-week average barely held positive (+0.4%). That new-listings deceleration is a genuine signal: sellers are pulling back into summer, which should cap inventory growth through August. Pending days on market edged up to 70.9 days, +6.7% YoY — homes are taking longer to go under contract than a year ago, but the pace of that deterioration is not accelerating. NAR existing home sales printed 4.09 million SAAR for June, beating the 3.85 million consensus by a meaningful margin and running +4.1% YoY — the NSA read of 422,000 units was +7.9% YoY, the cleanest directional read. New home sales, by contrast, fell to 580,000 SAAR in May (-7.5% YoY) with months supply at 10.3, a level that explains why builders are not leaning into new permits: single-family permits held barely positive YoY (+0.6%) while total permits missed consensus and turned -1.9% YoY. June housing starts beat estimates at 1,427,000 SAAR — but almost all of the gain came from multifamily (5+ units up 76% month-over-month, almost certainly noisy), while single-family starts fell a third consecutive month to 895,000 (-3.2% YoY). The starts-vs-permits divergence reinforces the read that builders are completing existing pipeline, not committing new supply. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.77% as of July 22, flat year-over-year. The mortgage-Treasury spread — the gap between the 30-year fixed and the 10-year yield, which historically runs around 1.7 percentage points — is at 2.12 points, still elevated but down 30 basis points from a year ago. That compression alone has quietly delivered roughly 30 basis points of effective affordability improvement without a Fed move. The MBA purchase index for the week ending July 17 turned +0.4% YoY on a spot basis after several negative weeks, but the 4-week average remains -1.2% YoY. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — a credit-inquiry-based measure that leads the MBA by roughly 2-4 weeks — turned up in its most recent 4-week average after declining, but its 13-week average is still -2.4% YoY and has been falling for seven weeks. The MII and MBA are not yet diverging sharply, but the MII's sustained weakness at the trend level is the leading indicator to watch: if the 13-week MII does not firm through August, MBA purchase applications will likely soften again into September.

The macro backdrop delivered two surprises that pull in opposite directions for housing. June CPI printed -0.4% month-over-month on headline and essentially flat on core, well below the consensus of +0.9% and +0.3% respectively — the largest downside miss in the current cycle. Core CPI is now +2.6% YoY. That softness, if confirmed by the June Core PCE print due at month-end (May's read was +3.4% YoY, still sticky), would meaningfully shift rate-cut expectations and could pull the 10-year Treasury, currently at 4.71%, lower — which would compress the mortgage-Treasury spread further and push the 30-year toward the mid-6s. The mechanism is direct: each 20-25 basis point decline in the 10-year translates to a similar move in the 30-year, assuming the spread holds or compresses. The labor market is the counterweight. June payrolls came in at 159,000 versus a 59,000 consensus — the deviation is unusual and will likely be revised, but the unemployment rate fell to 4.2% against a 4.4% expectation, and initial jobless claims for the week of July 18 came in at 187,000, far below the 212,000 forecast. A labor market this firm keeps the Fed patient. The one number that does not fit the benign narrative is the long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 weeks or longer reached 1.937 million in June, +17.3% YoY.
