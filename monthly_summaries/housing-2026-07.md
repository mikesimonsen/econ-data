# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The past four weeks showed a housing market that is holding together beneath some noise in the weekly data. Altos new pending sales printed -3.0% YoY for the week of June 26 — the first negative weekly read after a 55-week positive streak — but that single-week dip is not the story: the 4-week average remains +4.4% YoY and the 13-week average sits at +4.8% YoY, still rising after 14 consecutive weeks of improvement. Demand has not reversed; it has decelerated into the slow holiday weeks, which is normal for late June. Total active inventory reached roughly 1.07 million units, up 1.2% YoY — growth that has been decelerating all spring as the new-listings pipeline tightened. New listings fell 7.1% YoY in the most recent week, though the 4-week average held barely positive at +0.4% YoY and the 13-week average has now risen 14 straight weeks. The interpretation: sellers did not flood the market this spring, which is keeping inventory growth from running away. Pending days on market at 70.9 days is up 6.7% YoY, a gradual lengthening that reflects more buyer selectivity, not a market seizing up. NAR existing home sales for May came in at 4.17 million SAAR — well ahead of the 3.85 million consensus — up 3.2% YoY with the YoY rate accelerating. The NSA count was flat YoY at 390,000, consistent with the SAAR beat being seasonal-adjustment-driven rather than a pure volume leap. New home sales told the opposite story: May printed 580,000 SAAR, down 7.5% YoY, with months supply rising to 10.3 — builders are sitting on a lot of completed and unsold inventory, which is why single-family starts fell 7.1% YoY and May starts missed consensus by nearly 175,000 units. The 30-year fixed rate ended July 2 at 6.60%, ticking down 5 basis points on the month; the spread to the 10-year Treasury compressed to 2.13 percentage points, now 30 basis points tighter YoY and tracking toward the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That compression is the affordability lever agents should be explaining to hesitant buyers — rates can fall meaningfully without any Fed action if credit markets continue to normalize. MBA purchase applications ended the period at +3.1% YoY on a 4-week average, but the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA by two to four weeks — is still running negative YoY (-2.3% on its 4-week average) even as it reversed higher this week. That divergence says the near-term application bounce may be modest. Watch whether the MII 4-week average crosses back to positive in July; if it does, August closings will be firm.

The macro backdrop shifted modestly in housing's favor this month. June payrolls came in at 159,000 — above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.2% against a 4.4% expectation, easing the near-term fear of a labor market deteriorating fast enough to pull buyers off the sideline. Wage growth at 3.5% YoY continues to outpace home price appreciation running near 2%, which slowly improves the income-to-payment ratio independent of rate moves. The complication is inflation: Core PCE for May printed 3.4% YoY — still well above the Fed's 2% target — and the 10-year Treasury has held near 4.47%, keeping the rate ceiling intact for now. The Fed does not have the room to cut that the bond market has intermittently priced in, and agents should not be promising clients a rate reprieve on that basis. The number that deserves more attention is long-term unemployment: workers out 27 or more weeks rose 17.3% YoY even as the headline rate improved, and the JOLTS hires rate at 3.3% and quits rate at 1.9% are both running below year-ago levels — signs of a labor market where people are less confident about moving between jobs. That cohort does not affect this selling season, but it is the pipeline for mortgage stress in 2027 if it keeps accumulating. On the regional front, the Case-Shiller April data (remember it lags two months) showed Dallas still negative at -1.7% YoY while the Midwest and Northeast metros continued to hold positive — Chicago, Detroit, Cleveland all above zero — confirming the two-speed dynamic that is shaping which listing narratives are defensible right now. The one macro signal worth tracking in July: whether Core PCE begins to decelerate toward 3.0%. If it does, the 10-year has room to ease, the mortgage-Treasury spread has room to compress further, and the 30-year fixed could move into the low-to-mid 6s — which the MII data suggests would bring a measurable application response.
