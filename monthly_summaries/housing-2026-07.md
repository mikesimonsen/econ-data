**July 2026 | Monthly Housing Summary**

The most important housing signal this month is the divergence between the spot and trend readings on pending sales. The Altos new pending sales count for the week of June 26 fell 3.0% year-over-year — the first negative YoY weekly print after what had been a prolonged positive streak — but the 4-week average held at +4.4% YoY and the 13-week average sits at +4.8% YoY, now positive for 55 consecutive weeks. That spread between the spot read and the trend tells you one holiday-affected week isn't reversing the underlying demand picture; watch whether the 4-week average begins to roll before concluding otherwise. Total Altos inventory reached 1.07 million units as of June 26, up 1.2% YoY, still rising but decelerating — inventory growth has been flattening against seasonal norms for several weeks now. New listings on a 4-week average basis are effectively flat YoY (+0.4%), after running modestly positive for much of the spring; the single most recent week printed -7.1% YoY, though that likely reflects the pre-holiday pull-forward. Pending days on market have been running about 7% above year-ago levels, a modest friction signal worth flagging at listing appointments but not yet a market-turning one. On the transaction side, NAR existing home sales came in at 4.17 million SAAR for May — a meaningful beat of the 3.85 million consensus and +3.2% YoY, the best print in several months. The NSA read was flat YoY, consistent with a market that is healing rather than accelerating. New home sales, by contrast, fell to 580K SAAR in May, -7.5% YoY, with months of supply at 10.3 — a level that explains why builders pulled single-family starts back to 882K SAAR (-7.1% YoY) even as single-family permits ticked slightly higher; they are working off standing inventory, not adding to the pipeline. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.60% as of July 2, down about 5 basis points over the month. The more actionable number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, currently 213 basis points versus a historical norm near 170 basis points. That 40-plus-point excess means rate relief does not require Fed action — if credit and volatility conditions normalize, the 30-year can move meaningfully lower without a single cut. The 10-year itself pulled back to 4.37% as of July 3. MBA purchase applications are holding: the 4-week average is +3.1% YoY, though the 13-week trend has been decelerating. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks by measuring credit-pull activity before an application is formally submitted — ticked up on its most recent weekly print after seven consecutive weeks of decline in its 13-week average; watch whether that uptick holds over the next two weeks, because it will show up in MBA data by late July.

The macro backdrop remains the principal constraint on housing volume rather than any structural breakdown in buyer intent. June payrolls came in at roughly 159K net new jobs, and the unemployment rate dropped a tenth to 4.2%, beating expectations of 4.4% — the labor market is slowing but not breaking. What bears watching underneath the headline is the long-term unemployed cohort: workers unemployed 27 weeks or more now number 1.94 million, up 17.3% year-over-year even as that count pulled back slightly in June. This group does not affect the current selling season, but its accumulation is the early signal for delinquency pressure 6-12 months out. On inflation, May Core PCE came in at +3.41% year-over-year, and Core CPI at +2.82% — both cooler than expected, which is the relevant development for rate markets. Shelter CPI continues to run at +3.35% YoY, still elevated, but the gap between observed rent indices and shelter CPI has been closing; as new leases flow through the BLS calculation, shelter's drag on core inflation should ease further. That combination — softening labor on the margin, inflation decelerating faster than feared — is what pushed the 10-year off its recent high and allowed the mortgage-Treasury spread to compress 30 basis points year-over-year. Regionally, the Case-Shiller April data (reflecting transactions closed in roughly February-April) shows the Midwest and Northeast still printing positive price appreciation — Chicago, Detroit, and Boston continue to hold — while Dallas remains the clearest soft spot at -1.65% YoY for 13 consecutive negative months. The Sun Belt correction is not spreading; it remains concentrated in the markets that overbuilt during 2020-2022. The one signal to watch for in August: whether the Xactus MII 4-week average, which turned up this past week after a multi-week slide, confirms a recovery in purchase intent heading into the fall season, or whether that uptick fades — that reading will tell you more about the back half of 2026 than almost anything else in the data.
