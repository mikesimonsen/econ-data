# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The four weeks ending July 7 present a market that is holding better than the rate environment suggests it should. Altos new pending sales pulled back to -2.99% YoY on the most recent weekly read — breaking a streak that had run 55 consecutive weeks positive — but the 4-week average remains +4.37% YoY and the 13-week average is still running at +4.81% YoY, positive for 55 straight weeks and still rising. That divergence between a soft spot week and a resilient trend is the right frame here: seasonal patterns around the Fourth of July week routinely knock single-week pending counts, and the smoothed trend has not broken. Active inventory on Altos reached 1.07 million, up 1.2% on the week and 1.2% YoY — growth that is decelerating rather than accelerating. New listings ticked down 7.1% YoY on the latest week, though the 4-week average holds near flat (+0.4% YoY) and the 13-week average has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks at +0.7% YoY, so supply is not compressing. Pending days on market held at 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — still elevated, meaning the typical home is taking longer to go under contract than a year ago, which is consistent with buyers having modest leverage. NAR existing home sales for May came in at a 4.17M SAAR, up 3.2% YoY and well ahead of the 3.85M consensus — the cleanest upside beat in several months. Note that the NSA read was flat YoY, which is the more honest comparison given seasonal adjustment volatility; the beat is real but the magnitude deserves some humility. New home sales fell to a 580K SAAR in May, down 7.5% YoY, and months supply of new homes climbed to 10.3 — builders are sitting on elevated unsold inventory even as construction spending keeps rising. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.63% this morning, up 4 basis points on the week after a brief decline, while the 10-year Treasury held near 4.49%. The spread between the two is 2.14 percentage points — still roughly 45 basis points above the long-run norm of about 1.7 points. That gap remains the most underappreciated affordability lever: if lenders and markets normalize that spread even partially, the 30-year could drift toward 6.1–6.2% without the Fed moving at all. MBA purchase applications came in at +3.1% YoY on the 4-week average, but the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA by 2–4 weeks — showed its 13-week average falling for 7 straight weeks and sitting at -2.4% YoY. The spot MII did bounce +3.5% in the latest week, and the 4-week average reversed higher, so watch whether that single-week uptick in intent becomes a trend. The key signals to track over the next four weeks: whether the Altos 4-week pending average holds above +3% YoY through the post-holiday reset, whether MII sustains its bounce or resumes its slide, and whether new listings recover from last week's drop — because a continued pullback in seller supply is what would tighten the market heading into fall.

June's labor data was the macro story of the month, and it reads as modestly constructive for housing. Payrolls added approximately 159K jobs, the unemployment rate fell to 4.2% against a 4.4% consensus expectation, and average hourly earnings rose 3.5% YoY — that earnings growth, running above the current home price appreciation pace in most markets, slowly rebuilds affordability on the income side. The mechanism is direct: a labor market that holds together keeps the purchase-eligible population stable and limits distressed-sale pressure. However, the 27-weeks-or-longer unemployed cohort stands at 1.937 million, up 17.3% YoY even as it ticked down slightly in June. That cohort does not threaten the current selling season — these households are not yet in mortgage distress — but if it continues to accumulate, it will shape delinquency volume in 6–12 months. On inflation, core PCE ran at 3.4% YoY in May, and core CPI printed 2.8% YoY — both cooler than consensus expected, which is why the 10-year has stayed relatively contained near 4.5% and not pressed toward 5%. Shelter CPI continues to decelerate slowly at 3.35% YoY, a lagged reflection of rent softening that is now apparent in the Zillow ZORI (up 2.95% YoY) — the two series are converging, which removes one source of upward stickiness in measured inflation.
