# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% YoY and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks — a durable demand signal that the weekly noise tends to obscure. The most recent spot week came in at -2.99% YoY and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37%, so momentum is softening at the edges without reversing the underlying trend. Watch whether the 4-week average holds above zero through August, when seasonal withdrawal by sellers typically meets the back-to-school lull in buyer urgency. Inventory at Altos reached roughly 1.075 million units, up about 1.2% YoY — growth that has been rising for eight consecutive weeks but remains contained. New listings ticked down on the spot week (-7.1% YoY) while the 4-week average held barely positive (+0.41% YoY) and the 13-week average has now risen for 14 consecutive weeks; supply is accumulating slowly, not flooding in. Pending days on market at 70.9 days is running about 6.7% above last year, confirming that homes are moving, but buyers are taking a little more time. NAR June existing home sales printed 4.09 million SAAR, beating the consensus of 3.85 million and up 4.1% YoY on an accelerating trend — the NSA read of 422,000 was +7.9% YoY, the cleanest apples-to-apples comparison. New home sales (628,000 SAAR) edged back up after several down months but remain 5.1% below year-ago levels, and the average new-home price dropped sharply, suggesting builders are actively mixing down toward entry-level product to move inventory. The 30-year fixed rate closed July at 6.77%, essentially flat for the month. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at roughly 2.11 percentage points, it has narrowed about 26 basis points YoY but remains well above the historical norm of around 1.7 points. That gap represents the compression runway — if lender risk premiums normalize even partially, rates can fall meaningfully without any Fed action. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is -1.35% YoY and has fallen for four consecutive weeks, which warrants attention. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — a credit-inquiry-based measure that typically leads MBA purchase applications by two to four weeks — ticked up in late June after a multi-week decline, with its 4-week average reversing higher, so the MBA softness may not deepen further. That divergence is worth tracking through the first two weeks of August.

June's macro data was cleaner than the consensus expected on nearly every dimension that matters to housing. Headline CPI fell 0.42% month-over-month against an expectation of +0.9%, and core CPI was essentially flat (-0.02%) versus an expected +0.3%; core PCE for June came in at +0.13% month-over-month, also below the +0.3% consensus, leaving the 12-month core PCE at 3.29%. Inflation is not yet at the Fed's target, but the trajectory — three consecutive softer-than-expected prints — shifts the probabilities on rate timing. The 10-year Treasury yield ticked back up to 4.66% at month-end, which is the ceiling variable: if the 10-year holds here, spread compression alone can carry the 30-year lower, but if the 10-year firms further, that runway narrows. The labor market came in stronger than feared: the June unemployment rate fell to 4.2% (consensus was 4.4%), initial claims for the week of July 25 printed 197,000 against an expected 212,000, and average hourly earnings rose 3.52% YoY — enough wage growth to sustain purchasing power without reigniting inflation. The one signal to track quietly: the long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) is 17.3% above year-ago levels. It ticked down in June and the median unemployment duration also fell, which is encouraging, but the YoY accumulation matters for delinquency pipelines 6–12 months out, not for this selling season. On price geography, the May Case-Shiller data (released this week) shows Chicago at +6.9% YoY, New York at +4.0%, Boston at +2.6%, and Cleveland at +3.1% — Midwest and Northeast markets continuing to appreciate while Dallas (-1.0%), Denver (-1.7%), Las Vegas (-1.9%), Phoenix (-1.3%), Seattle (-1.8%), and Tampa (-1.8%) remain in negative YoY territory. That two-speed dynamic is now 12-plus months old and is the correct frame for any price conversation with a seller or buyer depending on their geography. The signal to watch in August: whether the Xactus MII recovery translates into a stabilizing MBA purchase index, and whether the 10-year yield holds below 4.75% — those two together will determine whether September shapes up as a real fall market or a quiet one.
