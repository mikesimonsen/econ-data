# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales slipped to -3.0% YoY in the week ending June 26, snapping a 55-week streak of positive readings on a spot basis — but the 4-week average held at +4.4% YoY and the 13-week average remained at +4.8% YoY, both still constructive. One soft week does not rewrite the trend; the streak on the 13-week average itself extends to 55 weeks, and that is the number that matters for seasonal comparisons at listing appointments. Inventory continued its measured climb: Altos total supply was up 1.2% YoY with 8 consecutive weeks of gains, but new listings decelerated sharply — the spot read fell 7.1% YoY, pulling the 4-week average to essentially flat (+0.4%). The slowdown in fresh supply, if it persists, is a constraint on transaction volume more than a price support in this environment, given that pending days on market held at 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are not moving faster. NAR's June existing sales print landed at 4.09M SAAR, 4.1% above a year ago and well ahead of the 3.85M consensus, though the month-over-month read ticked down 1.9% from May's revised pace. On an NSA basis, June came in at 422,000 units, up 7.9% YoY — the more reliable directional read given seasonal volatility in the SA series. New home sales tell a different story: 628K SAAR in June, down 5.1% YoY despite a month-over-month uptick, with the average new-home price falling 9.5% in a single month — an unusually large move that likely reflects builders shifting mix toward entry-level product to clear 9.3 months of supply. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.81% as of July 24, essentially flat year-over-year, with a recent downtick after a brief rise. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, currently 2.11 percentage points — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 pp, but down 27 basis points year-over-year. If that spread compresses another 20-25 bp toward the high end of normal, the 30-year rate falls meaningfully without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications edged up 5.5% week-over-week in the July 17 reading but the 4-week average remains -1.2% YoY after three consecutive weekly declines, and the 13-week average just turned lower. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA applications by 2-4 weeks — bounced off its recent trough with a 3.5% weekly gain and the 4-week average also reversed higher, suggesting the MBA weakness may be near its floor. Watch for that to confirm in the next two MBA prints. Housing starts delivered a statistically unusual 19% monthly jump to 1,427K SAAR in June, beating the 1,350K consensus, but the headline was almost entirely driven by multifamily — 5+ unit starts rose 76% in the month while single-family starts were flat, down 3.2% YoY for a third consecutive month. Building permits told the opposite story: total permits missed at 1,374K, and South permits fell 8.7% YoY — the region where the most new supply pressure has been concentrated. Single-family permits did turn positive YoY at the national level for the first time since early last year, which is a supply signal worth watching through year-end.

The macro picture shifted more than the housing headlines captured. June CPI printed at -0.4% month-over-month against a consensus of +0.9% — a large miss — pulling core CPI to essentially flat on the month and 2.57% YoY. That matters for housing through two channels: it relieves upward pressure on the 10-year Treasury (now at 4.64%, down from recent highs), and it changes the calculus around how long the spread-to-mortgage-rate remains elevated. Shelter CPI, the component most directly tied to housing costs in the index, rose 3.3% YoY, continuing its slow deceleration from the 2023-24 peak — still above headline core but no longer driving it. Core PCE as of May stood at 3.41% YoY, above the Fed's 2% target, which is why rate cuts remain conditional rather than imminent. The June labor market report was meaningfully better than expected: the unemployment rate fell to 4.2% against a 4.4% consensus, payrolls rose 159K, and initial jobless claims for the week of July 18 came in at 187K against a 212K expectation. Strong enough employment keeps purchase demand from deteriorating further. The one labor signal that warrants ongoing attention is the long-term unemployed cohort — workers unemployed 27 weeks or more. At 1.937 million, it fell slightly in June but remains 17.3% above year-ago levels. This group does not affect the current selling season; its relevance is 6-12 months out
