# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales closed June with a week that looked soft on the surface — down about 3% year-over-year for the week of June 26 — but the four-week average held at +4.4% YoY, and the 13-week average has now been positive for 55 consecutive weeks, running at +4.8% YoY. That streak is the more honest read on underlying demand: buyers have been showing up consistently all year, even as individual weekly reads bounce around with the calendar. Altos total inventory reached 1.07 million the week of June 26, up about 1.2% year-over-year — still growing, but the growth rate is decelerating. New listings told the same story: the single-week reading turned negative YoY (-7%), though the four-week average is barely positive (+0.4%) and the 13-week average has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks at +0.7% YoY. Supply growth is not disappearing, but it is losing momentum heading into late summer, which is seasonally normal. Pending days on market ticked up to 70.9, now running 6.7% above year-ago levels for 103 consecutive weeks — homes are still taking longer to go under contract than they did in 2024-25. NAR existing home sales for June came in at a 4.09 million SAAR, beating consensus of 3.85 million by a meaningful margin and running +4.1% YoY on a seasonally adjusted basis — though the NSA read of 422,000 units closed, up 7.9% YoY, is the cleanest direction indicator here. New home sales were a different story: 628,000 SAAR in June, up slightly month-over-month but still -5.1% YoY, with the average new home price falling nearly 9.5% in a single month — an unusual move roughly two standard deviations below normal — as builders continue to compete on price and incentives to clear standing inventory. Months supply for new homes ticked down to 9.3 but remains elevated. The 30-year fixed mortgage rate sits at 6.76% today, essentially flat over the past month and down only five basis points year-over-year. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, which has tightened to 2.13 percentage points — down 26 basis points from a year ago, though still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap is where the hidden affordability story lives: if lenders continue normalizing their risk premium, rates can fall meaningfully without any move from the Fed. On leading indicators, the MBA purchase index for the week of July 17 ticked back to +0.4% YoY after running negative — but the four-week average remains -1.2% YoY and the 13-week average, while still positive at +4.2% YoY, turned down for the second consecutive week. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which captures credit-pull activity two to four weeks before it shows up in MBA data — reversed higher as of June 26, with its four-week average at -2.3% YoY but improving. The MII and MBA are both slightly negative or flat on a YoY basis in the four-week averages right now; if the MII's uptick is real, MBA should follow in August. Watch whether new listings can hold their mild YoY gains through July — if supply stalls further while the demand streak holds, pricing pressure in the fall will build faster than the headline inventory numbers suggest.

The macro backdrop this month is more constructive for housing than the headlines have conveyed. June payrolls printed at 159,000 — a clean beat against a 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, again well below the 4.4% expected. Initial jobless claims for the week of July 18 came in at 187,000, roughly 25,000 below consensus, and are now down nearly 14% year-over-year. For housing, this matters directly: employed, confident buyers show up. The labor market softening story has not arrived yet, and the lock-in effect loosens at the margin every time a worker changes jobs voluntarily. The quits rate at 1.9% YoY is still below pre-pandemic norms, which means geographic mobility — the historical driver of relocation-driven sales — remains suppressed. The June CPI print was the clearest positive surprise of the month: headline fell 0.4% month-over-month against an expectation of +0.9%, and core came in essentially flat against a +0.3% expectation. Shelter CPI, however, continued its slow grind higher at +0.12% for the month and +3.3% YoY — a reminder that the official inflation measure still captures lagged lease renewals, not the current rental market. Core PCE for May ran at +3.4% YoY, still above target, which is why the 10-year Treasury remains at 4.63% and the Fed has not moved.
