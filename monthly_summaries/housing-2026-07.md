# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales pulled back in the most recent Altos weekly read — down 3.0% year-over-year for the week ending June 26 — but the framing that matters is the 13-week average, which held at +4.8% YoY and has now run positive for 55 consecutive weeks. The four-week average stood at +4.4% YoY, though it decelerated from the prior read. That gap between the noisy weekly print and the durable trend is the signal to hold onto. Inventory continued rising — total active supply was up 1.2% YoY at roughly 1.07 million units — and the new listings four-week average turned back positive at +0.4% YoY after a brief dip, while the 13-week average held a 14-week rising streak at +0.7% YoY. New listings are not accelerating, but they are no longer retreating. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9, up 6.7% YoY — homes are taking longer to go under contract than a year ago, a consistent pattern now 103 weeks running. On closings, NAR's June existing home sales came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and running +4.1% YoY on an NSA basis — +7.9% YoY in raw count terms. The beat was real; it was not a revision artifact. New home sales told a quieter story at 628,000 SAAR in June, technically a reversal upward but still down 5.1% YoY, and builder pricing continued to give: new home median fell to $398,300, off 2.7% YoY. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.80% today, essentially flat over the month. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.16 percentage points — well above the historical norm near 1.7 points. That gap is the affordability story hiding in plain sight: the 10-year closed today at 4.64%, and if lenders allow spread compression back toward 1.9-2.0 points, mortgage rates fall meaningfully without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications recovered week-over-week to 165.8 but the four-week average is down 1.2% YoY and the 13-week average has begun rolling over after 75 weeks of positive YoY readings. More important: the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — a credit-inquiry-based leading indicator that tends to precede MBA by two to four weeks — turned up in the most recent read, with the four-week average at +2.3% off its recent low, though still -2.3% YoY. Watch whether the MII sustains that reversal over the next two weeks; if it does, the MBA softness is likely a seasonal air pocket rather than a demand break. On supply, June single-family permits came in at 872,000 SAAR — positive YoY for the first time in several months — but the multi-family permit pipeline is contracting, down 5.9% YoY for 5+ units. Single-family completions rose to 964,000 SAAR, +4.8% YoY, meaning finished homes are still arriving into the market even as the forward pipeline thins.

The macro picture is doing more to support housing than the headlines suggested this month. June payrolls printed at roughly 159,000 — modest but positive — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, beating expectations of 4.4%. That is not an economy shedding jobs; it is an economy adding them slowly. Average hourly earnings are running +3.5% YoY, which at current home prices is tight, but positive real wage growth is better than the alternative. The inflation read was the month's real surprise: headline CPI fell 0.4% month-over-month in June (against an expected +0.9%), and core CPI was essentially flat at -0.02%. Both prints were substantially cooler than consensus. The mechanism for housing is direct: softer inflation data shifts rate expectations, which moves the 10-year yield, which sets the floor for mortgage rates. The 10-year has fallen to 4.64% off recent highs, and if the June CPI trend persists into the July read — which lands in August — expect another leg down in Treasury yields. Shelter CPI continues to run +3.3% YoY, which lags actual market rents by roughly 12-18 months; as that catches down toward the Zillow ZORI's current +3.3% read, it will remove an upward floor from core inflation and give the Fed more room. Core PCE for May was still at +3.4% YoY, above the Fed's 2% target — that is the number keeping the Fed on hold, not the labor data.
