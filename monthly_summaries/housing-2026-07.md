**July 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held firmer than the headlines suggested through the four weeks ending June 26. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sat at +4.81% year over year — the 55th consecutive week in positive territory — even as the most recent weekly print slipped to -2.99% YoY, a normal late-June seasonal dip that inflated the week-over-week story. The 4-week average remained a more reliable read at +4.37% YoY, though that figure is decelerating from the mid-single-digit pace of early spring. Inventory continued its measured climb: total active supply reached roughly 1.07 million units, up 1.2% YoY, with the Altos 13-week new listings average holding a +0.71% YoY gain after 14 consecutive weeks of rising — though the most recent weekly new listings print turned negative (-7.1% YoY), a signal worth watching to see if seller hesitance is returning or simply seasonal. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY over 103 consecutive weeks — homes are taking longer to sell than a year ago across most of the country. The June NAR existing sales print at 4.09 million SAAR beat expectations of 3.85 million by a meaningful margin, up 4.1% YoY on the seasonally adjusted read and +7.9% YoY on the unadjusted count — the cleanest number to use for direction. New home sales, by contrast, fell to 580,000 SAAR in May, down 7.5% YoY, with new supply at 10.3 months — builders are sitting on finished inventory and pulling back starts sharply (single-family starts -7.1% YoY in May, a significant miss vs. consensus). The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.64% as of July 10, little changed over the month, but the more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.09-2.13 points depending on the averaging period, it remains roughly 40 basis points above the historical norm of ~1.70 points. That spread has been compressing — down about 30-35 basis points YoY — and if it continues toward normal, the 30-year can fall to roughly the low-6% range without the Fed moving at all. On leading indicators: the MBA purchase index 4-week average is essentially flat YoY (+0.50%), a notable deceleration from the 5%+ pace of the 13-week average, and the most recent weekly print turned negative (-6.3% YoY) on what appears to be a holiday-week distortion around July 4th. More telling is the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — a credit-inquiry-based measure that leads MBA by roughly two to four weeks — which reversed off recent lows, with its 4-week average ticking up to +2.31% period-over-period after consecutive weeks of decline. That divergence, MII stabilizing while MBA softens, suggests the MBA weakness may be noise rather than the start of a demand rollover. Watch the next two MBA readings to confirm.

The macro backdrop shifted modestly in housing's favor this month on two counts and remained a headwind on one. June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 jobs — not robust, but above a threshold that keeps labor income growing — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, beating the 4.4% consensus. A tighter labor market than expected supports mortgage qualification and reduces the probability of forced selling, both near-term positives. Average hourly earnings are running +3.52% YoY, which exceeds house price appreciation in most Sun Belt markets and keeps affordability from worsening further on the income side. The countervailing pressure is inflation: Core PCE held at +3.41% YoY in May, well above the Fed's 2% target, and headline CPI came in at +4.17% YoY in May — cooler than expected on the month but still elevated enough that rate cuts remain remote. The 10-year Treasury sits at 4.57% today, and with Core PCE sticky, the path to meaningfully lower mortgage rates runs through spread compression, not Fed action. The one data point to monitor carefully is the long-term unemployed cohort: those out of work 27 or more weeks fell in June to 1.937 million, a relief, but the YoY figure is still +17.3%. This group does not affect the current selling season, but it is accumulating at a pace that will show up in delinquency data in two to three quarters if hiring does not absorb them. On price geography, the April Case-Shiller data (the most recent available, referencing a two-month lag) confirms the two-speed market is intact: Chicago +6.4% YoY, New York +3.7%, Cleveland +3.2% — Midwest and Northeast continuing to appreciate — while Dallas is -1.7%, Denver -1.8%, Phoenix -1.8%, Seattle -2.2%, and Las Vegas -1.7% YoY, with Las Vegas posting its lowest reading since September 2023
