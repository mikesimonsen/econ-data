# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales held at +4.81% YoY for the week ending June 26, extending a positive streak to 55 consecutive weeks — the clearest read on underlying demand in the current environment. The spot week came in at -2.99% YoY, which sounds alarming until you note that the 4-week average remains solidly positive at +4.37% YoY; the spot dip reflects normal late-June seasonality, not a demand reversal. Inventory on the Altos measure rose to 1.07 million active listings, up 1.17% YoY — still growing but decelerating meaningfully from the double-digit YoY gains of a year ago. New listings told a similar story: the spot week fell 7.1% YoY, though the 4-week average is essentially flat (+0.41% YoY) and the 13-week average is nudging higher (+0.71% YoY), suggesting sellers are entering the market at roughly the same pace as last year, not accelerating. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are taking longer to go under contract than they were a year ago, which is worth mentioning at a listing appointment even if it hasn't moved dramatically this month. NAR's May existing home sales printed at 4.17 million SAAR, up 3.2% YoY and well ahead of the 3.85 million consensus — the cleanest beat in several months. The NSA YoY comparison was flat at 0.0%, which is the more honest read given last May's base; the SAAR beat owes something to seasonal adjustment, but the direction is right. New home sales told the opposite story: May new single-family sales fell to 580,000 SAAR, down 7.5% YoY, and months supply for new homes climbed to 10.3 — an elevated level that reflects builders sitting on completed inventory they cannot move at current prices. The divergence between existing and new home sales is worth tracking: it partly reflects builder incentive fatigue, and partly that existing inventory in some markets is finally competitive on price. The 30-year fixed closed June at 6.54%, essentially unchanged on the month after a brief dip that reversed late in the period. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, which sat at 2.15 percentage points as of June 30 — roughly 45 basis points above the long-run norm of ~1.70pp. That gap is the hidden affordability lever: if it compresses toward historical norms as credit markets stabilize, the 30-year could fall to the low-to-mid 6s without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications for the week of June 26 ticked up 0.5%, with the 4-week average at +3.1% YoY. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA by roughly 2-4 weeks by capturing credit-pull activity before consumers formally apply — reversed off recent lows to 123.3, with its 4-week average rising to 127.5. The MII's 13-week average is still falling (-2.38% YoY) and has been for seven weeks, which means the spot-week bounce is encouraging but not yet a trend change. Watch whether the MII 4-week average holds its upward reversal over the next two weeks; if it does, the MBA purchase data should firm in mid-July and point toward a decent August closing volume.

The macro backdrop has two competing forces pulling on affordability simultaneously. On the labor side, May payrolls came in at +172,000 — nearly three times the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.3%, beating the 4.4% expected. That payroll number argues against imminent demand destruction. What it obscures is the deterioration inside the labor market: the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose 8.5% in May alone and are now up 35.8% year-over-year, at 1.988 million. The quits rate sits at 1.9%, down 0.2pp YoY — workers are less confident about voluntary job changes, which compresses household formation and delays move-up purchases. Neither of these signals affects the current selling season, but the accumulation of long-term unemployed is the future distressed-borrower story; watch that number monthly. On inflation, May Core PCE came in at +3.41% YoY and +0.32% month-over-month — still running above the Fed's target in a way that keeps rate cuts off the table for now.
