# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held in positive year-over-year territory for 55 consecutive weeks on the 13-week average, which remains the most reliable signal of underlying demand — but that streak is showing early stress at the edges. The spot week for June 26 printed -3.0% YoY, snapping a positive run, and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.4% YoY from stronger readings earlier in the spring. The 13-week average itself is still rising, now at +4.8% YoY, so the trend has not broken — but the directional shift in the spot read warrants watching. Altos inventory sat at roughly 1.075 million active listings as of late June, up about 1.2% YoY, with new listings on the 4-week average essentially flat YoY (+0.4%) after a brief positive stretch; the single-week read slipped to -7.1% YoY, suggesting sellers may be pulling back into the summer. Pending days on market extended to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — the 103rd consecutive week of YoY expansion, meaning homes are taking longer to go under contract by every comparable measure. NAR's June existing home sales printed at 4.09 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and running +4.1% YoY on a seasonally adjusted basis; the NSA read was +7.9% YoY, a cleaner comparison given seasonal distortions. New home sales came in at 628,000 SAAR for June, still running -5.1% YoY, and new home average prices fell sharply — down 9.5% month-over-month and 6.6% YoY — which reflects builders actively cutting effective prices to move inventory that stands at 9.3 months of supply. The 30-year fixed ended July at 6.78%, ticking up two basis points in the final days after declining through most of the month. The spread to the 10-year Treasury sits at 2.16 points — still roughly 45 basis points above the historical norm of about 1.70 points — meaning a meaningful portion of today's rate is premium, not Fed policy. The 10-year itself firmed to 4.66% on July 30. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is running -1.4% YoY and has fallen for four consecutive weeks, a softening that conflicts with the still-positive Altos 13-week average. More telling: the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which measures credit-pull activity and typically leads MBA applications by two to four weeks — saw its 4-week average turn upward after a sustained decline, and the 13-week average has been falling for seven weeks with YoY now at -2.4%. That divergence between a spot MII uptick and a weakening 13-week trend suggests the next four weeks of purchase applications will be noisy. Watch whether the MBA 4-week average stabilizes or continues lower as the MII's trailing signal works through; that will determine whether the June NAR beat was a genuine spring carryover or a one-month outlier.

The macro picture for July was shaped by three data points that matter more than the rest. June payrolls printed around 159,000 — modest, not alarming — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, beating a 4.4% consensus, which removes one source of rate-cut urgency for the Fed. The more consequential number was June CPI: headline fell 0.4% month-over-month against an expectation of +0.9%, and core came in essentially flat (-0.02%) against a +0.3% consensus — the largest combined miss in recent memory. That read directly compresses the inflation risk premium embedded in the 10-year yield, and if it's sustained rather than a one-month anomaly, it is the mechanism by which mortgage rates could fall without any Fed action: a spread that is already 45 basis points wide has room to compress further if Treasury yields respond to cooling inflation, and even a 20-25 basis point compression in the spread alone would bring the 30-year to roughly 6.50-6.55% at current Treasury levels. Core PCE for May was still at 3.4% YoY, however, so the Fed will want corroboration. One number worth flagging for its longer horizon: the long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 weeks or more — rose 17.3% YoY in June even as the headline rate improved. That cohort is not selling homes today, but it is the population most likely to experience mortgage stress in 2027 if their unemployment extends. On regional prices, the May Case-Shiller data (the most recent available, lagged roughly two months) makes the geographic split plain: Chicago +6.9% YoY, New York +4.0%, Boston +2.6%, Cleveland +3.1% — all Midwest and Northeast markets holding or accelerating. Meanwhile Dallas is -1.0% YoY for the 14th consecutive month, Denver -1.7% for the 13th, Phoenix -1.3% for the 12th, Tampa -1.8% for the 19th, and Las Vegas -1.
