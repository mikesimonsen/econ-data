# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive over the trailing four weeks even as the most recent weekly reading slipped. The Altos 4-week average for new pending sales came in at roughly 89,200, up 4.4% year-over-year, and the 13-week average has now logged 55 consecutive weeks of positive YoY comparisons — a streak long enough to call this a genuine, sustained recovery in transaction volume rather than a seasonal quirk. The spot week of June 26 did turn negative (-3.0% YoY), worth watching but not yet alarming given the Fourth of July timing effect that typically distorts late-June and early-July readings. Inventory on the Altos panel reached 1.07 million active listings, up 1.2% YoY, but new listings decelerated sharply — the 4-week average is barely positive at +0.4% YoY after the single-week reading dropped to -7.1% YoY, suggesting the spring listing pulse is fading on schedule. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9, up 6.7% YoY and running positive for 103 consecutive weeks — properties are simply taking longer to clear than they did in the rate-suppressed years, which is the honest price-discovery environment most buyers now face. NAR reported June existing home sales at 4.09 million SAAR, well ahead of the 3.85 million consensus, though the month-over-month read dipped 1.9% from May's revised level; on an NSA basis, June closings ran 7.9% above year-ago, reflecting real improvement in signed-contract volume from April and May. New home sales diverged materially — the May print of 580,000 SAAR fell 7.5% YoY, and months supply of new homes jumped to 10.3, signaling builders are sitting on finished inventory they cannot move at current prices. Single-family starts also fell to 882,000 SAAR in May, down 7.1% YoY, while single-family permits have now been negative YoY for 24 consecutive months — builders are clearing the existing pipeline but not committing to future supply, a dynamic that tightens the resale-versus-new competition for buyers over a 12-to-18-month horizon. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.64% today, essentially flat over the past month, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.09 percentage points — still roughly 40 basis points above the long-run norm of about 1.7 points. That gap matters: if credit and volatility conditions normalize and the spread compresses toward historical levels, the 30-year rate falls toward the low-to-mid 6% range without any Fed action at all. MBA purchase applications printed at -6.3% YoY for the week ending July 3 — the 4-week average held at +0.5% YoY, but the trend is clearly decelerating. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks, ticked back up to 123.3 on June 26 after several weeks of softening, with its 4-week average recovering to 127.5; the 13-week average, however, has fallen for seven straight weeks. When MII and MBA diverge as they are now — MII firming at the weekly level while the 13-week trend fades — the more reliable signal for the coming month is the 13-week direction. Watch whether the MII 4-week average holds its recovery through mid-July; if it stalls again, expect MBA purchase applications to soften further heading into the August data window.

The June payroll report was the cleanest macro positive of the month: 159,000 jobs added, unemployment at 4.2% versus a 4.4% consensus, and average hourly earnings up 3.5% YoY — enough income growth to keep qualified buyers at the table even at current rates. The mechanism is direct: wage growth above 3% running alongside mortgage rates in the mid-6s keeps the monthly payment-to-income ratio from deteriorating further, which is what has allowed the 55-week pending streak to persist. The labor market caveat is the long-term unemployed cohort — workers unemployed 27 weeks or more reached nearly 1.94 million in June, up 17.3% YoY. That number does not affect this selling season, but it is the leading indicator for future mortgage delinquency and potential distressed-seller inventory in 2027 and beyond; agents should track it monthly as a slow-moving early warning. On inflation, May Core PCE ran at 3.41% YoY, still well above the Fed's target, which removes rate-cut urgency and keeps the 10-year Treasury anchored near 4.55%. The more constructive signal is that the 30-10 spread has compressed 35 basis points YoY — that is the hidden affordability improvement that doesn't show up in headline rate stories.
