# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The most important housing story this month is hiding in the internals rather than the headline. NAR existing home sales came in at 4.09 million SAAR for June — well above the 3.85 million consensus and up 4.1% year over year, the fastest YoY pace in over a year — but the beat is partly a function of a strong seasonal pattern: on an NSA basis, June closings ran nearly 8% above a year ago. Read that alongside Altos weekly demand, and a more nuanced picture forms. The Altos 13-week pending average has now run positive YoY for 55 consecutive weeks, and the 4-week average sits at +4.4% YoY — solid, sustained demand. The problem is at the margin: the spot week of June 26 printed -3.0% YoY, the third consecutive weekly softening that tipped the single-week read negative for the first time in that streak. Pending days on market at 70.9 days are running 6.7% above year-ago levels — contracts are taking longer to close — and that alone doesn't break anything, but it is consistent with a market where buyers are exercising more patience. Inventory at roughly 1.07 million active listings is up just over 1% YoY, growth that has decelerated meaningfully from the double-digit YoY expansion of 2024-25. New listings on the 4-week average are effectively flat YoY (+0.4%), and the spot week turned -7.1% YoY — the 13-week trend is still positive but rising more slowly. Supply is not building the way it was; that keeps prices supported even as velocity softens. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.68% as of July 8, ticking up two basis points over two days after a brief decline. The more actionable number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.09-2.13 points, it remains roughly 40 basis points above the long-run historical norm near 1.7 points. That spread has compressed 30 basis points year over year. If it returns toward historical norms — driven by reduced rate-lock hedging demand or Fed communication — the 30-year could fall to the low-to-mid 6s without any policy move. That is the affordability lever agents should understand. Leading indicators are sending a caution flag: the MBA purchase index 4-week average is +0.5% YoY but has started rolling over, and the single-week read flipped -6.3% YoY. More telling is the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by two to four weeks: the spot MII bounced to +3.5% on the week of June 26, reversing a declining streak, and the 4-week MII average reversed higher as well — but the 13-week average has been falling for seven weeks and sits -2.4% YoY. The MII recovery is encouraging but not yet confirmed by the trend. New home sales fell to 580K SAAR in May, down 7.5% YoY, while months of new-home supply rose to 10.3 — builders are sitting on finished inventory in a way that will pressure their pricing. Single-family starts slipped again in May (-7.1% YoY) and single-family permits have been negative YoY for 24 consecutive months. The supply pipeline for 2027 and beyond is thinner than it looks. Watch the next two Altos pending reads and the July MBA data to see whether the MII bounce translates into stabilized purchase applications.

The macro backdrop is less alarming than the rate environment implies, but it is not uniformly reassuring. June payrolls came in at roughly 159,000 — a solid number relative to the 59,000 consensus implied by some forecasters — and the unemployment rate fell a tenth to 4.2%, beating the 4.4% consensus. That is a meaningful beat: a labor market that falls apart is the fastest path to a housing downturn, and this one held. Average hourly earnings grew 3.5% YoY, which keeps household income above the price appreciation rate in most markets. The mechanism is direct: employed, wage-growing households continue to service mortgages and form new households at roughly a 1.25% annual rate. The concern sits one layer deeper. Long-term unemployed — those out of work 27 weeks or more — reached 1.937 million in June, up 17.3% year over year, even as the headline rate improved. That cohort is not affecting the current selling season; it is the early accumulation of borrowers who will face hardship in 6-12 months if they remain sidelined. Watch that number.
