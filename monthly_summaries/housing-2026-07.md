# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through June but decelerated at the margin. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% year-over-year — now 55 consecutive weeks above year-ago levels — which is the cleanest signal that buyer activity has been running ahead of last year's pace for over a year. The spot week ending June 26 turned negative at -2.99% YoY, and the 4-week average has come in to +4.37%, so the streak is intact but the cushion is thinning. Inventory continues to rebuild slowly: Altos active supply was up 1.17% YoY as of June 26, rising for eight consecutive weeks, while new listings flipped negative in that same week (-7.14% YoY) though the 4-week average remained barely positive at +0.41% and the 13-week average has been rising for 14 weeks. That new-listings deceleration is worth watching — if sellers pull back into summer, the inventory rebuild stalls. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, a sign that homes are absorbing more slowly than a year ago even as volume holds. NAR's June existing home sales print came in at 4.09 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus by a wide margin and running +4.1% YoY on a seasonally adjusted basis; NSA, June sales were up 7.9% over last June. That said, the June SAAR was down from May, so a beat against a low bar is not the same as a trend acceleration. New home sales by contrast fell to 580,000 SAAR in May, down 7.4% YoY, with months of supply at 10.3 — elevated enough that builders are still working off inventory rather than chasing starts. The June housing starts headline of 1,427,000 beat expectations, but nearly all of the gain came from the 5-plus unit category (+76% month-over-month), which is volatile; single-family starts fell for a third consecutive month to 895,000 and are down 3.2% YoY. More telling for future supply: total permits missed at 1,367,000 and turned negative YoY, with South permits down 8.8% YoY — the region that has driven most of the post-pandemic inventory build is pulling back. Single-family permits were essentially flat YoY at +0.6%, so the new-supply pipeline is not growing. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.63% as of today, down about 5 basis points over the past month and 19 basis points below a year ago. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.11 percentage points — historically this spread runs around 1.7 points, so there remains roughly 40 basis points of compression available before the spread reaches its long-run norm; if the 10-year holds near 4.52%, that path takes the 30-year toward 6.20–6.25% without any Fed action. The spread ticked back up this week after several weeks of narrowing, so the compression story has stalled momentarily. MBA purchase applications for the week ending July 10 fell 1.5% YoY, and the 4-week average just turned negative at -0.6% YoY — the first negative reading in 74 weeks on the 13-week average. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, reversed higher in the June 26 reading: the spot came in at +3.5% week-over-week and the 4-week average turned up as well, though both remain negative YoY (-5.6% and -2.3% respectively). The MII uptick suggests MBA applications may stabilize in late July before the negative YoY on the 4-week becomes entrenched. Watch whether the MII holds its recovery in the next two readings — that will tell you whether the MBA weakness is a seasonal wobble or the start of a genuine demand step-down.

The most important macro development of the past four weeks was the June CPI print, which came in well below consensus: headline CPI fell 0.4% month-over-month against an expected +0.9%, and core CPI was essentially flat at -0.02% against an expected +0.3%. Core PCE for May was still running at +3.41% YoY, so the Fed's preferred measure has not yet confirmed the CPI softness, but the June CPI result shifts the probability distribution for rate policy in a direction that is constructive for mortgage rates. The transmission is straightforward: softer inflation data reduces the premium investors demand for holding longer-duration Treasuries, which pushes the 10-year yield down, which pulls the 30-year mortgage rate with it — and any additional compression in the mortgage-Treasury spread amplifies the move. The 10-year has already responded, falling 7 basis points this week to 4.52%. On labor, the June jobs report was cleaner than most expected: payrolls came in at roughly 159,000, the unemployment rate fell to 4.2% against a 4.4% consensus, initial claims this week hit 208,000
