# Monthly Housing Summary — July 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The most important housing number this month isn't the one that made headlines. NAR existing home sales printed at 4.17 million SAAR for May, up 3.2% year-over-year and well ahead of the 3.85 million consensus — the second consecutive monthly gain — but the Altos weekly demand data tells a more nuanced story underneath that strength. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales is running +4.8% year-over-year and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks, a durable streak that confirms the underlying bid is real. The 4-week average sits at +4.4% YoY. The caution: the most recent weekly read flipped to -3.0% YoY, the third consecutive week of softening, which is consistent with normal late-June seasonal deceleration but worth watching to see whether it rebounds in the July 4th period as it typically does. Pending days on market at 70.9 days is running about 6.7% above last year, so the market is not tightening — buyers have more negotiating room than a year ago. On inventory, the Altos total count rose to roughly 1.07 million active listings, up 1.2% YoY, but the pace of YoY growth has decelerated meaningfully from the double-digit gains of 2024-2025. New listings tell the same story: the single-week read turned negative YoY (-7.1%), though the 4-week average held at a slim +0.4% and the 13-week average is up +0.7% — the supply pipeline is not expanding the way it was. New home sales diverged sharply from the existing market, falling to 580,000 SAAR in May, down 7.5% YoY, with new home months of supply climbing to 10.3 — builders are sitting on inventory they cannot move at current rates and prices. Single-family starts fell to 882,000 SAAR, off 7.1% YoY, and single-family permits have been negative YoY for 24 consecutive months, confirming that builders are working down an existing pipeline rather than committing to new one. That pipeline contraction matters for 2027 supply, not this selling season. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.65% as of July 1, ticking up 11 basis points over the past two days. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.19 points today, it remains roughly 50 basis points above the long-run historical norm of around 1.70 points. The 4-week average spread has been compressing — down 32 basis points year-over-year — and that compression is the mechanism that could deliver meaningfully lower mortgage rates without any Fed action. If the spread normalizes another 20-25 basis points, the 30-year falls to roughly 6.35-6.40% on current Treasury levels. MBA purchase applications ended the most recent week at +3.2% YoY with the 4-week average at +3.1% — positive but decelerating from the +5.1% pace of the 13-week average. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, turned up on the week after falling for several weeks, with its 4-week average now rising. That uptick is tentatively constructive for applications into mid-July, though the MII's 13-week average has been falling for seven weeks and sits at -2.4% YoY — so don't overread a single week. Watch whether the MII 4-week average can sustain its reversal as the primary leading indicator for purchase demand heading into August.

The macro backdrop in June was better than the consensus feared on several dimensions, which matters directly for housing. May payrolls came in at +172,000 — nearly three times the 59,000 consensus estimate — and the unemployment rate held at 4.3%, a tenth better than expected. A job market that surprises to the upside reduces mortgage default risk and keeps household formation on track; both support demand. The offset is that core PCE for May printed at +3.4% year-over-year and +0.32% month-over-month, above the Fed's 2% target and above the FactSet consensus of +0.3% MoM — which keeps the Fed on hold and keeps the 10-year yield elevated, currently at 4.46%. Shelter CPI at +3.35% YoY is still running above core goods and services ex-shelter, meaning the official inflation gauges will stay stickier than the private-market rent data (Zillow ZORI at +2.95% YoY) would suggest — that gap reflects the well-known lag in how BLS captures rent, and it will take several more months to close fully. The long-term unemployment number is the data point that deserves more attention than it is getting: those unemployed 27 weeks or longer rose 8.5% in a single month and now sit 35.8% above last year.
