# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through October, though the picture is more complicated than the headline YoY numbers suggest. Altos weekly new pending sales were running +5.85% year-over-year as of early July (the most recent Altos read in the snapshot), with the 4-week average at +3.57% YoY — decelerating but still positive for the 56th consecutive week. The 13-week average, at +5.48% YoY, continues to trend constructively even as the weekly series has been softening on a period-over-period basis. Inventory on the Altos side was up only modestly YoY — essentially flat — while new listings ran +8.46% YoY on the spot week, with the 13-week average at +1.50% YoY and rising for 14 consecutive weeks; sellers are gradually returning, but not fast enough to shift the supply balance materially. Pending days on market at 70.8 days are running +6.21% YoY, a slow drift worth watching. NAR existing home sales for October came in at 3.75 million SAAR, missing the consensus expectation of 3.85 million, down 8.76% YoY — the weakest October print since 2023 — and the West region was the notable drag, falling 14.47% YoY. Months supply ticked to 5.1, up nearly 16% YoY, confirming that inventory is accumulating relative to demand. New home sales (September, the most recent read) fared somewhat better at 676K SAAR, though also down 5.98% YoY; builder new-house inventory has contracted for 11 consecutive months YoY, so the builder channel is not adding a supply problem even as it absorbs rate-sensitive buyers. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.42% today, up roughly 12 basis points over the past three days and 113 basis points year-over-year — the highest level in this cycle's recent range. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.31 percentage points, against a historical norm of roughly 1.7 points; that 60-basis-point excess means the mortgage market is pricing in meaningful risk and uncertainty on top of the underlying Treasury move. If that spread compresses toward its norm — which happened in 2024 when credit conditions normalized — the 30-year rate falls toward 6.7-6.8% without any Fed action. That is the affordability lever most buyers are not watching. The leading indicators are sobering: MBA purchase applications are running -18.51% YoY on the 4-week average, the weakest since early 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks — turned positive YoY on the spot week (at +1.56%) but the 4-week average is -1.59% and the 13-week average has been falling for eight weeks, suggesting the spot-week MII uptick is noise rather than signal. Single-family permits fell 3.86% YoY in September, turning negative for the first time in several months; starts fell 4.56% on the month. Builders are not adding to their pipeline, which matters for 2027 supply even if it does not affect what you are showing this month. Watch whether the MII 4-week average turns positive in November — that would be the first credible sign that early-December pending sales could firm.

The macro context explains why rates are where they are and why they are not coming down soon. October payrolls printed +165,000, well above the +59,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% against a 4.4% expectation. Average hourly earnings came in at +0.4% month-over-month, hotter than the +0.3% forecast. That combination — stronger employment, hotter wages — directly pushed the 10-year Treasury yield to 5.11%, up 11 basis points in just the past five days, which is the mechanical reason mortgage rates are at 7.42% today. The Fed has no near-term easing case; a strong labor market at above-target inflation (Core PCE at +3.11% YoY through September, CPI shelter still running +3.54% YoY) keeps the Fed on hold, and rate futures are repricing accordingly. CPI headline for October was cooler than expected at +0.3% month-over-month, but the YoY rate is 4.08% — the disinflation story has stalled. The regional price divergence in Case-Shiller (through August, the most recent period) remains the most agent-actionable data in the deck: Chicago is up 6.61% YoY, New York up 6.24%, Cleveland up 4.26%, Miami up 4.33% — Midwest and Northeast metro appreciation is real and ongoing. Meanwhile Dallas is -0.68% YoY, Las Vegas -1.06%, Seattle -2.01%, Tampa -0.27% — Sun Belt markets that benefited most from the pandemic-era migration are still working through that correction.
