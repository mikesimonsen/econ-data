**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive year-over-year for the 56th consecutive week by the Altos 13-week pending sales average, though the signal is softening at the edges. The spot weekly read came in at +5.9% YoY as of early July (the most recent Altos pull), while the 4-week average decelerated to +3.6% YoY and the 13-week average — the smoothest read of underlying momentum — sat at +5.5% YoY, still rising but no longer accelerating. Pending days on market ticked to 70.8 days, up 6.2% YoY for the 104th consecutive week, confirming that buyers are taking more time even as they remain active. Inventory on the Altos side stood near 1.09 million active listings, essentially flat YoY (+0.02%), with new listings running +8.5% YoY on the spot week but only +1.2% on the 4-week average — the kind of divergence that tends to mean one noisy week rather than a supply breakout. The NAR's October existing home sales print landed at 3.75 million SAAR, missing the 3.85 million consensus by 100,000 units and printing the weakest reading since October 2023. On a NSA YoY basis, total sales fell 11.7%, with the West the softest region at -15.2% YoY. Months supply rose to 5.1, up 15.9% YoY — still not a buyer's market in most price tiers, but the highest supply reading in several years, and it accelerated in October rather than stalling. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.32% today, up 100 basis points YoY, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.41 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That spread has been widening on the 4-week average for 11 consecutive weeks, which means rates are not falling even when the Treasury yield holds flat. If that spread were at its historical norm, the 30-year would be closer to 6.6%. The leading indicators for December are cautious: the MBA purchase index 4-week average is running -18.6% YoY, the worst reading since January 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks — turned its spot weekly reading negative in early July after a brief positive run, while the 13-week MII average has been falling for eight weeks, suggesting the MBA weakness is not yet bottoming. Single-family permits fell 12.9% YoY in October to their lowest level since April 2023, sharply missing consensus; starts bounced but only because multifamily came back after prior weakness. Watch the spread and the MII over the next four weeks — those are the two levers most likely to move before the December NAR print.

The macro backdrop explains why demand is struggling despite a labor market that, on the surface, still looks constructive. October payrolls came in at +165,000 versus a 59,000 consensus — a genuine beat — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, also better than expected. But underneath those headline numbers, the long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) has risen 8.2% YoY over three consecutive months, and median unemployment duration is up 17.8% YoY. That cohort does not affect the current selling season; it represents the pipeline of future mortgage stress and distressed listings, likely 6-12 months out. More immediately relevant is inflation: headline CPI came in at +4.1% YoY in October, and core PCE — the Fed's preferred measure — is running at +3.1% YoY through September. Shelter CPI held at +3.5% YoY and has risen for seven consecutive months, keeping the Fed from cutting with any conviction. The 10-year Treasury yield is at 4.91% today, up 87 basis points YoY, and the spread environment described above means the mortgage market is amplifying that Treasury move rather than cushioning it. On the regional price picture, the September Case-Shiller data (which reflects closings from roughly July-August) showed Chicago at +6.9% YoY and New York at +5.9% YoY, while Dallas sits at -0.8%, Denver at -1.0%, Seattle at -2.1%, and Tampa at essentially zero after 23 consecutive months of YoY declines. That Midwest/Northeast-versus-Sun-Belt divergence is not narrowing — it is the most durable regional story in the data right now, and it matters for every price conversation you are having with sellers in those markets. The one macro signal to watch for December: the November Core PCE release. If it stays above 3%, the Fed holds and the 10-year likely holds with it, keeping mortgage rates at or above current levels through the spring setup period.
