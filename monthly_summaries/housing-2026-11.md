**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but decelerated meaningfully heading into autumn. The Altos weekly new pending sales read for the week of July 3 came in at 84,611, up 5.85% year-over-year, and the 13-week average has been positive for 56 consecutive weeks — a streak that remains intact but is showing wear. The 4-week average YoY is running at +3.57%, a step down from levels earlier in the cycle, and the 13-week average itself is essentially flat week-over-week. That is not collapse; it is deceleration. Inventory continues to build — Altos total supply was up slightly year-over-year, and new listings ran 8.46% above a year ago on a spot basis, though the 4- and 13-week averages are much tamer at +1.22% and +1.50% respectively, suggesting the inventory growth is real but not accelerating. Pending days on market sit at 70.8 days, up 6.2% year-over-year for 104 consecutive weeks — meaning homes that go under contract are simply taking longer to get there than they did a year ago. NAR existing home sales for September printed at 3.99 million SAAR, edging past the 3.85 million consensus and reversing a string of declines, though the NSA YoY reading is -3.9% — the SAAR beat reflects seasonal adjustment mechanics more than a demand revival. New home sales at 676,000 SAAR are down 6.0% year-over-year, with months supply at 8.4 — builders are digesting existing pipeline rather than commissioning new one. Single-family permits fell 3.9% year-over-year in September after being positive, and starts missed expectations badly (1,221K vs. 1,350K expected). The 30-year fixed is at 7.17% today — up 80 basis points year-over-year — and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is running 2.35-2.36 points, well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That 65-basis-point excess spread is the number agents should internalize: if credit markets normalize even partially, rates fall without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications are the counter-signal to watch — the 4-week average is down 18.0% year-over-year, the weakest read since February 2024, and has fallen for six consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-inquiry-based series that leads MBA by two to four weeks) turned its 4-week average lower in early October after a brief rise, suggesting the MBA weakness is not yet bottoming. The divergence between a still-positive Altos pending streak and deeply negative MBA applications is the central tension in the data right now. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the Xactus MII 4-week average stabilizes — if it firms, MBA purchase apps should follow by mid-December.

The macro backdrop explains most of the rate pressure. September payrolls came in at roughly +29,000 against a +59,000 consensus, and average hourly earnings grew only 0.1% month-over-month — both soft readings that, in isolation, would point toward lower rates. But Core PCE is running at 3.11% year-over-year and has risen for seven consecutive months; the Fed has little room to pivot. Shelter CPI at +3.22% year-over-year continues to hold inflation elevated even as real-time rent measures soften — the Zillow ZORI dropped an unusual 0.82% in September and is up only 1.42% year-over-year. That shelter-CPI lag means printed inflation will stay stickier than the actual rental market warrants for several more months, keeping the Fed on hold and long-end rates from declining cleanly. The labor picture has an important undercurrent: the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose to 1.944 million in September, up 7.1% year-over-year and rising for two consecutive months after a brief pause. This cohort does not threaten the current selling season, but its accumulation over six to twelve months is the early signal for future mortgage stress — worth noting before it shows up in delinquency data. On the regional picture, the Case-Shiller August data (released October 27, so referencing the August price period) confirms the two-speed market is widening: Chicago is up 6.6% year-over-year and New York up 6.2%, while Dallas is -0.7%, Las Vegas -1.1%, Seattle -2.0%, and Tampa -0.3% and still falling after 22 consecutive months of negative year-over-year readings. Agents in Sun Belt markets where supply has expanded should not expect the Northeast/Midwest price dynamic to apply locally. The macro signal to watch next month is October Core PCE — if it comes in above 3.1%, rate-cut expectations will recede further and the spread story becomes secondary to the absolute rate level.
