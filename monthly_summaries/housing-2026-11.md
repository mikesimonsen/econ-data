# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through October but is showing real strain in the forward indicators. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has been in positive YoY territory for 56 consecutive weeks, and the spot reading for the week of July 3 came in at +5.9% YoY — a healthy headline. The 4-week average, however, has decelerated to +3.6% YoY and the trend is flattening. Inventory on the Altos platform stood at roughly 1.09 million active listings, up just 0.02% YoY, meaning the supply build that defined the first half of 2026 has essentially stalled against the same period last year. New listings on the Altos weekly read came in at +8.5% YoY on the spot but the 13-week average is a more modest +1.5% YoY — still rising for 14 consecutive weeks, which matters for seller confidence, but the gap between the spot and the trend suggests some choppiness. NAR's September existing home sales printed at a 3.99 million SAAR, a small beat against the 3.85 million consensus, with a slight uptick month-over-month that breaks a recent run of declines. On a NSA YoY basis, September sales fell 3.9%, consistent with the broader picture of a market transacting below prior-year levels. New home sales by contrast came in at 676,000 SAAR in September, down nearly 6% YoY, with months supply still elevated at 8.4 — builders are carrying more finished product relative to their sales pace than the existing-home market, which explains continued price concessions (new home median down 3.9% YoY). The 30-year fixed sits at 7.17% as of November 5, which is 80 basis points higher than a year ago. The 30Y–10Y Treasury spread is 2.35–2.36 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap is the hidden affordability story: even without the Fed moving, if credit conditions normalize and the spread compresses toward historical levels, the 30-year rate falls into the mid-to-high 6s without any change in the 10-year. Watch that spread. MBA purchase applications are the clearest warning sign right now: the 4-week average is down 18% YoY as of October 30, at its weakest reading since February 2024. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — a credit-inquiry-based measure that tends to lead MBA by 2–4 weeks — showed its 4-week average rolling over after a brief uptick, with the 13-week average down 1.5% YoY and falling for eight consecutive weeks. The divergence between the still-positive Altos pending streak and deeply negative MBA applications is worth sitting with: applications measure rate-sensitive buyers who need financing at today's 7.17%; the Altos pending signal captures whatever is actually going to contract. The two tend to reconcile, and right now MBA is pointing toward where pendings are headed. Single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY in September — flipping negative after months of positive readings — while starts missed expectations badly at 1,221,000 total SAAR versus 1,350,000 expected. Builders are completing their pipeline (single-family completions at 840,000 SAAR) but pulling back on authorizing new supply. That is relevant for 2027 inventory, not the current selling season, but it is worth filing. Watch whether the Altos 13-week pending average cracks below the 56-week positive streak in the next four weeks — that would be the signal that the MBA weakness is showing up in contracts.

October's payroll report was the dominant macro event: 165,000 jobs added, well above the 59,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% from 4.2%. That print does two things for housing simultaneously: it supports buyer confidence and income, which stabilizes demand, and it reduces the probability of near-term Fed cuts, which keeps the 10-year Treasury elevated (4.84% as of November 6, up 2 basis points on the day, reversing a brief softening). Average hourly earnings came in at +0.4% month-over-month, hotter than the +0.3% expected, putting the YoY at +3.1% — meaningful real income growth, but also a data point the Fed will read as reason to stay patient. Core PCE for September was +3.1% YoY and +0.32% month-over-month, still running above target, which reinforces that dynamic. The shelter CPI component was +3.2% YoY in September, still elevated but on a path that most forecasters expect to decelerate as market rents — Zillow's ZORI fell 0.8% month-over-month in September — continue to roll into the lagged CPI calculation over the next two to three months. That matters for housing: if shelter CPI visibly decelerates into year-end, it removes one of the stickier arguments for holding rates higher, potentially allowing the spread between the 30-year and the 10-year to compress.
