# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos weekly demand read through early July showed new pending sales up 5.9% year-over-year on the spot week, with the four-week average running +3.6% and the 13-week average holding at +5.5% — positive for 56 consecutive weeks, a streak that has been one of the quieter success stories of this cycle. Inventory stood just above 1.08 million active listings, essentially flat year-over-year (+0.02%), while new listings were up roughly 8.5% on the spot week and 1.2% on the four-week average — supply growing but not accelerating away from seasonal norms. Pending days on market at 70.8 days ran 6.2% above year-ago levels, a signal that absorption is grinding rather than snapping. The NAR existing home sales print for October came in at 3.75 million SAAR, missing consensus by 100,000 units and falling 8.8% below October 2025 — an unusually large drop that was most pronounced in the West (-14.5% YoY NSA) and South (-10.8% YoY NSA), where affordability and inventory pressure are sharpest; months supply ticked to 5.1, up nearly 16% year-over-year. New home sales for September held at 676,000 SAAR — down 6% year-over-year but far less stressed than the existing market, consistent with builders continuing to buy down rates. The mortgage picture is the harder story this month: the 30-year fixed has firmed to 7.50% as of November 13, up roughly 116 basis points year-over-year, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury sits at 2.33-2.35 points on a four-week average — still nearly 65 basis points above the historical norm of around 1.70 points. The 10-year itself has climbed to 5.15%, so the rate burden right now is coming from both directions: elevated Treasury yields and an elevated spread. If lenders allow even partial spread compression toward 2.0 points without any Treasury move, the 30-year could drift toward 7.15-7.20% — meaningful at the margin. The leading indicators are flashing caution: the MBA purchase index four-week average is running 18.5% below year-ago levels, its weakest read since January 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which typically leads MBA applications by two to four weeks — showed its four-week average rolling over after a brief rise, with the 13-week average down eight straight weeks and negative year-over-year. That divergence suggests the brief uptick in the most recent MBA weekly print is not yet a turn. Single-family permits fell to 846,000 in September, negative year-over-year for the first time after a period of modest growth; combined with starts missing by 129,000 units, builders appear to be working down existing pipeline rather than committing to future supply. Watch whether the Xactus MII stabilizes or continues to erode over the next four weeks — that will tell you whether the MBA demand picture has found a floor before year-end.

The macro backdrop explains the rate and demand picture cleanly. October payrolls came in at +165,000 — nearly triple the consensus estimate of +59,000 — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, also well below the 4.4% expected. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% month-over-month, slightly hotter than consensus. A strong labor market is typically good for housing, and it is supporting the 56-week pending sales streak, but it is also the primary reason the 10-year Treasury is holding above 5.0%: the Fed has little incentive to ease when job growth is running this firmly, and that keeps the rate ceiling elevated. Core PCE held at 3.11% year-over-year through September — running above the Fed's 2.0% target for 31 consecutive months — which further anchors the view that rate relief will be gradual at best. October CPI came in cooler than expected on the headline (0.3% month-over-month vs. a 0.9% consensus), but core ran 2.87% year-over-year and shelter CPI remained at 3.54% — still feeding into inflation readings in a way that keeps the Fed cautious. The long-term unemployed cohort deserves attention: workers unemployed 27 or more weeks reached 1.963 million in October, up 8.2% year-over-year and rising for three consecutive months. That cohort does not affect the current selling season — buyers in the market today are employed — but it represents a building pocket of potential mortgage stress 6-12 months out if it continues to accumulate. On the regional price picture, the August Case-Shiller data (reported in late October) underscores the two-speed market: Chicago is up 6.6% year-over-year, New York up 6.2%, Cleveland up 4.3%, Miami up 4.3%; meanwhile Dallas is -0.7%, Las Vegas -1.1%, Seattle -2.0%, Tampa -0.3%.
