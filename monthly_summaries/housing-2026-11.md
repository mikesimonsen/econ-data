**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but decelerated meaningfully heading into the fall. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sat at +5.5% year-over-year as of early October, sustaining a 56-consecutive-week positive streak — which is the right number to anchor on when a client asks whether buyers have left the market. They haven't. The spot week came in at +5.9% YoY, and the 4-week average at +3.6% YoY, both still green but both fading from summer levels. Inventory continued to build: Altos total active supply was running just above 1.08 million units, up slightly year-over-year, with new listings +8.5% YoY on the spot week (the 13-week average held a more modest +1.5% YoY). Pending days on market ticked up 6.2% YoY — not alarming, but a signal that absorption is slower than a year ago. NAR existing home sales for September printed at 3.99 million SAAR, a modest uptick that beat the 3.85 million consensus by a notable margin and snapped a string of monthly declines; on a true apples-to-apples NSA basis, however, September sales were down 3.9% YoY, so the SAAR beat reflects seasonal adjustment mechanics more than a demand revival. New home sales fell to 676K SAAR in September, off 6% YoY, widening the divergence from existing: builders are still carrying 8.4 months of supply and are pulling back — single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY and total permits turned negative YoY for the first time in several months, while starts missed badly (1,221K vs. 1,350K expected). The rate environment is not helping: the 30-year fixed sat at 7.17% as of November 5, up 80 basis points from a year ago. The mortgage-to-Treasury spread — the gap between the 30-year rate and the 10-year yield, historically around 1.7 percentage points — was 2.35-2.36 points, still roughly 65 basis points above normal. That excess spread is the hidden affordability story: if it compresses toward historical norms, the 30-year could fall materially without any Fed move. It has not compressed; in fact the 4-week average spread has risen for nine consecutive weeks. MBA purchase applications are the number most worth watching right now — the 4-week average was down 18% YoY and at its lowest since February 2024, the 13-week average down nearly 11% YoY. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-inquiry-based leading indicator that tends to run 2-4 weeks ahead of MBA) showed its own 4-week average rolling over after a brief uptick, with the 13-week average negative and still falling. MII is not confirming a turn higher. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the MBA 4-week average stabilizes or continues to erode — that will tell you whether the October rate environment (the 10-year briefly pushed above 4.80%) has choked off spring contract activity before it even started.

The macro picture running into November is complicated in ways that matter directly to housing. September payrolls came in at roughly +29K, well below the +59K consensus — a soft print, but initial jobless claims as of October 31 dropped to 189K against a 212K expectation, suggesting the labor market is not deteriorating rapidly so much as it has downshifted. The more telling labor signal is in JOLTS: the quits rate ticked back up to 2.1% in September, which historically correlates with household confidence in job mobility and, with a lag, with move-up buyer activity. Set against that, the long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) rose 7.1% YoY and the median unemployment duration climbed to 11.5 weeks, up nearly 14% YoY — this is not the story of Q4 2026 sales volume, but it is the beginning of the distressed-borrower pipeline that will shape 2027-2028 if it continues to accumulate. On inflation, Core PCE came in at +3.11% YoY in September, still running above the Fed's 2% target, and CPI shelter remains at +3.2% YoY — shelter inflation is decelerating from its 2023 peaks but has stalled well above where it needs to be, which keeps the Fed's hands tied. The 10-year Treasury at 4.82% reflects that stalemate: markets are not pricing in rate cuts that would pull mortgage rates lower soon. Case-Shiller data through August (the most recent available, released October 27) shows the two-speed market in sharp relief: Chicago +6.6% YoY, New York +6.2%, Cleveland +4.3%, Miami +4.3% — Midwest and Northeast metro appreciation remains robust, the kind of price appreciation your sellers there can legitimately reference.
