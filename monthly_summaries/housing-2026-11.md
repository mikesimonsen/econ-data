# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive year over year for the week ending November 7, with the Altos 4-week average running roughly +3.6% YoY — a deceleration from the mid-single-digit pace that held through most of Q3, and the 13-week average, which had been positive for 56 consecutive weeks, is now trending softer. Inventory on the Realtor.com active count reached 1.18 million in October, up 7.6% YoY and rising for seven consecutive months — supply growth is continuing at a steady pace, not stalling. New listings on Realtor.com fell 7.3% YoY in October, the lowest count since September 2023, which means the inventory build is coming from slower absorption rather than a fresh wave of sellers. Pending days on market have been running about 6% above year-ago levels for over 100 consecutive weeks — not a sudden change, but a persistent signal that buyer urgency has been lower than the prior cycle. The NAR October existing home sales print came in at 3.75 million SAAR, down 8.8% YoY and 100,000 units below the consensus estimate of 3.85 million — the West region led the miss, falling 14.5% YoY on an NSA basis. Months supply hit 5.1, up 15.9% YoY, the highest ratio in roughly three years. New home sales (September, the most recent available) printed at 676,000 SAAR, down 6.0% YoY; builders have been absorbing their existing pipeline — completions rose 6.5% in October — but single-family permits just fell to their lowest level since April 2023, down 12.9% YoY, which is the clearest signal yet that builders are pulling back on future production. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.45% today, essentially unchanged over the past month, and 109 basis points above a year ago. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 240 basis points against a 10-year at 5.05%, the spread remains roughly 70 basis points above its long-run historical norm of about 170 basis points. That spread has been widening, not compressing — the 4-week average has risen for 11 consecutive weeks — which means rates have no technical tailwind even if Treasury yields cooperate. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is running 18.6% below year-ago levels, the lowest since January 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus MII data through early July was already showing the 13-week average declining for eight straight weeks, diverging from what had briefly been a positive YoY spot reading — when MII leads MBA lower like this, the purchase application softness tends to persist 2-4 weeks further. Watch whether the MBA 4-week average stabilizes or continues to erode through the December reporting window; that will tell you whether the October sales weakness was seasonal or something more structural heading into 2027.

The macro picture this month is complicated by a genuine split across indicators. October payrolls came in at +165,000 — well above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1%, beating expectations by 30 basis points. On the surface, this is supportive for housing demand: employed households buy homes. But look one layer deeper and the picture softens. The long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) now number 1.963 million, up 8.2% YoY and rising for three consecutive months. These are households that have largely exhausted savings buffers and are increasingly at risk of mortgage delinquency 6-12 months out — this cohort does not affect your Q4 selling season, but it is quietly building a pipeline of distressed-borrower situations that will begin surfacing in late 2027. On inflation, headline CPI came in at +0.3% for October against an expected +0.9% — a significant undershoot — but Core PCE for September was running at 3.1% YoY, still well above the Fed's 2% target, and shelter CPI continues to run at +3.5% YoY, which keeps the Fed cautious. The 10-year at 5.05% reflects that caution: until core PCE makes visible progress toward 2.5%, the Treasury market is unlikely to give the mortgage market meaningful relief, and with the spread widening rather than compressing, rate relief through the spread mechanism is also off the table for now. Regionally, the Case-Shiller August data (released October 27, so referencing conditions from two months ago) confirms the two-speed market: Chicago +6.6% YoY, New York +6.2%, Cleveland +4.3%, Miami +4.3% — Midwest and Northeast metro appreciation is real and agents in those markets can defend prices at the listing table. Meanwhile Dallas is -0.7% YoY after 17 consecutive months of negative readings, Las Vegas -1.1% for 12 months running, Seattle -2.0% for 13 months, and Tampa -0.3% for 22 months.
