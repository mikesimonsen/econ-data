# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but decelerated entering the fall. Altos weekly new pending sales were running +5.85% year-over-year as of early October, with the four-week average at +3.57% YoY — still positive, but slipping from the mid-summer pace. The 13-week average, which had been trending higher for over a year, registered +5.48% YoY and remained in positive territory for 56 consecutive weeks, the most durable demand streak in the current cycle. Inventory on the Altos side was up modestly YoY as of early October, with new listings showing a +8.46% YoY spike in the spot week (the four-week average a more measured +1.22% YoY), suggesting some seasonal front-loading rather than a structural supply shift. Pending days on market ticked to 70.8 days, up 6.2% YoY — not a crisis, but a gradual softening of urgency. The September NAR existing sales print (3.99 million SAAR) beat expectations by roughly 140,000 units and snapped a short declining streak, though on a NSA YoY basis sales were still down 3.9% — a more honest read of underlying activity. New home sales (676,000 SAAR) fell slightly and are now down nearly 6% YoY, with builders sitting on 8.4 months of supply; combined with single-family permits down 3.9% YoY and starts missing expectations by 129,000 units, the builder pipeline is contracting in a way that matters more for 2027 supply than the current selling season. The 30-year fixed sat at 7.17% as of November 6, essentially flat on the month but up 88 basis points year-over-year. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is running 2.33-2.36 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points — which means the affordability lever of spread compression remains available if credit markets settle. If the spread normalized even halfway toward historical, the 30-year would fall toward the mid-to-low 6s without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications are the number to watch closely: the four-week average was down 18% YoY as of October 30 and has fallen for six consecutive weeks, its worst level since early 2024. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-inquiry-based measure that tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks) had already turned lower in its four-week average in early October, which is consistent with the MBA weakness now printing. If MII stabilizes in November data, the MBA slide may be near its floor; if it continues falling, January contract signings will feel it.

The macro picture is genuinely mixed in a way that makes rate forecasting difficult. The October payroll report was the standout: +165,000 jobs against a consensus of +59,000, with the unemployment rate falling to 4.1% — both significant beats. Hires and quits rates rose in September JOLTS data, suggesting the labor market may be firming rather than fraying, which is good for buyer confidence but reduces the Fed's urgency to cut. The problem is that average hourly earnings also came in hotter than expected at +0.4% month-over-month (+3.12% YoY), and core PCE held at +3.11% YoY through September — both above the Fed's 2% target. The 10-year Treasury responded, ticking back up to 4.84%, which is the proximate reason the 30-year mortgage hasn't come down despite hopes earlier this fall. The shelter component of CPI is still running +3.22% YoY, which keeps core inflation elevated even as market rents (Zillow's ZORI) fell in September — the lag between market rents and CPI shelter typically runs 12-18 months, so relief in measured inflation from this channel is still ahead. The long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) grew to 1.963 million, up 8.2% YoY over three consecutive months of increases — not a headline number but worth tracking, as it is where future mortgage stress concentrates. Regionally, the Case-Shiller August data (released October 27, so referencing the August close) confirmed the two-speed market is intact: Chicago +6.6% YoY, New York +6.2%, Cleveland +4.3%, Miami +4.3% — while Dallas slipped to -0.7%, Seattle held at -2.0%, and Tampa extended to -0.3% with 22 consecutive months of negative readings. If you're pricing a Sun Belt listing today, that's a materially different conversation than the one happening in the Midwest or Northeast. The signal to watch in December: whether the 10-year Treasury holds above 4.75% after the strong payroll print, and whether the Xactus MII gives any early indication that the MBA application weakness is stabilizing or deepening into the winter.
