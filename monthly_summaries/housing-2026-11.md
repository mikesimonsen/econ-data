**November 2026 Housing Summary**

The demand story this month is straightforward and not encouraging. Altos weekly new pending sales carried a +5.85% year-over-year reading as of early July — part of a 56-consecutive-week positive YoY streak on the 13-week average — but the more current signals have deteriorated sharply. The 4-week average YoY on pending sales was decelerating even at that read, and the leading indicators have since moved decisively negative: the MBA purchase application 4-week average is running -18.5% year-over-year as of early November, the worst level since January 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks, now down 12.2% YoY. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which typically leads MBA by 2-4 weeks — was already rolling over in early July, with its 13-week average falling for 8 straight weeks. That signal is now showing up fully in the MBA data, and notably the MII has not yet shown a convincing reversal, so a near-term floor in purchase applications is not yet visible. NAR existing home sales confirmed the softness: October's print came in at 3.75 million SAAR, missing expectations by 100,000 units, down 8.76% year-over-year, and the weakest reading since October 2023. The West was the softest region at -14.5% YoY NSA, with the South also posting an unusual statistical drop. New home sales held better in relative terms — September came in at 676,000 SAAR, down only 6% YoY — but builder behavior tells a more cautious story: single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY in September, turning negative for the first time in a while, while starts missed expectations by 130,000 units and total starts are down 8% YoY. Builders are completing the pipeline they already started — single-family completions remain elevated — but they are pulling back on future authorizations. That matters for 2027-2028 supply, not the current selling season. On inventory, Realtor.com active listings were up 7.6% YoY in October, months supply at NAR rose to 5.1 months (up 15.9% YoY), and the Zillow ZHVI has fallen four consecutive months to a +0.47% YoY pace, decelerating. Affordability remains the central constraint: the 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.48% today, up 110 basis points year-over-year. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.38 points on today's daily read — roughly 70 basis points above the historical norm of about 1.7 points. The 10-year itself is at 5.10%. Even if the Fed holds, any normalization of that spread would pull the 30-year meaningfully lower without any policy move. Watch pending sales and the MII over the next four weeks for any sign of seasonal stabilization heading into December.

The macro backdrop explains most of the demand weakness, and a few details inside October's data complicate the consensus read. The headline: payrolls came in at +165,000 for October, well above the +59,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% against a 4.4% expectation — on the surface a strong labor market. But look underneath. The long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) now number 1.963 million, up 8.2% year-over-year and rising for three consecutive months. Median unemployment duration is up 17.8% YoY. The hires rate ticked up to 3.4% and quits rose sharply to a 2.1% rate — the quits rebound is a mild positive for housing mobility — but the accumulating long-duration unemployed cohort is worth tracking. It does not threaten the current selling season, but it is the pool from which distressed borrowers emerge in 6-12 months if conditions do not improve. On inflation, October CPI headline came in at +4.08% YoY with a 0.35% monthly gain, but cooler than the 0.9% expectation; core CPI is running at 2.87% YoY. The more Fed-relevant Core PCE (through September) is at 3.11% YoY and rising for seven consecutive months — still above target, which keeps rate-cut expectations constrained and the 10-year elevated. Shelter CPI at +3.54% YoY continues to run hot, though it lags real-time rent data; Zillow's ZORI fell 0.97% in October — an unusual drop — with YoY rent growth decelerating to +0.63%.
