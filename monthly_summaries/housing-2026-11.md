**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive for the 56th consecutive week on the Altos 13-week pending sales average, though the trend is decelerating. The spot week (ending July 3) showed new pending sales up 5.9% YoY, the 4-week average up 3.6% YoY — both still in expansion but off the mid-year pace. Inventory rose for the ninth straight week, with Altos total active listings essentially flat YoY (+0.02%) at roughly 1.09 million, while Realtor.com's October active count climbed 7.6% YoY to 1.18 million — the spread between those two reads is a composition story worth tracking. New listings on the Altos 13-week average ticked up 1.5% YoY, still modest growth. Pending days on market at 70.8 days ran 6.2% above last year for the 104th consecutive week — the market is not tight by any recent historical standard. NAR existing home sales came in at 3.99 million SAAR for September, beating the 3.85 million consensus and reversing two months of decline; on an NSA YoY basis, closings were down 3.9%, the more accurate directional read given seasonal noise. New home sales fell to 676K SAAR, down 6.0% YoY, with months supply at 8.4 — builders are carrying more inventory than they are moving. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.30% as of today, up roughly 12 basis points over the past few days, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.31 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That 60-basis-point excess spread is the number your buyers should understand: rates can fall meaningfully without any Fed action if lenders begin normalizing their risk premium, and the spread has been holding near these elevated levels for nine consecutive weeks. MBA purchase applications are the clearest near-term concern: the 4-week average is down 18.0% YoY and has fallen for six straight weeks, reaching its lowest level since February 2024. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks — also rolled over in early July, with its 4-week average down 1.6% YoY after a recent uptick, suggesting MBA weakness is not yet bottoming. Watch whether the MII stabilizes over the next two weeks; if it firms, that is your first signal that the application slump has a floor.

The macro backdrop is genuinely two-sided this month. October payrolls printed +165,000 against a consensus of +59,000, the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% — beating the 4.4% expected — and initial claims came in at 189,000 versus 212,000 forecast: the labor market is not breaking. For housing, employed buyers are the demand pool, so a resilient jobs market matters more than any single rate print. The complication is that average hourly earnings rose 0.4% month-over-month, hotter than expected, and Core PCE for September ran at 3.11% YoY, which keeps the Fed on hold and anchors the 10-year Treasury — currently at 4.99% — at a level that makes the 7.30% mortgage rate structurally sticky rather than transitory. CPI shelter held at 3.54% YoY in October; that component lags actual rent by roughly 12–18 months, and with Zillow's ZORI down 0.82% month-over-month for the third consecutive month, shelter inflation is likely to drift lower through early 2027, which will mechanically pull core readings down even without broader disinflation. That is the path most likely to give the Fed cover to ease, which then compresses the 10-year, and — if the mortgage-Treasury spread also normalizes — could bring the 30-year rate down more than the Fed move alone would imply. The more immediate concern is the long-term unemployed cohort: those out of work 27 weeks or more reached 1.963 million in October, up 8.2% YoY and rising for three consecutive months. That does not threaten the current selling season but is the metric that will shape distressed-seller volume in 2027. Regionally, the Case-Shiller August data (released October 27) confirms the two-speed market is intact: Chicago +6.6% YoY and New York +6.2% are running four to five times faster than Dallas (–0.7%), Seattle (–2.0%), Tampa (–0.3%), and Las Vegas (–1.1%). If you are in a Sun Belt market where prices are still negative YoY, the conversation with sellers is structurally different from the one agents are having in the Northeast or Great Lakes — and the data supports framing them that way. The signal to watch next month is whether the 10-year Treasury holds below or breaks above 5.0%; a sustained move higher from here would push the 30-year toward 7.5% and likely extend the MBA weakness into the spring setup window.
