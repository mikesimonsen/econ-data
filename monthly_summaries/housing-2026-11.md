**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but decelerated meaningfully over the past four weeks. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales remained in positive YoY territory — running a 56-consecutive-week streak — but the spot read of +5.9% YoY and the 4-week average of +3.6% YoY both softened from prior months, and pending days on market stretched to 70.8 days, up 6.2% YoY, suggesting buyers are taking longer to commit even where volume stays ahead of last year. Inventory on the Altos platform stood near 1.09 million active listings, essentially flat YoY (+0.02%), with new listings up 8.5% YoY on a spot basis but only +1.2% on the 4-week average — the new-listing growth that looked meaningful in September has largely been absorbed by the calendar. NAR's September existing home sales printed at 3.99 million SAAR, edging past the 3.85 million consensus and reversing two months of declines, though the NSA YoY read of -3.9% keeps the underlying trend in perspective: we are not in a recovery, we are in a floor-finding. New home sales came in at 676K SAAR for September, down 6% YoY, with builders' months supply still elevated at 8.4 — which explains why single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY in September after two months positive, while starts missed expectations at 1,221K SAAR (-8.1% YoY). Builders are completing their pipeline — single-family completions ran 840K SAAR — but pulling back on future authorization, particularly in the West (-7.8% YoY on permits). The 30-year fixed sits at 7.25% as of today, down only 5 basis points over the past month despite the 10-year Treasury easing to 4.89%. The mortgage-Treasury spread is 2.36 percentage points — roughly 65 basis points above the long-run historical norm of about 1.7 points — meaning rates are being held artificially high relative to underlying bond markets. If that spread compresses toward its norm, the 30-year could fall materially without any Fed action. That is the affordability story agents should be telling sellers who think rates won't budge until the Fed moves. The more urgent signal for November is the MBA purchase index: the 4-week average dropped -18.0% YoY to its lowest level since February 2024, and the 13-week average is down -10.9% YoY across 14 consecutive falling weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which typically leads MBA by 2-4 weeks — turned negative on its 4-week average in early October, confirming the softness rather than contradicting it. Watch for whether MBA stabilizes before the Thanksgiving seasonal break compresses the data window.

The macro backdrop explains much of the demand hesitation. September payrolls came in at just +29K against a +59K consensus — a soft print that, combined with an unemployment rate tick to 4.2%, points to a labor market that is cooling unevenly rather than collapsing. What deserves more attention than the headline is the long-term unemployed cohort: those out of work 27 or more weeks reached 1.944 million in September, up 7.1% YoY, with median unemployment duration rising to 11.5 weeks, up 13.9% YoY. This group does not affect the current selling season, but it is quietly building the conditions for elevated mortgage delinquencies 6-12 months out, particularly in markets where equity cushions are thin. On inflation, Core PCE came in at +3.11% YoY in September — running above the Fed's 2% target and above what many expected — which is what is keeping the 10-year elevated and the mortgage spread wide. CPI shelter at +3.22% YoY is decelerating but sticky. The September JOLTS data offered the one genuinely encouraging signal: the quits rate rose to 2.1%, up 0.2 points YoY, suggesting workers still feel confident enough to leave jobs voluntarily — historically a reasonable proxy for household formation appetite. On the regional price picture, the August Case-Shiller data (reference period: through August 2026) confirmed the two-speed market is intact. Chicago is up 6.6% YoY and New York up 6.2%, both rising for seven months; Dallas is -0.7% YoY, Las Vegas -1.1%, Seattle -2.0%, and Tampa -0.3%. Agents in appreciating Midwest and Northeast markets are working a supply-constrained story; agents in Sun Belt metros are negotiating against price-reduced inventory and elevated months supply. The macro signal to watch for December: the November payrolls print. A second consecutive soft number — particularly if long-term unemployment continues to build — will pressure the 10-year lower and potentially compress the mortgage spread, which is the fastest path to meaningful rate relief that doesn't require the Fed to move at all.
