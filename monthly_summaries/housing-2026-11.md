# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos weekly demand read through early November shows new pending sales running roughly +6% year-over-year on the spot reading, with the four-week average at +3.6% YoY and the 13-week average holding at +5.5% YoY positive for 56 consecutive weeks — a streak that has survived two rate spikes and remains the clearest evidence that underlying demand has not broken. That resilience is worth noting because the NAR October existing home sales print landed at 3.75 million SAAR, down 8.8% YoY and 100,000 units below the consensus of 3.85 million — the weakest reading since October 2023, with the West recording a statistically unusual drop of 2.5 standard deviations. The divergence between sticky pending activity and weak closed sales largely reflects mortgage rate timing: the 30-year fixed is at 7.42% as of today, up roughly 12 basis points over the past month, with the 10-year Treasury at 5.11% — a spread of 2.31 points, still more than half a point above the long-run norm of about 1.7 points. That elevated spread means rates could fall meaningfully if lenders normalize risk pricing, without any Fed move required. Altos active inventory was rising YoY through early October, and Realtor.com active listings confirmed that trend with a 7.6% YoY gain in October — supply is rebuilding, but months supply at 5.1 is now up nearly 16% YoY, tilting negotiating leverage further toward buyers in rate-sensitive markets. MBA purchase applications posted a single-week rebound to 141 on the index, but the four-week average sits 18.5% below year-ago levels — the weakest since January 2024 — and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, was already rolling over in early October with its 13-week average down eight consecutive weeks, suggesting the MBA weakness is not yet finished. Watch the MII's four-week average: if it stabilizes or firms through November, the MBA drag should bottom before the holidays; if it continues lower, January contract activity will be weaker than seasonal norms.

The macro backdrop is genuinely two-sided, which is why forecasting rate direction is harder than it looks. October payrolls came in at +165,000 — nearly three times the consensus of +59,000 — and the unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1%, beating the 4.4% consensus. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% month-over-month, hotter than expected. That combination keeps the Fed on hold and pushed the 10-year yield back above 5.1%, which is the direct mechanism for the mortgage rate move this month: stronger-than-expected labor data repriced rate-cut expectations, the 10-year followed, and mortgage rates moved with it. The partial offset is in inflation: headline CPI rose just 0.3% in October against a 0.9% consensus — a material undershoot — and core CPI is running at 2.87% YoY, though core PCE through September was still at 3.1% YoY and rising. Shelter CPI at 3.5% YoY is decelerating and should continue to do so as lease-roll data works through the index, which will gradually pull core readings lower. The regional price picture from Case-Shiller (through August, so two months lagged) reflects exactly what agents on the ground are already seeing: Chicago +6.6% YoY, New York +6.2%, Cleveland +4.3% — all rising — while Seattle is -2.0% and falling for 13 consecutive months, Dallas -0.7%, Las Vegas -1.1%, and Tampa -0.3% with 22 consecutive months of negative YoY readings. The Midwest and Northeast are functioning as genuinely distinct markets from the inventory-heavy Sun Belt metros, and price conversations at listing appointments in those two geographies require different anchors. The one number to watch next month: the long-term unemployed count (27+ weeks) reached 1.963 million in October, up 8.2% YoY and rising for three consecutive months. That cohort does not affect the current selling season — their job losses have not yet produced mortgage stress — but its continued growth is the early signal for where distressed inventory originates 9 to 12 months out.
