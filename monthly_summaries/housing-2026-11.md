# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos weekly demand read through early July showed new pending sales up 5.9% year-over-year on the spot week and +3.6% on the four-week average, with the 13-week average running +5.5% YoY and positive for 56 consecutive weeks — a streak that reflects steady if unspectacular underlying demand. Inventory stood at roughly 1.09 million active listings, essentially flat year-over-year (+0.02%), with new listings up 8.5% YoY on the spot week though the four-week average was only +1.2% YoY, suggesting the new-listings acceleration was not yet broad-based. Pending days on market held at 70.8 days, up 6.2% YoY — buyers are not rushing. The October NAR existing home sales print was the month's sharpest data point: 3.75 million SAAR, down 8.8% YoY and 100,000 units below consensus, with the West falling at a -2.5 standard-deviation pace and the South not far behind. NSA transactions came in at 317,000, down 11.7% year-over-year — the weakest October since 2023. New home sales (September, the latest available) held better at 676,000 SAAR, only -6% YoY, partly because builders have been buying down rates; that divergence between existing and new is widening. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.46% as of today, up 108 basis points year-over-year and 69 basis points above the historical norm spread to the 10-year Treasury — the spread is currently 2.39 basis points versus the long-run average near 1.7, and it has been rising for 11 consecutive weeks. That is the hidden affordability story: even with the Fed on hold, if the spread normalized to 1.7, the 30-year would fall toward 6.75-6.80% without a single rate cut. MBA purchase applications are the loudest warning signal right now — the 4-week average is down 18.6% YoY, the worst reading since January 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus MII, which typically leads MBA by 2-4 weeks, was already pointing lower in early July with its 4-week average rolling over after a brief recovery; the subsequent MBA deterioration confirms that signal. Builder permits are the other thing to watch: single-family permits fell to 772,000 in October, down 12.9% YoY and the lowest since April 2023, even as single-family starts ticked up to 864,000. Builders are completing existing pipeline — completions rose 6.3% in October — but pulling back sharply on future authorizations. That starts-permits gap implies the single-family supply pipeline will thin in 2027. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the MBA 4-week average stabilizes or continues to decelerate into the holiday slowdown, and whether any softening in the 10-year yield (currently 5.07%) begins to compress the spread.

The macro backdrop is frankly complicated for housing right now, and the data does not support a simple bullish or bearish read. October payrolls came in at +165,000, well above the 59,000 consensus — a genuine beat that reduces near-term recession risk and therefore supports housing demand in principle. The hires rate ticked up to 3.4% in September and quits rose 10.5%, both signs that workers retain some labor market optionality, which historically correlates with household formation and move-up buying. But set against that: core PCE is running at 3.1% YoY through September and core CPI at 2.9% through October, both above the Fed's target, which is why the 10-year Treasury is at 5.07% — a level that makes the 7.46% mortgage rate not anomalous but arithmetically inevitable given the spread. The shelter CPI component printed +3.5% YoY in October and continues to run hot, which keeps measured inflation elevated even as actual market rents (Zillow ZORI) fell 1.0% in a single month in October and are up only 0.6% YoY — the lag between real-time rent softness and official shelter inflation will eventually pull core CPI lower, but the Fed is reading lagged data. The one shadow forming on the demand side is the long-term unemployed cohort: 1.963 million workers have been jobless 27 or more weeks as of October, up 8.2% YoY and rising for three consecutive months, with median unemployment duration at 11.9 weeks and climbing. This group does not threaten the current selling season, but if it continues to accumulate, it becomes the delinquency story of 2027-2028. Regionally, the Case-Shiller August data (the most recent available, released October 27) confirms the two-speed market is still running: Chicago +6.6% YoY and New York +6.2% YoY versus Dallas -0.7%, Las Vegas -1.1%, Seattle -2.0%, and Tampa -0.3
