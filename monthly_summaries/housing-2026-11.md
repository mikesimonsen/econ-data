**November 2026 Housing Summary**

The demand picture darkened meaningfully over the past four weeks. Altos weekly new pending sales were running +5.85% year-over-year as of early July — 56 consecutive weeks of positive YoY readings — but the 4-week average had already begun decelerating to +3.6% YoY, and the trajectory since then is not favorable. The NAR's October existing home sales print confirmed what the leading data implied: 3.75 million SAAR, down 8.8% YoY, missing consensus by 100,000 units, and the weakest reading since October 2023. The West was the softest region at -14.5% YoY NSA, an unusual two-standard-deviation drop; the South fell 11% NSA YoY. New home sales (September, the most recent read) fared only marginally better at 676,000 SAAR, down 6% YoY. Inventory on the NAR side rose to 1.59 million units, 4.6% above a year ago, with months' supply climbing to 5.1 — up nearly 16% YoY — as demand retreated faster than supply grew. The Realtor.com pending listing count fell to its lowest level since September 2023, down 10.4% YoY. Against all of this, the mortgage rate environment offers little near-term relief: the 30-year fixed sits at 7.36% today, up 100 basis points from a year ago. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, now at 2.45 points against a historical norm of roughly 1.7 points — meaning roughly 75 basis points of the current rate reflects elevated lender risk premiums rather than benchmark yields. The 10-year itself fell to 4.91% today, its lowest in several days, but the spread has been widening, not compressing, for 11 consecutive weeks — the hidden affordability story is moving in the wrong direction. MBA purchase applications underscore the problem: the 4-week average is down 18.6% YoY, the weakest since January 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks — showed its 4-week average rolling over as of early July, consistent with what MBA subsequently confirmed. Builder permits are flashing a supply warning that extends beyond the current selling season: single-family permits fell to 772,000 SAAR in October, down 12.9% YoY and the lowest since April 2023, while single-family starts (864,000) rose modestly in October but remain down 3.3% YoY. Builders are completing the existing pipeline — single-family completions rose 6.3% in October — but pulling back sharply on future authorizations. That starts-permits divergence implies meaningful single-family supply contraction in 2027-2028. What to watch over the next four weeks: whether the 10-year Treasury holds below 4.9% and whether the mortgage-Treasury spread begins to compress; either without the other does not move the needle for buyers.

The macro backdrop explains much of the demand softness, but it also contains a few genuine surprises. October payrolls came in at +165,000, well above the 59,000 consensus — hurricane and strike distortions appear to have unwound — and the unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1% against a 4.4% expectation. On the surface, that is a strong labor market, and wage growth at +3.1% YoY supports nominal purchasing power. But the signal that matters for housing demand over the next two to three quarters is not the headline unemployment rate — it is the long-term unemployed cohort. Workers unemployed 27 or more weeks reached 1.963 million in October, up 8.2% YoY over three consecutive months of increases, with median unemployment duration now at 11.9 weeks, up nearly 18% YoY. That cohort does not show up in delinquency data immediately; it takes 6-12 months to work through savings, then forbearance, before it surfaces as distressed inventory. It is worth tracking as a forward indicator of supply, not current demand. On inflation, October headline CPI rose 4.1% YoY and core PCE (September) printed at 3.1% YoY — both above the Fed's target by meaningful margins — which keeps the Fed on hold and 10-year yields elevated.
