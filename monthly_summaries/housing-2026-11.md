**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through October but the signals beneath the surface warrant attention. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has been in positive YoY territory for 56 consecutive weeks, and the spot reading for the week of July 3 came in at +5.9% YoY — healthy on its face. The 4-week average told a softer story at +3.6% YoY, and that average has been decelerating. Inventory at Altos stood just above 1.08 million active listings, essentially flat YoY (+0.02%), with new listings running +8.5% YoY on the spot but only +1.2% on the 4-week average — meaning the recent new-listing strength is narrower than the headline suggests. Pending days on market held at 70.8 days, up 6.2% YoY for the 104th consecutive week, a quiet confirmation that the market is not tightening on the buyer side. NAR existing sales for September printed at 3.99 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and reversing a string of declines — but on an NSA basis, September existing sales were down 3.9% YoY, so the SAAR beat reflects seasonal-adjustment mechanics more than a genuine volume recovery. New home sales ran the other direction: 676,000 SAAR in September, down 6.0% YoY, with months' supply still elevated at 8.4. Builders are clearly working through completed inventory rather than adding pipeline — single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY in September, turning negative after a stretch of positive readings, while starts missed expectations materially (1,221K actual vs. 1,350K expected). The 30-year fixed sits at 7.25% as of November 4, down only 5 basis points over the past month. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: 2.36 percentage points, against a historical norm near 1.7 points. That 66-basis-point excess spread is what keeps buyers from feeling rate relief even as the 10-year has ticked down. On the leading-indicator side, the picture is notably cautious: the MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 18.0% YoY — its weakest level since February 2024 — and has fallen for six consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by roughly two to four weeks, turned its 4-week average lower in early July after a brief improvement, and its 13-week average has been falling for eight weeks. When MII leads MBA lower in this sequence, the subsequent purchase application data tends to confirm it. Watch whether the MBA 4-week average stabilizes or continues to erode through November; that is the clearest near-term read on December contract activity.

The macro backdrop explains much of the demand restraint. September payrolls came in at roughly +29,000 — a significant miss against the +59,000 consensus — while the unemployment rate ticked up to 4.2%, also above consensus. Average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% month-over-month, well below the 0.3% expected. Taken together, this is a labor market that is cooling faster than forecasters anticipated, and slower income growth directly compresses the pool of qualified buyers at current price levels. The quits rate rose to 2.1% in September, up 0.2 points on the month — quits are a voluntary-mobility signal, and a firming quits rate suggests workers retain some labor-market confidence, which is a mild offset. The more concerning labor statistic for housing is the long-term unemployed: 1.944 million people have been out of work 27 or more weeks, up 7.1% YoY and rising for two consecutive months. This cohort does not affect the current selling season, but its accumulation is the early thread of a future delinquency story — worth tracking quarterly. On inflation, Core PCE came in at 3.11% YoY in September, above the Fed's target and still rising on a seven-month trend, which keeps the Fed from providing meaningful rate relief. Shelter CPI at +3.22% YoY continues to run above core CPI ex-shelter, though real-time rent data (Zillow ZORI down 0.82% month-over-month in September) suggests the shelter component will soften in the official data with a lag. The regional price picture from August Case-Shiller reinforces the two-speed market: Chicago (+6.6% YoY) and New York (+6.2%) continue to appreciate at a pace that supports confident listing conversations, while Dallas (−0.7%), Las Vegas (−1.1%), Seattle (−2.0%), and Tampa (−0.3%) remain in negative or barely-positive territory — markets where sellers need a different price narrative entirely. The one macro signal to watch in November is the October payroll report: if the labor market continues to undershoot, rate-cut expectations will firm, the 10-year may drift lower, and any spread compression from 2.36 toward 2.10 could move the 30-year meaningfully without a single Fed action.
