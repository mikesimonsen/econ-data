**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive on a year-over-year basis through the most recent Altos read, with new pending sales running +5.85% YoY on a spot basis and the 4-week average at +3.57% YoY — the 56th consecutive week of positive YoY pending sales on the 13-week measure, though that average has decelerated and the 4-week trend has turned slightly negative week-over-week, a signal worth watching as we move into the seasonally slower stretch. Active inventory reached roughly 1.19 million on Realtor.com, up 7.6% YoY, and Altos data through early July showed inventory rising for nine straight weeks — supply is expanding, but the pace is not alarming relative to seasonal norms. New listings on the 13-week Altos average were up about 1.5% YoY and rising for 14 consecutive weeks, a mild but steady loosening. Pending days on market have been running about 6% above year-ago levels for two years straight, which is a consistent if quiet signal that the market is absorbing homes a little more slowly than it was in 2024. NAR's October existing home sales print came in at 3.75 million SAAR, missing the 3.85 million consensus by 100,000 units and falling 8.8% YoY on a seasonally adjusted basis — the weakest reading since October 2023. The West was the notable weak point, down 14.5% YoY NSA; the South also deteriorated sharply. New home sales for September printed at 676,000 SAAR, down 6% YoY, with builders' months supply ticking up to 8.4 — elevated but not yet at distress levels. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.48% today, up 110 basis points from a year ago and still the dominant affordability constraint. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.38 points on a daily basis, against a historical norm near 1.7 points — meaning roughly 65-70 basis points of that 7.48% rate reflects spread premium above what the underlying Treasury would imply. If that spread compresses toward its norm, the 30-year could fall meaningfully without any Fed action. The 4-week average spread is 2.33 points, which is at least directionally tighter than its recent peak, though still well above normal. MBA purchase applications are running -18.5% YoY on the 4-week average — the weakest since January 2024 — and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 straight weeks. The Xactus MII, which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks, turned positive YoY briefly in early July but its 4-week average was rolling over at the same time; watch whether the MII firms or continues to fade when November data arrives, because that will set the tone for purchase application volume heading into December. Single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY in September after turning negative for the first time in the cycle — builders are pulling back on future authorizations even as they work through existing pipeline, which keeps near-term completions elevated but signals 2027 single-family supply will be tighter than it looks today.

The macro backdrop is genuinely conflicted in ways that matter for housing. October payrolls came in at +165,000 — well above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% against a 4.4% expectation, which removes the near-term case for Fed rate cuts and helps explain why the 10-year Treasury remains at 5.10%. A labor market this firm keeps purchase intent alive at the margin, but it also keeps the Fed on hold, which keeps the 10-year anchored, which keeps mortgage rates at levels that suppress existing-home transaction volume. The inflation picture is more nuanced: headline CPI for October came in at +4.1% YoY and core at +2.9% YoY, while core PCE through September was running at 3.1% — all above the Fed's target, reinforcing the hold. Shelter CPI at +3.5% YoY is still the stickiest inflation component, a statistical lag that will not fully clear until 2027, so do not expect that number to give the Fed cover anytime soon. What the inflation data does confirm is that real wage growth (hourly earnings +3.1% YoY against headline CPI at +4.1%) is now slightly negative, which chips at buyer purchasing power over time.
