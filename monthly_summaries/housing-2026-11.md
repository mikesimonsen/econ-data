# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive in October but the leading indicators are flashing a meaningful deceleration. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales was running +5.5% year-over-year as of early October, with 56 consecutive weeks of positive YoY readings — a durable streak — but the 4-week average had already slipped to +3.6% YoY and the spot week to +5.9%, while the 4-week trend line itself turned negative on the period. That divergence between the resilient 13-week average and the softening near-term reads is worth watching closely. Active inventory on Realtor.com reached 1.18 million in October, up 7.6% YoY and rising for seven consecutive months, while Altos total inventory was up modestly YoY as of early July (the most recent Altos read in this snapshot). New listings on Realtor.com fell 7.3% YoY in October — the lowest count since September 2023 — which tells you sellers are pulling back seasonally but buyers aren't absorbing what's there at the same pace; pending listings on Realtor.com dropped 10.4% YoY, the lowest since September 2023 as well. Pending days on market remained elevated at 70.8 days, up 6.2% YoY for the 104th consecutive week, confirming the market is not tightening. NAR existing home sales printed 3.99 million SAAR for September, a narrow beat of the 3.85 million consensus and a marginal uptick from August, but on a NSA YoY basis they were down 3.9% — the more honest read for direction. New home sales came in at 676,000 SAAR, down 6.0% YoY, with months' supply at 8.4 — elevated by historical standards and a signal that builders are still working through inventory even as they pull back on future authorizations. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.30% as of today, up roughly 96 basis points year-over-year, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.31 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That spread has been rising on the 4-week and 9-week averages, meaning affordability is not getting the relief it would if lender risk appetite were normalizing. The MBA purchase index is the most direct warning sign right now: the 4-week average is down 18.0% YoY, the worst reading since February 2024, and the 13-week average is down 10.9% — falling for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by roughly 2-4 weeks, was also rolling over in early October, with the 13-week average down 1.5% YoY and falling for 8 weeks. That MII signal arrived before the MBA weakness deepened, which is precisely how it's supposed to work. Single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY in September, turning negative for the first time after a string of positive reads, while starts missed expectations at 1,221,000 total — down 8.1% YoY. The starts-versus-permits divergence on multifamily (5+ unit completions up sharply while 5+ starts fall 27%) suggests the apartment pipeline is delivering but not being replenished, which matters for rent trajectories in 2027. Watch the November MBA purchase index and whether the Xactus MII stabilizes — those two reads will tell you whether the demand softness is a seasonal pause or something stickier heading into spring setup season.

The macro picture is more complicated than the headline beats suggest. October payrolls came in at +165,000 — far above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, also better than expected. Those are unambiguously positive for housing confidence and purchase qualification. But the internals deserve scrutiny: the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose to 1.96 million, up 8.2% year-over-year and rising for three consecutive months. That cohort doesn't disqualify current buyers, but it accumulates into future delinquency pressure if it persists — worth monitoring in the 6-to-12-month frame. The quits rate jumped back to 2.1% in September, up from recent lows, which historically correlates with household formation and willingness to make a move; that's a mild positive signal for demand. On inflation, October CPI came in at +0.3% month-over-month headline versus the 0.9% consensus expectation — a meaningful undershoot — and core CPI printed 2.87% YoY. Shelter CPI was +0.30% for the month, running at 3.54% YoY, and still the stickiest component keeping core elevated. Core PCE for September was 3.11% YoY, rising for seven consecutive months, which is the number the Fed is actually watching. The 10-year Treasury is at 4.99% today, up 86 basis points YoY, and with the spread at 2.31 points, any compression back toward the historical 1.
