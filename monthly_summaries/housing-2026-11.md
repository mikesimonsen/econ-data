**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held softer through November. The Altos weekly new pending sales read sat at a +5.85% YoY gain for the week of July 3 — the 56th consecutive positive week — but the four-week average decelerated to +3.57% YoY and the 13-week average, while still positive at +5.48%, has flattened. That streak is real, but its momentum is fading. Inventory on the Altos side continued rising, up just over 1% for the week and essentially flat YoY (+0.02%), while new listings ran +8.46% YoY on a spot basis; the 13-week new-listings average has been climbing for 14 weeks, adding gradual supply pressure without yet overwhelming demand. Pending days on market held at 70.8, up 6.2% YoY — not alarming, but the trend has worsened for 104 consecutive weeks, meaning buyers are taking longer to commit in every market condition this cycle has thrown at them. The October NAR existing home sales print came in at 3.75 million SAAR, missing the 3.85 million consensus by 100,000 units and landing 8.76% below October 2025 — the weakest reading since October 2023. The West was the weakest region, off 14.47% YoY on the NSA count, and the South posted an unusual 2.0 standard-deviation drop. New home sales (September, the most recent available) ran at 676,000 SAAR, down 5.98% YoY — both channels softening in tandem, which removes the narrative that existing-home lock-in explains all the weakness. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.34% today, 100 basis points above a year ago, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.42 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. The spread has actually widened over the past 11 weeks, compressing affordability beyond what Treasury yields alone imply. The good news embedded there: if lender risk appetite normalizes even partially, rates can fall meaningfully without Fed movement. The leading indicators are not encouraging for the next four weeks. MBA purchase applications four-week average is running -18.64% YoY, the worst reading since January 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus MII — which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks — turned negative on its spot reading and its four-week average after a brief positive turn, with the 13-week average falling for eight weeks. When the MII rolls over before MBA, it usually means purchase activity softens further before it recovers. Single-family permits fell 12.87% YoY in October, their lowest since April 2023, even as single-family starts ticked up 3.97% — builders are completing existing pipeline while pulling back sharply on future authorizations. That starts-permits divergence will constrain resale-competing new inventory in 2027, but it offers no near-term demand relief. Watch whether the spread compresses in December, and whether the Xactus MII stabilizes before year end.

The macro backdrop explains the demand softness without requiring a dramatic narrative. October payrolls came in at +165,000, well above the 59,000 consensus — the labor market is not breaking. The unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1%, also better than expected. Those are constructive for housing in the long run. The problem is the inflation picture. CPI headline ran +4.08% YoY in October, core PCE was +3.11% YoY through September, and shelter CPI continues to rise (+3.54% YoY) — the very component that the Fed is waiting to see break before cutting. As long as Core PCE holds above 3%, the Fed has limited cover to ease, the 10-year stays near 4.92%, and the 30-year stays north of 7%. Average hourly earnings came in at +3.12% YoY, running below headline inflation — that is a real-income squeeze for prospective buyers making purchase decisions right now. The long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose to 1.963 million, up 8.15% YoY and climbing for three consecutive months. That cohort does not affect the current selling season; it represents future delinquency pressure in roughly six to twelve months if the trend continues. On the regional price picture, the August Case-Shiller data (released late October, so the most current) confirms the two-speed market: Chicago +6.61% YoY, New York +6.24%, Cleveland +4.26%, and Miami +4.33% are still appreciating at a meaningful pace; Dallas is -0.68%, Denver -0.77%, Seattle -2.01%, Tampa -0.27%, and Las Vegas -1.06% are either flat or still correcting. Agents in Sun Belt markets working pricing conversations should lean on those local figures rather than the national +2.15% — the national number flatters conditions on the ground in the South and West.
