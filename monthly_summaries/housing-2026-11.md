**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but decelerated meaningfully heading into autumn. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has been positive year-over-year for 56 consecutive weeks, a streak that reflects a genuine recovery in contract activity that began in late 2024 — but the 4-week average slowed to +3.6% YoY as of early October, and the spot reading of +5.9% YoY came alongside four straight weeks of falling weekly volume, a pattern that typically reflects normal seasonal compression rather than a demand breakdown. Inventory stood at roughly 1.09 million active listings, up just barely year-over-year, with new listings running +8.5% YoY on the spot read but the 4-week and 13-week averages settling into the low single digits — supply growth is present but not accelerating. Pending days on market held at 70.8 days, up 6.2% YoY, confirming that buyers are taking more time even as transactions clear. The NAR existing home sales SAAR for September came in at 3.99 million, beating expectations of 3.85 million and reversing three months of declines, though the NSA YoY reading of -3.9% keeps the longer view in check; months supply ticked down to 4.8. New home sales at 676K SAAR ran nearly 6% below last year, with builder inventory contracting and single-family permits falling nearly 4% YoY in September — builders are delivering into the pipeline but pulling back on what they authorize next, which matters more for 2028 supply than for this selling season. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.17% as of today, up 88 basis points year-over-year and essentially unchanged over the past month. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.33 points today against a historical norm near 1.7 points, there is roughly 60 basis points of excess spread still embedded in that rate. The 4-week average spread has been rising for nine weeks, which means the hidden affordability improvement that many expected has not materialized. The MBA purchase index 4-week average fell 18% YoY as of October 30 — the weakest read since early 2024 — and has declined for six consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-inquiry-based leading indicator that tends to precede MBA by 2-4 weeks) turned negative on its 4-week average in early October after a brief positive run, suggesting the MBA weakness is not yet bottoming. Watch whether the MBA 4-week average stabilizes or continues lower through the last week of November; if it holds above its August trough, the spring pipeline remains intact.

The macro picture is genuinely two-sided, and agents should read it carefully rather than accepting either the bearish or the bullish headline. October payrolls came in at +165K against a consensus of +59K — a substantial beat — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, also well ahead of the 4.4% consensus. The hires rate rose to 3.4% and the quits rate moved up to 2.1%, both constructive signals: workers quitting for better positions are historically a leading indicator of household formation and eventual purchase intent. That is the demand-side positive. The complication is that the long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 weeks or more — has risen 8.2% year-over-year and has been growing for three consecutive months. This group does not threaten the current selling season, but at the margin it accumulates future mortgage stress; it is worth tracking. On inflation, Core PCE came in at 3.1% YoY for September, still above the Fed's 2% target and running hotter than core CPI's 2.6%, which limits how quickly the Fed can ease. The 10-year Treasury yield ticked back up to 4.84% today after a brief retreat — that move, combined with the still-elevated mortgage-Treasury spread, is the primary reason affordability has not improved despite the labor market's relative strength. Regionally, the Case-Shiller August data (released October 27, so reflecting contracts signed in June-July) confirms the two-speed market is intact: Chicago +6.6% YoY and New York +6.2% YoY contrast sharply with Dallas -0.7%, Seattle -2.0%, Tampa -0.3%, and Las Vegas -1.1% — agents in those Sun Belt markets are pricing into a fundamentally different supply-demand balance than their Midwest and Northeast counterparts. The number to watch next month is Core PCE for October, due in late November: if it decelerates toward 2.8-2.9%, the bond market will reprice Fed cut expectations forward and the 10-year may ease enough to pull the 30-year below 7.0% — that is the threshold where purchase application volume has historically responded within two to three weeks.
