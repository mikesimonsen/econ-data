**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but decelerated meaningfully heading into the holiday slowdown. The Altos 4-week average for new pending sales was running +3.6% year-over-year as of early July — the most recent read in the dashboard — with the 13-week average at +5.5% YoY on a 56-consecutive-week positive streak, a streak that speaks to the durability of the demand base even as the spot weekly number softened. That durability is now being tested: the NAR October existing home sales print came in at 3.75 million SAAR, missing the 3.85 million consensus by 100,000 units and landing 8.8% below October 2025 on an NSA basis — the weakest YoY pace since October 2023. The West was the weakest region, off 15.2% NSA YoY; the South fell 10.8%. Against that backdrop, new home sales told a different story entirely: October came in at 701,000 SAAR, up 7.5% YoY, with months supply falling from roughly 9.3 to 8.0 as builders absorbed standing inventory — the divergence between existing and new is as wide as it has been in this cycle, and it reflects builders cutting prices to move product (new home average sale price down 12.1% YoY, now at its lowest since April 2009). Altos active inventory was +4.6% YoY per NAR's October read, with months supply at 5.1, up nearly 16% YoY — still not a buyer's market by historical norms, but loosening. Pending days on market had been running elevated at +6.2% YoY, confirming that homes are sitting longer. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.26% as of today, down modestly over the past week but up 106 basis points from a year ago — and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.41 points, well above the long-run norm of roughly 1.7 points. If credit markets normalize toward that historical spread, rates could fall 50-70 basis points without any Fed move — that is the affordability story most buyers are not hearing from their lender. MBA purchase applications offered a brief bright spot: the week ending November 20 jumped 10.2% week-over-week, but the 4-week average is still -18.1% YoY and the 13-week average has been falling for 14 consecutive weeks, now at its lowest since June 2024. The Xactus MII — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks — had already signaled softness in early October, with the 4-week average rolling over after a brief rise. Single-family permits fell to 777,000 SAAR in October, down 12.3% YoY and the lowest reading since April 2023, telling you clearly that builders are not laying the groundwork for a 2027 supply increase. Watch whether the MBA 4-week average can sustain the November uptick through December, and whether the spread begins compressing on any softening in the 10-year.

The macro picture is genuinely complicated right now, and that complexity is what is keeping buyers on the sideline. October payrolls printed at +165,000, more than double the +59,000 consensus — headline labor is not breaking. Unemployment ticked down to 4.1%, beating the 4.4% forecast. But beneath that, the long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) has risen 8.2% year-over-year and has been climbing for three consecutive months, and median unemployment duration is up 17.8% YoY. That cohort does not threaten the current selling season, but it is building a reservoir of potential distress that will matter for lenders and servicers six to twelve months from now — worth flagging to any seller who thinks distressed supply will stay off-market indefinitely. The inflation picture is the more immediate housing problem: core PCE came in at 3.2% YoY in October — still running well above the Fed's target — and the 10-year Treasury is at 4.85%, up 84 basis points from a year ago. That is the ceiling sitting on mortgage rates. CPI shelter at +3.5% YoY is decelerating, which is the right direction, but it is not falling fast enough to change the Fed's posture in the near term. On the regional price picture, the September Case-Shiller data (reported this month, so reflecting conditions through September) shows the two-speed market intact: Chicago +6.9% YoY, New York +5.9%, Miami +4.6%, San Francisco +3.6% — the Northeast and select coastal metros are holding or accelerating. Meanwhile Dallas is -0.8% YoY for 18 consecutive months, Denver -1.0% for 17, Seattle -2.1% for 14, Tampa -0.01% (barely clinging to flat after 23 months negative).
