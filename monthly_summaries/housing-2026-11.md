**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held in positive territory through early November, though the Altos data we're working with runs through the first week of July — the most recent weekly read showed new pending sales up 5.9% year-over-year, with the four-week average at +3.6% YoY and the 13-week average running at +5.5% YoY positive for 56 consecutive weeks. That streak is the underlying story: even as the weekly figure decelerated and the four-week trend dipped, the longer-term trend remained constructive. Altos inventory was essentially flat year-over-year at roughly 1.09 million units, with new listings up about 8.5% YoY on the spot week — a healthy supply addition without the kind of accumulation that tips the balance toward buyers. Pending days on market ticked up 6.2% YoY, a continued softening of urgency that is worth watching. The October NAR existing home sales print told a different story: 3.75 million SAAR, down 8.8% year-over-year and roughly 100,000 below the consensus of 3.85 million — an unusually large miss that reached its lowest level since October 2023, and the NSA count confirmed it at -11.7% YoY. The South and West drove the weakness, each posting statistically unusual drops. Months supply climbed to 5.1, up nearly 16% year-over-year, the fastest YoY acceleration we've seen in this cycle. Meanwhile, new home sales came in at 701,000 SAAR in October — up 14% from September and 7.5% year-over-year, the first positive YoY reading after a string of negatives — with new home supply falling to 8.0 months. That divergence between existing and new home sales is real and widening: builders are clearing inventory at prices buyers will accept (median new home price -3.2% YoY, average -12.1% YoY) while the existing market remains locked by the rate-and-equity dynamic. On rates: the 30-year fixed sat at 7.26% as of November 25th, up 106 basis points year-over-year, but has been drifting lower over the past week. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, now at 2.41 percentage points against a historical norm of roughly 1.7 points — meaning roughly 70 basis points of rate relief is sitting in the spread rather than in Treasury moves. If credit conditions normalize and that spread compresses even 30-40 basis points, the 30-year falls meaningfully without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications showed a sharp week-over-week bounce (+10%) for the week ending November 20th, but the four-week average remains down 18% year-over-year and the 13-week average is at its lowest since June 2024 — the spike looks more like volatility than reversal. The Xactus MII, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, was already rolling over in early July with its 13-week average falling for eight consecutive weeks. That divergence between the spot MBA jump and the MII trend is the leading indicator to watch: if the MII doesn't firm in December data, the bounce in purchase apps is noise. Single-family permits fell to 777,000 SAAR in October — down 12.3% year-over-year and the lowest since April 2023 — even as starts bounced. Builders are pulling back on future authorizations while completing their existing pipeline, which matters more for 2027 supply than the current selling season.

The macro backdrop is genuinely two-sided, which is why the consensus rate narrative keeps getting complicated. October payrolls came in at +165,000, well above the 59,000 consensus — a beat so large it effectively reset the near-term Fed conversation. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, also better than the 4.4% expected, and average hourly earnings rose 0.4% month-over-month, hotter than forecast. That combination — strong payrolls, low unemployment, firm wages — keeps the Fed on hold and the 10-year anchored near 4.85%, which is the ceiling pressing on mortgage rates. Core PCE for October printed at +3.2% year-over-year, still running above the Fed's 2% target and not decelerating in a way that gives the bond market permission to rally. Headline CPI at +4.1% YoY was actually cooler than the consensus expected, which is a mild offset. The mechanism matters here: until core PCE moves toward 2.5% or lower, the bond market won't price in meaningful cuts, the 10-year won't fall, and the mortgage spread story is the only affordability lever available to buyers. The labor data has one shadow worth noting: the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose to 1.96 million in October, up 8.2% year-over-year for the third consecutive month, and median unemployment duration climbed to 11.9 weeks, up 17.8% YoY. This cohort does not affect the current selling season — these households are not buyers and are not yet delinquent
