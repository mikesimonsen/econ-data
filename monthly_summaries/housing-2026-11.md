# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held in positive year-over-year territory for the 56th consecutive week through early July per the Altos 13-week average, though the most recent four-week average came in at +3.6% YoY as the spot read decelerated to +5.9% — a signal that summer momentum is fading on schedule rather than collapsing. Inventory continues to build: Altos total active supply was running just above 1.08 million units, up slightly YoY, with the 13-week new listings average +1.5% YoY and rising for 14 straight weeks. That supply growth, while modest, is enough to keep pending days on market elevated — 70.8 days, up 6.2% YoY — meaning buyers have more runway to negotiate than they did a year ago. The October NAR existing sales print was the more important number this month: 3.75 million SAAR, down 8.8% YoY, missing the 3.85 million consensus by a full 100,000 units, and flagged as an unusually large monthly drop. The NSA read confirmed the weakness — 317,000 units, off 11.7% YoY. The West led the decline at -14.5% YoY NSA, and the South turned negative after a brief positive stretch. By contrast, new home sales through September held at 676,000 SAAR, only modestly lower YoY, as builders continued using rate buydowns to move inventory — the divergence between existing and new is now meaningful enough to bring up at listing appointments in markets where new construction competes. The rate picture is the central constraint: the 30-year fixed sat at 7.51% as of November 16, up 113 basis points YoY, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.39 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. The spread has been rising for 10 consecutive weeks on a four-week average basis, which means rates are being held higher by lender risk premiums, not just the Treasury market. If that spread compresses 40-50 basis points toward historical norms, the 30-year could fall to roughly 6.9-7.0% without any Fed action — that is the hidden affordability lever to watch. MBA purchase applications offered a single-week bounce (+7.5% week-over-week) but the four-week average remained down 18.5% YoY, its worst reading since January 2024. The Xactus MII — which typically leads MBA by 2-4 weeks — was down on its four-week average as of early July data, suggesting the MBA improvement is not yet confirmed by early-funnel inquiry activity. Watch the MII for any sustained turn before treating the MBA bounce as a trend.

The macro backdrop is where the story gets genuinely complicated. October payrolls came in at +165,000, well above the 59,000 consensus — a beat that reflects both genuine labor resilience and some recovery from prior-month weather disruptions. The unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1%, also better than the 4.4% expected. Normally that combination would support buyer confidence. The complication is what sits beneath it: the long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 weeks or more — rose 8.2% YoY to 1.96 million people, with median unemployment duration up nearly 18% YoY to 11.9 weeks. This cohort does not threaten the current selling season, but it is quietly accumulating the conditions for distressed-borrower pressure 6-12 months out, particularly in markets where equity cushions are thinner. On inflation, headline CPI at +4.1% YoY was actually cooler than the 0.9% monthly expectation, while core PCE through September held at +3.1% YoY — still above the Fed's target, still keeping the Fed anchored. Shelter CPI at +3.5% YoY continues to run hot, but the direction of travel is down, and as apartment lease rates (Zillow's ZORI fell 1.0% month-over-month in October, an unusually sharp drop) continue softening, shelter's lag contribution to CPI should ease into early 2027. The 10-year Treasury at 5.12% is the binding constraint on mortgage rates; at this level, even full spread compression to historical norms produces rates above 6.8%. Regional price data from Case-Shiller through August shows the two-speed market clearly: Chicago +6.6% YoY, New York +6.2%, Cleveland +4.3%, Miami +4.3% — supply-constrained metros continuing to appreciate. Meanwhile Dallas is -0.7%, Denver -0.8%, Seattle -2.0%, Tampa -0.3%, Las Vegas -1.1% — Sun Belt and Mountain West markets where the inventory correction is still working through prices. If you are listing in the Midwest or Northeast, the price story is different from the one your seller read in the national headlines. Watch the November 10-year yield trajectory: if it holds above 5.0%, the spring selling season will open with rates near current levels, and the existing-sales weakness seen in October will likely persist into Q1.
