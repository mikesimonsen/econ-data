**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held softer than the headline NAR print suggests. Altos weekly new pending sales were running +5.85% year-over-year as of early July — the 56th consecutive week of positive YoY readings — but the four-week average had decelerated to +3.57% and the period trend turned negative, a signal that the late-summer momentum was fading rather than building. The 13-week average, still up +5.48% YoY, confirms the underlying demand trend remains positive; the question is whether October's cooling was seasonal or the start of something more durable. Inventory on the Altos read was up roughly flat year-over-year at around 1.09 million units as of early July, with new listings running +8.5% YoY on a spot basis but only +1.2% on the four-week average — meaning new seller flow is inconsistent. Realtor.com's October active listing count was up +7.6% YoY and rising for seven consecutive months, while pending listings fell -10.4% YoY to their lowest level since September 2023, pushing the pending ratio down sharply. That gap between available supply and contracted demand is the clearest sign of market fatigue right now. Altos pending days on market have been running above year-ago levels for 104 consecutive weeks, and that clock is still ticking. The NAR October existing home sales print was 3.75 million SAAR — missing the 3.85 million consensus by 100,000 units, down -8.8% year-over-year, and the weakest reading since October 2023. The West was notably weak, off -14.5% YoY. New home sales (September, 676K SAAR) were also down -6.0% YoY, but builders still hold 8.4 months of supply — elevated, and consistent with continued price concessions. New home median prices are down -3.9% YoY even as existing home prices hold barely positive (+0.4% YoY on NAR median). The 30-year fixed is 7.48% today, up 110 basis points year-over-year — the single largest constraint on transaction volume. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.38 points on today's daily read and has been averaging 2.31-2.33 on a weekly basis, well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. If that spread compresses toward 2.0, rates fall meaningfully without any Fed action — that remains the hidden affordability lever most buyers and agents aren't watching. MBA purchase applications, however, are signaling real trouble: the four-week average is down -18.5% YoY, its worst reading since January 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus MII — which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks — was already rolling over in early July, with the four-week average reversing lower and the 13-week average falling for eight consecutive weeks. The MII gave the warning before MBA confirmed it; that pattern typically sustains for at least another month. Watch whether the MII stabilizes before December — if it doesn't, the January pending sales data will reflect it.

The macro backdrop is the central tension. October payrolls came in at +165,000, more than double the +59,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% against a 4.4% expectation — that is a labor market materially stronger than the forecast community expected. The mechanism for housing: job security supports hold decisions over list decisions, which partly explains why inventory is growing slowly despite high rates. Hourly earnings rose +0.4% month-over-month, hotter than expected, keeping nominal income growth near +3.1% YoY — that offsets some affordability erosion but not at 7.48% mortgage rates. The inflation picture is more complicated. Headline CPI was +4.1% YoY in October, but came in cooler than the BofA forecast of +0.9% month-over-month. Core PCE (September) was +3.1% YoY, still well above the Fed's target, rising for seven consecutive months. Shelter CPI at +3.5% YoY is decelerating — it lags real-time rent data by roughly 12-18 months, and Zillow's ZORI (October) was +0.6% YoY and falling for four consecutive months — so shelter's contribution to CPI should continue softening into 2027. That matters because it is one of the Fed's clearest green lights to ease. The long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 weeks or more — rose +8.2% year-over-year in October, with median unemployment duration up nearly 18% YoY. This cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is building the deferred delinquency story for late 2027.
