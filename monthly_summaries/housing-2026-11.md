**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through October but the leading indicators are flashing a meaningful deceleration. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has been in positive YoY territory for 56 consecutive weeks, running at roughly +5.5% YoY as of early July — a durable streak — but the 4-week average has slipped to +3.6% YoY and the spot week came in at +5.9%, so the trend is flattening rather than building. Inventory on the Altos side was still rising (up nine consecutive weeks, essentially flat YoY as of early July), while Realtor.com active listings hit +7.6% YoY in October, a 36-month positive streak that is still accelerating. New listings on Realtor.com, however, fell 7.3% YoY in October — the lowest count since September 2023 — which tells you sellers are stepping back as fall seasonality bites. Pending days on market have been running above year-ago levels for 104 consecutive weeks, so the pace of absorption has not improved even as demand has stayed nominally positive. NAR existing home sales for September printed 3.99 million SAAR, a small beat versus the 3.85 million consensus and a marginal uptick from August, but the NSA count was down 3.9% YoY — the cleaner read on direction. Months supply ticked down to 4.8 from a recent high, which provides some price support, but the market remains softer than the headline SAAR implies. New home sales fell to 676,000 SAAR in September, down 6% YoY, with builders' months supply still elevated at 8.4; single-family starts also reversed lower after several firmer months, and the September starts miss (1.221 million versus 1.350 million expected) was notable. Single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY — turning negative for the first time in several months — which is the cleaner supply signal: builders are pulling back on future pipeline even as they clear existing inventory. The rate picture is the most important variable for the next four weeks. The 30-year fixed sat at 7.17% as of November 5th, up 80 basis points YoY, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is running at roughly 2.35-2.36 points — well above the historical norm of about 1.7 points. The spread has not compressed; it has actually widened modestly over the past nine weeks on the 4-week average. If that spread were to normalize toward 1.9-2.0 points, the 30-year would fall meaningfully without any Fed action. Watch the spread, not just the Fed. The leading indicators for November are uninspiring: the MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 18% YoY — the weakest read since February 2024 — and has been falling for six consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks, was already softening in early July (the most recent read available), with its 13-week average down 1.5% YoY and deteriorating. When MII rolls over before MBA, you typically see purchase application weakness confirm within a month. That is the sequencing playing out right now.

The macro backdrop is genuinely two-sided, which makes this month's read harder than usual. October payrolls were a substantial beat — +165,000 versus a +59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, also well below the 4.4% expected. A strong labor market is the floor under housing demand; employed households form households and eventually buy. But the wage print came in hotter than expected at +0.4% month-over-month (+3.1% YoY), and core PCE is running at 3.1% YoY through September — both above the Fed's comfort zone. That inflation persistence is what is keeping the 10-year Treasury yield elevated at 4.84%, which in turn keeps mortgage rates above 7%. The mechanism is direct: until core PCE decelerates convincingly toward 2.5%, the bond market will not price in enough Fed cuts to bring the 10-year — and therefore mortgage rates — down materially. Shelter CPI continued to run at +3.2% YoY in September, moderating but still sticky, which will keep headline inflation elevated even if goods prices cool. There is one accumulating risk that is not visible in the current selling season but is worth tracking: the long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) rose again in October, now up 8.2% YoY over three consecutive months, and median unemployment duration hit 11.9 weeks, up 17.8% YoY. This cohort does not affect November closings, but it is the population that produces delinquency pressure in six to twelve months if it continues to build.
