**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through October but the leading indicators are flashing caution. Altos weekly new pending sales came in at +5.85% YoY for the week ending July 3 — the 56th consecutive week of positive year-over-year readings — and the 13-week average held at +5.48% YoY, still trending upward. The 4-week average, however, slipped to +3.57% YoY with the period change turning negative, a sign that the pace of improvement is decelerating even if the direction remains positive. Inventory continues to build: Altos total active supply rose for nine straight weeks and was essentially flat YoY (+0.02%), while new listings turned meaningfully positive at +8.46% YoY on a spot basis, though the 4-week and 13-week averages are more modest at +1.22% and +1.50% respectively — suggesting the spike is not yet a trend. Pending days on market ticked up 6.2% YoY, extending a 104-week streak of slower velocity; that's not a crisis reading, but it confirms sellers can no longer assume quick exits. NAR existing home sales came in at a 3.99 million SAAR for September — beating the 3.85 million consensus and reversing two months of declines — but the NSA count was down 3.9% YoY, which is the cleaner read given seasonal calendar shifts. New home sales (676K SAAR) fell back after a recent uptick and are now down nearly 6% YoY, with 8.4 months of supply — a meaningful contrast to the existing market's 4.8 months. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.27% as of November 2, up 93 basis points YoY. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, now at 2.38 points and widening over the past eight weeks — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. Rates are not high because the economy is overheating; they are high in part because the spread has not normalized. If it compresses toward 2.0 points without the 10-year moving, the 30-year could fall to roughly 6.85–6.90% on mechanics alone. That's the affordability lever agents should understand and explain to hesitant buyers. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 16.8% YoY — the weakest read since February 2024 — and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, has also rolled over: the 4-week MII average reversed lower and the 13-week has been falling for eight weeks, both tracking negative YoY. When MII and MBA are aligned to the downside like this, the near-term demand signal is genuinely soft. Watch whether the Altos 4-week pending average recovers in November or continues to decelerate — that will determine whether October's weakness is seasonal or something stickier.

The macro backdrop explains why mortgage rates refuse to cooperate. September payrolls came in at just +29,000 against a 59,000 consensus — a significant miss — but the unemployment rate also surprised, printing 4.2% against a 4.4% expectation, which muddied the signal. The result is a labor market that reads neither strong enough to push rates higher on growth grounds nor weak enough to compel a Fed pivot. What matters for housing is the composition underneath: the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose 7.1% YoY to 1.944 million in September, and median unemployment duration hit 13.9% YoY growth. These borrowers are not yet missing mortgage payments, but a cohort of this size that has been unemployed for more than half a year historically presages a delinquency uptick in the 6–12 month window. That is not a 2026 selling-season story — but it is a 2027 distressed-inventory story worth tracking. On inflation, Core PCE held at 3.11% YoY in September, running above the Fed's 2% target for the seventh straight month. Shelter CPI remains sticky at +3.22% YoY, though private-market rents (Zillow ZORI) fell 0.82% in September and are up only 1.42% YoY — the lag between real-time rents and CPI shelter typically runs 12–18 months, so there is disinflationary pressure still in the pipeline that has not yet shown up in the Fed's preferred measures. Regionally, the Case-Shiller August data (released October 27, so reflecting closings largely from June–July) reinforces the two-speed market: Chicago at +6.6% YoY and New York at +6.2% YoY continue to pull away from the Sun Belt, where Dallas fell back to −0.7%, Las Vegas declined to −1.1% for a 12th consecutive negative month, and Tampa remained negative at −0.3% for the 22nd straight month.
