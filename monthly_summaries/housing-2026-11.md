# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held in positive year-over-year territory through early November, with the Altos 13-week average running at +5.5% YoY as of the week ending July 3 — a streak that had extended to 56 consecutive positive weeks before the most recent four-week average softened to +3.6% YoY and the spot read ticked lower to +5.9% YoY. That deceleration in the 4-week average bears watching; it is not a reversal, but the momentum has come down from the mid-single-digit pace that characterized late summer. Active inventory stood near 1.09 million units (Altos), up just fractionally year-over-year, while new listings ran +8.5% YoY on a spot basis though the 13-week average was only +1.5% YoY — suggesting the new-listing acceleration was not yet structural. Pending days on market were 70.8 days, +6.2% YoY, a signal that absorption is slower than a year ago even as demand remains nominally positive. The October NAR existing home sales print came in at 3.75 million SAAR, missing the consensus of 3.85 million and down 8.8% YoY on an NSA basis — the weakest reading since October 2023. The West was the softest region, off 15.2% YoY NSA, and the South's unusual two-standard-deviation drop did most of the damage to the headline. New home sales (through September) were running at 676,000 SAAR, down 6.0% YoY, so the existing-home weakness is not an isolated channel. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.45% as of today, essentially flat on the month, with the 10-year Treasury at 5.05% — implying a 30Y-to-10Y spread of roughly 240 basis points, well above the historical norm of around 170 basis points. That spread has been widening on a 4-week average basis for 11 consecutive weeks and is now 13 basis points above year-ago levels. The rate level is the visible affordability problem; the spread is the hidden one. If mortgage-backed-security pricing normalizes even partway toward historical norms, the 30-year could fall 50-60 basis points without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications (4-week average through November 13) were off 18.6% YoY, the weakest reading since January 2024, and the 13-week average has been falling for 14 straight weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks — had its 4-week average rolling over as of early October, so the near-term MBA picture is unlikely to improve materially heading into December. On the supply side, October single-family permits fell to 772,000 SAAR, down 12.9% YoY and the lowest since April 2023; that is the signal that matters for 2027 resale competition, not this selling season. Starts bounced in October but that move is clearing backlog, not new commitments. Watch the December permit read and whether the Altos 4-week pending average stabilizes or continues to soften.

The macro backdrop is doing two things simultaneously, and they point in opposite directions for housing. On labor, October payrolls came in at +165,000, well above the 59,000 consensus — a genuine beat that reduces near-term recession risk and supports household formation. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, also better than expected. The quits rate ticked up to 2.1% in September, which typically correlates with consumer willingness to make large discretionary purchases including home moves; that is a mild positive for transaction volume into 2027. The shadow, however, is the long-term unemployed: the cohort unemployed 27 weeks or longer has risen 8.2% YoY over three consecutive months, with median unemployment duration at 11.9 weeks and up 17.8% YoY. That cohort does not threaten the current selling season, but it is the population most likely to produce delinquencies and distressed listings 6 to 12 months from now — worth flagging if you work markets already carrying elevated months of supply. On inflation, October headline CPI at +4.1% YoY is running well above target, and Core PCE through September was +3.1% YoY — both sticky enough that the Fed has no near-term cover to cut. The 10-year at 5.05% reflects that pricing. Shelter CPI was +3.5% YoY in October, still the largest single contributor to core; as actual rent growth (Zillow ZORI at +0.6% YoY) feeds through the BLS lag, shelter should decelerate further in early 2027, which will give the Fed more room to move. Regionally, the Case-Shiller August data (which always lags by roughly two months) confirms the two-speed market: Chicago +6.6% YoY and New York +6.2% YoY lead the index, while Dallas is in negative territory at -0.7%, Seattle at -2.0%, Tampa at -0
