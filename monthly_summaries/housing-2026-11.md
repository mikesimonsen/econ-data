**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but decelerated meaningfully over the past four weeks. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales remained in positive territory at +5.48% YoY — now 56 consecutive weeks of year-over-year gains — but the 4-week average slipped to +3.57% YoY and the most recent weekly read ticked down to +5.85%, with the 4-week trend clearly decelerating. That's not a reversal, but it is a signal worth watching. Inventory on the Altos side held near 1.09 million active listings, essentially flat year-over-year (+0.02%), with new listings running +8.46% YoY on the spot week — though the 4-week and 13-week averages remain far more modest at +1.22% and +1.50% respectively, suggesting the spot spike is noise rather than a new supply wave. NAR's September existing home sales printed at 3.99 million SAAR, a modest uptick that beat expectations of 3.85 million, though the NSA read was down 3.9% YoY — a reminder that the headline number benefits from seasonal adjustment in a soft month. New home sales told a quieter story: 676,000 SAAR in September, down 6% YoY, with builder supply still elevated at 8.4 months. Single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY and have now turned negative after months of positive readings, while starts missed badly at 1,221,000 versus an expected 1,350,000 — builders are completing their existing pipeline but visibly pulling back on future commitments, which matters more for 2027 supply than the current selling season. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.25% as of today, having ticked down slightly over the past few days, but the more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: 2.36 percentage points, against a historical norm near 1.70. That 66-basis-point excess spread is the hidden affordability story — if it compresses even partially toward normal as credit markets stabilize, mortgage rates can fall meaningfully without any Fed action. The leading indicators are more cautionary: MBA purchase applications came in at a 4-week average of -17.96% YoY, the weakest since February 2024 and falling for six consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks — printed at +1.56% YoY on its spot read but the 4-week average was -1.59% and the 13-week has been falling for eight weeks, suggesting the MBA weakness is not yet bottoming. Watch whether MBA stabilizes or continues lower through mid-November; that will tell you whether the pending sales deceleration in Altos becomes something more serious heading into December.

The macro backdrop explains much of the current hesitation. September payrolls came in at roughly +29,000 — well below the +59,000 consensus — and while the unemployment rate beat at 4.2% versus an expected 4.4%, the softer employment gain matters more for housing demand because it reflects fewer new households with the confidence to buy. The long-term unemployed (27-plus weeks) rose to 1.944 million, up 7.1% YoY and now two months into a renewed climb; this cohort does not affect the current selling season but is the early-warning indicator for future mortgage stress if it continues to accumulate. On inflation, Core PCE came in at +3.11% YoY in September — still comfortably above the Fed's 2% target — and the 10-year Treasury is at 4.89%, which at current spread levels anchors the 30-year right where it is. The Fed has limited room to cut without inflation re-accelerating, and the bond market knows it; breakevens (the inflation expectations embedded in TIPS pricing) have not given the Treasury yield much room to fall. The regional price picture from Case-Shiller through August sharpens this: Chicago (+6.6% YoY), New York (+6.2%), and Cleveland (+4.3%) are still appreciating at a pace that agents in those markets can defend in a listing conversation, while Dallas (-0.7%), Seattle (-2.0%), Las Vegas (-1.1%), and Tampa (-0.3%) remain in negative YoY territory — the two-speed market is not collapsing in the Sun Belt but it is not recovering quickly either, with months supply still elevated and price-reduced share ticking up on Realtor.com. The one macro signal to watch most closely next month is Core PCE for October, due in late November: if it prints above 3.1% again, rate cut expectations get pushed further out, the 10-year holds near 5%, and the mortgage rate relief that would restart demand stays out of reach through year-end.
