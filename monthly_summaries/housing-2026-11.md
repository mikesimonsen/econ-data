**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive on a year-over-year basis through the most recent Altos read, with the 13-week average of new pending sales running roughly +5.5% above year-ago levels and the streak of positive YoY weeks extending to 56 consecutive weeks — though the four-week average decelerated to +3.6% YoY and the weekly figure ticked down on a period basis, a pattern consistent with normal seasonal withdrawal rather than a demand break. Inventory continued building: Altos active listings were running just above flat YoY in early July (the most recent weekly read), while Realtor.com active listings were up 7.6% YoY through October, with pending listings down 10.4% YoY — a ratio that implies supply is arriving faster than it is being absorbed. Pending days on market remained elevated, running about 6% above year-ago levels for over 100 consecutive weeks, which is a steady signal that buyers have more time to decide than they did in 2021-22. The October NAR existing home sales print came in at 3.75 million SAAR, missing the 3.85 million consensus by 100,000 units and landing 8.76% below October 2025 on the NSA YoY comparison — the weakest pace since October 2023. The West was the softest region, down 15.2% NSA YoY; the South fell 10.8% NSA YoY. New home sales through September ran at 676,000 SAAR, down 6% YoY, though months' supply eased slightly to 8.4, suggesting builders are selling through standing inventory. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.36% as of today, up 100 basis points from a year ago and still well above the level that would unlock rate-locked sellers. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.45 percentage points — historically that spread runs around 1.70 pp, meaning roughly 75 basis points of the current rate is spread, not yield. If credit conditions normalize toward that historical norm without any Treasury move, the 30-year falls to the low-to-mid 6s without the Fed touching a thing. That is the affordability story agents should understand heading into 2027. MBA purchase applications, however, are not confirming a demand recovery: the four-week average is down 18.6% YoY, the worst reading since January 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus MII — which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks — was itself softening in early July, so the MBA weakness is not a surprise. Single-family permits fell 12.9% YoY in October to their lowest level since April 2023, even as single-family starts ticked up 4% on the month. That starts-permits divergence tells you builders are completing pipeline they already authorized and pulling back meaningfully on future authorizations — a supply constraint that matters more for 2027-2028 than for the current selling season. Watch whether the MBA four-week average stabilizes or extends its decline through the Thanksgiving-adjusted weeks ahead.

The macro picture is genuinely mixed in its housing implications, and it is worth separating what helps from what doesn't. October payrolls printed at +165,000 — well above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating the 4.4% expectation. Those are supportive numbers for housing demand at the margin: employed households form and buy. The hires rate ticked up to 3.4% and the quits rate rose to 2.1% in September, both moving in directions that historically correlate with consumer willingness to make large purchases. But the long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 weeks or more — has risen 8.2% YoY to 1.963 million and the median duration of unemployment is up 17.8% YoY. That is not a 2026 selling-season problem; it is a 2027 mortgage-stress and potential-distressed-inventory story worth tracking. On inflation: headline CPI at +4.1% YoY is running hot, and core PCE at +3.1% YoY is above the Fed's target, limiting the case for near-term rate cuts. Shelter CPI at +3.5% YoY is decelerating gradually, which is consistent with what the Zillow rent index (+0.6% YoY) has been signaling for over a year — the disinflation in asking rents is slowly working its way into the official series.
