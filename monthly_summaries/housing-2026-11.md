# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive on a year-over-year basis through the most recent Altos read, with the spot week at +5.9% YoY and the four-week average at +3.6% YoY — extending a 56-consecutive-week streak of positive YoY pending sales. The 13-week average is running +5.5% YoY and continues to improve, which suggests the underlying demand trend is intact even as the seasonal deceleration that typically arrives in November takes hold. Inventory rose for a ninth consecutive week, with Altos total active supply up roughly even with a year ago (+0.02% YoY); new listings on a 13-week average basis are +1.5% YoY and rising for a 14th straight week, so sellers are coming to market in modest but consistent numbers. Pending days on market ticked to 70.8 days, +6.2% YoY — a steady reminder that homes are sitting longer than they were two years ago, even where the transaction count is positive. NAR October existing home sales printed at 3.75 million SAAR, missing the 3.85 million consensus by a full 100,000 units and falling 8.8% YoY on the NSA count — the weakest pace since October 2023 and an unusually large single-month step down. The October softness was broadest in the West (-15.2% YoY NSA) and South (-10.8%), while the Northeast held relatively better (-8.7%). Months' supply ticked to 5.1, up 15.9% YoY, the fastest YoY expansion in over a year — a number worth quoting at listing appointments in softening markets. New home sales (September, the most recent read) came in at 676K SAAR, also off nearly 6% YoY, so the existing/new divergence has closed: both channels are contracting. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.51% as of today, up 113 basis points from a year ago and still rising on a five-day streak. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.39 percentage points on today's daily read (weekly average 2.31pp) — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7pp. That gap matters: if the spread returned to normal, the 30-year would be closer to 6.8% with the 10-year where it is; it has actually been widening slightly over the past 10 weeks rather than compressing, so the hidden affordability relief agents might hope for is not arriving yet. MBA purchase applications offered a one-week reprieve — the index rose 7.5% week-over-week in the November 6 reading — but the four-week average is -18.5% YoY, the weakest since January 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-pull-based leading indicator that tends to precede MBA by two to four weeks) also showed a mid-summer softening in its four-week average, corroborating rather than contradicting the MBA weakness. On the builder side, single-family permits fell to 846K SAAR in September, turning negative YoY for the first time in several months, while starts missed expectations badly at 1,221K total (versus a 1,350K consensus). Builders are completing the existing pipeline — total completions ticked up in September — but pulling back on new authorizations, which limits incremental supply in 2027. Watch the October permits release and whether MBA's four-week average stabilizes above -15% YoY heading into December.

The macro backdrop is genuinely conflicted, and the conflict matters for how you frame conditions with clients. October payrolls came in at +165K — a substantial beat against the +59K consensus — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% against a 4.4% expectation, so the labor market is not the demand-destruction story. The quits rate rose to 2.1% in September (up 0.2pp month-over-month), which historically correlates with household formation and willingness to make housing moves, so that is a mild positive. The problem is on the rate and inflation side: headline CPI rose 4.1% YoY in October (cooler than the 0.9% MoM consensus, but still running hot on an annual basis), core PCE is at 3.1% YoY through September, and shelter CPI is +3.5% YoY — still elevated but decelerating. As long as core PCE sits above 3%, the Fed's path to easing is narrow, and the 10-year Treasury at 5.12% (up 99bp YoY) reflects that reality. That is the mechanism: elevated inflation keeps the Fed on hold, the 10-year stays high, and without spread compression the 30-year stays north of 7.5% — a level at which a large share of would-be sellers are locked into sub-4% mortgages they will not willingly abandon.
