# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive ground through October but the trajectory deserves a close read. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has run positive year-over-year for 56 consecutive weeks, and the most recent weekly read came in at +5.9% YoY — a figure that looks encouraging on its face. The 4-week average tells a more cautious story at +3.6% YoY, down from stronger readings earlier in the quarter, and the 4-week average has been falling for five weeks. Inventory continues to rise — Altos total active supply was up year-over-year in early October, with new listings running +8.5% YoY on a spot basis and +1.2% on the 4-week average — a pace that is slowly outrunning demand absorption. Pending days on market have been positive YoY for 104 consecutive weeks, sitting at 70.8 days, which tells you homes are not moving faster even as the YoY demand streak continues. NAR existing home sales for September printed at 3.99 million SAAR, edging past the 3.85 million consensus, but on an NSA basis sales fell 3.9% YoY — consistent with the underlying softness the SAAR smoothing obscures. New home sales came in at 676K SAAR, off nearly 6% YoY, with months supply at 8.4 — elevated enough that builders are still working down existing pipeline rather than committing to future production. Total starts fell 8% YoY and missed consensus by a wide margin; single-family permits turned negative YoY for the first time in several months, a signal worth watching because it implies the new-supply cushion that has helped affordability in Sun Belt markets begins thinning 12–18 months out. On rates: the 30-year fixed sits at 7.30% as of November 3, up slightly from recent lows, while the 10-year Treasury has firmed to 4.95%. The 30Y–10Y spread is 2.35 percentage points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap is the number to track: if it compresses even 30–40 basis points (the spread has edged down slightly this week), the 30-year rate falls meaningfully without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications are the most direct leading indicator right now, and they are not confirming the Altos demand optimism — the 4-week average is down 16.8% YoY and sitting at its lowest since February 2024. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by 2–4 weeks, turned positive YoY on a spot basis (+1.6%) in early October but its 4-week and 13-week averages remain negative — a split reading that says the floor may be forming but no sustained recovery is yet underway. Watch whether MBA's 4-week average begins to stabilize or worsens through November; that will tell you whether October's Altos pending strength was real demand or seasonal noise.

The macro backdrop is doing two things simultaneously, and they point in opposite directions. On labor: September payrolls came in at roughly +29,000 — a significant miss against the +59,000 consensus — while the unemployment rate ticked up to 4.2%. The long-term unemployed (27 weeks or more) rose to 1.944 million, up 7.1% YoY and climbing for two consecutive months. These are not people missing mortgage payments today, but this cohort historically leads distressed-borrower volume by 6–12 months; agents in markets already running elevated days on market should note it. The modest counterweight is that quits rose sharply in September — the quits rate moved up 0.2 points to 2.1% — which suggests voluntary labor mobility has not locked up, and voluntary movers are a meaningful driver of listing supply and trade-up demand. On inflation: Core PCE ran at 3.11% YoY through September, and CPI shelter held at 3.22% YoY — both above where the Fed wants them, and both creating the conditions that keep the 10-year elevated and, by extension, keep mortgage rates pinned. The spread between the 30-year fixed and the 10-year — not the Fed funds rate — is the more immediate affordability variable, and it has been rising for eight weeks on a 4-week average basis. Regional price data from Case-Shiller (through August) underscores the two-speed market agents are navigating: Chicago is up 6.6% YoY and New York up 6.2%, while Dallas is down 0.7%, Las Vegas down 1.1%, Seattle down 2.0%, and Tampa negative for 22 consecutive months. The price narrative you bring to a listing appointment should not come from the national index — it should come from which of those two worlds your market is living in. The signal to watch next month is whether Core PCE decelerates toward 2.8–2.9%; if it does, rate-cut expectations will firm and Treasury yields may ease, which is the most direct path to spread compression and a lower 30-year rate without waiting on the labor market to deteriorate further.
