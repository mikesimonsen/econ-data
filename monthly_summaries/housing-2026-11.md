**November 2026 Housing Summary**

New pending sales held positive on a year-over-year basis for the week ending July 3 — the 56th consecutive week in positive territory on the 13-week average — but the trajectory is softening. The 4-week average YoY came in at +3.6%, down from recent readings, and the spot week printed +5.9% YoY even as the raw count fell for the fourth straight week, a normal seasonal pattern. The 13-week average, the cleanest signal for trend, sits at +5.5% YoY and continues to decelerate. Inventory has grown modestly, with Altos total supply running essentially flat YoY (+0.02%) and new listings up roughly 8% YoY on a spot basis, though the 4-week and 13-week averages are running closer to +1–2% YoY — meaning the new-listings pop is not yet building a sustained supply overhang. Pending days on market at 70.8 days are running 6% above last year, a quiet signal that buyers are taking more time. The October NAR existing sales print was the more important data point this month: 3.75 million SAAR, down 8.8% YoY, missing consensus by 100,000 units, and statistically unusual — the South and West both registered drops in excess of two standard deviations. The NSA YoY read confirms the direction: -11.7% year-over-year. Months of supply ticked to 5.1, up nearly 16% YoY, the fastest acceleration in this cycle. New home sales (676K SAAR through September) are softer too, down 6% YoY, but new home inventory at 8.4 months supply means builders are still carrying meaningful spec stock. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.46% as of today, up roughly 108 basis points from a year ago and effectively unchanged over the past month. The mortgage-Treasury spread — the gap between the 30-year rate and the 10-year yield, historically around 1.7 percentage points — is at 2.39 points and has been rising for 11 consecutive weeks. That spread widening is what is keeping rates elevated even as the 10-year itself has drifted; compression back toward 2.0 would bring the 30-year to roughly 7.1% without any move in Treasury yields. MBA purchase applications are the clearest warning light right now: the 4-week average is down 18.6% YoY, the worst reading since January 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which measures credit-pull activity and tends to lead MBA by 2–4 weeks — showed its spot reading turn negative in early July even as the 4-week average was still holding, a divergence that has since been confirmed by the MBA slide. Building permits are the forward supply signal worth watching: single-family permits fell to 772,000 SAAR in October, down 12.9% YoY and the lowest since April 2023, well below the 1,410,000 consensus expectation. Starts bounced, but permits lead starts by 1–3 months; the permit collapse implies single-family starts will contract into early 2027. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the MBA 4-week average stabilizes above its current -18% YoY floor, and whether the 10-year Treasury holds near 5.07% or moves — that yield level is the governor on everything else.

The macro backdrop this month is genuinely two-sided, which makes it harder to read than a clean deterioration. October payrolls came in at +165,000, more than double the +59,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% against a 4.4% expectation — that is a strong labor print on its face, and a healthy labor market is the single most important foundation for housing demand. The problem is that the gains are not fully filtering into housing confidence because rates are absorbing the good news: a stronger economy keeps the Fed on hold longer, which keeps the 10-year elevated, which keeps the spread-elevated 30-year at 7.46%. Core PCE through September ran at 3.1% YoY — still above the Fed's 2% target — and October CPI came in at 4.1% headline YoY, though the monthly print of +0.3% was cooler than the 0.9% BofA had flagged as a risk. Shelter CPI at +3.5% YoY is decelerating, which matters because it is the stickiest component of core inflation; as market rents (Zillow's ZORI fell to $1,913, down nearly 1% month-over-month) continue rolling into the lagged CPI measurement, shelter's drag on overall inflation should diminish over the next two to three quarters. That is the path by which core inflation drifts toward the Fed's target without a recession — but it is a slow path.
