**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive for another month but the numbers carry a caveat worth flagging at your next listing appointment. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has been in positive YoY territory for 56 consecutive weeks — that streak is real — but the most recent weekly read (+5.85% YoY as of early July) and the 4-week average (+3.57% YoY) are both decelerating, and the weekly figure has fallen four straight weeks in absolute terms. Pending days on market sits +6% YoY at roughly 71 days, which signals that the pool of active buyers is thinner even when the YoY count is positive. Inventory on the Altos side was running just barely above flat YoY (+0.02%) as of early July, while Realtor.com's active count reached +7.6% YoY through October — that gap reflects timing, but the direction is clear: supply is rebuilding. New listings on Realtor.com fell 7.3% YoY in October, their lowest count since September 2023, so inventory growth is coming from slower absorption, not a fresh wave of sellers. NAR's October existing home sales print landed at 3.75 million SAAR, missing the 3.85 million consensus by 100,000 units and sitting 8.76% below a year ago on an NSA basis — the weakest reading since October 2023. The West was the softest region, down 15% YoY NSA; the South was not far behind. New home sales (September, the most recent read) came in at 676,000 SAAR, down 6% YoY, with months supply at 8.4 — elevated but no longer rising rapidly. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.32% today, up 100 basis points from a year ago, and that is where the real story is: the spread between the 30-year and the 10-year Treasury is 2.41 points, well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That 70-basis-point excess premium means rates can fall materially if credit markets normalize, without the Fed moving at all — but right now the spread is actually widening on a 4-week average basis, not compressing. MBA purchase applications are down 18.6% YoY on the 4-week average, the weakest level since January 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen 14 straight weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which typically leads MBA applications by two to four weeks — showed a spot reading that turned positive YoY in early July but its 4-week and 13-week averages are still negative and rolling lower, so there is no early confirmation of a coming rebound in applications. Single-family permits fell to their lowest since April 2023, down 12.9% YoY — builders are pulling back meaningfully on future pipeline. Starts bounced in October but the permit signal is what matters for 2027 supply. Watch the spread and the MII over the next four weeks: if the spread begins compressing toward 2.1–2.2, the rate relief story has teeth; if the MII stays negative on the 4-week average, the application weakness persists into January.

The macro backdrop is the reason rates are where they are, and it deserves more than a headline read. October payrolls came in at +165,000 — well above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, also better than expected. A labor market that continues to add jobs at that pace keeps the Fed on hold. The problem for housing is the combination of that labor resilience with still-elevated inflation: headline CPI ran +4.1% YoY in October, core PCE was +3.1% YoY through September, and shelter CPI — which lags actual rent by roughly 12–18 months — is still running +3.5% YoY and rising, which keeps core inflation elevated even as market rents (Zillow's ZORI fell 0.97% in October, a statistically unusual drop) are softening. The mechanism is straightforward: as long as core PCE stays above 3%, the Fed is not cutting, the 10-year stays near 4.9%, and a 2.4-point spread keeps the 30-year above 7%. Average hourly earnings at +3.1% YoY provide some affordability offset, but not enough at a 7.32% rate on a $416,700 median price. The one labor signal that does not show up in the headline unemployment rate but belongs in your mental model: the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose to 1.96 million in October, up 8.2% YoY and rising for three straight months. That cohort does not affect this selling season, but it is where delinquency pressure builds over a 6-to-12-month lag — worth tracking if you work markets already carrying elevated inventory. On prices, the September Case-Shiller data (reference period: through September) confirms the two-speed market. Chicago is +6.9% YoY, New York +5.9%, Miami +4.6%, and San Francisco +3.
