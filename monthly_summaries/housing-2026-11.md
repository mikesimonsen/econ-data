# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales tracked by Altos held positive on a year-over-year basis through the week ending July 3 — the 56th consecutive week in positive territory on the 13-week average — with the spot reading at +5.9% YoY and the four-week average at +3.6% YoY, though that four-week figure has been decelerating as the spot reading fell for four consecutive weeks before the most recent uptick. The 13-week average remained constructive at +5.5% YoY and was still trending higher, which matters more than the week-to-week chop. Total active inventory stood near 1.09 million units, up roughly 0% YoY on the Altos read — essentially flat — while new listings showed some life at +8.5% YoY on the spot and +1.2% on the four-week average; the 13-week new-listings average has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks, suggesting sellers are gradually returning without yet overwhelming demand. Pending days on market at 70.8 days ran +6.2% YoY, a signal that absorption is loosening at the margin. The October NAR existing sales print landed at 3.75 million SAAR, missing the 3.85 million consensus by a full 100,000 units, down 8.8% YoY on a NSA basis and at the weakest pace since October 2023 — the West was the most strained region, off 15.2% NSA YoY. Months supply ticked to 5.1, up nearly 16% YoY, reinforcing that the market is rebalancing toward buyers more quickly than many expected. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.45% as of today, essentially unchanged over the past week but up 109 basis points year-over-year — and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.40 points, well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That 70-basis-point excess spread is the number agents should internalize: if credit and volatility conditions normalize even partially, the 30-year could fall toward the low-to-mid 6s without any Fed action. The MBA purchase index four-week average is running -18.6% YoY, its weakest since January 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks — that is a persistent demand headwind, not a blip. The Xactus MII, which typically leads MBA applications by two to four weeks, also turned lower in early July after a brief positive stretch, suggesting the MBA weakness is not yet bottoming. Builder permits tell a cautionary forward story: single-family permits fell to 772,000 SAAR in October, down 12.9% YoY and the lowest since April 2023, even as single-family starts ticked up to 864,000. Builders are completing existing pipeline — single-family completions rose 6.3% in October — but pulling back sharply on future authorizations. That starts-permits divergence implies new supply will tighten in 2027, not 2026. Watch whether the MII stabilizes over the next four weeks; if it does not recover into positive YoY territory, the MBA purchase index likely stays under pressure through year-end.

The macro backdrop explains why demand is struggling despite a labor market that, on the surface, looks fine. October payrolls came in at +165,000, well above the 59,000 consensus — a genuine beat — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, also better than the 4.4% expected. But the signal beneath those headlines is less clean: the long-term unemployed (27-plus weeks) now number 1.96 million, up 8.2% YoY and rising for three consecutive months, and median unemployment duration has extended to 11.9 weeks, up 17.8% YoY. That cohort is not distorting the headline rate yet, but it is accumulating steadily; households in long-term unemployment are the future delinquency pipeline, and agents in higher-loan-balance markets should note it. The inflation picture is mixed in its housing implications. Core PCE ran at 3.1% YoY through September, and headline CPI printed 4.1% YoY in October — still well above the Fed's target — which is why the 10-year Treasury sits at 5.05% and the 30-year mortgage rate is 7.45%. CPI shelter decelerated slightly to +3.5% YoY; as that series continues to converge toward real-time rent data (Zillow ZORI is running just +0.6% YoY), it will put modest downward pressure on core CPI in coming months, giving the Fed slightly more room. But with average hourly earnings running +3.1% YoY and core PCE elevated, the Fed is unlikely to pivot quickly. The Case-Shiller data through August (released October 27) confirmed the two-speed market: Chicago +6.6% YoY, New York +6.2%, Cleveland +4.3%, Miami +4.3% — all appreciating firmly — while Dallas is -0.7% YoY, Denver -0.8%, Seattle -2.
