# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos weekly demand read through early July showed new pending sales running +5.85% year-over-year on a spot basis, with the four-week average at +3.57% YoY — the 13-week average has held positive for 56 consecutive weeks, currently sitting at +5.48% YoY and still in an improving trend. That is a durable demand signal, even as the spot and four-week reads have been decelerating off their summer highs. Inventory rose to roughly 1.09 million units, up just 0.02% YoY — supply growth has essentially stalled relative to where it was tracking earlier in the year. New listings ticked +8.46% YoY on a spot basis, though the four-week average is only +1.22% YoY, which suggests a single strong week rather than an accelerating supply wave; the 13-week average at +1.50% YoY confirms modest, not meaningful, supply expansion. Pending days on market held at 70.8 days, up 6.2% YoY — still elevated, still telling us homes are sitting longer than a year ago, but not materially worse than last month. September existing home sales printed at 3.99 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus and snapping a run of declines — though the NSA read at 341,000 actual closings was still down 3.9% YoY, a more honest picture of underlying volume. New home sales told a softer story at 676,000 SAAR, off 6% YoY, with 8.4 months of supply — builders are sitting on elevated finished inventory and their pricing reflects it: median new home prices are down 3.9% YoY. The 30-year fixed rate stands at 7.27% as of October 30, up 94 basis points from a year ago. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.32-2.34 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap is the affordability story most agents are missing: the rate problem is not just the Fed, it is the spread. If credit markets normalize even partially and the spread compresses toward 1.9-2.0 points, the 30-year falls meaningfully without a single Fed cut. The MBA purchase index four-week average is down 16.8% YoY and has fallen for five consecutive weeks — the weakest reading since February 2024. The Xactus MII, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, turned negative period-over-period in early July with its four-week average also rolling over; when MII leads MBA lower, as it did here, the subsequent MBA weakness is not a surprise. Watch whether the MII stabilizes or continues falling — that will be the clearest early signal of whether the November purchase market firms or softens further. Single-family permits fell 3.9% YoY in September and turned negative for the first time in several months; starts fell 8.1% on a total basis and missed expectations by a wide margin. Builders are clearing their existing pipeline — completions are still running — but pulling back on future authorizations. That matters for 2027 supply, not the current selling season.

The September jobs report delivered the most consequential macro miss of the month: payrolls came in at roughly +29,000 against a +59,000 consensus, with average hourly earnings rising only 0.1% versus the expected 0.3%. A weak payrolls print accompanied by softening wage growth is a mixed signal for housing — it reduces the inflationary pressure that keeps the Fed on hold, but it also undermines the household income growth that supports purchase demand, particularly at the current entry-level price points where debt-service ratios are most stressed. Unemployment ticked up to 4.2%, better than the 4.4% consensus feared, but the more telling figure is the long-term unemployed: workers out 27 weeks or more rose 7.1% YoY in September and median unemployment duration is up nearly 14% YoY. This cohort does not affect the current selling season — they are not buying homes — but they are the seed of future mortgage delinquency volume. Watch this number accumulate. Core PCE ran at 3.11% YoY through September, above the Fed's 2% target, and shelter CPI remained sticky at 3.22% YoY. The 10-year Treasury closed October 30 at 4.95%, up 84 basis points YoY — that is the anchor keeping the 30-year rate elevated and the primary reason affordability has not improved despite the spread compression narrative. On prices, the Case-Shiller data through August (released October 27) confirms the two-speed market is intact: Chicago +6.6% YoY, New York +6.2%, Cleveland +4.3%, Miami +4.3% — tight-inventory markets with constrained new supply — while Dallas sits at -0.7%, Las Vegas at -1.1%, Seattle at -2.0%, and Tampa at -0.3%, all markets where builders and investors added supply aggressively during the pandemic years and are now working off the overhang.
