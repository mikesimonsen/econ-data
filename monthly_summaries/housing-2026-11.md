**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive on a year-over-year basis through the most recent Altos read, with the new pending sales 13-week average sitting at a +5.5% YoY gain and the streak of consecutive positive YoY weeks extending to 56 — a run that has persisted through two full rate cycles. The 4-week average came in at +3.6% YoY, decelerating from earlier in the fall, which is worth watching but not yet alarming given typical November seasonal softness. Inventory on the Realtor.com tracker stood at 1.18 million active listings, up 7.6% YoY and rising for seven consecutive months — supply is building, but gradually. New listings have actually pulled back, down 7.3% YoY on the Realtor.com series and falling for six straight months, which means the inventory growth is coming from homes sitting longer rather than fresh supply entering. Pending days on market confirmed that dynamic, running 6.2% above last year. NAR existing home sales for October came in at a 3.75 million SAAR, missing the 3.85 million consensus by a full 100,000 units and down 8.8% YoY — the weakest print since October 2023. The West was the softest region, off 14.5% YoY on an NSA basis. That weakness stands in notable contrast to new home sales, which printed 701,000 SAAR for October, up 7.5% YoY and reversing several months of declines; builders are clearly absorbing demand that existing-home sellers cannot capture. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.29% as of today, ticking back up after a brief retreat, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury (which closed at 4.94%) is running about 2.35-2.41 percentage points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That excess spread is the hidden affordability story: if it compresses toward normal as lender pipelines rebuild, rates fall meaningfully without any Fed action required. MBA purchase applications offered a confusing signal this week — the weekly index jumped 10% and hit +10.2% week-over-week, but the 4-week average remains down 18% YoY and the 13-week average is at its lowest since June 2024. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (which leads MBA by two to four weeks by capturing credit-pull activity before applications are submitted) was running slightly positive YoY in early October but showed its own deceleration; watch whether December's MII reading confirms or contradicts the late-November MBA spike before treating it as a demand turn. Single-family permits fell 12.3% YoY in October to their lowest level since April 2023 — builders are clearing the existing pipeline rather than committing to future starts, which matters more for 2027 supply than the current selling season.

The macro backdrop is genuinely two-sided. October payrolls came in at +165,000 — well above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, both pointing to a labor market that keeps qualified buyers employed and limits forced selling. The quits rate also ticked up to 2.1% in September, which historically correlates with household formation and move-up buying confidence. The complication is inflation: core PCE ran at 3.2% YoY in October, still above the Fed's 2% target, and CPI shelter remains elevated at +3.5% YoY — a figure that feeds directly into the Fed's hesitancy to cut and therefore keeps the 10-year anchored near 4.9%. With the 30-year at 7.29%, affordability remains the primary transaction brake; each 25 basis points of rate relief translates to roughly $60-70 per month on a $400,000 mortgage, which is not trivial for move-up buyers at the margin. On the regional picture, September Case-Shiller (the most recent data, covering closings roughly from July-August) shows Chicago still running +6.9% YoY and New York at +5.9%, while Dallas is -0.8%, Denver -1.0%, and Seattle -2.1% — the Midwest/Northeast versus Sun Belt divergence remains intact and is the most agent-actionable price story in the data. Miami is an outlier worth noting: +4.6% YoY and accelerating, with its September monthly gain the highest since August 2024. The signal to watch next month is whether the 10-year yield holds below 5% or pushes through it — a sustained break above 5% would put the 30-year toward 7.5% and further compress the already-thin buyer pool heading into January.
