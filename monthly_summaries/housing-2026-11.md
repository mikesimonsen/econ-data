# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos demand signal held positive for the 56th consecutive week on the 13-week average as of early October, with new pending sales running +5.85% year-over-year on the spot read and the 4-week average at +3.57% YoY — both decelerating but still in expansion territory. That deceleration is worth watching: the 4-week average turned negative week-over-week for the first time in several weeks, and pending days on market have been running above year-ago levels for 104 consecutive weeks, meaning buyers are taking longer to commit even as more of them are transacting. Inventory rose for the ninth consecutive week and sat just barely above year-ago levels (+0.02% YoY), with new listings up meaningfully YoY on the spot read (+8.46%) but the 13-week average only +1.50% — supply is coming back in fits rather than a steady stream. NAR's September existing home sales print came in at 3.99 million SAAR, a modest beat against consensus of 3.85 million and technically an uptick from August, though on an NSA basis sales ran 3.94% below September 2025 — the SAAR revision effect is doing some work here, so use the YoY NSA read for direction. New home sales at 676,000 SAAR ran nearly 6% below a year ago and months supply held at 8.4, which is why builders are clearing pipeline rather than pulling new starts: single-family starts fell in September to 859,000, single-family permits fell to 846,000, and both just turned negative YoY for the first time in several months. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.27% as of November 2, up 93 basis points year-over-year, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury has widened back to 2.38 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That spread is the number agents should internalize: even without a Fed move, if lender risk premiums normalize toward historical levels the 30-year could fall 50-60 basis points purely on spread compression, independent of where the 10-year goes. The leading indicators are less encouraging for near-term demand: the MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 16.8% year-over-year, its weakest reading since February 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks — turned negative period-over-period after a brief positive stretch, with its 13-week average falling for 8 consecutive weeks and sitting 1.46% below year-ago levels. The MII is not confirming a near-term demand recovery. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the spot Altos pending read can hold its YoY positive streak into the seasonally slower November period, and whether the spread compresses or widens further as the 10-year tests its current level near 4.95%.

The macro backdrop explains why the mortgage market is where it is. September payrolls came in at roughly 29,000 — a significant miss against a 59,000 consensus — but initial jobless claims through late October ran 11.8% below year-ago levels at 194,000, well below the 212,000 consensus. Those two signals are not contradictory: payroll growth is slow but the existing labor force is not losing jobs at an elevated rate, which keeps household income and mortgage-qualification capacity largely intact for current owners. The hires rate ticked up to 3.4% and the quits rate rose to 2.1% in September, both moving in directions associated with modestly more labor market confidence — quits in particular tend to correlate with voluntary household mobility, so that uptick is a mild positive for transaction volume over the medium term. The concern is not the headline unemployment rate (4.2%, which actually beat the 4.4% consensus) but the accumulation beneath it: the long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 weeks or more — has risen 7.1% year-over-year and median unemployment duration is up 13.9% YoY. That cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is the pool from which mortgage delinquencies eventually draw, and it has been building for two months. On inflation, core PCE ran 3.11% year-over-year in September and shelter CPI remained sticky at +3.22% YoY — both above the Fed's target in ways that keep the 10-year anchored and constrain rate relief. The Case-Shiller August data (released with the usual two-month lag) reinforces the geographic split that matters most for agent conversations: Chicago +6.6% YoY, New York +6.2%, Cleveland +4.3%, and Miami +4.3% contrast sharply with Seattle -2.0%, Dallas -0.7%, Denver -0.8%, Las Vegas -1.1%, and Tampa -0.3%. Agents in Midwest and Northeast markets are working a fundamentally different price environment than those in the Sun Belt markets that overbuilt during 2021-2023.
