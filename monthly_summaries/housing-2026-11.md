**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive on a year-over-year basis through the most recent Altos read, with the new pending sales 4-week average running roughly +3.6% YoY and the 13-week average at +5.5% YoY — the latter now 56 consecutive weeks in positive territory. That streak is real, but it deserves context: it is decelerating, and the spot week has been choppy. Inventory on the Altos/Realtor.com side continued to build, with active listings on Realtor.com up 7.6% YoY through October, now 36 consecutive months of positive YoY inventory growth. New listings, however, contracted sharply — Realtor.com new listings fell 7.3% YoY in October, the lowest since September 2023 — which tells you sellers are still hesitant even as buyers face more choice than they have in years. Pending days on market have been running elevated, up roughly 6% YoY, so homes are sitting longer. The NAR existing sales print for October landed at 3.75M SAAR, missing the 3.85M consensus and down 8.8% YoY on the SAAR basis; NSA transactions were off 11.7% year over year, the weakest October since 2023. Months supply ticked to 5.1, up nearly 16% YoY — not crisis territory, but it confirms the buyer-pace problem. The notable divergence: new home sales came in at 701K SAAR, up 7.5% YoY, with builders clearing inventory aggressively at prices now running 3.2% below year-ago levels (median $391K) and average prices down more than 12% YoY — builders are buying down rates and trimming margins to move product. That price flexibility is something resale sellers simply cannot match. On mortgage rates, the 30-year fixed sits at 7.29% as of today, up slightly from last month's low and 107 basis points above a year ago. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.35 points — still roughly 65 basis points above the historical norm of about 1.70 — and the 4-week average spread has been rising for 12 consecutive weeks, which means the affordability improvement that could come from spread normalization is not materializing. The 10-year itself is at 4.94%, up 9 basis points on the month. MBA purchase applications printed a sharp week-over-week bounce (the November 20 week was up 10% from the prior week) but the 4-week average is still down 18% YoY and the 13-week average is at its lowest since June 2024 — that single-week pop is noise around Thanksgiving week timing. Single-family permits fell to 777K SAAR in October, down 12.3% YoY and the lowest since April 2023, while starts bounced but completions are running 10.7% below year-ago levels. Builders are delivering the pipeline they started, but they are not replenishing it. Watch the December MBA 4-week average and whether the Xactus MII — which as of early July was running just barely positive YoY on the spot week — can confirm any demand stabilization before it shows up in applications.

The macro picture is complicated in ways that matter directly for housing. October payrolls came in at +165K, well above the 59K consensus, and unemployment fell to 4.1% — both readings that reduce the near-term probability of Fed easing and help explain why the 10-year has drifted back toward 5%. Average hourly earnings rose 3.1% YoY, solid enough to support buyer purchasing power in markets where prices are flat or falling, but insufficient to offset a 7.29% mortgage rate against 2021–22 purchase prices in most metros. The number to watch more carefully is long-term unemployment: workers out 27 weeks or more now total 1.963 million, up 8.2% YoY and rising for three consecutive months, with median unemployment duration up 17.8% YoY to 11.9 weeks. This cohort is not selling homes today, but if it continues to accumulate, it becomes a distressed-borrower pipeline in roughly 6–12 months. Core PCE for October came in at 3.2% YoY, above the Fed's target and still rising — the 7th consecutive monthly increase — which keeps the Fed on hold and keeps the 10-year anchored. Shelter CPI at 3.5% YoY is a lagging measure of rent pressures that peaked 18 months ago; Zillow's ZORI is up only 0.6% YoY and fell nearly 1% month over month in October.
