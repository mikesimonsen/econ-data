# Monthly Housing Summary — November 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The four weeks ending November 18 presented a market pulled in two directions. On the demand side, the Altos 13-week average for new pending sales remained positive on a year-over-year basis — 56 consecutive weeks of YoY gains — though the 4-week average decelerated to +3.6% YoY and the spot week showed a sequential softening, consistent with normal seasonal patterns in early November. Inventory on the Altos read held just above 1.08 million units, nearly flat YoY, with new listings running +8.5% YoY on a spot basis and the 13-week average for new listings up +1.5% — supply is arriving but not accumulating aggressively. Pending days on market have drifted higher, now +6.2% YoY at 70.8 days, a signal that absorption has slowed at the margin. The NAR's October existing home sales print was the clearest surprise of the month: 3.75 million SAAR, down 8.8% YoY and 100,000 units below consensus, with the West (-14.5% YoY NSA) and South (-10.8% YoY NSA) driving most of the miss. Months supply rose to 5.1, up nearly 16% YoY — the highest read since 2019 at this time of year. New home sales (September, the latest available) came in at 676,000 SAAR, also softer YoY, with builders responding: single-family permits fell to 772,000 SAAR in October, down 12.9% YoY and to their lowest level since April 2023. That divergence — starts bouncing back off lows while permits roll over — tells you builders are completing the pipeline they already committed to but pulling back meaningfully on future authorizations. On rates, the 30-year fixed sits at 7.46% as of today, 108 basis points above a year ago. The 30Y-to-10Y spread is 2.39 points, well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points and widening again after a brief compression — that spread is the hidden affordability lever agents should be watching. Were it to normalize, the 30-year rate would fall toward 6.7-6.8% with the 10-year where it is, without any Fed move required. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is running -18.6% YoY, its weakest read since January 2024, and the 13-week average has been falling for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks — showed a negative 4-week average trend as of early October, and that signal has since been confirmed by the MBA deterioration. Watch whether the MII stabilizes before year-end; if it doesn't turn by mid-December, January purchase application volume will likely extend its underperformance into the new year.

The macro picture is genuinely complicated right now, and agents operating off headline reads alone are likely misreading the conditions their buyers and sellers face. The October payrolls print was +165,000 — well above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1%, beating a 4.4% expectation. On the surface, that is a strong labor market. But the JOLTS data for September showed quits rising sharply (+12.5% YoY), which historically reflects worker confidence, while the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) reached 1.963 million in October, up 8.2% YoY over three consecutive months of increases. That cohort does not affect the current selling season, but its accumulation is worth tracking as a leading indicator of future mortgage stress. On inflation, October CPI came in at +0.3% month-over-month against a 0.9% consensus forecast — a significant undershoot — while Core PCE for September held at +3.1% YoY. Shelter CPI is running +3.5% YoY and rising, which will keep measured inflation elevated even as goods and energy deflate. The Fed's preferred measure remaining above 3% while payrolls run hot all but forecloses a near-term rate cut, which means the 10-year Treasury near 5.1% is unlikely to retreat without a meaningful softening in either jobs or inflation. The regional price story in the August Case-Shiller data (reference period: through August 2026) continues to reflect a two-speed market: Chicago +6.6% YoY and New York +6.2% YoY remain strong, while Seattle is -2.0%, Dallas -0.7%, and Tampa -0.3% — the Sun Belt markets that overshot during the pandemic are still working through that excess. The number to watch next month is Core PCE for October; if it decelerates toward 2.8-2.9%, the rate outlook shifts and the spread compression story becomes live for the spring selling season.
