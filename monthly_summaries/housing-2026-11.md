**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but decelerated meaningfully over the past four weeks. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +5.5% YoY and has been positive for 56 consecutive weeks, which is the cleaner read on trend — but the spot week came in at +5.9% YoY and the 4-week average has pulled back to +3.6%, a signal that the autumn buying season is fading faster than the headline streak implies. Pending days on market are running 6.2% above last year, now at 70.8 days, and that figure has been elevated for 104 consecutive weeks; homes are simply taking longer to close, which means the demand signal is real but not urgent. Inventory continues to build: Realtor.com active listings were up 5.6% YoY in September and the total listing count held above 1.56 million through early November, though the 4-week direction on that count is now falling. New listings from Altos were up 8.5% YoY on the spot week but the 4-week and 13-week averages are running just +1.2% and +1.5% YoY — so the new-supply pipeline is not accelerating. NAR existing home sales came in at a 3.99 million SAAR for September, slightly above the 3.85 million consensus, and technically reversed the recent declining streak — but on a NSA YoY basis sales were still down 3.9%, and months supply at 4.8 is 6.7% above last year, so the inventory-to-sales ratio is not tight. New home sales at 676K SAAR fell 6.0% YoY, and builder single-family permits declined 3.9% YoY in September after two months of falling — the clearest sign that builders are pulling back future supply even as they work through the existing pipeline. The 30-year fixed is at 7.17% today, up 80 basis points year-over-year, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is running 2.35-2.36 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap is worth watching: if lender risk appetite normalizes even partially, rates could fall 40-50 basis points without any Fed action, which is the affordability lever most buyer conversations are missing. The leading indicators, however, do not point that direction yet. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 18.0% YoY and sitting at its lowest level since February 2024. The Xactus MII 4-week average, which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks, has also rolled over — the 13-week MII average has been falling for eight consecutive weeks, and the spot MII turned negative in period after a positive reading. When MII and MBA are both pointing the same direction, the signal is less ambiguous. Watch whether MBA purchase applications stabilize after the election-period noise clears; that will be the first confirmation of whether November demand firms or continues to soften.

The macro backdrop explains why agents are seeing hesitant buyers even in markets where prices have not fallen. September payrolls came in at roughly +29K versus a consensus of +59K, a meaningful miss, and average hourly earnings grew only 0.1% month-over-month against an expected 0.3% — both readings that cool the income-growth story buyers use to justify stretching on price. The unemployment rate ticked up to 4.2% in September, though initial jobless claims just printed 189K for the week of October 31, well below the 212K consensus, which complicates the picture slightly. The more important labor signal for housing is the long-term unemployed: the cohort unemployed 27 weeks or more reached 1.944 million in September, up 7.1% YoY and rising for two months after a brief dip. This group does not threaten the current selling season, but it is the population most likely to produce delinquencies and eventual distressed supply in the 6-to-12-month window — worth flagging at listing appointments in markets where affordability is already stretched. On inflation, Core PCE came in at 3.11% YoY in September, still running above the Fed's 2% target and accelerating for seven consecutive months, which keeps the Fed on hold and the 10-year anchored near 4.82%. Shelter CPI at 3.22% YoY is finally decelerating from its 2024-2025 peak, which matters because it is the last inflation component keeping Core PCE sticky — if shelter continues to soften over the next two to three prints, the rate environment could shift faster than the futures market currently implies. Regionally, the Case-Shiller August data (released October 27) confirms the two-speed market is intact: Chicago +6.6% and New York +6.2% YoY remain in a different conversation than Dallas -0.7%, Seattle -2.0%, Las Vegas -1.1%, and Tampa -0.3%.
