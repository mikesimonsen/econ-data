**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but is decelerating at the leading edge. Altos weekly new pending sales came in at 84,611 for the week of July 3, up 5.85% year over year, and the 4-week average sits at +3.57% YoY — still positive, but fading from the stronger reads earlier in the cycle. The 13-week average remains up 5.48% YoY and has been positive for 56 consecutive weeks, which is the more durable signal. Inventory continued its measured climb: total active supply reached 1.09 million units, essentially flat YoY (+0.02%), with new listings running +8.46% YoY on the spot week and +1.5% on the 13-week average — supply is growing, but not at a pace that shifts the balance materially. Pending days on market ticked up 6.2% YoY, a slow grind that reflects buyers taking slightly longer to commit. NAR's September existing home sales printed at 3.99 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus and reversing the recent pullback — a cleaner result than the headline YoY decline of -1.5% (NSA: -3.9% YoY) implies, given the seasonal pattern. New home sales told a softer story: 676,000 SAAR in September, down roughly 6% YoY, with months supply at 8.4 — builders remain competitive on price (new home median fell nearly 4% YoY) but are pulling back on future commitments. Single-family permits dropped to 846,000 SAAR, now -3.9% YoY and flipping negative after months of positive readings; starts fell further to 859,000 SAAR. That permits-to-starts divergence — builders finishing the existing pipeline while throttling new authorizations — is the supply story that matters for 2027, not the current selling season. On rates, the 30-year fixed sits at 7.27% as of November 2, up 93 basis points YoY. The mortgage-to-Treasury spread (the gap between the 30-year rate and the 10-year yield, historically around 1.7 percentage points) has widened to 2.38 points and is moving the wrong direction — up six basis points in two days and rising for eight consecutive weeks on the 4-week average. That spread is the hidden affordability story: with the 10-year at 4.89%, rates are not where they should be relative to Treasury yields, and any compression back toward historical norms would deliver meaningful payment relief without a Fed move. The leading indicators are less encouraging. The MBA purchase application 4-week average is down 16.8% YoY — its lowest read since February 2024 — and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (MII), which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks by measuring borrower intent before a full application is filed, shows its own 4-week average down 1.6% YoY after recently reversing lower. When MII leads MBA down, it tends to confirm rather than contradict the weaker applications trend — watch whether the MII 4-week average stabilizes or continues falling over the next four weeks, as that will signal whether November pending sales hold their YoY gains or give some back.

The macro backdrop tightened meaningfully this month, and the housing implications are direct. September payrolls came in at roughly +29,000 — well below the +59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate ticked up to 4.2%, even as initial jobless claims remain low at 194,000. The divergence between low claims (few layoffs) and weak payrolls (few hirings) is consistent with a labor market that is not deteriorating rapidly but is not generating the income growth that supports new household formation. The quits rate held at 1.9%, near a post-pandemic low, which matters for housing because workers who don't quit jobs don't move houses — labor market fluidity and residential mobility tend to track together. The longer-term unemployment signal deserves attention: 1.944 million workers have been unemployed 27 or more weeks, up 7.1% YoY, and median unemployment duration rose to 11.5 weeks, up 13.9% YoY. This cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is the forward indicator for mortgage stress — if it continues accumulating, distressed-borrower volume will surface in 2027. On inflation, Core PCE held at 3.11% YoY in September, running above the Fed's 2% target and above the prior consensus of +0.3% month over month. Shelter CPI was +3.22% YoY, still decelerating from its peak but providing a floor under overall inflation that complicates any Fed pivot narrative. With the 10-year Treasury at 4.89% and the spread still wide, the path to meaningfully lower mortgage rates runs through either a compression in the mortgage-Treasury spread or a material drop in long-term yields — and Core PCE above 3% makes the latter unlikely in the near term.
