**November 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held in positive territory through early November, though the signals beneath the headline are worth reading carefully. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has been positive year-over-year for 56 consecutive weeks, and the 4-week average came in at +3.6% YoY as of early October — still constructive, but decelerating from the high single-digit pace we saw earlier in the year. The most recent weekly read was +5.9% YoY, so the trend has not broken; it has simply lost some momentum entering the seasonally slow stretch. Inventory on the Altos side remained essentially flat YoY — up just 0.02% — while Realtor.com active listings were +7.6% YoY through October, a sign that the platforms measuring slightly different pools are both pointing to supply that has rebuilt but not overwhelmed. New listings on the Altos 13-week average were +1.5% YoY and rising for 14 consecutive weeks, which matters more than the monthly noise. Pending days on market ticked up 6.2% YoY — not alarming, but it means buyers are taking a little longer to commit than a year ago. The NAR October existing sales print — 3.75 million SAAR — missed consensus by 100,000 units and landed at the softest pace since October 2023, down 8.8% YoY on the NSA read. The West was the weakest region, off 15.2% NSA YoY. New home sales told a meaningfully different story: 701,000 SAAR in October, up 7.5% YoY, with months supply falling to 8.0 from 9.3 — builders are moving product, helped in part by continued price concessions (the average new home sale price is down 12% YoY, lowest since April 2009). The 30-year fixed sat at 7.26% as of November 25, essentially unchanged over the past month but up 106 basis points YoY. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, now at 2.41 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points and widening on a 12-week trend. If that spread were at its long-run average today, the 30-year would be closer to 6.5%. That compression story remains the single largest latent affordability lever in the market. MBA purchase applications showed a sharp week-over-week bounce (+10.2%) in the November 20 reading, but the 4-week average is still down 18.1% YoY and the 13-week average has fallen for 14 consecutive weeks — the weekly number is likely noise around the holiday calendar. The Xactus MII, which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks, was trending lower as of early October, suggesting the MBA softness is not yet finished. Single-family permits fell 12.3% YoY in October and hit their lowest level since April 2023 — builders are pulling back on future authorizations even as they work through existing pipeline. Watch the December Altos 4-week pending average and whether MBA applications stabilize after the Thanksgiving distortion.

The macro backdrop is genuinely two-sided, which is unusual. October payrolls came in at +165,000, well above the 59,000 consensus — the headline labor market remains resilient. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, also better than expected. The quits rate ticked back up to 2.1% in September, which matters for housing because voluntary job-switchers are more likely to relocate and transact. That is the constructive side. The complication is inflation: core PCE rose to 3.2% YoY in October — still running hot and moving in the wrong direction for rate relief. The 10-year Treasury is at 4.85%, and with the mortgage-to-Treasury spread still elevated and not compressing, the Fed's next move has limited near-term transmission to what buyers actually pay. CPI shelter at +3.5% YoY continues to run below where it was in 2024, and the methodological lag in that series means the shelter print will keep drifting lower — but it is not moving fast enough to give the Fed cover to cut. On the regional price story, the September Case-Shiller data (reference period: July–September closings) is worth an agent's attention: Chicago is up 6.9% YoY, New York 5.9%, Miami 4.6% — the dense, supply-constrained Northeast and Midwest markets continue to hold. Meanwhile Dallas is -0.8% YoY for 18 consecutive months, Denver -1.0% for 17 months, Seattle -2.1% for 14 months, and Tampa is now 23 consecutive months negative. That is not a temporary soft patch in those Sun Belt markets — it is a repricing that agents there need to build into their CMA conversations. The long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 weeks or more — rose another 8.2% YoY in October and has been climbing for three straight months.
