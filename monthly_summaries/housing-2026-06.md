# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive for the 51st consecutive week, with the Altos 13-week average up 4.3% year over year and still rising — the most reliable read on underlying demand in the current data set. The spot week came in at +1.0% YoY, and the 4-week average at +5.5%, though both decelerated over the month as Memorial Day seasonality compressed the weekly numbers. Inventory crossed 1.02 million active listings (Altos, week of May 29), essentially flat year over year — the YoY growth rate that ran positive for much of the past year turned negative this week for the first time since October 2023, meaning the inventory accumulation cycle that had been building since early 2024 has stalled. New listings held a modest +1.9% YoY on the 4-week average, but the 13-week average is barely flat (-0.1% YoY), so the new supply pipeline is not expanding meaningfully. Pending days on market ticked up to 72.1 days YoY (+9%), a pace that has held elevated for nearly two years — buyers are not moving faster even as they return. NAR April existing sales printed at 4.02 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and marking the first positive YoY read after a string of negative prints; the NSA count of 354,000 was up 1.4% year over year. New home sales told the opposite story: April came in at 622,000 SAAR, down 11.9% YoY, with months of supply at 9.4 — builders are sitting on a growing finished-home inventory even as single-family starts fell 9% in April and single-family permits have now been negative YoY for 23 consecutive months. The divergence between existing and new matters: builders are pulling back on future pipeline while the resale market absorbs modest demand improvement. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.61% as of June 3, up 4 basis points on the month after a brief dip — the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.11 percentage points, still roughly 40 basis points above the long-run norm of around 1.7 points. That gap remains the hidden affordability story: if credit and policy conditions allow the spread to compress toward historical norms, rates could fall meaningfully without any Fed move. MBA purchase applications (4-week average) were up 6.1% YoY as of May 22, a solid read — but the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks, has fallen for six consecutive weeks and is now down 3.0% YoY on its 4-week average. That divergence is worth watching: MII measures credit-pull activity before an application is filed, so a sustained negative reading there often arrives ahead of MBA softness by half a month or more. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the MII stabilizes or continues lower — that will tell you whether the MBA strength is a lagging echo or a durable trend heading into the summer selling season.

The macro backdrop is, on balance, still constructive for housing demand but with visible soft spots accumulating beneath the headline numbers. April payrolls came in at +115,000, well above the +59,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate held at 4.3% — both supportive of purchase demand in the near term. The concern is in the labor flow data: the JOLTS hires rate fell to 3.2% and the quits rate dropped to 1.9%, both moving in the wrong direction. Fewer hires mean less household formation mobility — the "I got a new job in a new city" transaction is the engine of relocations, and a structurally lower hires rate suppresses that. The long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) rose again in April, now up 9.6% year over year to 1.83 million — this is not a 2026 selling-season problem, but it is a 2027 delinquency-pipeline story worth tracking as it accumulates. On inflation, Core PCE came in at +0.24% month over month in April, just below the +0.3% consensus, and shelter CPI held at +3.3% YoY — still sticky, but the direction is more important than the level right now. The Fed is unlikely to move until Core PCE sustains below 2.5%, and the 10-year Treasury at 4.50% (up 5 basis points this week, reversing a recent decline) reflects a market that has priced out near-term cuts. On the regional price picture, the March Case-Shiller data (released May 26, so reflecting contract activity from roughly January–February) sharpens the two-speed story: Chicago +6.0% YoY, New York +4.0%, Boston +2.0% — the Midwest and Northeast continue to appreciate. Dallas -1.7%, Phoenix -1.8%, Denver -1.9%, Seattle -2.4%, Tampa -1.8% — Sun Belt and West Coast markets where inventory rebuilt fastest are still working through that overhang at the price level. The national index edged down 0.22% on the month, an unusual move for this time
