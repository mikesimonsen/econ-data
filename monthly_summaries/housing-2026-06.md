# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held firm through June even as the leading edge softened. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has now run positive year-over-year for 54 consecutive weeks, sitting at +5.15% YoY as of June 19, and while the 4-week average has drifted to +5.50% YoY after five weeks of sequential decline, the spot week still printed +7.16% — so the trend is decelerating, not reversing. NAR's May existing home sales report was the cleaner story: 4.17 million SAAR, up 3.2% year-over-year and well ahead of the 3.85 million consensus, with the South and West driving the acceleration and the Midwest flipping positive YoY for the first time in several months. The divergence worth noting is new home sales, which fell to 580K SAAR in May — down 7.5% YoY — while months supply for new homes climbed to 10.3, the highest reading in years. Builders are sitting on finished inventory they cannot move at current prices, which is why single-family starts are now down 7.1% YoY and single-family completions have fallen to their lowest level since June 2011. Altos active inventory reached 1.06 million the week of June 19, up a modest 0.15% YoY — barely positive, and the YoY growth rate is decelerating — while new listings on the 4-week average are up 2.79% YoY, slowing from prior weeks. Pending days on market at 70.7 days is still running 6.4% above last year but has been falling for 14 consecutive weeks, which tells you homes that do go under contract are moving faster. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.53% today, down about 26 basis points from a year ago, but the spread to the 10-year Treasury remains at 2.14 percentage points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap is the affordability story hiding in plain sight: if spreads compress toward their long-run average as Fed uncertainty lifts and MBS volatility recedes, the 30-year rate could fall meaningfully without any move from the Fed. What to watch over the next four weeks: the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA purchase applications by two to four weeks, flipped negative YoY this week at -1.98% and its 4-week average turned negative as well. MBA purchase apps are still positive — +3.85% on the 4-week average — but decelerating for several consecutive reads. When MII leads MBA lower like this, it typically shows up in pending sales data within a month. Watch whether that gap closes or whether MII stabilizes.

The macro backdrop is genuinely two-sided right now, which is why rate forecasting is harder than usual. May payrolls printed 172,000 — well above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.3%, but the internals are worth reading carefully. The JOLTS hires rate fell to 3.2% in April, quits dropped back, and the number of workers unemployed 27 or more weeks rose 35.8% year-over-year to nearly 2 million. That long-duration unemployment cohort does not affect the current selling season — these households are not listing or buying in June — but it is the pool from which mortgage delinquencies eventually emerge, and its accumulation bears watching through year-end. On inflation, May CPI came in cooler than expected at +0.21% core month-over-month, and core PCE for May printed +0.32% — still running at 3.4% annualized, meaningfully above the Fed's 2% target. Shelter CPI at +3.35% YoY continues to mechanically hold core readings elevated even as private-sector rent indexes (Zillow ZORI at +2.95% YoY, with an unusually strong month) suggest the pipeline is gradually easing. The 10-year Treasury at 4.39% reflects a market that believes the Fed stays on hold until inflation data cooperates; until core PCE drops closer to 3% or the labor market cracks more visibly, rate relief will come from spread compression rather than Fed cuts. Regionally, the March Case-Shiller data (the most recent available, lagged roughly two months) sharpens the two-speed story: Chicago at +6.0% YoY and New York at +4.0% YoY are still appreciating at a solid clip, while Dallas, Denver, Phoenix, Seattle, and Tampa are all in negative YoY territory — some by nearly 2 points. If you work the Sun Belt, your price narrative is fundamentally different from your counterpart in the Midwest or Northeast, and that divergence is not closing. The one signal to watch for next month is whether the June core PCE print (due late July) shows any further deceleration — that is the variable most likely to shift rate expectations, spread behavior, and by extension the affordability math your buyers are running.
