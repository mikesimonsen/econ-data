# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held firm through most of the four weeks ending June 12, with Altos new pending sales running +4.69% year-over-year on the spot read and the four-week average up +6.0% YoY — part of a 53-consecutive-week streak of positive YoY pending sales. The 13-week average is also positive for the 14th straight week and still rising, which is the more durable signal; the mild week-over-week softening in the four-week trend is worth watching but does not break the underlying pattern. Inventory continued its measured climb: Altos total active supply reached approximately 1.047 million units, up about 1% on the week but still running -1.1% below year-ago levels — the YoY gap is actually widening slightly, not narrowing, which is a tighter supply backdrop than the headline count implies. New listings are cooperating; the four-week average of 95,305 is running +3.3% YoY and accelerating, and the 13-week average has risen 14 consecutive weeks. Pending days on market ticked down again to 70.9 days, though it remains 7% above last year, indicating the market is still moving but not as urgently as in 2024. NAR Existing Home Sales for May printed at a SAAR of 4.17 million — well above the consensus expectation of 3.85 million — and up 3.2% YoY, with two consecutive months of gains. The South led regionally (+5.9% YoY) while the Northeast contracted -8.0% YoY on the SAAR print; on the NSA read, which is the right comparison for direction, the Northeast was -7.0% YoY, confirming that tight listing supply there is constraining transactions even as prices hold. New home sales told a different story: April's print fell to a 622K SAAR, down 11.9% YoY, with months supply rising to 9.4 — builders are sitting on inventory and slowing. The May starts report reinforced the pullback: total starts fell to 1,177K SAAR, missing consensus of 1,350K by a meaningful margin, and single-family starts are down 7.1% YoY. Notably, single-family permits came in at 886K and have now been negative YoY for 24 consecutive months, which is the clearest signal that builders are not replenishing future supply. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.54% as of today, off modestly from its recent range and down 37 basis points year-over-year. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, currently 2.10-2.14 basis points — historically the norm runs closer to 1.70pp. That 40-plus basis point excess spread means rates have meaningful room to compress even without Fed action; if the spread reverts toward 1.80pp on its own, the 30-year could move to the low-to-mid 6s without the 10-year moving. MBA purchase applications came in at a four-week average of +4.3% YoY, positive for the 70th consecutive week at the 13-week level, though that longer average has ticked down two consecutive readings. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks — just turned its four-week average back up (+0.09% WoW) after declining, but its 13-week average is still down 2.3% YoY and falling for five straight weeks, a mild caution flag. The divergence between a firming MBA and a softer MII trend is what to watch over the next four weeks: if the MII 13-week average stabilizes or turns, the purchase application trend holds; if it continues to slide, the MBA four-week average likely follows.

The macro backdrop in June delivered a series of cleaner-than-feared prints, and the cumulative effect on housing is meaningful. May payrolls came in at +172,000 — nearly three times the +59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.3%, beating the expected 4.4%. A functioning labor market matters more to housing than rate levels in the short run: employed households with income stability are the marginal buyer, and this report does not threaten the current selling season. That said, the long-term unemployment picture is worth flagging separately: the 27-weeks-and-over unemployed count rose to 1.988 million in May, up 35.8% YoY, and the median duration of unemployment jumped to 11.6 weeks, 22% above last year. This cohort does not affect June closings, but it is accumulating steadily and will matter for mortgage delinquency volumes in the 6-to-12 month window — the leading edge of a distressed-inventory story that does not yet exist but is being built. On inflation, May CPI core came in at +0.21% month-over-month against a +0.3% consensus, and April core PCE printed +0.24% against the same expectation; shelter CPI was +0.32% MoM and +3.35% YoY, continuing its slow deceleration.
