# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive year-over-year for the 53rd consecutive week, with the Altos 13-week average running +4.9% YoY and still rising — the most durable demand signal in the data right now. The spot weekly read came in at +4.7% YoY, and the 4-week average sits at +6.0% YoY, though both have ticked lower over the past four weeks as the spring buying season begins its seasonal fade. Inventory tells a more nuanced story: total Altos active supply of just over 1.04 million units is actually running -1.1% below year-ago levels, a YoY gap that has widened in recent weeks and now sits at its worst reading since October 2023. New listings are providing some relief — the 4-week average is up +3.3% YoY and accelerating — but that fresh supply is being absorbed quickly enough to hold overall inventory flat. Pending days on market at 70.9 days is elevated +7.1% YoY, so homes are sitting a bit longer, but the Realtor.com pending ratio just hit its highest level since April 2022, which cuts against any notion that demand is cracking. NAR existing sales came in at 4.17 million SAAR for May, beating expectations by a wide margin and running +3.2% YoY — the strongest print in over a year. On an NSA basis, YoY sales were flat, which is the more conservative read; the SAAR beat partly reflects favorable seasonal adjustment. New home sales diverged sharply: April printed at 622K SAAR, down nearly 12% YoY, with months supply at 9.4 — builders are carrying meaningful inventory at a time when starts are falling hard. May total starts missed badly at 1,177K SAAR versus 1,350K expected, down 8.2% YoY, with single-family starts off 7.1% YoY. Single-family permits have now been negative YoY for 24 consecutive months — that is the supply constraint that will matter in 2027 and 2028. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.66% today, up modestly over the past month, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.16 points — well above the historical norm near 1.7 points. The 4-week average spread has been rising for six weeks and now stands at 2.12 points YoY. That gap is the affordability story most agents are not telling their clients: rates could fall 40-45 basis points without any Fed action if spreads simply revert toward normal. MBA purchase applications are +4.3% YoY on a 4-week basis, but the 13-week average has started rolling over — decelerating from +5.1% — so the trend is softening at the margin. The Xactus MII, which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks, flipped positive YoY (+0.08%) for the first time in months on a spot basis, but its 13-week average is still -2.3% YoY and falling. When MII and MBA diverge, the MII tends to win. Watch whether the MII 4-week average — currently -1.7% YoY — follows the spot print higher or fades back; that will signal whether July applications hold their gains.

The macro backdrop is more constructive than the headlines suggest, but there are fault lines worth tracking. May payrolls came in at +172K, well above the +59K consensus, and the unemployment rate held at 4.3% — both readings that support continued household formation and purchase intent. The more important labor signal, though, is what is happening beneath the headline: the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose 8.5% in a single month and are now 35.8% above year-ago levels. That cohort does not threaten the current selling season — these households are not active buyers — but if the accumulation continues, it will shape distressed-borrower volume and potential listing supply roughly 6-12 months out. Watch it. On inflation, May CPI core came in at +0.21% month-over-month, cooler than the +0.3% consensus, and core PCE for April was also below expectations at +0.24%. Shelter CPI is still running at +3.35% YoY — that component keeps measured inflation elevated even as market rents, per Zillow's ZORI, are running at only +2.95% YoY. As shelter CPI continues its delayed convergence toward actual rent trends, it gives the Fed more cover to ease without abandoning credibility on inflation.
