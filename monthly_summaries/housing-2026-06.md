# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% year-over-year and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks — the streak continues, and the underlying trend remains constructive. The spot week came in at -2.99% YoY and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37%, so the top-line read is softer than it was in May, but that is partly a seasonality artifact in a year when spring selling peaked a touch earlier than 2025. Inventory at Altos reached 1,074,577 active listings the week of June 26, up 1.17% YoY — still growing, but the YoY growth rate has narrowed steadily from the high teens of early 2025. New listings flipped to -7.14% YoY on the spot week, though the 4-week average is still marginally positive at +0.41% and the 13-week average at +0.71% is rising for a 14th consecutive week, suggesting the single-week dip reflects the pre-holiday lull rather than a seller retreat. Pending days on market at 70.9 days is running 6.7% above a year ago, which is a real signal: homes are sitting longer even as the transaction count holds up. NAR existing home sales for May printed at 4.17M SAAR — well above the 3.85M consensus — up 3.22% YoY and accelerating; the NSA count was flat year-over-year at 390,000, so the SAAR beat is partly seasonal-adjustment mechanics, but the direction is genuine. New home sales diverged sharply, falling to 580K SAAR in May, down 7.5% YoY, and months supply of new homes jumped to 10.3 — a level that will continue pressing builder pricing power. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.52% today, essentially unchanged over the past four weeks. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.14 percentage points today, it remains well above the long-run norm of roughly 1.70pp. That 44-basis-point excess spread is the hidden affordability story — if it normalizes even partway, the 30-year rate falls meaningfully without any Fed action. The 4-week average spread is 2.13pp and has been rising for seven weeks, so compression is not happening yet, but the year-over-year comparison is now 35bp tighter than June 2025, which tells you the direction of travel over a longer horizon. MBA purchase applications (4-week average) are +3.85% YoY but decelerating — from near +7% in March — and the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks, turned up on both the spot week (+3.53%) and the 4-week average (+2.31%) after a multi-week decline. That MII reversal is the most actionable forward signal in the dashboard right now: if it holds, MBA applications should firm in mid-July. Housing starts missed badly in May at 1,177K SAAR versus 1,350K expected, and single-family starts have now fallen in two consecutive months. Single-family permits edged up to 892K but remain negative YoY for the 24th consecutive month. Starts are running below permits — builders are permitted but not breaking ground — which means the supply pipeline for 2027 deliveries is quietly contracting. Watch whether July MII sustains its reversal and whether the 30-year rate responds to any further Treasury rally.

The macro backdrop is unusual in a specific way: the labor market looks fine at the headline but is showing internal stress that agents should track. May payrolls came in at +172K, well above the +59K consensus, and the unemployment rate held at 4.3%. But the JOLTS hires rate fell to 3.2% in April and quits fell to 1.9% — both at multi-year lows — which means fewer people are voluntarily changing jobs. Job-to-job moves are one of the most reliable triggers for home purchases, so a sticky labor market with low turnover is a genuine headwind for organic demand, independent of rates. The long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) reached 1,988K in May, up 35.8% year-over-year and rising for two consecutive months. This cohort does not affect the current selling season — these households are not in contract right now — but at the current accumulation rate it will shape distressed-borrower volume in six to twelve months and is worth monitoring on the charts. On inflation: headline CPI ran at 4.17% YoY in May but the monthly print came in softer than expected, and core PCE for May printed at 3.41% YoY with a 0.32% monthly gain — above the Fed's 2% target and above the consensus expectation of 0.3%. Shelter CPI at +3.35% YoY is still elevated and will keep core PCE sticky through summer, which is the primary reason the Fed has not moved. As long as Core PCE stays above 3%, the Fed's path to cuts remains narrow, and the case for rate relief rests on spread compression rather than Fed action.
