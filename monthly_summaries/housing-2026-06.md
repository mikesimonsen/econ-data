# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held firm through June while the supply picture sent mixed signals. The Altos new pending sales 13-week average has been positive year-over-year for 54 consecutive weeks, running at +5.15% YoY as of June 19, and the 4-week average sits at +5.50% YoY — both measures still constructive even as the spot week softened slightly and the 4-week average has eased for five consecutive weeks. That deceleration is worth watching but not alarming; the 13-week trend, which filters out weekly noise, continues to rise. Inventory reached 1,062,719 active listings the week of June 19, up a thin 0.15% YoY — the first positive YoY reading after months in negative territory — while new listings on a 4-week average basis came in +2.79% YoY, though the 13-week average at +1.26% YoY tells a more modest story. Pending days on market at 70.7 days is running 6.4% above last year, still decelerating — the market is slower than 2024 but not deteriorating further. NAR existing home sales printed 4.17 million SAAR for May, beating expectations of 3.85 million by a notable margin, up 3.2% YoY with the YoY pace accelerating — the South and West drove the gain regionally. NSA, total existing sales were flat YoY at 390,000, so the seasonal adjustment is doing meaningful work in that headline. New home sales told a different story: 580,000 SAAR, down 7.5% YoY, with months supply of new homes at 10.3 — elevated enough that builders are discounting rather than starting. Single-family starts fell to 882,000 SAAR, down 7.1% YoY, and single-family completions at 872,000 SAAR are at their lowest since June 2011 — pipeline delivery is thinning, which will constrain resale competition from new homes in the back half of 2026. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.55% as of June 24, down roughly 27 basis points year-over-year. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.15 percentage points — well above the historical norm of around 1.7 points. That gap still represents a meaningful affordability reserve: if lender risk premiums normalize even partially, rates can fall to the low-to-mid 6% range without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications on a 4-week average basis are +3.85% YoY but decelerating. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — a credit-inquiry-based measure that tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks — has turned negative YoY, with the 4-week average now down 1.1% and the 13-week average falling for six consecutive weeks. That divergence between a still-positive MBA and a negative MII is the signal to watch in July; historically it has preceded a softening in purchase applications by a month or less.

The macro backdrop entering July is genuinely two-sided. May payrolls came in at +172,000 against a consensus of +59,000, and the unemployment rate held at 4.3% — both results that reduce near-term recession risk and support household formation. But the labor market's internal composition is less reassuring: the hires rate fell to 3.2% and the quits rate slipped to 1.9%, both below year-ago levels, indicating workers are less confident about job-switching — historically a leading indicator of wage deceleration. More important for housing's medium-term outlook, the number of workers unemployed 27 weeks or longer rose to 1,988,000, up 35.8% year-over-year and climbing for two consecutive months. That cohort does not threaten the current selling season, but if it continues to grow it will shape distressed-borrower volume in 2027. On inflation, May Core PCE printed at +3.41% YoY — still running above the Fed's 2% target — while Core CPI came in at +2.82% and shelter CPI at +3.35%, both cooler than expected. The spread between Core PCE and the inflation breakeven implied by Treasury markets has been narrowing; if that continues, it argues for modest rate relief into year-end without a Fed cut. The Case-Shiller data through March (the most recent available) sharpens the geographic divergence: Chicago +6.0% YoY, New York +4.0%, Boston +2.0% — Midwest and Northeast appreciation is real and, for agents in those markets, price conversations should reflect it.
