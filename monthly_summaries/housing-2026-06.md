# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive for the 53rd consecutive week on the Altos 13-week average, which rose to +4.90% YoY, and the four-week average came in at +6.00% YoY — both readings pointing to a market that is absorbing supply at a healthy clip even as the spring peak softens. The most recent weekly read ticked down slightly to +4.69% YoY, which is worth watching but does not break the trend; the 13-week average has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks. Altos active inventory stood at 1,046,775 units as of June 12, still down 1.05% YoY — the YoY gap has been narrowing all year, but a true supply surplus has not materialized. New listings accelerated: the four-week average reached +3.29% YoY and the 13-week average +1.02%, both improving, which means sellers are testing the market at a faster clip than this time last year. Pending days on market held at 70.9 days, up 7.15% YoY — pace is slower than 2024 but has been ticking down for 14 straight weeks, suggesting buyers are acting more quickly as the season matures. NAR existing home sales for May printed at 4.17 million SAAR, up 3.22% YoY and well above the 3.85 million consensus — the largest beat in several months. NSA total sales were flat YoY at 390,000, which is the honest read: the SA beat partly reflects a favorable seasonal factor. New home sales, by contrast, fell to 622,000 SAAR in April, down 11.9% YoY, with months of new-home supply rising to 9.4 — builders are sitting on elevated finished inventory while resale demand firms. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.56% as of June 15, down 35 basis points YoY. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.09 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points, meaning meaningful rate relief can arrive even without Fed action if lender margins compress. The spread ticked up slightly this week after five weeks of narrowing, so watch whether that reversal holds. MBA purchase applications rebounded in the latest week to +3.51% YoY, with the 13-week average at +5.75% YoY positive for 69 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA by two to four weeks — shows a more cautious picture: the spot reading just turned positive YoY at +0.08% but the 13-week average has been falling for five straight weeks and sits at -2.32% YoY. When MII diverges from MBA like this, the earlier signal tends to be right. Watch whether the MBA four-week average, currently falling, continues to soften into early July as the MII implies it should. Housing starts missed badly in May at 1,177,000 SAAR versus a 1,350,000 consensus, driven by a collapse in multifamily; single-family starts fell a more modest 7.1% YoY. Single-family permits held nearly flat month-over-month at 886,000, but remain down 1.45% YoY and negative for 24 consecutive months — the pipeline is not refilling.

The macro backdrop delivered several genuine surprises this month, and taken together they complicate the simple "rates down, housing up" narrative. May payrolls came in at +172,000, nearly three times the 59,000 consensus — a result that reduces near-term recession risk and supports household formation, but also removes urgency from the Fed to cut. Unemployment held at 4.3%, a beat versus the 4.4% expected. The employment picture is not as clean underneath, however: the long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) rose 8.5% in a single month and sits 35.8% above a year ago, with median unemployment duration up 22% YoY. This group does not affect the current selling season — they are not yet defaulting in volume — but the accumulation rate is worth flagging now because it tends to translate into delinquency pressure with a 6-to-12-month lag. On inflation, May CPI came in cooler than expected at +0.21% core month-over-month, with Core PCE for April also printing softer than forecast at +0.24%. Core PCE is still running at 3.29% annually, above the Fed's target, so the cooler prints ease rather than resolve the inflation constraint on rate policy. Shelter CPI at +3.35% YoY continues to decelerate, which is the mechanism connecting lagging rent data to eventual disinflation — as market rents (Zillow ZORI at +2.95% YoY) flow into the BLS measure, shelter's contribution to core keeps fading. The 10-year Treasury sits at 4.44%, nearly unchanged YoY, which is why the mortgage rate improvement has come almost entirely from spread compression rather than Treasury moves.
