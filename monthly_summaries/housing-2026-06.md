# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive on a year-over-year basis for the 53rd consecutive week through June 12, with the Altos 13-week average running +4.9% YoY and still rising — the sustained trend that matters more than any single week's print. The four-week average came in at +6.0% YoY, though the most recent weekly read ticked to +4.7% as the four-week average has drifted lower for four consecutive weeks, a mild softening worth watching without overstating. Inventory at 1.047 million active listings sits 1.1% below year-ago levels, the weakest YoY comparison since October 2023, while new listings' four-week average is running +3.3% YoY and accelerating — supply is growing but not fast enough to reverse the inventory deficit. Pending days on market at 70.9 days is up 7.2% YoY and has been rising for 101 consecutive weeks, signaling that homes are taking modestly longer to trade even as demand remains positive. NAR existing home sales for May printed at 4.17 million SAAR, up 3.2% YoY and well above the 3.85 million consensus — the second consecutive monthly gain. On an NSA basis, May sales were flat YoY, which is the cleaner read given seasonal patterns; still, that flat line against the prior year's weak comparison is not the acceleration the SAAR headline implies. New home sales diverged sharply: April's Census read came in at 622,000 SAAR, down 11.9% YoY, with months supply rising to 9.4 — builders are sitting on elevated finished inventory with demand softening, which partly explains why single-family starts fell again in May, down 7.1% YoY. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.58% as of June 18, essentially flat on the month after a brief dip; the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.14 percentage points against a historical norm near 1.7pp. That 44-basis-point excess spread is the market's hidden affordability lever — if it compresses toward its norm as credit conditions normalize, the 30-year rate could fall meaningfully without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications' four-week average is +4.3% YoY, but the 13-week average has ticked lower for the first time in months and the YoY rate is decelerating. More notable: the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads the MBA by two to four weeks by capturing credit-pull activity before applications are formally filed — just turned barely positive YoY (+0.08%) after several negative weeks, but its 13-week average is still down 2.3% YoY and has fallen five consecutive weeks. The MII's 13-week trend is diverging from the MBA's positive YoY read; that divergence warrants attention through early July, when it should either resolve upward or pull the MBA data weaker. Watch whether the MII four-week average, now at -1.7% YoY but ticking off its low, continues to recover.

The macro picture is more constructive than the housing-specific leading indicators. May payrolls came in at +172,000 against a 59,000 consensus — a clean beat that removes near-term recession risk from the rate calculus, and an employed household is a household that can qualify for a mortgage. The unemployment rate held at 4.3%, beating the 4.4% expectation. What does not show up in the headline is the long-term unemployed cohort: those out of work 27 or more weeks rose 8.5% month-over-month and are up 35.8% year-over-year, now at nearly 2 million. That cohort does not threaten the current selling season, but it is the population that eventually pressures mortgage servicers; it is worth tracking as a slow-moving distressed-supply signal for late 2026 and into 2027. On inflation, May core CPI came in at +0.21% month-over-month, below the 0.3% consensus, and April core PCE printed +0.24% against a 0.3% expectation — both cooler than feared. Shelter CPI ticked up 0.32% in May and sits at +3.35% YoY, still elevated but decelerating as market rents catch up in the lagged index. The 10-year Treasury is at 4.50%, and with the spread still wide, the rate story for housing is less about where the Fed moves and more about whether that spread compresses. Regionally, the March Case-Shiller data (the most recent available) shows the two-speed market intact: Chicago +6.0% YoY, New York +4.0%, Boston +2.0% — Northeast and Midwest agents can support price narratives.
