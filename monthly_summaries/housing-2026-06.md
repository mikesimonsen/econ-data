# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held firmly positive through the four weeks ending June 19, with the Altos weekly read up 7.2% year-over-year on the spot and the four-week average running +5.5% YoY — now 54 consecutive weeks of positive YoY pending sales, and the 13-week average accelerating to +5.2% YoY after 14 consecutive weeks of gains. That is a durable demand signal, not a seasonal blip. Inventory reached 1.063 million active listings, up just 0.15% YoY — the growth rate that dominated the narrative for the past year has effectively stalled, with the four-week new listings average up only 2.8% YoY and the 13-week average at +1.3% YoY, both decelerating. Pending days on market ticked down to 70.7 days, still elevated (+6.4% YoY) but the pace of that YoY gap has been narrowing for 14 straight weeks. NAR existing home sales for May printed at 4.17 million SAAR — a meaningful beat against the 3.85 million consensus — up 3.2% YoY and accelerating, driven by the South (+5.9% YoY) and a Midwest that flipped to positive for the first time in months. The NSA read shows flat YoY nationally, which is the more honest comparison and suggests less seasonal distortion than the SAAR print implies, but the directional beat against expectations was real. New home sales told the opposite story: 580K SAAR for May, down 7.5% YoY, with months' supply rising to 10.3 — builders are sitting on completed inventory that isn't moving fast enough. Single-family starts fell to 882K SAAR, down 7.1% YoY, and the gap between permits (892K SF, essentially flat) and completions (872K SF, down 17.4% YoY and at a 15-year low) signals that the pipeline is thinning from both ends. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.53% today, essentially unchanged over the past month and 19 basis points below a year ago. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.15 points — still roughly 45 basis points wide of the historical norm near 1.70 — and has ticked back up after several weeks of compression. That spread is the affordability lever agents should watch: it remains the gap between rates as they are and rates as they could be if credit markets normalize. MBA purchase applications (four-week average) are up 3.9% YoY but decelerating. More importantly, the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which measures mortgage credit inquiries and typically leads MBA applications by two to four weeks — has turned negative YoY, with the four-week average now down 1.1% YoY and falling for two consecutive weeks. When MII diverges from MBA like this, it tends to resolve with MBA weakening to meet it. Watch whether the MBA four-week average follows MII lower in July.

The macro backdrop has two competing forces. May payrolls came in at +172,000, well above the 59,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate held at 4.3% — both supporting household formation and purchase intent in the near term. But underneath the headline, the JOLTS data showed the hires rate falling to 3.2% and the quits rate dropping to 1.9%, the lowest in years; workers are less confident about job mobility, which historically dampens move-up buying. The more immediate tension is in inflation: headline CPI for May ran at +4.2% YoY and core PCE at +3.4% YoY — both cooler than feared but still well above the Fed's 2% target, which keeps the 10-year anchored near 4.38% and the Fed on hold. Shelter CPI was +3.4% YoY, still decelerating from its peak but providing no near-term relief for buyers calibrating payment affordability. The mechanism is straightforward: as long as core PCE stays above 3%, the Fed has little room to cut, the 10-year stays range-bound, and the 30-year fixed stays in the mid-sixes absent spread compression. Regionally, the March Case-Shiller data (the most recent available, reported in late May) sharpens the two-speed story: Chicago gained +6.0% YoY and New York +4.0%, while Dallas fell 1.7%, Denver fell 1.9%, Seattle fell 2.4%, and Tampa fell 1.8% — the Midwest and Northeast continuing to outperform while Sun Belt markets absorb inventory overhangs built during the pandemic run-up. The one number to track next month is the Xactus MII: if it stays negative YoY through early July while MBA holds positive, the divergence is noise; if MBA begins to follow it lower, the demand picture for August closings softens meaningfully.
