# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has held positive for 55 consecutive weeks, and the four-week average sits at +4.4% year-over-year — still constructive, even as the most recent weekly read slipped to -3.0% YoY, the first negative spot print after a run of positive weeks. That weekly dip is worth watching but not overreading: the seasonal pattern tends to soften demand readings in late June, and the 13-week trend has not yet turned. Inventory reached 1.07 million active listings on Altos as of June 26, up 1.2% YoY — modest growth that continues to decelerate from the double-digit YoY gains of eighteen months ago. New listings told a similar story: the four-week average is essentially flat YoY (+0.4%), and the most recent weekly print turned negative (-7.1% YoY), which likely reflects the holiday-week compression typical of late June rather than a structural withdrawal. Pending days on market edged up to 70.9 days, +6.7% YoY — homes are taking longer to go under contract than a year ago, but the market is not seizing. May existing home sales printed at a 4.17 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million by a notable margin and rising 3.2% YoY; the NAR NSA count of 390,000 was flat year-over-year, a more tempered read, but the directional story is the same — transaction volume is recovering. New home sales told the opposite story: 580,000 SAAR in May, down 7.5% YoY, with months of supply rising to 10.3 — builders are sitting on completed inventory they cannot move at current prices. The 30-year fixed rate closed June at 6.52%, essentially flat over the past four weeks and 15 basis points below a year ago. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury, currently 2.14 percentage points against a historical norm near 1.7 — that 44-basis-point excess premium is the affordability story the headlines are missing. If the spread normalized even partially, the 30-year would fall into the low-to-mid sixes without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications ran a four-week average of +3.9% YoY through June 19, decelerating from the mid-single-digit pace of prior months. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — a credit-inquiry-based measure that tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks — reversed higher this week after several weeks of decline, with its four-week average turning up and the YoY gap improving to -2.3% from worse reads earlier in June. That divergence is worth flagging: if MII continues to recover into July, MBA applications should follow. Single-family starts fell to 882,000 SAAR in May (-7.1% YoY), and single-family permits at 892,000 remain negative YoY for the 24th consecutive month — builders are delivering units from an existing pipeline but not committing meaningfully to new ground-up supply, which has implications for resale competition in 2027 and beyond.

The macro picture is more complicated than the rate narrative suggests. May payrolls came in at +172,000, well above the 59,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate held at 4.3% — both readings supportive of continued household formation and mortgage qualification. But the JOLTS data told a quieter story: the hires rate fell to 3.2% in April and the quits rate slipped to 1.9%, both at levels associated with a labor market where workers feel less confident about mobility. That matters for housing because job-change-driven relocations are a meaningful source of listings and purchases; a frozen labor market suppresses both sides of the transaction ledger. The signal that deserves the most attention is the long-term unemployed cohort: workers jobless 27 weeks or more rose 8.5% month-over-month in May and are up 35.8% year-over-year, now sitting at 1.99 million. This cohort does not threaten the current selling season, but it is the leading population for mortgage delinquency stress in six to twelve months — worth monitoring if it continues to accumulate. On inflation, core CPI printed +0.21% in May and core PCE came in at +0.32% month-over-month; both ran cooler than consensus, and shelter CPI at +3.35% YoY continues its slow descent from the 2023 peaks. The 10-year Treasury ended June at 4.39%, slightly firmer on the day — the spread compression thesis depends on the 10-year staying rangebound or drifting lower, and that is the rate signal to watch through July. Regionally, the March Case-Shiller data (the most recent available, released in May) continue to show the two-speed market: Chicago at +6.0% YoY, New York at +4.0%, Boston at +2.0%, all holding positive, while Seattle (-2.4%), Dallas (-1.7%), Denver (-1.9%), Phoenix (-1.8%), and Tampa (-1.8%) remain in negative YoY
