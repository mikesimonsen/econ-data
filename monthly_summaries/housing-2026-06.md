# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive year-over-year for the 53rd consecutive week, with the Altos 13-week average up 4.9% YoY and still rising — the durable signal underneath what has been a noisy few weeks at the spot level. The 4-week average came in at +6.0% YoY, though it has drifted lower for four straight weeks, and the most recent weekly read ticked down to +4.7% YoY. That deceleration deserves watching but does not reverse the trend. Inventory at 1.047 million active listings (Altos, week of June 12) sits 1.1% below year-ago levels — the YoY gap has been narrowing, and this is the tightest reading since October 2023. New listings are running ahead of last year: the 4-week average is up 3.3% YoY and accelerating on the 13-week view, which means sellers are returning, just not fast enough to rebuild the stock. Pending days on market at 70.9 days remains elevated (+7.2% YoY), a sign that buyers are not rushing, but it has been falling for 14 consecutive weeks — a quiet firming. NAR's May existing home sales print came in at a 4.17 million SAAR, up 3.2% YoY and well above the 3.85 million consensus; on an NSA basis, May was flat YoY, which is the honest read on actual transaction volume. New home sales diverged sharply: the April SAAR of 622,000 fell 6.2% from March and was down 11.9% YoY, with months supply at 9.4 — builders have inventory and are not moving it. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.58% as of June 18, essentially unchanged over the past month. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.14 percentage points — still roughly 45 basis points above the long-run norm of ~1.70 pp, meaning there is rate-relief potential that does not require the Fed to move; if the spread compresses toward historical norms as volatility settles, the 30-year could fall meaningfully without any change in the 10-year. MBA purchase applications (4-week average) are up 4.3% YoY, but the 13-week average is decelerating. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA by roughly 2-4 weeks by capturing credit-pull activity before an application is filed — tells a more cautious story: the 4-week MII average is down 1.7% YoY and the 13-week average has fallen for five straight weeks, now off 2.3% YoY. When MII and MBA diverge in this direction, it typically means the MBA YoY gain softens in the coming weeks. Housing starts missed badly in May — 1,177,000 SAAR versus a 1,350,000 consensus, down 8.2% YoY — driven by a 41.6% single-month collapse in multifamily. Single-family permits held at 886,000 but remain negative YoY for the 24th consecutive month, confirming builders are clearing existing pipeline without committing to new volume. Watch whether the Xactus MII 4-week average turns positive YoY in the next two releases; if it does not, MBA purchase applications likely soften into July.

The May jobs report was the dominant macro event of the month and it landed cleanly: 172,000 nonfarm payrolls versus a 59,000 consensus, unemployment steady at 4.3%, and average hourly earnings up 3.5% YoY. That payroll beat matters for housing because employed, wage-earning households are the marginal buyer — a labor market that held up removes one of the more credible bearish scenarios for summer demand. But the JOLTS data from April complicates the picture: the hires rate fell to 3.2% and the quits rate dropped to 1.9%, both at or near post-pandemic lows. Quits are the mobility signal — workers who quit typically buy homes; a quits rate this low suppresses both household formation and trade-up activity. Meanwhile, the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose 8.5% in a single month and are now 35.8% above year-ago levels — nearly 2 million people. This cohort does not threaten today's selling season, but it is the leading indicator for mortgage stress and distressed-borrower volume in 2027; watch it accumulate. On inflation: May headline CPI came in at +4.2% YoY but printed softer than expected on a monthly basis (+0.5% vs. +0.9% consensus), and core CPI at +2.8% YoY also undershot. Core PCE for April was +3.3% YoY, still above the Fed's target but trending in the right direction. Shelter CPI at +3.4% YoY continues its slow deceleration — private-market rents (Zillow ZORI up 2.95% YoY) are running below shelter CPI, so further deceleration in that component
