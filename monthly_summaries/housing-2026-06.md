# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held up better than the headlines suggest this month. Altos new pending sales came in at +7.2% year-over-year for the week ending June 19, and the 13-week average is running +5.2% YoY — now positive for 54 consecutive weeks. The 4-week average has softened to +5.5% YoY after five weeks of gradual deceleration, which is worth watching but is still solidly positive, and the 13-week trend is still accelerating. Total active inventory reached 1.06 million units, up a thin +0.15% YoY — the first positive YoY reading after a stretch of negative comparisons — while new listings came in at about 92,500 for the week, up just +0.5% YoY on the spot but +2.8% on the 4-week average. The listing supply story is one of deceleration: the 13-week average has been rising for 14 straight weeks but is growing more slowly than earlier in the spring. Pending days on market ticked down to 70.7, off 0.3% on the week but still running +6.4% YoY — the market is moving slightly faster than a week ago but remains slower than a year ago. NAR's May existing home sales printed at 4.17 million SAAR, up 3.7% from April and 3.2% YoY, beating the consensus expectation of 3.85 million by a notable margin — the South and Midwest drove the gain, while the Northeast was flat to soft. The divergence with new home sales is worth flagging: new single-family homes sold fell to 580,000 SAAR in May, down 7.4% from April and 7.5% YoY, with months supply ballooning to 10.3 — builders are sitting on completed inventory and the sales pace has not kept up. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.55% today, down about 10 basis points over the past two days after the 10-year Treasury pulled back to 4.40%. The mortgage-to-Treasury spread is 2.15 percentage points — elevated relative to the long-run norm of roughly 1.7 points, which means a meaningful affordability improvement is available without any Fed action if lenders' risk premiums compress. MBA purchase applications were up +3.9% YoY on the 4-week average as of June 19, still positive but decelerating from above 5% a month ago. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks — has turned a different direction: its 4-week average flipped negative this week at -1.1% YoY after running positive, and the 13-week average has been falling for six consecutive weeks, now -2.4% YoY. That divergence between current MBA readings (still green) and MII (already red) is the clearest near-term warning signal in the data right now. Watch whether MBA purchase applications follow the MII lower into early July, or whether MII stabilizes; that gap will tell you a lot about whether the summer selling season ends strong or fades.

The macro backdrop shifted more than the rate headlines captured this month. May payrolls came in at +172,000 — a substantial beat against a consensus that had been anchored around +59,000 — and the unemployment rate held at 4.3%, also better than expected. That keeps the household income foundation intact and removes the near-term recession scenario that had been weighing on buyer psychology. Wage growth is running at +3.45% YoY, above headline CPI's recent prints, which restores some real purchasing power. On inflation, May core CPI printed +0.21% month-over-month and +2.82% YoY, cooler than the +0.3% consensus; core PCE for April came in at +0.24% MoM, also below the +0.3% forecast, with shelter CPI at +3.35% YoY and still decelerating — that deceleration in shelter feeds directly into future Fed optionality and, more proximately, into the spread compression story on mortgage rates. The Fed has not moved, but the combination of cooling core inflation and a Treasury yield that has pulled back to 4.40% creates conditions where the spread could compress toward historical norms without any policy change; every 20-25 basis points of spread compression translates to roughly that amount of rate relief for buyers. The labor market signal that deserves the most attention over the next month, however, is not the headline payroll number — it's the long-term unemployed cohort. Workers unemployed 27 or more weeks rose to 1.988 million in May, up 8.5% in a single month and 35.8% year-over-year. The hires rate fell to 3.2% and the quits rate dropped to 1.9%, both declining in April — workers are less mobile, and longer spells of unemployment are accumulating. None of this affects the current selling season, but it is the leading edge of a future distressed-borrower story; agents working markets with high concentrations of trade-exposed employment should note the trajectory.
