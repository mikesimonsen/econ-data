# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held firm through May and into early June, though the pace is moderating from its peak. Altos weekly new pending sales came in at 90,205 for the week ending June 12, up 4.69% year-over-year, with the 4-week average running at 89,357 — still +6.0% YoY despite four consecutive weeks of decline in the average. More durably, the 13-week average sits at 89,288, up 4.90% YoY and positive for 53 consecutive weeks — a streak that tells a cleaner story than any single week. Inventory continues to build: Altos total active supply reached 1,046,775, up 1.08% on the week but still 1.05% below year-ago levels — the tightest reading since October 2023, which means the YoY inventory growth story that defined the past two years has essentially closed. New listings are accelerating on both the weekly (+4.13% YoY) and 13-week average (+1.02% YoY, rising 14 weeks), a constructive sign for fall supply, though pending days on market at 70.9 days remains 7.15% above last year, suggesting buyers are still taking their time. The NAR existing sales print for May was the headline of the month: 4.17 million SAAR, up 3.22% year-over-year and beating consensus by a wide margin (+320,000 units). The NSA read was flat YoY at 390,000 — a more measured confirmation that the SAAR number isn't an artifact of seasonal adjustment. New home sales told the opposite story: April came in at 622,000 SAAR, down 11.9% YoY, with months supply jumping to 9.4 — builders are sitting on inventory while existing-home buyers transact. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.58% today, roughly 29 basis points below a year ago, but the spread to the 10-year Treasury remains at 2.14 percentage points against a historical norm near 1.70 — meaning the mortgage market is still charging a meaningful premium for rate uncertainty. That spread has compressed 35-37 basis points over the past year, and any further normalization would deliver real affordability relief without the Fed moving at all. MBA purchase applications for the week ending June 12 were +3.02% YoY, with the 4-week average at +4.34% YoY; the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks — flipped to barely positive YoY (+0.08%) on the spot read but remains negative on the 13-week average (-2.32%), a soft caution worth watching. On starts, May's 1,177,000 SAAR badly missed expectations (consensus was 1,350,000) and fell 8.19% YoY, with single-family starts down 7.06%; completions are running 17.4% below year-ago for single-family — a number that implies the new-home supply pipeline is thinning faster than permits suggest. Watch whether the Xactus MII firms over the next four weeks; if it turns decisively positive, MBA applications should follow, and July pending sales will confirm whether this selling season has a second act.

The macro backdrop is genuinely mixed in ways that matter for housing. May payrolls printed +172,000 — well above the +59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.3%, beating expectations of 4.4%. That combination normally supports buyer confidence and reduces distress risk in the near term. But underneath those topline numbers, the JOLTS data for April showed the hires rate falling to 3.2% and the quits rate dropping to 1.9%, both deteriorating month-over-month; workers quitting less freely signals declining labor market confidence, and lower hires mean fewer households forming or relocating — both headwinds for transaction volume with a 3-6 month lag. The more acute signal is the long-term unemployed cohort: workers unemployed 27 weeks or more now number 1,988,000, up 35.79% year-over-year and still climbing. This group does not affect the current selling season, but it is the leading indicator for mortgage delinquencies 9-12 months out, and agents in markets with concentrated industries should note it. On inflation, May CPI came in cooler than expected — core at +2.82% YoY, headline at +4.17% — and Core PCE for April was +3.29% YoY, also below consensus. Shelter CPI continues to decelerate (+3.35% YoY), which feeds directly into the Fed's calculus; as measured shelter inflation catches down to real-time rent data (Zillow's ZORI is running at +2.95% YoY), Core PCE has room to soften further without any change in actual housing conditions. The 10-year Treasury is at 4.44%, steady, and the rate market is not pricing in imminent Fed cuts — but cooler inflation prints reduce the risk of rate re-acceleration.
