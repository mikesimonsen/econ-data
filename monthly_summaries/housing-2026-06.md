# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held firmly positive for the 53rd consecutive week on the Altos 13-week average, with that average up 4.9% year-over-year and still rising — the durable trend beneath the noise. The 4-week average sits at +6.0% YoY, though it has softened modestly over the past four weeks, and the most recent weekly print ticked down slightly to +4.7% YoY. That mild deceleration is worth watching but does not break the trend. Inventory at just under 1.05 million active listings is up 1.1% week-over-week but still running 1.1% below year-ago levels — the YoY gap has narrowed to its tightest since October 2023, which tells you supply growth is stalling relative to where it was heading six months ago. New listings are firming: the 4-week average is +3.3% YoY and accelerating, while the 13-week average has risen for 14 consecutive weeks. Pending days on market at 70.9 days remain elevated (+7.2% YoY), a persistent signal that buyers have more negotiating room than the headline demand numbers imply. The NAR May existing home sales print — 4.17 million SAAR, up 3.2% YoY — beat consensus by a wide margin and confirms that closed sales momentum is real; the NSA figure was flat YoY, which is the more honest read of volume, but even that represents stability at a firmed level. New home sales, by contrast, fell to 622K SAAR in April, down nearly 12% YoY, with months supply rising to 9.4 — builders are not clearing inventory the way existing-home sellers are. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.54% today, down 37 basis points year-over-year and easing slightly over the past week. The spread to the 10-year Treasury remains at 2.10 percentage points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points — meaning there is meaningful rate-relief potential without any Fed action if lenders' risk appetite normalizes even partially. The MBA 4-week purchase index average is +5.6% YoY and the 13-week average has been positive for 69 consecutive weeks, both constructive. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks, is a different story: the 13-week MII average has been falling for five weeks and sits at -2.3% YoY, while the spot reading just turned negative after a brief positive. That divergence between MII and MBA is the most important leading indicator to watch heading into July — if MII is right, purchase application momentum softens before the end of the month. Housing starts in May fell sharply to 1,177K SAAR, missing consensus by roughly 175K, with single-family starts down 7% YoY and the pipeline of units under construction contracting for a 34th consecutive month YoY. Permits held closer to flat at 1,413K, but single-family permits are negative YoY for the 24th straight month. The divergence — starts falling faster than permits — signals builders are burning down their existing pipelines while pulling back on ground-breaking, a supply constraint that will matter more for 2027 than for this selling season.

The macro backdrop is genuinely more constructive than it was a month ago, but with one accumulating risk that agents should not dismiss. May payrolls came in at +172,000 — nearly three times the +59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.3%, beating expectations. Wage growth at +3.5% YoY supports purchasing power without being inflationary enough to push the Fed toward tightening. More importantly for housing, headline CPI printed at +4.2% YoY but core CPI came in at +2.8%, below the +3.0% expected, and core PCE for April was +3.3% — cooler than forecast. Cooling core inflation without a labor-market collapse is the rate environment housing has been waiting for: the 10-year Treasury at 4.44% and a spread that has already compressed 35 basis points year-over-year suggest the path toward a 6.1-6.2% conventional rate is open if core PCE continues to decelerate, even without a Fed cut. That is the hidden affordability story — the rate can fall on its own. The regional picture from March Case-Shiller (the most recent available) sharpens the two-speed market: Chicago is up 6.0% YoY and New York up 4.0%, while Dallas is -1.7%, Denver -1.9%, Phoenix -1.8%, Seattle -2.4%, Tampa -1.8%, and Atlanta has turned negative for the first time since August 2012. If you are pricing a listing in the Midwest or Northeast, appreciation is on your side; if you are in the Sun Belt metros that overbuilt through 2022-23, you are negotiating against inventory overhang and falling comps simultaneously.
