# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held firm through June, with the Altos weekly read coming in at roughly +7% year-over-year for the week ending June 19 and the four-week average tracking at +5.5% YoY — both positive, though the four-week average has softened for five consecutive weeks after peaking earlier in the spring. The 13-week average tells the more durable story: up +5.1% YoY, rising for 14 consecutive weeks and positive for 54 straight weeks. Demand has been consistent; it has simply decelerated from its spring peak, which is typical seasonal behavior. Inventory reached 1.06 million active listings the week of June 19, up just +0.15% YoY — the first positive YoY reading after a prolonged stretch below year-ago levels, a shift worth noting even if the gap is thin. New listings came in at 92,504 for the week, up a modest +0.5% YoY on the spot reading but +2.8% on the four-week average and +1.3% on the 13-week average, suggesting sellers are incrementally more willing to list than a year ago, though supply growth is not accelerating. Pending days on market sit at 70.7, up +6.4% YoY — still elevated versus pre-pandemic norms but the YoY gap is narrowing for the 14th consecutive week, meaning the market is gradually absorbing pace rather than slowing further. The May NAR existing home sales print landed at 4.17 million SAAR, up +3.2% YoY and well ahead of the 3.85 million consensus, continuing a two-month rise. The NSA count of 390,000 units was flat year-over-year, which is the cleaner read on underlying volume; the SAAR beat likely reflects favorable seasonal adjustment. New home sales diverged sharply: 580,000 SAAR in May, down -7.5% YoY and -7.4% month-over-month, with months supply rising to 10.3 — a level that historically prompts builders to cut prices or incentives rather than start new product. Single-family starts fell to 882,000 SAAR in May, down -7.1% YoY, and total starts missed consensus by nearly 175,000 units; single-family permits ticked up slightly to 892,000 but remain -0.8% YoY and have not posted a positive YoY reading in 24 months, meaning the new-supply pipeline is not rebuilding. The thirty-year fixed rate sits at 6.52% as of June 29, essentially unchanged over the past month and down only 15 basis points from a year ago. The spread to the ten-year Treasury is 2.14 percentage points — still roughly 45 basis points above the long-run norm of about 1.7 points. The four-week average spread has been compressing slowly, now 35 basis points below year-ago levels; if that compression continues toward historical norms, rates could ease toward the low-to-mid sixes without any movement from the Fed. MBA purchase applications for the week of June 19 were up +2.7% YoY on the spot read and +3.9% on the four-week average — positive, but decelerating. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by roughly two to four weeks, is signaling more caution: the MII fell -10.4% on the most recent week, is down -2.0% YoY on the spot, and the four-week average has turned negative at -1.1% YoY. When MII diverges downward from MBA this sharply, purchase application softness typically follows within a month. Watch whether the MBA four-week average holds above flat through July.

The most consequential macro development this month was May payrolls: 172,000 jobs added against a consensus of 59,000, with the unemployment rate holding at 4.3%. That is a strong enough labor market to sustain housing demand at current levels, and it is almost certainly why existing home sales beat so cleanly. The mechanism is direct — employed households with wage growth of +3.5% YoY are the buyer pool, and that pool has not shrunk. What is worth watching beneath the headline is the long-term unemployed cohort: those out of work 27 weeks or more rose to 1.99 million in May, up +35.8% YoY. This group does not affect the current selling season — they are not buying homes this summer — but as they exhaust savings and, eventually, forebearance options, they represent latent pressure on delinquency volume 6-12 months out. Inflation came in softer than feared: headline CPI rose +4.2% YoY in May but the monthly print was cooler than consensus, and core CPI held at +2.8% YoY. Core PCE, which the Fed targets, remains elevated at +3.4% YoY — still above the 2% target, which is why the Fed has not moved and why the ten-year sits at 4.38%.
