# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held above year-ago levels for the 53rd consecutive week, with the Altos 13-week average running +4.90% YoY — a streak that has been quietly widening even as the spot read ticked down modestly in the most recent week. The 4-week average came in at +6.00% YoY, though it has softened over the past four weeks; that deceleration is worth watching but does not yet break the trend. Inventory at 1.047 million active listings sits just 1.1% below last year's level, the tightest YoY gap since October 2023 — meaning the inventory expansion that ran for most of 2025 has effectively stalled. New listings are growing, with the 4-week average up 3.3% YoY and the 13-week average up 1.0% YoY and accelerating, so supply is being added; it is just being absorbed quickly enough that the stock is not building the way it was. Pending days on market at 70.9 are still running 7.1% above last year, which tells you homes are taking longer to find buyers, but that figure has been falling for 14 consecutive weeks — the market is gradually absorbing that friction. NAR's May existing home sales print came in at a 4.17M SAAR, 3.2% above May 2025, and beat expectations by a wide margin (consensus was 3.85M); NSA sales were flat YoY at 390,000, consistent with a market that is transacting more than the prior year's rate but not accelerating beyond seasonal norms. New home sales for April told a different story — 622K SAAR, down 11.9% YoY, with months supply jumping to 9.4 — builders are sitting on elevated finished inventory even as their starts fell sharply in May (single-family starts down 7.1% YoY, multifamily off 9.3%), which signals less pipeline competition for resale in late 2026. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.65% as of today, essentially unchanged over the past month; the more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.17 percentage points, still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap is where the hidden affordability story lives — if credit conditions normalize even partially, rates fall without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications are up 4.3% YoY on the 4-week average, and the Xactus MII — which typically leads MBA by two to four weeks — has its 4-week average at -1.7% YoY, still negative despite recently reversing upward. The divergence between MBA's positive YoY read and MII's still-negative trend is the signal to watch over the next four weeks; if MII firms further, purchase applications should follow. If it rolls back, MBA's positive trend could fade heading into July.

The macro backdrop improved more than the consensus expected on almost every print this month. May payrolls came in at +172K versus a +59K consensus, the unemployment rate held at 4.3% (a beat versus 4.4% expected), and hourly earnings are running +3.45% YoY — enough to hold real purchasing power modestly positive. For housing, steady employment income is the floor under demand; the payroll beat removes one of the downside scenarios agents were pricing in. Inflation also cooperated: core CPI printed +0.21% month-over-month against a +0.3% consensus, and core PCE at +0.24% similarly undershot expectations. That matters because lower-than-expected core inflation compresses the market's view of where the Fed needs to stay restrictive, and — crucially for mortgages — it keeps long-run inflation expectations (breakevens, which are the bond market's real-time read on future inflation priced into Treasuries) from drifting higher. The 10-year Treasury is at 4.48% today; if breakevens hold and the Fed signals any pivot, the mechanism for rate relief runs through that number first, then through spread compression, before agents see it at the point-of-sale. The one number that does not fit the constructive narrative is the long-term unemployed cohort: workers unemployed 27 weeks or longer rose 8.5% in May and are now 35.8% above a year ago, with median unemployment duration up 22% YoY. This cohort does not drive transactions today, but it accumulates toward mortgage stress and distressed inventory in a 6–12 month window — something to file, not act on yet.
