# Monthly Housing Summary — June 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales on the Altos weekly tracker came in at 89,915 for the week ending June 19, up 7.2% year-over-year — the 54th consecutive week of positive YoY demand. The four-week average is running +5.5% YoY, and the 13-week average has now risen for 14 straight weeks at +5.2% YoY, still accelerating on that longer horizon even as the nearer-term reads have drifted softer over the past five weeks. That softening in the four-week average is worth watching but not yet alarming — we are in the seasonal plateau that follows the spring peak. Inventory reached 1.06 million active listings as of June 19, up just 0.15% year-over-year, the first positive YoY reading after an extended stretch below zero; new listings are running +2.8% YoY on the four-week average, healthy but decelerating off a higher base. Pending days on market at 70.7 days is still elevated versus prior years (+6.4% YoY) though that gap has been narrowing for 14 straight weeks. NAR's May existing home sales came in at a 4.17 million SAAR — well ahead of the 3.85 million consensus and up 3.2% year-over-year, with YoY growth accelerating. On an NSA basis, May closed flat year-over-year at 390,000 units, which is the cleaner read: volume is holding, not expanding. The divergence with new home sales is worth flagging: the Census May print on new single-family sales fell to 580,000 SAAR, down 7.5% YoY, with months of supply climbing to 10.3 — builders are carrying finished inventory that is not clearing at current prices, even as the median new home price barely budged year-over-year. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.53% today, essentially unchanged over the past month. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.15 percentage points — still roughly 45 basis points above the long-run norm of about 1.70 points, though it has compressed 31 basis points year-over-year. That spread compression is the affordability story the headlines are not telling: if the spread normalizes another 25 basis points without any Fed move, the conventional rate falls to roughly 6.25–6.30% on the current 10-year. The MBA purchase application four-week average is +3.9% YoY, positive for 71 consecutive weeks, but the trend is decelerating. More importantly, the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA applications by two to four weeks — just turned negative year-over-year, with the four-week average down 1.1% YoY and the 13-week average falling for six straight weeks. When MII diverges from MBA to the downside, the MBA read tends to follow within a month. That is the primary indicator to watch through late July.

The macro backdrop is genuinely two-sided right now. May payrolls came in at +172,000, well above the 59,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate held at 4.3%, beating the 4.4% expectation. A labor market that beats by that margin keeps household formation intact and supports would-be buyers who are waiting for rate relief rather than forced to the sidelines by income stress. But the quality indicators inside the labor data are softening: the hires rate fell to 3.2% in April and the quits rate dropped to 1.9%, both declining year-over-year — less job mobility means fewer voluntary relocations, which is one of the quieter demand suppressors in the current market. The long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) rose 8.5% in a single month and is now up 35.8% year-over-year. That group does not affect the current selling season, but at this rate of accumulation it represents a delinquency and distressed-inventory signal to monitor 6–12 months out. On inflation, May core PCE printed at +3.41% year-over-year — still running above the Fed's 2% target and accelerating, even as core CPI came in cooler than expected at +2.82% YoY. Shelter CPI held at +3.35% YoY; it is still contributing meaningfully to headline inflation, which limits the Fed's room to move even if other components cool. The 10-year sits at 4.38%, leaving the 30-year rate anchored unless the spread compresses further. Case-Shiller data through March (released in late May) deepens the geographic split: Chicago is +6.0% YoY and New York +4.0%, while Dallas is -1.7%, Denver -1.9%, Seattle -2.4%, and Tampa -1.8% and accelerating to the downside. Agents in appreciation markets and depreciation markets are operating in functionally different environments right now — price narratives that work in the Midwest will actively mislead clients in the Mountain West and Florida.
