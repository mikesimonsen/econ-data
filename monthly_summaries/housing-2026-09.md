**September 2026 Housing Summary**

Weekly demand held positive through the summer, but the trend is decelerating in ways that matter. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has run positive year-over-year for 55 consecutive weeks — a durable streak — but the four-week average came in at +4.4% YoY as of late June, down from a stronger pace earlier in the cycle, and the most recent weekly print turned negative at -3.0% YoY. That single-week dip bears watching; it may reflect normal late-summer seasonality or the beginning of a softer demand patch. Inventory at roughly 1.07 million active listings was up just 1.2% YoY, and new listings growth has stalled — the four-week average was barely positive at +0.4% YoY after the weekly print turned negative at -7.1%, suggesting sellers remain cautious rather than opportunistic. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, a signal that the pace of absorption is gently slowing. NAR's August existing home sales SAAR came in at 3.98 million — beating the 3.85 million consensus — but that headline flatters the underlying read: on a non-seasonally-adjusted basis, sales fell 3.5% YoY, and the YoY comparison turned negative for the first time in several months. New home sales were weaker still, with the July SAAR at 607,000, down 5.0% YoY, and months supply for new homes jumping to 9.6 — well into buyer's-market territory for builders. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.17% today, up 82 basis points from a year ago, and the spread over the 10-year Treasury is 2.22 points — roughly 50 basis points above the long-run norm of about 1.7 points. That elevated spread is the affordability story the headline rate misses: if credit conditions normalize even partially, rates could fall meaningfully without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications are the leading indicator to watch — the four-week average is running -6.0% YoY, at its lowest since September 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for ten consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (which leads MBA by two to four weeks by measuring early-stage credit inquiries) was modestly firming in late June at -2.3% YoY on its four-week average, but the 13-week trend was still sliding. If MII does not confirm a September rebound, the MBA weakness is likely to persist into October. Single-family permits at 878,000 SAAR are up 2.3% YoY and at their highest since May 2024, but single-family starts ticked up only because multifamily pulled the headline down sharply; total completions fell to their lowest level since June 2011, down 27.5% YoY. That completion collapse limits near-term resale competition from new supply — a modest offset for existing inventory — but it also means the pipeline that was supposed to ease affordability is clearing faster than it is being replenished.

On the macro side, three data points frame the next four weeks. First, August payrolls came in at roughly 162,000 — well above the 59,000 consensus — and unemployment held at 4.1%, both readings that remove near-term recession urgency from the Fed's calculus and keep mortgage rates from falling sharply on flight-to-safety flows. The labor market is healthy enough at the top line, but the JOLTS data tells a quieter story: total hires fell 3.3% YoY and the hires rate dropped to 3.2%, meaning fewer workers are moving between jobs. In housing terms, a low-churn labor market suppresses geographic mobility and depresses voluntary listing activity — it is one reason new listings growth has stalled. Second, core PCE ran at 3.34% YoY through July and shelter CPI was still running at 3.0% YoY in August, keeping the Fed cautious about cutting even as the 10-year Treasury sits at 4.95%. The 10-year has risen 80 basis points over the past year; with the spread already elevated at 2.22 points, the path to sub-7% mortgage rates requires either the 10-year to fall or the spread to compress — the latter is more likely near-term if credit markets stabilize. Third, the long-term unemployed cohort — workers out of work 27 or more weeks — rose 9.0% in a single month to 1.93 million, reversing a recent decline.
