# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales posted a weekly reading down 3.0% YoY for the week of June 26, a reversal after the four-week average had been running at +4.4% YoY — a meaningful split between the spot read and the smoothed trend. The 13-week average held at +4.8% YoY and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks, which remains the steadier signal. Inventory continued its gradual climb, with Altos active supply up 1.2% YoY and rising for eight straight weeks, though the pace of growth is modest rather than accelerating. New listings told a more cautious story: the single-week read fell 7.1% YoY, the first negative YoY print after a stretch of gains, though the four-week average stayed barely positive at +0.4% YoY and the 13-week average has been rising for 14 weeks. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — still grinding higher, a sign that buyer urgency has not returned. NAR existing home sales came in at 3.98 million SAAR for August, down 0.5% YoY and beating the 3.85 million consensus, but the NSA count was down 3.5% YoY — a meaningful deterioration from the positive YoY reads earlier this year. New home sales diverged sharply: the July print landed at 607,000 SAAR, down 5.0% YoY and off 10.5% from June, with months supply jumping to 9.6 — builders are sitting on completed inventory that isn't clearing. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.19% as of today, essentially flat over the past several days after ticking lower. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: at 2.23 percentage points currently, against a long-run historical norm closer to 1.70 points, there is meaningful room for the mortgage rate to fall without any move from the Fed — simply from spread normalization. That compression has not arrived yet; the four-week average spread has actually been widening, rising for four consecutive weeks. MBA purchase applications are running at a four-week average of -6.0% YoY, the weakest reading since September 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 10 straight weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA applications by two to four weeks — showed a partial recovery off its recent low as of late June, with the four-week MII average ticking higher, but the 13-week average was still falling. That divergence between the short-term MII bounce and the deteriorating MBA trend is the leading indicator to watch over the next four weeks: if the MII uptick holds and translates into MBA applications by mid-October, the demand picture stabilizes. If not, the fall selling season opens soft. On supply, single-family permits at 878,000 SAAR remain above a year ago (+2.3% YoY) even after a slight pullback, but total completions collapsed to 1,128,000 SAAR in August — down 27.5% YoY and the lowest reading since June 2011. The near-term supply pipeline is thinning faster than most agents have priced in.

The macro backdrop this month is genuinely mixed in ways that matter differently for housing. August payrolls came in at +162,000, well above the 59,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate held at 4.1% versus a 4.4% expectation — the labor market is not deteriorating at the pace the pessimists expected. That matters for housing because job security is the primary gating factor for a first-time buyer entering a contract; a stable unemployment rate at 4.1% is consistent with a functional, if slow, market. What is less benign is the internal composition: the long-term unemployed cohort (those out of work 27 weeks or more) rose 9.0% in a single month to 1.93 million, and median unemployment duration jumped to 11.4 weeks, up 15% YoY. These workers are not showing up as defaults yet, but they represent the borrower stress that accumulates quietly and surfaces in FHA delinquency data 6–12 months later. Watch it. The JOLTS hires rate fell back to 3.2% and the quits rate slipped to 1.9%, both below year-ago levels — less job-switching means less household formation-driven demand for moves. On inflation, August CPI headline came in at +3.35% YoY with core at +2.45%, both cooler than feared, and shelter CPI held at +3.0% YoY, continuing its gradual descent. Core PCE through July was running at 3.34% YoY — still above the Fed's target and the reason the 10-year Treasury has not moved materially lower, sitting at 4.96% today.
