**September 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held firmer than the late-summer seasonal pattern implied. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales was running roughly 4.8% above year-ago levels as of the week ending June 26 — positive for 55 consecutive weeks — though the most recent weekly read slipped to -3.0% YoY as contract activity decelerated off its spring peak, and the 4-week average at +4.4% suggests the trend remains constructive even if momentum is fading. Inventory on the Altos side was up about 1.2% YoY and still climbing, while new listings turned negative YoY on a spot basis (-7.1%) though the 4-week average held fractionally positive; the 13-week average has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks, indicating sellers were steadily re-entering through the first half of the summer before pulling back at the margin. Pending days on market ticked up roughly 6.7% YoY — not alarming, but a consistent signal that buyers have more negotiating room than they did a year ago. July existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million by a notable margin, and were up 1.25% YoY on a seasonally adjusted basis — six consecutive months of positive YoY NSA comparisons. The divergence from new home sales is worth noting: new single-family sales fell to 607K SAAR in July, down 5% YoY, while new home months' supply jumped to 9.6 — builders are sitting on inventory and cutting median prices, which were off 0.9% YoY. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.89% as of September 4, essentially flat over the past month and up 44 basis points year over year. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.13 percentage points — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap matters: it means rates have room to fall 40-plus basis points without any Fed action if credit-market conditions normalize. MBA purchase applications are slightly negative YoY (-2.8% on the 4-week average), with the 13-week average now down eight consecutive weeks — a softening trend agents should watch. The Xactus MII, which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks, was also running negative YoY (-5.6% spot, -2.3% on the 4-week average) as of late June, suggesting the August application softness was signaled early; watch whether the MII stabilizes in coming weeks as a gauge of September contract activity. Total building permits beat expectations at 1,433K SAAR and are now up 5.2% YoY — a genuine reversal — but starts fell sharply to 1,239K, missing estimates by 111K units and down 12.75% YoY. The permits-leads-starts gap is widening: builders are reserving optionality without breaking ground, a pattern that constrains new supply in 2027 more than it affects this selling season.

The macro backdrop was more supportive than consensus anticipated, though not uniformly so. August payrolls came in at +162K — nearly triple the 59K consensus estimate — and the unemployment rate held at 4.1% against an expectation of 4.4%, which removes near-term pressure on the Fed to act aggressively. A stable labor market is the foundation for housing demand; households do not sign purchase contracts when they are uncertain about their income. But two labor-market signals deserve closer attention. The JOLTS hires rate fell to 3.2% in July, the quits rate dropped to 1.9%, and both reversed recent improvements — when workers stop voluntarily leaving jobs, discretionary large purchases tend to follow with a lag. More quietly, the count of workers unemployed 27 weeks or longer rose nearly 9% in a single month to 1.93 million, and median unemployment duration jumped to 11.4 weeks, up 15% YoY. This cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is the population from which delinquencies emerge 6-12 months out — worth monitoring on the servicer side. On inflation: core CPI printed at 2.47% YoY and came in cooler than expected, but core PCE rose to 3.34% YoY, running meaningfully above the Fed's 2% target for a seventh consecutive month. Shelter CPI contributed 3.16% YoY — still elevated but decelerating from earlier peaks, and the private-market rent data (Zillow ZORI at +3.0% YoY) suggests official shelter inflation has further to fall. The regional price picture in the June Case-Shiller data (reflecting April-June closings) continues to split cleanly: Chicago +6.9% YoY, New York +4.5%, Boston +2.7%, and Detroit +3.3% — Midwest and Northeast markets where inventory remains structurally thin are still appreciating at a healthy clip.
