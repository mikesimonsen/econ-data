# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through most of the summer but is showing cracks as September opens. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales stood at +4.81% YoY through late June — a streak that had run 55 consecutive weeks in positive territory — but the most recent weekly read flipped to -2.99% YoY, and the 4-week average has decelerated to +4.37%. That single-week dip may be noise, though it comes as Realtor.com's pending count also turned slightly negative YoY (-0.19%) in August and the Realtor.com pending ratio fell to 0.40, down nearly 5% on the month. Inventory continues to build — NAR's existing-home count reached 1.62 million units in August, up 5.9% YoY and 4.9 months of supply, the loosest since the rate shock of 2022-23 — while Altos total inventory was still rising week over week through late June, up 1.2% YoY. New listings have softened: the Altos 4-week average slipped to just +0.41% YoY after running positive for weeks, and the weekly read turned negative (-7.1% YoY). Pending days on market at 70.9 days is running 6.7% above year-ago levels, a two-year-plus streak of slower pace. NAR's August existing-home sales print — 3.98 million SAAR — beat the consensus of 3.85 million but still fell 0.5% YoY on a SAAR basis; the NSA read was -3.5% YoY, and the YoY comparison just turned negative after months of positive readings. New home sales were weaker: July came in at 607,000 SAAR, down 5% YoY, with months of supply at 9.6 — elevated enough that builders are already pulling back sharply on starts (808,000 single-family SAAR in July, -15% YoY, missing the 1.35 million total starts consensus by a wide margin), even as permits firmed to 894,000 single-family, the highest since May 2024. That starts-versus-permits divergence is the supply signal to watch: builders are approving future pipeline while throttling current production, which limits near-term completions but does not structurally resolve inventory. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.08% as of today, up 81 basis points year over year, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.13-2.15 points — still roughly 45 basis points above the historical norm of ~1.70. The 4-week spread average has ticked up slightly after declining, meaning the hidden affordability lever of spread compression has not engaged this month. MBA purchase applications are running -4.2% YoY on the 4-week average and -1.3% on the 13-week, which has fallen nine consecutive weeks — a meaningful deteriorating trend, not a blip. The Xactus MII (a credit-inquiry-based index that leads MBA by two to four weeks) turned upward in late June after a decline, with the 4-week average at +2.3%, but the 13-week average remains down 2.4% YoY after seven weeks of softening. The MII uptick is the one leading-edge positive; watch whether it sustains through September and begins to lift the MBA trend in the next two to three weeks, or fades.

The macro backdrop is genuinely mixed in ways that matter differently for housing. August payrolls came in at +162,000 — well above the +59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%, also beating expectations of 4.4%. On the surface that reads as labor market resilience. But the JOLTS data tells a more cautious story: the hires rate fell back to 3.2% in July and the quits rate slipped to 1.9%, both below year-ago levels, signaling that the job market is stable but not generating the household formation confidence that drives purchase decisions. The number of workers unemployed 27 weeks or longer rose to 1.93 million in August, up 9% from July and 15% higher than a year ago in median duration — this cohort does not threaten the current selling season, but its accumulation is the slow-building delinquency story to watch six to twelve months out. On inflation, today's August CPI print showed headline at +3.35% YoY and core at +2.45% YoY, both cooler than expected (headline consensus was +0.9% month-over-month; actual was +0.4%). Core PCE through July was running at +3.34% YoY — still above the Fed's target — but shelter CPI at +3.03% YoY is decelerating and, given the roughly 12-18 month lag from market rents into the index, should continue to ease, which will pull core readings lower over the coming quarters. The 10-year Treasury sits at 4.93%, up 92 basis points YoY, and is the primary force keeping the 30-year mortgage rate above 7%.
