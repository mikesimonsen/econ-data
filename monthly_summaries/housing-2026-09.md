# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales held at +4.81% YoY as of the week ending June 26 — positive for 55 consecutive weeks — though the single-week read slipped to -2.99% YoY, the four-week average remained a healthier +4.37% YoY. The streak and the trend are what matter here; one week's dip in late June is seasonal noise. Active inventory rose to roughly 1.07 million units on the Altos count, up about 1.2% YoY, while new listings' four-week average sat near +0.4% YoY and the 13-week average has been rising for 14 consecutive weeks — supply growth is modest but real, and it is not outrunning demand at the national level. Pending days on market (70.9 days, +6.7% YoY) continued to tick up, a quiet signal that absorption is slowing at the margin. NAR reported August existing sales at 3.98 million SAAR, slightly below July and down 0.5% YoY — the YoY comparison turned negative for the first time in several months, though the print beat the 3.85 million consensus. New home sales told a softer story: the July read came in at 607,000 SAAR, down 10.5% from June and 5% below a year ago, with months supply of new homes climbing to 9.6 — elevated by any standard, and a sign that builder pricing power has compressed. On rates, the 30-year fixed sits at 7.07% today, up roughly 10 basis points over the past two trading sessions with the 10-year Treasury at 4.92%. The mortgage-Treasury spread — the gap between the 30-year rate and the 10-year yield, historically around 1.7 percentage points — is currently 2.15 points, still roughly 45 basis points above normal. That excess spread is the hidden affordability story: if it compresses toward historical norms, the 30-year falls meaningfully without the Fed doing anything. The four-week spread average did tick up this week after several weeks of compression, so watch whether that reverses. The MBA purchase index four-week average is running -4.2% YoY as of September 4, and the 13-week average has fallen for nine consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA applications by two to four weeks — was showing a +3.5% week-over-week uptick in late June after several weeks of declines, and its four-week average reversed higher as well, suggesting that the MBA softness we are seeing now may be near a near-term floor. Builder starts fell sharply in July (808,000 single-family SAAR, -15% YoY) and missed expectations by a wide margin, but permits told the opposite story: single-family permits rose to 894,000 SAAR, the highest since May 2024 and up 2.2% YoY. That starts-permits divergence means builders are deliberately throttling current production while signaling confidence in future pipeline — the supply implication lands in 2027, not this selling season. Watch whether the MBA purchase index stabilizes in the next two weeks, and whether the Treasury yield retreats from the 4.9% range.

The macro backdrop is genuinely mixed in the ways that matter most for housing. August payrolls came in at +162,000 — well above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.1% against a 4.4% expectation, which removes one source of demand concern. But the JOLTS data for July told a different story underneath: total hires fell 5.2% in a single month and the hires rate slipped to 3.2%, while quits dropped nearly 5% — both reversing recent gains. A labor market where people are staying put and fewer are being hired is one where household formation slows and move-up demand stalls. The long-term unemployed count (those jobless 27 or more weeks) rose 9% in a single month to 1.93 million, and median unemployment duration jumped to 11.4 weeks, +15% YoY. This cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is where future delinquency volume will emerge if it continues to build. On inflation, Core PCE ran at 3.34% YoY in July — still above the Fed's target — while shelter CPI was +3.16% YoY, continuing to ease but doing so slowly. The 10-year Treasury at 4.92% reflects a market that is not convinced the Fed is close to cutting, and the 30-year rate following it higher confirms that the affordability ceiling remains in place for most buyers. Regionally, the Case-Shiller June data (released August 27, so it reflects contract signings from roughly April–May) shows the two-speed market sharpening: Chicago is up 6.9% YoY and New York up 4.5%, while Dallas is -0.6%, Phoenix -0.7%, Las Vegas -1.8%, Seattle -1.8%, and Tampa -1.5% — all still negative on an annual basis.
