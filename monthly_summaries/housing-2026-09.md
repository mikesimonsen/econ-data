# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales held positive on the four-week average through the week of June 26, with the Altos 4-week average running +4.4% year-over-year and the 13-week average at +4.8% YoY — a streak of 55 consecutive weeks in positive territory on that longer measure, though it has been decelerating. The spot week dipped to -3.0% YoY, the first negative print after that extended run, so watch whether that's seasonal noise or the start of a trend change. Total active inventory was up 1.2% YoY at 1.07 million, still building but at a slower pace than earlier in the year; new listings on the 4-week average were barely positive YoY (+0.4%) after the spot week fell 7.1% YoY, suggesting the new-supply impulse that drove inventory growth earlier in 2026 has largely stalled. Pending days on market have now been elevated YoY for 103 consecutive weeks, and at 70.9 days they remain well above pre-pandemic norms — homes are moving, but buyers are taking their time. NAR's July existing home sales print came in at 4.06 million SAAR, beating consensus of 3.85 million, but the 1.3% YoY gain and the slight sequential softening (-0.7%) tell you this is a market finding a floor rather than accelerating; the NSA read confirmed the same direction at +2.8% YoY. The contrast with new home sales is worth noting: new single-family sales fell to 607,000 SAAR in July, down 5% YoY, and months' supply jumped to 9.6 — builders are sitting on elevated finished inventory and cutting median prices (-0.9% YoY). The 30-year fixed is at 6.97% today, up roughly 8 basis points on the week and 68 basis points higher than a year ago. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.13 points — the weekly average is 2.11 — well above the historical norm of around 1.7 points. That gap is the affordability story most buyers don't understand: if spread compression returns toward 1.8-1.9 points without the 10-year moving, the 30-year would fall to roughly 6.60-6.70% under current conditions. The spread has ticked back up two weeks running after declining, so compression is not happening right now. MBA purchase applications for the week ending September 4 were down 6.9% YoY on the spot read, and the 4-week average is -4.2% YoY — both have now turned negative after a period of improvement. The Xactus MII, which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks (it measures credit pulls for mortgage intent before those buyers actually apply), showed a spot uptick in late June but its 13-week average has fallen for seven consecutive weeks and sits -2.4% YoY. The MII's modest late-June bounce has not shown up in MBA yet — and given that the 13-week trend is still declining, the leading read is cautionary. Over the next four weeks, watch whether that MBA 4-week average stabilizes or continues to erode; if the MII's brief uptick doesn't hold, September application volumes are likely to soften further. On construction: July starts missed badly at 1,239,000 SAAR (-12.8% YoY), but permits beat at 1,433,000 SAAR (+5.2% YoY) and single-family permits reached their highest level since May 2024. The starts-permits divergence — builders are permitted but not breaking ground — is consistent with a sector that is clearing existing pipeline rather than ramping new production, which has implications for 2027 resale supply rather than the current selling season.

The labor market print for August was the macro story of the month and it cuts in two directions for housing. Payrolls came in at +162,000, well above the +59,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate held at 4.1% versus the 4.4% expected — a combination that removes near-term pressure on the Fed to cut aggressively and keeps the 10-year yield firm. The 10-year is at 4.84% today, up 76 basis points year-over-year, and that elevated long-end yield is the primary reason the 30-year hasn't fallen despite any spread movement. The stronger jobs number is a double-edged signal for housing: it supports purchase demand among employed buyers but simultaneously keeps mortgage rates elevated. Beneath the headline, the JOLTS data showed the hires rate fell to 3.2% and the quits rate slipped to 1.9% — both softening from recent levels — which means labor market mobility (the churn that creates move-up buyers) is still contracting. The long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) rose 9% in August to 1.93 million; median unemployment duration jumped to 11.4 weeks, the highest in recent months.
