# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The four weeks ending September 16 present a market that is decelerating more clearly than the headlines suggest. Altos weekly new pending sales turned negative year-over-year in the most recent reading (–3.0% YoY), though the 4-week average holds at +4.4% YoY and the 13-week average remains positive for the 55th consecutive week at +4.8% YoY — that streak is narrowing, and the direction matters as much as the level. Inventory continued rising, with Altos total active supply up roughly 1.2% YoY and Realtor.com active listings up 3.8% YoY for a seventh consecutive month of gains, while new listings on both platforms softened — the Altos 4-week new listings average held only +0.4% YoY after turning negative on a spot basis. Pending days on market at 70.9 days is up 6.7% YoY, a signal that the absorption rate is easing. NAR existing home sales came in at 3.98 million SAAR for August, beating the 3.85 million consensus but still –0.5% YoY — the first negative YoY print after several months of positive comps — and the NSA read of 363,000 closed sales fell 3.5% YoY, which is the honest number to use for direction. New home sales dropped to 607,000 SAAR in July (–5.0% YoY, also the first negative YoY print in that series in some time), and months supply of new homes jumped to 9.6, underscoring that builders are sitting on finished inventory they cannot move at current rates. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.19% today, barely off its recent peak, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.23 percentage points — well above the long-run norm of roughly 1.7 pp. That 50-plus basis point excess spread is the understated affordability story: if credit conditions normalize even partially, the 30-year could fall meaningfully without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications averaged –6.0% YoY on a 4-week basis through September 11, the softest reading since September 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 10 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by 2–4 weeks — did tick up in late June from a declining trend, but its 13-week average was still –2.4% YoY and falling, so the MII uptick has not yet translated into application recovery; watch whether that June bounce carries into August and September MII readings. Builder permits told a more constructive story: total permits rose to 1,433,000 SAAR in July (+5.2% YoY), with single-family permits at their highest since May 2024. But starts collapsed to 1,239,000 SAAR (–12.8% YoY, missing the 1,350,000 consensus by a wide margin), with the South at its lowest since April 2023. The starts-vs.-permits gap confirms builders are authorized to build but are choosing not to break ground — a supply restraint that will matter for 2027 availability, not the current selling season. Watch the next four weeks for whether the MII confirms its June uptick, whether 10-year yields hold below 5%, and whether the weekly pending sales YoY recovers back toward the 4-week average.

The macro backdrop explains why demand is softening without a labor-market crisis to point to. August payrolls came in at +162,000 — well above the +59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%, also beating the 4.4% expected. Those are not recessionary numbers. But the JOLTS data for July showed the hires rate falling back to 3.2% and the quits rate at 1.9%, both down year-over-year: when workers stop voluntarily leaving jobs, household formation and move-up demand slow, because job confidence is what triggers a housing decision. The long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose to 1.93 million in August, up 9.0% from July and with median unemployment duration up 15% YoY — this cohort is not large enough to drive current delinquencies, but it is accumulating in a way that will shape distressed-borrower volume 6–12 months out. On inflation, August CPI came in at +3.35% YoY headline and +2.45% YoY core, both cooler than consensus; shelter CPI is running at +3.0% YoY and decelerating, which is the lagged reflection of the rent softening already visible in Zillow's ZORI (–0.7% month-over-month in August, down 2.1 standard deviations from trend). Core PCE for July was +3.34% YoY — still above the Fed's 2% target, which is why the 10-year Treasury remains near 5% and mortgage rates near 7.2%.
