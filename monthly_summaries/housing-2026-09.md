# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held through the summer but is now decelerating visibly heading into fall. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales is running +4.81% YoY — positive for 55 consecutive weeks — but the 4-week average has slipped to +4.37% and the most recent weekly read turned negative at -2.99% YoY, the first such dip in over a year. That single-week move bears watching but doesn't break the trend yet; the 13-week average is the signal to trust. Inventory stood at roughly 1.07 million active listings in late June, up about 1.2% YoY — growth that has been decelerating rather than accelerating, and new listings flipped negative YoY at -7.1% on a spot basis (the 4-week average is barely positive at +0.4%). Pending days on market are running 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, which confirms that homes are sitting slightly longer than a year ago even as the volume trend remains technically positive. The August NAR existing home sales print came in at 3.98 million SAAR — beating the 3.85 million consensus, but down 0.5% YoY, the first negative YoY read after a stretch of positives. On an NSA basis, August existing sales ran 3.5% below a year ago. New home sales for July were a sharper story: 607,000 SAAR, down 5% YoY, with months supply at 9.6 — a level that tells you builders are sitting on inventory and have less pricing power than the headline starts data implies. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.20% as of September 18, essentially flat on the month but up 83 basis points from a year ago. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.20 percentage points — still roughly 50 basis points wide of the historical norm near 1.7 points, and the 4-week average has been edging up rather than compressing. That spread is the mechanism keeping rates elevated even as the 10-year has drifted slightly lower; if it normalizes toward 1.80-1.90 points, the 30-year comes down meaningfully without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications are the flashing yellow: the 4-week average is running -5.97% YoY, a fresh low since September 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 10 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which leads MBA by two to four weeks — ticked back up in late June after a stretch of declines, with its 4-week average improving. That divergence between a recovering MII and a still-weakening MBA print is the key tension to resolve over the next four weeks: if the MII uptick was durable, purchase apps should firm in early October data. If it fades, the seasonal deceleration will likely be steeper than normal. Single-family permits eased to 878,000 in August — still up 2.3% YoY, but reversing the recent rise — while single-family starts bounced to 918,000 SAAR. The divergence between starts recovering and permits pulling back suggests builders are clearing the pipeline they already authorized rather than committing to new supply.

The macro picture is mixed in its housing implications, but two data points stand out. August payrolls came in at roughly +162,000 — well above the +59,000 consensus — and unemployment held at 4.1% against expectations of 4.4%. A labor market that keeps beating to the upside removes one reason the Fed would cut aggressively, which matters directly for where the 10-year anchors and therefore where mortgage rates land. The counterweight is that the JOLTS hires rate fell to 3.2% in July, down 0.2 points, and the quits rate dropped to 1.9% — both pointing to a labor market where workers feel less confident about mobility. Reduced job-switching is a housing headlock: people don't sell and relocate when they don't believe a better offer is waiting. Separately, the long-term unemployed (27 weeks or more) rose sharply in August to 1.93 million, up nearly 9% in a single month. That cohort doesn't affect this fall's selling season, but it is the seed of future mortgage stress — watch it for a second consecutive month before reading too much into one print. On inflation, August CPI came in at +3.35% YoY with core at +2.45%; shelter CPI at +3.03% YoY continues to decelerate, which will eventually pull core lower and give the Fed more room. Core PCE through July is at +3.34% YoY — still above target and rising for seven consecutive months — which is why the market is not pricing aggressive cuts. The 10-year Treasury sits at 4.96%, up 81 basis points from a year ago, and with the spread still elevated, that translates directly into affordability pressure that the headline rate alone understates.
