# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The most recent Altos weekly data in the snapshot runs through June 26, 2026 — roughly 12 weeks before today's date of September 18. That lag matters for the demand read, so I'll note the most current data available and flag the timing clearly. Here is the September 2026 Monthly Housing Summary.

---

Demand entered the fall season on softer footing than the headline numbers suggest. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales was running at roughly +4.8% year-over-year through late June — a streak that had held positive for 55 consecutive weeks — but the spot weekly read had already dipped to -3.0% YoY, and the 4-week average, while still positive at +4.4%, was decelerating. That softening has since shown up in downstream data: NAR's August existing home sales print came in at 3.98 million SAAR, down 0.5% year-over-year and below the prior month, though it did beat the consensus estimate of 3.85 million. On a non-seasonally-adjusted basis, the August YoY comparison turned negative for the first time in several months, and every region was off year-over-year in the NSA read — worth noting because that is the cleanest apples-to-apples comparison this time of year. Inventory at the national level rose to 1.62 million units with 4.9 months of supply, both up roughly 6% year-over-year, and Realtor.com's active count confirmed the trend at +3.8% YoY through August. New listings, meanwhile, decelerated sharply: the Altos 4-week average turned nearly flat on a YoY basis by late June, and Realtor.com's August new listing count was essentially unchanged year-over-year after months of positive growth, suggesting sellers are pulling back as buyer activity cools — a dynamic that may slow inventory accumulation into October. Pending days on market at 70.9 days are running nearly 7% above year-ago levels, a sign that homes are sitting longer even as list prices hold. The mortgage picture is the central constraint: the 30-year fixed sits at 7.19% as of September 17, up nearly a full point year-over-year, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.24 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. If that spread compresses toward its long-run average, rates could fall 50 basis points without any move from the Fed. MBA purchase applications (4-week average through September 11) are running 6% below year-ago levels and have been deteriorating for ten weeks on the 13-week average; the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — a credit-inquiry-based read that typically leads MBA by two to four weeks — was still negative year-over-year as of late June but had ticked up from its trough, a tentative early signal worth monitoring as September application data comes in. On the supply side, single-family starts recovered to 918,000 SAAR in August, turning positive year-over-year for the first time in months, but total starts missed expectations and completions fell to their lowest YoY level since June 2011 — meaning the near-term delivery pipeline is thinning even as permits remain above a year ago. Watch whether the September MBA 4-week average stabilizes or continues to deteriorate; that will tell you whether the fall selling season has a floor.

The macro picture this month is one of resilient employment data running against a rate environment that has not relented. August payrolls came in at approximately +162,000 — well above the +59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%, also better than expected, which removes near-term pressure on the Fed to cut aggressively. For housing, a strong labor market is a double-edged condition right now: it supports buyer income and reduces default risk, but it also gives the Fed reason to hold rates higher for longer, keeping mortgage costs elevated. The 10-year Treasury ticked back up to 5.00% on September 18 after a brief decline, which reinforces the rate ceiling on affordability. Inflation data offered a modest offset: August headline CPI came in at +3.35% year-over-year, cooler than the +0.9% monthly expectation, and core CPI was +2.45% YoY. Shelter CPI at +3.03% YoY continues to run above core, but it is decelerating — the private-market rent indices (Zillow's ZORI fell 0.7% in August alone) suggest more relief is coming through the pipeline, which will eventually pull shelter CPI lower and give the Fed additional room. Core PCE for July printed at +3.34% YoY — still above target — so the Fed is not yet at the point where the data compels action. The regional price picture from June Case-Shiller (the most recent available, reflecting conditions two to three months ago) shows the two-speed market still firmly in place: Chicago gained +6.9% YoY, New York +4.5%, Cleveland +4.1%, and San Francisco +3.4%, while Dallas fell -0.6%, Phoenix -0.7%, Las Vegas -1.
