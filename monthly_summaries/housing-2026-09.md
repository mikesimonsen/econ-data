# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales held positive for the 55th consecutive week as of late June, running at +4.81% YoY — a durable read on underlying demand that the spot week's -2.99% dip (one noisy week in a holiday-adjacent period) doesn't meaningfully challenge. The 4-week average sat at +4.37% YoY, though it has been decelerating from earlier highs, which is worth tracking. Inventory on the Altos weekly series rose for eight straight weeks through late June, up roughly 1.2% YoY — growth that remains modest and well below what would shift negotiating leverage materially. New listings ticked negative YoY on a spot basis but the 4-week and 13-week averages both held slightly positive, suggesting the pullback was short-lived rather than a trend reversal. Pending days on market at 70.9 days sits 6.7% above year-ago levels, a quiet signal that absorption has softened even as the headline demand streak continues. NAR's August existing home sales print came in at 3.98 million SAAR — down about 0.5% YoY and slightly below July's pace, though it beat the 3.85 million consensus by a meaningful margin; the NSA figure was down 3.5% YoY, which is the directionally honest read for this time of year. New home sales for July were softer, at 607K SAAR and -5% YoY, with months supply jumping to 9.6 — builders are sitting on more finished inventory than the resale market can absorb at current rates, and single-family starts for August actually firmed (+5.6% YoY) while completions collapsed to their lowest YoY reading since June 2011, a divergence that will tighten near-term supply further. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.20% today, essentially unchanged over the past month and up 83 basis points from a year ago. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.20 percentage points — elevated relative to the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points — and it ticked down four basis points today, the first break in a four-week rising streak. That spread is the rate story agents should understand: the 10-year has moved back to 5.00%, and even if it holds here, any spread normalization toward 1.9–2.0 points would bring the 30-year into the mid-to-high 6s without a single Fed cut. MBA purchase applications for the week ending September 11 were down 10.2% YoY, with the 4-week average at -5.97% YoY — the weakest since September 2024. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-inquiry-based leading indicator that tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks) was also negative YoY in late June but showed a 3.5% spot uptick, suggesting some demand re-engagement may have preceded the MBA deterioration we're seeing now. The gap between a firming MII and a worsening MBA suggests the next four weeks of application data warrant close attention — if MII was right, MBA should stabilize; if it was noise, the -10% YoY trend deepens heading into fall.

On the macro side, three data points matter most for housing right now. August payrolls came in at +162K, well above the 59K consensus — a labor market that continues to generate income for potential buyers, even if the hires rate fell to 3.2% in July and the quits rate held at 1.9%, both below year-ago levels and signaling a labor market where workers feel less mobile. Reduced mobility historically correlates with reduced relocation-driven transactions, which is part of why existing sales volume has been soft even where incomes are stable. The more concerning labor development is in the long-duration unemployed: the 27-weeks-plus cohort rose to 1.93 million in August, up 9% month-over-month, with median unemployment duration jumping to 11.4 weeks — a 15% YoY increase. This cohort doesn't threaten the current selling season, but it is the leading population for mortgage stress in 6–12 months; watch it accumulate. On inflation, August core CPI printed at +2.45% YoY and shelter CPI held at +3.03% YoY — shelter is decelerating, which eventually feeds into reduced Fed resistance to cuts, but core PCE through July was still at +3.34% YoY, above the Fed's target by enough that rate cuts this fall remain unlikely. The 10-year Treasury at 5.00% reflects that reality. The regional Case-Shiller data through June shows the two-speed market is intact: Chicago (+6.9% YoY), New York (+4.5%), Cleveland (+4.1%), and Boston (+2.7%) continue to appreciate while Dallas (-0.6%), Phoenix (-0.7%), Las Vegas (-1.8%), Seattle (-1.8%), and Tampa (-1.5%) remain in negative YoY territory — agents in those Sun Belt markets are working a fundamentally different pricing conversation than their Northeast and Midwest counterparts.
