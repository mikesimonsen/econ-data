# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through the summer but is showing the first real cracks heading into fall. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales was up 4.81% year-over-year as of late June, and the 4-week average was running +4.37% YoY — a streak of 55 consecutive weeks of positive YoY pending sales on the 13-week measure. That underlying momentum is real. But the most recent weekly read flipped negative at -2.99% YoY, and the Realtor.com pending count for August came in -0.19% YoY, the first negative print after several positive months — suggesting the seasonal slowdown arrived on schedule and may be biting a little harder than last year. Inventory on the Altos weekly series reached 1.07 million active listings in late June, up just 1.17% YoY, with new listings running slightly below year-ago levels on the spot week (-7.14% YoY) though the 4-week and 13-week averages remained marginally positive; new listing growth has clearly decelerated. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.71% YoY — 103 consecutive weeks of above-year-ago DOM, a quiet signal that the market continues to absorb homes more slowly than in the post-pandemic stretch. NAR existing home sales for August printed at 3.98 million SAAR, down 0.5% YoY — a beat versus the 3.85 million consensus but the first negative YoY reading after a brief positive streak, and the NSA count of 363,000 units was off 3.5% from August 2025. New home sales for July came in at 607,000 SAAR, down 5% YoY with months' supply at 9.6 — builders are carrying meaningful finished inventory even as total starts fell 12.75% YoY in July, badly missing the 1.35 million consensus. Permits told a different story: total permits rose 5.2% YoY to 1.433 million, with single-family permits at their highest since May 2024. The starts-permits divergence means builders authorized future supply while pulling back hard on current production — the pipeline implication is more for 2027-2028 delivery than anything that affects this fall's market. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.17% as of today, up 92 basis points year-over-year and drifting higher over the past week. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.22 percentage points on the daily read, against a historical norm closer to 1.7 points — that 50-basis-point excess spread remains the most underappreciated affordability lever in this market. If credit market volatility settles and the spread compresses toward 1.85-1.90 points, the 30-year falls to roughly 6.75-6.80% without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications for the week ending September 4 were down 4.2% YoY on the 4-week average and down 1.3% on the 13-week average — the 13-week measure has fallen nine consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, showed a modest uptick in late June after declining, but the 13-week MII average was still off 2.38% YoY and falling. When MII and MBA diverge — MII turning before MBA reacts — it gives you advance notice of application momentum shifts; the MII uptick in late June has not yet shown up as a sustained MBA recovery, which is the specific signal to watch over the next four weeks.

The macro backdrop entering fall combines a labor market that is better than feared on the surface with several subsurface readings worth monitoring carefully. August payrolls came in at approximately +162,000, a significant beat of the 59,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate held at 4.1% against a 4.4% expectation — on the headline, that is a healthy employment picture. For housing, a labor market this resilient reduces forced-sale pressure and keeps buyer pools intact. However, the JOLTS data through July told a more cautious story: total hires fell 5.2% in a single month and the hires rate dropped to 3.2%, while quits also fell — a combination that signals workers feel less confident about job-switching and employers are being more selective. Lower quits mean fewer household relocations, which directly suppresses organic housing turnover. The cohort to watch closely is the long-term unemployed (27-plus weeks), which rose nearly 9% in a single month to 1.93 million and median unemployment duration jumped to 11.4 weeks, up 15% year-over-year. This cohort does not affect the current selling season but is the leading indicator for delinquency pressure in 6 to 12 months — worth keeping in your peripheral vision, not your dashboard.
