# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The four weeks ending September 16 tell a story of demand decelerating faster than the headline numbers suggest. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales remains positive year-over-year — up roughly 4.8% and in its 55th consecutive positive week — but the most recent weekly read turned negative at -3.0% YoY, and the 4-week average has decelerated to +4.4% from stronger levels earlier in the quarter. That streak is intact, but it is narrowing. Inventory on the Altos series continued rising, up about 1.2% YoY on total active listings, while new listings growth has stalled: the 4-week average sits at just +0.4% YoY after the spot week turned negative at -7.1%, suggesting sellers are pulling back into the fall rather than accelerating supply. Pending days on market at 70.9 are running 6.7% above last year — the 103rd consecutive week of YoY softening in pace — which matters at your listing appointment when the seller expects a 2022 timeline. NAR's August existing home sales printed at 3.98 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus but still -0.5% YoY, and the NSA read confirms the softening more clearly at -3.5% YoY. The months-supply figure has moved to 4.9, up 6.5% YoY — not a distressed market, but no longer a seller's market in most price bands. New home sales fell harder in July, down 5% YoY to 607K SAAR, with builder months-supply jumping to 9.6; more telling, total housing starts missed badly at 1,239K versus 1,350K expected, down 12.75% YoY, while single-family starts fell 15% YoY. Permits diverge meaningfully — total permits rose to 1,433K, beating expectations, with single-family permits at their highest since May 2024 — which means builders are buying options on future production while mothballing current pipelines. On rates, the 30-year fixed sits at 7.19% today, essentially flat over the past month, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.23 percentage points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That spread has been widening, not compressing; it rose four straight weeks on the weekly average. If anything normalizes that spread toward 1.9-2.0 points, the 30-year falls to the low-to-mid 6s without the Fed doing a thing. That is the rate story most agents are not telling. MBA purchase applications were down 10.2% YoY on the most recent week and -6.0% on the 4-week average — the weakest reading since September 2024 — and the 13-week average has declined for ten consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks, ticked up in late June from a recent trough, a modest early signal, but its 13-week average was still falling. The divergence between an MII uptick and continued MBA deterioration is worth watching: if the MII follow-through holds into late September data, purchase applications may find a floor in October.

The macro backdrop explains why demand has softened without breaking. The August payroll print at +162K vastly exceeded the 59K consensus, and the unemployment rate held at 4.1% against a 4.4% expectation — both outcomes that remove near-term Fed urgency and keep the 10-year Treasury anchored near 5.0%, which is the ceiling on the rate story for buyers right now. The mechanism is direct: a resilient labor market reinforces the Fed's higher-for-longer posture, the 10-year stays elevated, and the 30-year mortgage follows. Headline CPI cooled to +3.35% YoY in August, softer than expected, and core CPI was +2.45%; shelter CPI at +3.03% continues to decelerate slowly, and when it reaches the 2-2.5% range in the index — which the real-time rental data already implies — the Fed's inflation calculus shifts more clearly toward easing. Core PCE through July was still running at 3.34% YoY, above the Fed's 2% target, which is why rate cuts are not imminent. The long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 weeks or more — rose to 1.93 million in August, up nearly 9% in one month and running at the highest median unemployment duration in years (+15% YoY). This cohort does not threaten the current selling season; it accumulates slowly and eventually pressures mortgage performance among recent borrowers who lose income before selling. Watch it monthly. Regionally, the Case-Shiller data through June (reported August 27) confirms the two-speed market: Chicago +6.9% YoY, New York +4.5%, Cleveland +4.1%, Boston +2.7% — Midwest and Northeast appreciation is real and accelerating in several metros. Meanwhile Dallas is -0.6%, Phoenix -0.7%, Las Vegas -1.8%, Denver -1.
