**September 2026 Housing Summary**

New pending sales printed negative year-over-year in the most recent Altos week (down 3.0% YoY for the week ending June 26), breaking what had been a 55-week positive streak when measured by the 13-week average — though that 13-week average itself remains up 4.8% YoY and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks, which is the more durable signal. The 4-week average held at +4.4% YoY, so the single-week dip reads more like seasonal noise than a trend break. Inventory stood near 1.075 million active listings, up about 1.2% YoY, with new listings running slightly negative on the week but the 4-week and 13-week averages still modestly positive — growth is clearly decelerating relative to the year-earlier pace. Pending days on market ticked up to 70.9 days, +6.7% YoY, a slow-burn signal that buyers are taking more time, not less. NAR's July existing home sales came in at a 4.06 million SAAR — above the 3.85 million consensus and up 1.25% YoY — but the headline masks a two-month pullback from the spring peak, and NAR inventory flipped negative YoY for the first time in several months, landing at 1.54 million units and 4.6 months of supply. New home sales diverged sharply: July printed 607,000 SAAR, down 10.5% month-over-month and 5.0% YoY, with months of supply on new homes rising to 9.6 — a level that historically pressures builder pricing. Median new home price fell to $393,800, down 0.9% YoY, while existing home median held at $434,100, up 2.0% YoY. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.88% as of today, down modestly from its recent peak, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.11-2.12 points — still roughly 40 basis points above the historical norm of ~1.7 points. That spread compression story remains the most underappreciated affordability lever: if it reverts even partially toward 1.85-1.90 points as credit-market volatility settles, the 30-year could fall to the 6.4-6.5% range without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications recovered slightly — the 4-week average is up 0.6% week-over-week but still down 2.8% YoY, and the 13-week average has been falling for eight consecutive weeks and just turned negative YoY. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-inquiry-based read that leads MBA applications by 2-4 weeks) was down 5.6% YoY on the most recent week with a 13-week average down 2.4% YoY — diverging meaningfully from what MBA showed in the spring and signaling that the application bounce may be tentative. On the supply side, the July starts miss (1,239,000 SAAR vs. 1,350,000 expected, down 12.8% YoY) contrasted with a permits beat (1,433,000, up 5.2% YoY), with single-family permits at their highest since May 2024 — builders are authorized to build but pulling back on breaking ground. That starts-permits gap implies the pipeline of future supply is intact but builders are watching demand signals before committing capital. Watch the September MBA and MII reads: if the MII doesn't recover alongside rates, the application bounce will fade before it shows up in pending sales.

The macro backdrop explains why demand has been hard to sustain at current rates. July payrolls missed badly — actual job gains came in at roughly -23,000 against a +59,000 consensus, and hourly earnings growth of +0.1% came in well below the +0.3% expected. Weaker income growth directly constrains purchasing power at the margin, particularly for first-time buyers who have the thinnest cushion between rate sensitivity and qualifying income. The unemployment rate did beat (4.1% vs. 4.4% expected), but the JOLTS data released this week complicates that positive read: the hires rate fell to 3.2% and the quits rate to 1.9%, both declining — a softening labor market typically produces fewer voluntary moves, and voluntary movers are a disproportionate share of existing home buyers. Long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) ticked down to 1.77 million in July, but that cohort has been accumulating over the past year and remains elevated; it doesn't threaten the current selling season, but it is where future mortgage stress will originate if the labor market continues softening. On inflation, Core PCE ran at +3.34% YoY through July — above the Fed's 2% target and still rising sequentially — while Core CPI came in at +2.47% YoY, cooler than expected.
