# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales held at +4.81% year-over-year through the week of June 26 — positive for 55 consecutive weeks — but the most recent weekly read slipped to -2.99% YoY, and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37%, flagging that the streak has softened at the edges even if the underlying trend remains constructive. Inventory on the Altos platform continued rising, up 8 consecutive weeks and +1.17% YoY, while the Realtor.com active count reached 1.14 million, +3.76% YoY after 34 months of positive YoY growth. New listings, however, have stalled: the Altos 4-week average was barely positive YoY (+0.41%) and turned negative on a spot basis (-7.14% YoY the week of June 26), suggesting sellers pulled back through the early summer. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.71% YoY — 103 consecutive weeks above the prior-year level — which tells you the average contract is taking longer to close even as some fast-moving markets remain tight. NAR's August existing home sales printed at 3.98 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus but still -0.50% YoY on a seasonally adjusted basis; on a NSA basis the YoY read was -3.46%, and both Midwest and Northeast regions turned YoY negative in August for the first time in months. New home sales (July, 607K SAAR) fell -10.5% on the month and went YoY negative at -5.0%, while new home supply jumped to 9.6 months — a combination that suggests builders overbuilt into softening demand, which is already showing up in July starts collapsing -12.4% to 1,239K SAAR, well below the 1,350K consensus, with single-family starts -15% YoY. Permits told a different story: total permits rose to 1,433K, beating expectations, and single-family permits reached their highest level since May 2024 — the starts-vs.-permits gap implies builders are authorizing future work while clearing a bloated current pipeline, not adding to near-term supply. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.17% today, up 92 basis points year-over-year and ticking higher for four consecutive days, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.22 points — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. The weekly average spread has been rising for three consecutive weeks. That elevated spread remains the housing market's hidden affordability lever: if credit conditions normalize even partially, the 30-year could fall meaningfully without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications (4-week average through September 4) were -4.20% YoY and just reversed lower after several weeks of improvement — a caution flag. The Xactus MII, which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks, showed a spot uptick in late June (+3.53% week-over-week) but remained -5.59% YoY, and the 13-week average has been falling for 7 consecutive weeks. The divergence between a constructive MII spot reversal and a deteriorating 13-week trend is worth watching: if the MII 4-week average (currently -2.30% YoY) does not continue firming into September, the MBA will likely remain under pressure through early October. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the MII sustains its late-June uptick and whether the spread begins compressing as Treasury volatility settles post-CPI.

August's macro data was more reassuring than the headlines suggested, and the mechanisms matter for housing. Payrolls came in at +162K versus a 59K consensus — a large positive surprise — and the unemployment rate held at 4.1% against a 4.4% forecast, which removes one near-term source of forced-seller risk. Wage growth of +3.09% YoY provides some offset to affordability pressure, though not enough to move the needle materially at 7.17% mortgage rates. The more concerning signal is inside the labor data: the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) jumped 8.98% in a single month to 1.93 million, and median unemployment duration rose to 11.4 weeks, up 15% YoY. This cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is the precursor population for mortgage delinquency in 6-12 months — worth tracking. On inflation, August CPI came in well below expectations (headline +0.40% vs. a 0.90% consensus, core +0.29%), and shelter CPI held at +3.03% YoY, continuing its slow deceleration that will eventually feed into lower owner-equivalent rent components. Core PCE for July was +3.34% YoY — still above the Fed's 2% target — so rate cuts are not imminent, but the CPI print keeps the door open for Treasury yields to stabilize or ease from the current 4.95%.
