**September 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through the summer but is showing early signs of softening. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks, running at roughly +4.8% YoY as of late June — a durable streak. The 4-week average was also positive at +4.4% YoY, though decelerating. The single-week reading slipped to -3.0% YoY in the final week of June, so watch whether that single-week dip was noise or the leading edge of a trend change. Inventory grew modestly — total active listings up about +1.2% YoY in the Altos weekly read, with new listings decelerating: the 4-week average was barely positive at +0.4% YoY after the single-week print turned negative at -7.1% YoY. The 13-week new listings average is still rising but the growth rate is stalling, which means supply is not accelerating into fall. Pending days on market ticked up to 70.9 days, +6.7% YoY — homes are taking longer to go under contract than they were a year ago, even as volume stays positive. NAR July existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus by a wide margin, though the print still sits only +1.25% above July 2025 and reversed slightly from June. New home sales diverged sharply in the other direction: 607,000 SAAR in July, down 10.5% from June and now -5.0% YoY, with months supply jumping to 9.6 — builders are accumulating unsold inventory at a rate that is starting to matter. Speaking of builders: starts fell to 1,239,000 SAAR in July, -12.7% YoY, missing the 1,350,000 consensus badly, while single-family starts dropped -15.0% YoY. That is the supply contraction story for 2027-2028. Against that, permits rose to 1,433,000 SAAR in July, beating expectations and turning positive YoY for the first time in several months — the starts/permits gap implies builders are completing existing pipeline but not yet committing new foundation-pours at the same rate. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.89% as of September 8, up 61 basis points YoY. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.10 points — still about 40 basis points above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. Incremental compression from here could move the effective rate meaningfully without any Fed action. The leading indicators tell a cautionary story: the MBA purchase index 4-week average is running -4.2% YoY as of the September 4 week, and the 13-week average has fallen for nine consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks — was also negative YoY at -5.6% on the single-week read in late June, though its 4-week average had ticked back up. That MII uptick did not translate into a sustained MBA recovery, which is the divergence to watch through September. The four weeks ahead: whether the single-week pending sales dip becomes a trend, and whether MBA applications stabilize or continue their slow bleed as the 10-year Treasury, now at 4.84%, keeps pressure on rate-sensitive buyers.

The macro picture is genuinely mixed in the way that matters for housing. August payrolls came in at +162,000 — well above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.1% versus the 4.4% expected, which removes one of the cleaner rate-cut arguments the Fed had been building toward. Strong employment data directly supports mortgage qualification and household formation; it also keeps the Fed on hold longer than the bond market had priced, which is why the 10-year Treasury firmed to 4.84% following the report. On inflation, core PCE rose to +3.34% YoY in July — still running above the Fed's 2% target — and core CPI came in at +2.47% YoY with a +0.22% monthly gain. Shelter CPI continues to slow, up just +3.16% YoY, which is the one inflationary tailwind for the Fed since lagged rent measures are finally decelerating into the index. Core PCE at 3.34%, however, gives the FOMC little cover to cut without a clear labor deterioration. Here is the housing-specific tension: the labor market headline looks solid, but the internals are softening in ways that accumulate slowly. The hires rate fell to 3.2% and the quits rate dropped to 1.9% in July — both indicators of a labor market where workers feel less confident about changing jobs, which historically precedes reduced household formation risk-taking. More concerning: the count of workers unemployed 27 weeks or longer jumped 9% in a single month to 1.93 million, and median unemployment duration rose to 11.4 weeks, +15% YoY.
