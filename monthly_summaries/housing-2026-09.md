**September 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but is decelerating. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sat at +4.8% YoY through late June, a streak that has now run 55 consecutive weeks, but the 4-week average had cooled to +4.4% and the spot week printed -3.0% YoY — the first negative spot reading since the streak began. That single-week dip is likely seasonal noise, but the direction of travel in the 4-week average bears watching as we move into fall. Inventory continued to build: total active supply was up roughly 1.2% YoY through Altos, Realtor.com active listings were up 2.1% YoY in July, and Zillow's for-sale count rose 1.2% YoY — a consistent picture of modest supply growth, though the pace is decelerating from earlier in the year. New listings were softening on the same basis: the Altos 4-week new-listings average was barely positive YoY (+0.4%) after turning negative on a spot basis (-7.1%), and Realtor.com new listings hit their lowest since October 2023. Pending days on market ticked up 6.7% YoY — not alarming, but a consistent signal that homes are taking a little longer to clear. NAR existing sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR in July — up 1.3% YoY and roughly 210,000 ahead of consensus, a meaningful beat — though the SAAR slipped slightly from June. NSA YoY tells the same story: +2.8%. New home sales, by contrast, fell to 607K SAAR in July, down 5% YoY and 10.5% from June, with months of supply at 9.6 — elevated enough that builders have real incentive to cut prices, which the median new-home price (down 0.9% YoY) confirms. The starts/permits divergence is the key builder signal: total starts fell 12.4% in July and missed estimates by 111K, with single-family starts down 15% YoY, while total permits rose 4.3% and beat consensus, with single-family permits at their highest since May 2024. Builders are pulling back on what they break ground on now while still filing permits for future projects — the pipeline is being managed down, not abandoned. The 30-year fixed rate was 6.88% as of September 3, essentially flat on the month (-3bp), with the 10-year Treasury at 4.76%. The 30-10 spread sits at 2.11-2.12pp against a historical norm of roughly 1.7pp — still elevated by 40bp. If that spread compresses toward its norm as the Fed's balance sheet policy normalizes, the 30-year could move meaningfully lower without any Fed rate action. MBA purchase applications were -2.8% YoY on the 4-week average through August 28, and the 13-week average has now been falling for eight weeks and turned negative YoY — a cleaner signal of softening demand than the spot volatility. More telling: the Xactus MII (a credit-pull-based measure of purchase intent that leads MBA by 2-4 weeks) was -5.6% YoY on its spot reading and -2.3% YoY on the 4-week average through late June, both worse than the contemporaneous MBA read. That divergence between MII and MBA suggests the purchase-application weakness is not yet fully priced into the pending-sales data — watch whether the Altos 4-week pending average continues to decelerate into October.

The macro backdrop is genuinely mixed in ways that matter for housing. The July payroll print was the sharpest miss of the year: actual job gains came in roughly 82,000 below the 59K consensus addition — the data shows total nonfarm at 158,858K, implying a net change well below expectations — while the unemployment rate beat at 4.1% versus the 4.4% consensus, dropping 10bp on the month. Those two readings tell a different story about the labor market: the headline claims suggest weakening hiring, but the rate suggests less displacement than feared. The JOLTS data reinforced the hiring softness — nonfarm hires fell 5.2% in July and the hires rate dropped 20bp to 3.2%, while quits fell to 3.056 million (quits rate 1.9%). A lower quits rate means workers are less confident about finding the next job, which historically translates into fewer voluntary moves and, with a lag, fewer discretionary home purchases. The long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) actually fell 8.6% in July to 1.771 million — a small positive — but the cohort remains elevated YoY and the median unemployment duration of 10.5 weeks is still running 3% above a year ago. This group is not a 2026 selling-season story; it becomes a delinquency story in mid-2027 if the softness in hiring persists. On inflation: Core PCE ran at 3.34% YoY in July, rising for seven consecutive months
