**September 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive but is losing momentum heading into fall. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has been in positive YoY territory for 55 consecutive weeks, a streak that tells you the underlying market is still functioning — but the spot reading for the week of June 26 turned negative YoY (-3.0%), and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.4% from higher levels earlier in the summer. That divergence between the 13-week trend and the near-term spot data is the signal worth watching: the structural bid is intact, but buyers pulled back through late June, likely in response to rates that have drifted back toward 6.9%. On the supply side, Altos inventory rose for eight consecutive weeks through late June, up about 1.2% YoY — modest growth, not a flood — while new listings' 4-week average was barely positive YoY (+0.4%) and the spot week turned negative (-7.1%), suggesting the new-listing pipeline is tightening again even as overall inventory remains slightly elevated. NAR's July existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR, +1.3% YoY and well above the 3.85 million consensus — a clean beat — though the month-over-month dip of 0.7% (SA) and inventory falling back below year-ago levels for the first time in several months are worth noting. New home sales told a different story: 607K SAAR in July, down 10.5% on the month and 5.0% YoY, with months supply jumping to 9.6 — builders are sitting on finished inventory while starts fell 12.4% in July to 1,239K SAAR, missing expectations by roughly 110K units. Permits, however, rose 4.3% to 1,433K and beat consensus, with single-family permits at their highest since May 2024 — meaning builders are pausing activity now but signaling intent to restart. The starts-to-permits gap is the supply story for 2027, not this selling season. The 30-year fixed closed September 2 at 6.91%, up 38 basis points YoY, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury widened slightly to 2.12 points after compressing earlier in the summer — still roughly 40 basis points above the historical norm of around 1.7 points, which means a meaningful rate improvement is available without any Fed action if lenders and the secondary market normalize. MBA purchase applications' 4-week average was -2.8% YoY through August 28, technically negative, but the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tracks borrower credit-pull activity 2-4 weeks ahead of MBA — turned up in late June after seven weeks of decline, suggesting the August softness in applications may be close to a floor. Watch whether the Xactus 13-week average (still -2.4% YoY and falling) confirms that reversal or not — that's the earliest read on whether September contract activity firms.

The macro backdrop is genuinely mixed in ways that matter differently for housing. July payrolls missed by roughly 80,000 jobs (actual -23K vs. +59K consensus), average hourly earnings came in at +0.1% versus the +0.3% expected, and yet the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% — 30 basis points below consensus — which means the headline miss reflects composition and timing, not a labor market in distress. The July JOLTS data, released this week, showed hires falling 5.2% on the month and the hires rate dropping to 3.2%, while the quits rate ticked down to 1.9% — both readings that suggest workers are less confident about voluntary job-changing, which historically precedes softer household formation and reduced move-up activity within 6-9 months. Long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) fell in July to 1,771K and is down 2.8% YoY — the slight improvement is worth acknowledging, but the cohort remains large enough to seed delinquency risk into 2027 if it doesn't continue declining. On inflation: Core PCE ran at +3.34% YoY in July, still above the Fed's target and slightly above core CPI (+2.47%), which limits the Fed's room to cut meaningfully.
