**September 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive on a 13-week basis through late June but showed clear deceleration heading into the back half of summer. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales stood at roughly 89,700 contracts, up 4.8% year-over-year and positive for 55 consecutive weeks — a durable streak, though the trend is thinning. The 4-week average came in at +4.4% YoY, and the single-week read for the week of June 26 turned negative at -3.0% YoY, the first negative weekly print in that streak. Inventory continued building slowly, with Altos total active supply up about 1.2% YoY across eight consecutive weeks of gains, but new listings decelerated sharply — the 4-week average flipped to just +0.4% YoY after running hotter earlier in the year, and the single-week read fell 7.1% YoY. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, a quiet signal that absorption is softening at the margin. NAR's July existing home sales printed at 4.06 million SAAR — a beat relative to the 3.85 million consensus, but still down 0.7% from June and only +1.25% YoY. The NSA read confirms the picture: +2.8% YoY, positive for six months. New home sales told a different story, falling to 607,000 SAAR in July, down 10.5% on the month and now -5.0% YoY — the divergence with existing sales likely reflects builders pulling back pricing and still working through an elevated 9.6-month supply overhang. The 30-year fixed sat at 6.89% as of September 1, up 36 basis points year-over-year, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.10 points — still roughly 40 basis points above the long-run norm of about 1.70. That gap matters: if spreads compress back toward historical levels without any move in the 10-year, the 30-year could fall to the low 6.40s on spread alone. Instead, the 10-year moved the other direction, reaching 4.79% and keeping rates sticky. The MBA purchase index 4-week average was -3.3% YoY through August 21 and has been falling for seven weeks on the 13-week basis. More telling: the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which measures credit-pull activity 2-4 weeks ahead of MBA — showed its 13-week average down 2.4% YoY and falling for seven consecutive weeks through late June, suggesting the MBA softness was not a surprise and has likely persisted into August. Builder permits offered the one constructive signal: total permits rose 5.2% YoY in July, beating expectations, with single-family permits at their highest since May 2024. Starts, however, collapsed — down 12.7% YoY and well below the 1,350,000 consensus — confirming builders are permitting future supply while completing the existing pipeline, not ramping new production. Watch whether the MII 4-week average reverses through September; if it firms, purchase applications should follow in early October.

The macro backdrop for housing has two competing forces pulling in opposite directions. July payrolls missed badly — the economy added roughly 23,000 jobs against a 59,000 consensus — and the hires rate fell 20 basis points to 3.2%, with quits slipping as well. A softening labor market directly compresses the first-time buyer pool and erodes the confidence that drives discretionary move-up activity. The one bright spot: the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, beating the 4.4% consensus, and long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) declined to 1.77 million, reversing a recent climb. That cohort remains the slow-moving delinquency risk to watch — it does not threaten this selling season, but if it reaccumulates through fall it will shape distressed inventory volumes in 2027. On inflation, Core PCE held at 3.34% YoY in July, running above the Fed's 2% target for a seventh consecutive month, while core CPI printed cooler than expected at +0.22% month-over-month.
