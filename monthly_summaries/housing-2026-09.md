# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The four weeks ending September 8 present a market that is holding together at the headline level while the underlying current quietly shifts cooler. Altos weekly new pending sales came in at 85,813 for the week of June 26, down 3.0% year-over-year on the spot read, snapping what had been a 55-week positive streak on the 13-week average — though that longer-run average is still running +4.8% YoY and its streak remains intact, providing important context against the single-week softness. The 4-week average sits at 89,198, up 4.4% YoY, so the demand picture is decelerating rather than reversing. Inventory at 1.07 million units is up 1.2% YoY and has risen for eight consecutive weeks, but new listings are telling a more complicated story: the 4-week average is barely positive at +0.4% YoY while the spot week printed -7.1% YoY, suggesting the seller pipeline is narrowing even as total supply builds from prior accumulation. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — a signal of modest friction, not gridlock. NAR's July existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus by a meaningful margin but still down slightly from June; the NSA YoY of +2.8% confirms the headline is genuine, not a seasonal artifact. New home sales diverged sharply, falling to 607,000 SAAR in July, off 10.5% from June and down 5.0% YoY — while new home months supply jumped to 9.6, the highest spread over existing-home supply in recent memory. Builders are sitting on inventory they cannot move at current prices, which is why the median new home price is down 0.9% YoY even as the average ticked up, a mix-shift effect worth noting at listing appointments. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.89% as of September 4, essentially unchanged on the month, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.13 percentage points — still roughly 40-45 basis points above the long-run norm of ~1.70pp. That compression story remains the most underappreciated affordability lever: if the spread normalized even halfway, the 30-year would be in the low-to-mid 6s without any Fed movement. The MBA 4-week purchase index is down 2.8% YoY, and the 13-week average has now fallen for eight consecutive weeks and just turned negative YoY — a meaningful deterioration from the positive reads of spring. The Xactus MII, which leads MBA by 2-4 weeks, turned up on the 4-week average in late June (up 2.3%) while still negative YoY at -2.3%; watch whether that uptick in the MII translates into a stabilization of MBA purchase applications through late September, or whether it fades. Building permits beat at 1,433,000 SAAR while starts badly missed at 1,239,000 — the divergence means builders are authorizing future supply while pulling back sharply on breaking ground today, a pattern that constrains 2027 inventory without doing much for 2026 pipeline.

The macro backdrop is genuinely mixed in a way that matters specifically to housing. August payrolls came in at 162,000 against a 59,000 consensus — a large beat that pushed the unemployment rate to 4.1%, better than the 4.4% expected. The surface-level read is reassuring for housing demand: employed buyers are qualified buyers. But the mechanism worth watching more carefully is beneath the headline. The JOLTS hires rate fell to 3.2% in July, down 0.2 points on the month, and the quits rate is at 1.9%, both indicating a labor market that is becoming more static — workers are staying put because they cannot find better options, not because conditions are ideal. A labor market that is not generating lateral moves is a labor market that is not generating life-event relocations, and that is a direct headwind to transaction volume. More pointed: the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose 9.0% in a single month to 1.93 million, and median unemployment duration jumped to 11.4 weeks, up 15% YoY. This cohort does not threaten the current selling season, but its accumulation over the coming quarters will shape distressed-inventory volume in 2027 — flag it now. On inflation, Core PCE printed 3.34% YoY in July, running above the Fed's 2% target, while core CPI came in cooler than expected at +0.22% month-over-month. Shelter CPI remains at +3.16% YoY, continuing its gradual deceleration; when shelter inflation fully catches down to real-time rent data (Zillow ZORI at +3.0% YoY, itself cooling), the Fed will have one less argument for holding rates elevated, and that is when spread compression becomes most plausible.
