# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales posted a negative week in late June (-3.0% YoY for the spot reading), but the data underneath that number is less alarming than the headline. The 4-week average held at +4.4% YoY and the 13-week average sat at +4.8% YoY — the latter positive for 55 consecutive weeks. The single soft week looks like seasonal noise against a trend that remains constructively positive. Inventory rose for an eighth straight week to roughly 1.07 million active listings, up about 1.2% YoY, and new listings' 13-week average continued climbing (+0.7% YoY, a 14-week streak). What's worth noting is that new listings' spot reading dipped 7.1% YoY in the final week of June — a potential early signal that seller participation may be softening into summer rather than accelerating. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9, up 6.7% YoY and positive for 103 consecutive weeks, meaning homes are taking measurably longer to go under contract than a year ago. NAR existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR for July — above the 3.85 million consensus, and up 1.3% YoY — though the two-month downtick and an NAR inventory reading that flipped YoY negative (1.54M units, -0.65% YoY) suggest the beat was partly a function of lean supply rather than robust demand. New home sales diverged sharply: 607,000 SAAR in July, down 10.5% from June and 5.0% YoY, with months supply jumping to 9.6 — elevated enough to weigh on builder pricing. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.97% as of today, up about 8 basis points over the past week, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury widened slightly to 2.13 percentage points. The historical norm is around 1.7 points, so there remains roughly 40 basis points of potential compression if credit and volatility conditions normalize — that alone could move the conforming rate below 6.6% without any Fed action. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is down 4.2% YoY and has ticked negative for the first time in recent weeks; the 13-week average has fallen nine straight weeks. The Xactus MII — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks by capturing mortgage-related credit inquiry activity earlier in the funnel — showed a spot uptick in late June after seven weeks of decline, with the 4-week average also reversing higher. That divergence from MBA is worth watching: if the MII improvement is durable, purchase application volumes may stabilize in late September. Permits beat expectations at 1,433,000 SAAR in July (+5.2% YoY), with single-family permits at their highest since May 2024. Starts, however, missed badly at 1,239,000 SAAR (-12.8% YoY), particularly in the South (-23.6% YoY). Builders are permitted ahead but pulling back on ground-breaking — a posture that clears near-term pipeline without committing to new supply. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the Xactus MII uptick translates into stabilizing MBA volumes and whether the 10-year yield holds below 4.90%, which would determine whether the 30-year drifts back toward 6.85% or presses toward 7.10%.

The macro picture this month delivered a genuine surprise on payrolls — 162,000 net new jobs against a consensus of 59,000, with unemployment holding at 4.1% versus an expected 4.4%. That strength matters for housing in a direct way: job security is the precondition for a purchase decision, and a labor market that continues to produce more jobs than expected keeps potential buyers in the pool rather than on the sidelines. The complication is that wages grew 3.1% YoY, Core PCE printed 3.34% YoY in July (rising for seven consecutive months), and the 10-year Treasury yield sits at 4.84% — up 76 basis points YoY. That combination — solid employment, sticky inflation above the Fed's target, and a rising long end — explains why mortgage rates climbed back toward 7% this week even as the housing data softened. The spread story remains the key variable: if Core PCE begins to decelerate in the August print (due in late September), the 10-year may stabilize and the spread compression thesis gets more room to work. Meanwhile, the long-term unemployed cohort bears watching: those unemployed 27 weeks or more rose to 1.93 million in August, up 9.0% in a single month after a recent decline. Median unemployment duration jumped to 11.4 weeks, up 15.2% YoY. Neither number is at a level that signals systemic mortgage stress yet, but the direction matters — this is how future distressed supply accumulates, slowly and then less slowly.
