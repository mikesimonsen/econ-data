# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The four weeks ending September 16 tell a story of softening demand meeting rising rates — neither catastrophic, but the direction matters. On the demand side, the Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at +4.81% YoY and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks, which is the underlying current worth anchoring to. But the spot week printed -2.99% YoY and the 4-week average has decelerated to +4.37%, down from stronger readings earlier in the summer — momentum is fading even if the streak holds. Inventory at roughly 1.07 million active listings is up about 1.2% YoY, modest growth by any measure, while new listings on a 4-week average basis are essentially flat YoY (+0.41%); the new-listings pipeline is not building meaningfully. Pending days on market held at 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are taking longer to go under contract than a year ago. NAR existing home sales for August came in at 3.98 million SAAR, which beat the 3.85 million consensus but still fell 0.5% YoY, ending a brief stretch of positive YoY readings. On an NSA basis — the apples-to-apples comparison — August sales were 363,000, down 3.5% from a year ago, with every region negative. New home sales for July were a more pointed 607,000 SAAR, down 5% YoY, with months' supply at 9.6 — builders have inventory to move and are sitting on it. Against all of this, the 30-year fixed rate is at 7.22% as of September 15, up nearly a full point YoY and rising for five consecutive days. The 30Y-10Y spread is 2.21 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points — meaning rates could fall 50 basis points without the 10-year moving at all if lender risk appetite normalizes; that compression is the affordability lever agents should understand before their next listing conversation. MBA purchase applications (4-week average) are down 6.0% YoY, the weakest reading since September 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for ten consecutive weeks. The Xactus MII — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks — was still in recovery mode as of late June at -2.3% YoY on its 4-week average, but it has not confirmed a turn higher since, and the gap between MII and MBA has narrowed from divergence to alignment on the downside. Watch whether the MII holds its late-June uptick through October; if it rolls back over, MBA applications will follow into November.

The macro picture explains why demand is softening without requiring a recession narrative. August payrolls came in at +162,000 — well above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%, also beating the 4.4% expectation. On the surface that reads as labor-market strength. But the JOLTS data through July tells a more nuanced story: the hires rate fell to 3.2% and the quits rate to 1.9%, both declining, which means workers are less mobile and employers are being more selective. Lower quit rates historically translate into fewer discretionary household moves — people don't buy a house in a new city if they're not confident enough to leave their current job. More consequentially for the medium term, long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) ticked up to 1.93 million in August, +9% in a single month, and the median duration of unemployment rose to 11.4 weeks — up 15% YoY. These workers are not buying homes this fall, and if the cohort continues to grow, it will shape delinquency volume twelve months from now. On inflation: headline CPI for August came in at +3.35% YoY, cooler than the 0.9% monthly consensus, while core CPI was +2.45% YoY; shelter CPI is at +3.03% and still rising, which means the Fed's target remains out of reach on its own timeline. Core PCE through July was 3.34% — above the 2% target by a wide margin — and that keeps the 10-year at 4.96% today, which is the ceiling holding mortgage rates elevated. The regional Case-Shiller data through June (released August 27) reinforces what agents in different markets are already feeling: Chicago is up 6.9% YoY, New York up 4.5%, Boston up 2.7%, Cleveland up 4.1% — Midwest and Northeast price appreciation is running at two to four times the national rate. Meanwhile Dallas is -0.6%, Phoenix -0.7%, Las Vegas -1.8%, Seattle -1.8%, and Denver -1.2% — Sun Belt markets that overcorrected on inventory are still working through that overhang at the price level.
