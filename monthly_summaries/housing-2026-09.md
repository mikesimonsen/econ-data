# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive for the four weeks ending September 21, though the picture is more nuanced than the headline suggests. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sits at a +4.81% YoY gain and has been positive for 55 consecutive weeks — a streak worth noting — but the most recent weekly read slipped to -2.99% YoY, and the 4-week average has decelerated to +4.37% from stronger readings earlier in the summer. That deceleration is the story, not the negative week in isolation. Inventory continued its gradual climb: active listings are up roughly 1-6% YoY depending on the source (Altos, Realtor.com, and Zillow all confirm growth, Redfin's SA series shows a sharper pullback), and new listings growth has effectively stalled — the Altos 4-week average turned barely positive at +0.41% YoY after running ahead of prior year for much of the spring. Pending days on market at 70.9 days are up 6.7% YoY, a sign that buyers are moving more deliberately. NAR's August existing home sales printed at 3.98 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus but still down 0.5% YoY — and the NSA read, which you can use apples-to-apples, came in -3.5% YoY. That's the first YoY decline after a stretch of positive comparisons. New home sales were weaker, dropping to 607,000 SAAR in July, down 5% YoY, with months supply jumping to 9.6 — builders are sitting on product. The 30-year fixed is at 7.19% today, up 84 basis points from a year ago, essentially flat on the month. The spread between the 30-year and the 10-year Treasury is 2.23 points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. That gap matters: if credit and rate-risk premiums compress even modestly toward 2.0 points, rates fall meaningfully without any Fed action. The leading indicators are flashing caution. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is -5.97% YoY and has been sliding for ten weeks on the 13-week basis. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (which tends to lead MBA by 2-4 weeks) ticked up on its most recent 4-week read but remains -2.3% YoY, and the 13-week average has fallen for seven consecutive weeks. The MII uptick is a signal to watch — if it firms over the next two weeks, MBA applications should follow in early October. Single-family permits pulled back slightly in August but are still +2.3% YoY and at their highest since May 2024; single-family starts reversed higher to +5.6% YoY. The divergence to watch: multifamily starts are collapsing (-16% YoY on 5+ units) while single-family is expanding, and completions across both categories fell to their lowest level since June 2011. Less new supply entering the market over the next 6-12 months may provide a floor under prices even if demand stays soft.

The macro environment is genuinely mixed in ways that matter for housing. August payrolls came in at +162,000, roughly three times the consensus expectation of +59,000, and the unemployment rate held at 4.1% against a 4.4% forecast — both readings that reduce the probability of an imminent Fed cut and help explain why the 10-year has held near 4.96%. But look past the headline: the JOLTS hires rate fell to 3.2% in July and the quits rate dropped to 1.9%, both of which suggest the labor market is cooling underneath a still-acceptable unemployment number. Workers are less willing to change jobs, which correlates with softening household formation and reduced move-up buying. More pointedly, the count of workers unemployed 27 weeks or longer rose to 1.93 million in August, up 9% on the month, and median unemployment duration jumped to 11.4 weeks — up 15% YoY. This cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is the leading indicator for mortgage stress in 2027; watch it accumulate. On inflation, August core CPI came in at +2.45% YoY, cooler than expected, and shelter CPI held at +3.03% — still elevated but decelerating. Core PCE through July is running at +3.34%, above the Fed's 2% target, which keeps the Fed on hold and the 10-year anchored near 5%. The Case-Shiller data (June, released August 27) confirms the regional two-speed market: Chicago is up 6.9% YoY and New York up 4.5%, both with momentum, while Dallas (-0.6%), Phoenix (-0.7%), Seattle (-1.8%), and Las Vegas (-1.8%) are still in negative YoY territory. The Atlanta index just turned positive for the first time in over a year. If you are in a Midwest or Northeast market, your pricing conversation is fundamentally different from a colleague in Sun Belt metros where correction is ongoing
