# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

New pending sales have been positive year-over-year for 55 consecutive weeks on the Altos 13-week average, which remains the cleanest read on underlying demand — but that streak is showing some wear at the edges. The spot week ending June 26 printed -3.0% YoY, and the 4-week average has decelerated to +4.4% YoY from stronger readings earlier in the summer. The 13-week average held at +4.8% YoY and is still rising, so this is not yet a trend break, but it bears watching. Active inventory on Altos reached 1.07 million, up about 1.2% YoY — growth that had been accelerating through the spring has now stalled near that level. New listings told a similar story: the 4-week average is only +0.4% YoY, and the spot week turned -7.1% YoY, a notable softening from the positive readings earlier in the year. Pending days on market held at 71 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are taking modestly longer to go under contract than a year ago, which is consistent with a market that has more supply relative to demand than 2025. The July NAR existing home sales print came in at 4.06 million SAAR, a beat against the 3.85 million consensus, and +1.25% YoY — encouraging, though the underlying NSA count was also up 2.8% YoY, which confirms the direction. New home sales diverged sharply: July came in at 607,000 SAAR, -10.5% for the month and -5.0% YoY, with months supply jumping to 9.6 — builders are carrying elevated unsold inventory and cutting median prices accordingly (median new home price -0.9% YoY). On rates, the 30-year fixed sits at 6.97% as of September 9, up 8 basis points over the past month and 68 basis points above a year ago. The 10-year Treasury is at 4.92%, which puts the mortgage-Treasury spread — the gap between the 30-year fixed and the 10-year, historically around 1.7 percentage points — at 2.13 points, still roughly 40-45 basis points above normal. That excess spread means mortgage rates can fall meaningfully if lenders and MBS investors grow more comfortable with prepayment risk, even without any move from the Fed. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is -4.2% YoY as of the September 4 week, and the 13-week average has now fallen nine consecutive weeks to -1.3% YoY — the trend in purchase applications has clearly softened since midsummer. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-pull-based leading indicator that tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks) showed a tentative uptick in late June to 123.3 on the spot week, with the 4-week average reversing higher to 127.5 — but the 13-week average was still falling at -2.4% YoY. The MII and MBA are telling a consistent story: demand cooled through the summer and has not yet convincingly re-accelerated. What to watch over the next four weeks: whether the Altos 13-week pending average holds above +4% YoY as the seasonal slowdown deepens, and whether the MII's late-June uptick translates into a stabilization in MBA applications by mid-September.

The macro backdrop this month was genuinely mixed in ways that matter for housing. August payrolls came in at 162,000 — well above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%, also beating the 4.4% expected. Employed households buy houses; a labor market that keeps outrunning expectations supports the demand floor even when affordability is strained. The counterweight is that the hires rate fell back to 3.2% in July (from 3.4%) and the quits rate slipped to 1.9%, both signaling a labor market that is healthy but becoming less fluid — fewer people voluntarily changing jobs means less household formation and relocation-driven buying. The inflation picture is more concerning for rates than the headlines suggested: Core PCE for July came in at +3.34% YoY, still running above the Fed's target, and the 10-year Treasury has moved back toward 4.92% — which, combined with the still-elevated spread, keeps the 30-year above 6.9% and meaningfully constrains affordability. CPI shelter at +3.16% YoY continued its slow deceleration but remains the stickiest component; as actual rents (Zillow ZORI at +3.0% YoY) continue to run below shelter CPI, that gap will keep pulling the official inflation read lower over time, which is the path toward Fed easing — but it is a slow path. The one signal that deserves more attention than it is getting is the long-term unemployed cohort: workers unemployed 27 weeks or more rose 9% in a single month to 1.93 million, and median unemployment duration jumped to 11.4 weeks, up 15% YoY.
