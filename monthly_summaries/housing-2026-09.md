# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales held positive for the 55th consecutive week through late June, running at +4.81% year-over-year — the steadiest demand signal in the dataset — even as the spot week dipped to -2.99% YoY and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37%. That deceleration matters: the spot reading has now gone negative, which breaks a streak that had been running for over a year, and it arrived alongside NAR's August existing home sales print of 3.98 million SAAR — down 0.50% YoY, just turning negative after several months of positive comps, though it beat the 3.85 million consensus by a meaningful margin. On the NSA basis, the August read was 363,000 units, down 3.46% YoY, so the direction is consistent. New home sales were weaker still: the July Census print came in at 607,000 SAAR, off 5.0% YoY, with new-home months supply jumping to 9.6 — a level that typically pushes builders toward incentives rather than new production. That dynamic is already visible in the starts-versus-permits split: July single-family starts fell 15% YoY and missed expectations by a wide margin, while single-family permits rose 2.2% YoY to their highest level since May 2024. Builders are clearing the pipeline, not adding to it — which constrains resale competition now but tightens supply options for buyers in 2027 and beyond. Altos inventory through late June sat at roughly 1.075 million active listings, up just 1.2% YoY, with the 13-week new-listings average also barely positive at +0.71% YoY; the YoY new-listings growth that characterized early 2026 has faded. Pending days on market at 70.9 days — up 6.7% YoY for the 103rd consecutive week — confirm that the market is slower than a year ago even if it hasn't stalled. The 30-year fixed rate is 7.17% as of today, up 92 basis points from a year ago and drifting higher over the past four days; the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.22 points on today's daily read, against a weekly average of 2.13 — still roughly 45-50 basis points above the long-run historical norm of around 1.7 points. That spread is actually ticking back up after a brief compression earlier this year, which removes one of the quiet affordability levers agents could point to. MBA purchase applications are down 4.2% YoY on the 4-week average and falling; the 13-week average has declined for nine consecutive weeks. The Xactus MII through late June was still negative YoY at -5.6% on the spot read and -2.3% on the 4-week average, diverging from any near-term recovery signal — when MII leads MBA down by 2-4 weeks, the MBA deterioration visible now was telegraphed. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the 13-week Altos pending average holds its positive YoY streak or follows the spot and 4-week readings into negative territory; if it breaks, that is the most concrete sign that demand has genuinely shifted rather than just seasonally softened.

The macro picture this month is unusually legible, and it is not particularly friendly to housing. The August payroll print was +162,000 — well above the +59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.1% against expectations of 4.4%, so the labor market surface looks firm. But underneath it, the hires rate fell back to 3.2% in July (down 10 basis points month-over-month), the quits rate slipped to 1.9%, and — most relevant for the 6-to-12-month horizon — the long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose nearly 9% in a single month to 1.93 million, with median unemployment duration jumping to 11.4 weeks, up 15% year-over-year. A low headline unemployment rate co-existing with a rising long-term unemployed cohort is the pattern that precedes delinquency pressure; it does not affect this fall's selling season, but agents pricing properties that will close in 2027 are working in a market where that pressure will be building. On inflation, August CPI came in at +0.40% month-over-month against an expected +0.90% — a material miss to the downside — with core at +2.45% YoY. Shelter CPI was +3.03% YoY and still rising; it remains the stickiest component and keeps Core PCE (last read at +3.34% YoY through July) above the Fed's 2% target in a way that makes near-term rate cuts difficult to justify.
