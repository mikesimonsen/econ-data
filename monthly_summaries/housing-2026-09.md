# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The most recent Altos weekly data in the snapshot is from the week ending June 26, 2026 — roughly ten weeks before today's September 5 date. That lag is wider than normal and means I cannot accurately characterize "the last four weeks" of pending sales, inventory, or new listings using Altos data as if it were current. I'll cite the Altos figures with their actual reference period and note the gap where it matters, rather than present them as real-time reads.

---

Demand held its footing through most of the summer but is showing the first signs of fatigue heading into fall. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales sat at about 89,700 units as of the week ending June 26 — up roughly 4.8% year-over-year and positive for 55 consecutive weeks — a streak that has been the backbone of the demand story all year. The 4-week average was also positive at +4.4% YoY, though decelerating from earlier highs, and the spot week itself briefly went negative at -3.0% YoY, which is worth watching. Inventory on the Altos side was running about 1.075 million units at that same date, up just over 1% YoY — modest growth that has since been corroborated by Realtor.com's August read of 1.14 million active listings, up 3.8% YoY and rising for seven consecutive months. New listings on the Altos 13-week average were essentially flat YoY (+0.7%), and the spot week turned negative (-7.1%), suggesting the new-listings pipeline is softening rather than expanding — a dynamic that is keeping inventory growth well below what the Sun Belt would need to clear its overhang. Pending days on market at 70.9 days are running about 6.7% above last year, a quiet signal that homes are taking slightly longer to contract. NAR's July existing home sales came in at a 4.06 million SAAR — beating the 3.85 million consensus by a meaningful margin — but the YoY gain is only +1.25%, and it follows two months of sequential softening on the seasonally adjusted basis. The NSA read of 400,000 closed transactions in July is +2.83% YoY, six consecutive months of positive territory. New home sales told a different story: 607,000 SAAR in July, down 10.5% from June and -5.0% YoY, with months supply at 9.6 — elevated enough that builders have already begun pulling back. Single-family starts fell to 808,000 SAAR in July (-15% YoY), missing the consensus badly, while single-family permits ticked up to 894,000 — the highest since May 2024. That permits-above-starts divergence says builders are keeping options open without breaking ground, a posture that limits near-term completions but doesn't yet signal a lasting supply contraction. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.89% as of September 4, essentially flat over the past month, and 44 basis points above a year ago. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.13 percentage points — still roughly 40 basis points wide of the historical norm near 1.7 points — meaning the affordability improvement that would come from spread compression has not materialized. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is -2.8% YoY as of the week ending August 28, with the 13-week average now negative for eight consecutive weeks, down -0.5% YoY. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead the MBA by two to four weeks — was showing a spot reading of 123.3 as of late June, down 5.6% YoY, though its 4-week average had ticked up 2.3%. The MII's 13-week average was still falling. Given the MII's lead time, that earlier softness likely contributed to the MBA weakness we are seeing now. Watch whether the MBA 4-week average can recover above flat YoY over the next month — that would signal the demand slowdown is seasonal rather than structural.

The macro picture in August was, on balance, better than the consensus expected, but the details underneath the headline numbers deserve attention. Payrolls came in at +162,000 for August against a 59,000 consensus — a large beat driven in part by construction employment, where residential building added jobs at a statistically unusual pace (+0.8% month-over-month, a 2.6-standard-deviation move, turning positive YoY for the first time in months). The unemployment rate held at 4.1%, also well below the 4.4% consensus. For housing, a labor market this firm supports household formation and gives potential buyers the income confidence to move forward — which is partly why existing sales beat in July. But the JOLTS data released September 1 complicated that picture: the hires rate fell back to 3.2% in July (-0.2 percentage points) and the quits rate slipped to 1.9%, both signals that worker mobility is declining.
