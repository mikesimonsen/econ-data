# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

The Altos 13-week pending sales average held positive for the 55th consecutive week through late June, with the four-week average running +4.4% year-over-year — a reassuring base beneath a spot week that turned briefly negative (-3.0% YoY) as seasonal softening arrived. Inventory continued to expand, with Altos total active supply up roughly 1% YoY and the Realtor.com active listing count up 3.8% YoY as of late August, though new listings decelerated sharply — the Altos new-listings 4-week average was nearly flat YoY (+0.4%) after running positively for weeks, and the Realtor.com new listing count was essentially unchanged YoY (-0.1%). That supply is accumulating more from slower turnover than from fresh seller activity. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are sitting a week longer than they were a year ago, which agents in most markets are already feeling. NAR reported August existing sales at 3.98 million SAAR, down 0.5% YoY — a beat against the 3.85 million consensus, but the first negative YoY read in several months. The NSA count of 363,000 was down 3.5% YoY, and all four regions posted YoY declines. New home sales told a softer story: July printed 607,000 SAAR, down 5.0% YoY, with months supply at 9.6 — builders are working off existing inventory rather than adding to it. On rates: the 30-year fixed sits at 7.07% as of September 10, up 78 basis points YoY, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.15 points — still roughly 45 basis points above the historical norm of about 1.7 points. The 4-week spread average has ticked back up after a brief compression, meaning the affordability relief that spread normalization would deliver has stalled for now. MBA purchase applications confirmed the softness, with the 4-week average down 4.2% YoY and the 13-week average falling for nine consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index — which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks — showed a modest uptick in late June (the 4-week average reversed off its lows), but the 13-week MII remained negative YoY at -2.4%. The MII's tentative turn bears watching: if it firms through September, MBA applications could stabilize in October. Over the next four weeks, watch whether the Altos 13-week pending average holds above zero YoY as fall seasonality compresses the spot data, and whether the spread compresses if the 10-year settles — even a 20-25 basis point tightening would move the 30-year meaningfully without any Fed action.

The macro backdrop this month was genuinely mixed in its housing implications. August payrolls came in at 162,000, well above the 59,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate held at 4.1% against a 4.4% expectation — on its face, a healthy labor market is the single most important support for housing demand. But the JOLTS data told a more cautious story: hires fell to a 3.2% rate in July (down 0.2 points) and quits fell to 1.9%, both reversing recent improvements. A lower quits rate matters for housing because voluntary job-changers — who tend to trigger relocations and move-up purchases — are pulling back. More concerning is the long-term unemployed cohort: workers unemployed 27 or more weeks rose to 1.93 million in August, up 9% in a single month, with median unemployment duration jumping to 11.4 weeks and up 15% YoY. This group does not affect your listing appointments today, but it is the early signal for delinquency pressure in 6-12 months — worth tracking quarterly. On inflation: August headline CPI came in at +3.35% YoY, cooler than the 0.9% monthly consensus expected, and core CPI held at +2.45% YoY. Shelter CPI rose 3.0% YoY — still above the Fed's target but continuing to decelerate from its peaks, which should eventually pull core PCE (July: +3.34% YoY) lower.
