**September 2026 Housing Summary**

The most reliable demand signal in the dashboard — the Altos 13-week average of new pending sales — held positive for the 55th consecutive week as of late June, running at roughly +4.8% year-over-year, though the 4-week average had decelerated to +4.4% and the spot week dipped to -3.0% YoY. That spot dip is seasonal noise more than a trend break, but it bears watching: when the spot week underruns the 13-week average by this margin three weeks running, the 4-week average typically follows down within a month. Altos inventory at 1.07 million units was up only +1.2% year-over-year as of late June — growth that has clearly decelerated from the double-digit YoY gains seen through 2025 — and new listings flipped briefly negative YoY (-7.1% spot) even as the 13-week average held modestly positive. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9 days, up nearly 7% year-over-year, which is consistent with buyers taking longer to commit in a rate environment that has refused to ease. NAR's July existing home sales print of 4.06 million SAAR beat consensus of 3.85 million handily, but the YoY gain of +1.25% is the full story — the absolute level remains historically subdued and the month-over-month dip of -0.7% snapped a brief rising streak. More notable: NAR's inventory reading of 1.54 million units turned negative year-over-year for the first time in several months, confirming the Altos deceleration. New home sales tell a different story — July came in at 607,000 SAAR, down 10.5% on the month and now -5.0% year-over-year after running positive; months supply of new homes jumped to 9.6, the highest since the post-pandemic correction. Builders are not selling through the way they were, which explains why July single-family starts fell 9.9% on the month to 808,000 SAAR and missed expectations significantly, even as total permits actually beat at 1,433,000 — a starts/permits divergence that signals builders are greenlighting future supply while pulling back on current production. That pipeline gap matters for 2027 availability, not this selling season. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.89% as of today, essentially flat on the month, and the 10-year Treasury holds at 4.76%. The spread between them is 2.13 percentage points — still well above the long-run norm of roughly 1.7 points, meaning the rate market is handing buyers roughly 40-45 basis points of rate relief the moment lender risk appetite normalizes, without any Fed action required. That spread has compressed 15 basis points year-over-year, which is the quiet affordability story most buyers' agents are not telling yet. MBA purchase applications posted a modest recovery week-over-week but the 13-week average is now -0.5% year-over-year and has fallen eight consecutive weeks — the first sustained negative YoY stretch in over a year. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-inquiry-based leading indicator that tends to run 2-4 weeks ahead of MBA) showed a bounce in its 4-week average in late June but the 13-week trend remained negative. When MII and MBA are both negative on a 13-week basis simultaneously, as they are now, the September pending sales read warrants close attention.

The August payroll report delivered the month's most consequential surprise: +162,000 jobs against a consensus of +59,000, and unemployment held at 4.1% versus expectations of 4.4%. That is not a labor market on the verge of triggering forced selling or demand destruction — it removes one of the cleaner arguments for an imminent Fed cut and keeps the 10-year anchored where it is, which in turn keeps the 30-year mortgage rate anchored. Wage growth of +3.1% year-over-year is running above shelter CPI (+3.2%) by only a whisker, so affordability is not improving through the income channel as fast as hoped. On inflation: Core PCE came in at +3.3% year-over-year in July, core CPI at +2.5%, and shelter CPI at +3.2%. Shelter is the stickiest component, and its deceleration from the 5-6% range of 2023-24 is real but gradual — the lagged effect of rent normalization is still feeding through. With core PCE at 3.3% and a resilient labor market, the Fed has limited room to move rates lower near-term, which anchors the 10-year and — absent spread compression — keeps the 30-year in the high 6s.
