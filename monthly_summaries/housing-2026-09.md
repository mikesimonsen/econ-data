# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held through the summer but is showing the first meaningful cracks heading into fall. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales remained positive YoY at +4.81% — a streak that has now run 55 consecutive weeks — but the spot week ending June 26 turned negative (-2.99% YoY) and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37%, both flagging that the underlying bid is softening at the margin. Inventory rose for an eighth consecutive week, reaching roughly 1.075 million active listings at +1.17% YoY, while new listings at the single-week level dipped to -7.14% YoY after a brief run of positive prints — the 4-week average held barely positive at +0.41%. Pending days on market extended to 70.9 days, up 6.71% YoY, which has now ticked higher for 103 consecutive weeks; homes are taking longer to contract. NAR August existing sales came in at 3.98 million SAAR, down slightly from July and now 0.50% below a year ago — the YoY flip to negative is the headline, but the beat against a 3.85 million consensus matters too; the underlying pace is weak but not collapsing. New home sales told a harder story: July's read was 607,000 SAAR, down more than 10% from June and 5% below a year ago, with months of supply at 9.6 — elevated enough that builders are cutting prices (new home median fell to $393,800, -0.88% YoY). The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.19% as of today, up 84 basis points from a year ago and essentially unmoved over the past month; the spread to the 10-year Treasury has widened back to 2.23 percentage points against a historical norm near 1.70 — meaning roughly half a point of the current rate level is spread, not yield. If credit conditions normalize, that spread compresses without any Fed action. The MBA purchase index 4-week average is running -5.97% YoY, at its lowest since September 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for 10 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (a credit-inquiry-based measure that leads MBA by two to four weeks) was showing a tentative uptick in late June at -5.59% YoY, but its 13-week average had been falling for seven weeks — the two are not yet diverging in a bullish direction. Watch whether the MII 4-week average begins to recover; if it does, MBA applications should follow in mid-October.

The macro backdrop explains why demand is soft without explaining a breakdown. August payrolls came in at +162,000 — well above the 59,000 consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%, also better than expected. On the surface that looks supportive of housing, but the JOLTS data through July told a more cautious story: the hires rate fell to 3.2% and quits dropped to 1.9%, both reverting after brief rebounds. The quits rate is a proxy for labor-market confidence and household willingness to take on a new mortgage; at 1.9% it is at the low end of the post-pandemic range, which restrains move-up demand more than it shows in headline unemployment. The signal worth watching carefully is the long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 weeks or more — which jumped 8.98% in a single month to 1.93 million. It remains near flat YoY (+0.31%), but reversals in that series tend to accumulate quietly before they matter; this is not a 2026 selling-season story, but it is the early chapter of a potential distressed-borrower story in 2027. On inflation, August CPI core printed +2.45% YoY and shelter CPI held at +3.03% YoY — both moving in the right direction but still above levels that would prompt the Fed to ease aggressively. Core PCE through July was +3.34% YoY, keeping real rates restrictive. Regionally, the Case-Shiller June data (released late August, so two months lagged) confirmed the two-speed market is widening: Chicago gained +6.94% YoY, New York +4.49%, Cleveland +4.06%, while Dallas fell -0.58%, Phoenix -0.67%, Las Vegas -1.84%, Seattle -1.80%, and Tampa -1.47%. Agents in Midwest and Northeast markets are working a fundamentally different price environment than those in Sun Belt metros where supply absorbed the pandemic-era run-up. The macro variable to track next month is whether the 10-year Treasury yield — currently at 4.96% and just beginning to tick lower — sustains any move below 4.75%; at that level, and with the spread at 2.23 points, the 30-year fixed would approach 6.90-7.00%, which is not a rescue but is a directional shift that would show up in MBA applications within two to three weeks.
