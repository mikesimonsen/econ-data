**September 2026 Housing Summary**

Demand held positive through August, though the trend line is softening at the edges. The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales was running +4.81% YoY as of late June — a streak that has now extended to 55 consecutive weeks of positive year-over-year readings — but the 4-week average had decelerated to +4.37% YoY, and the spot week dipped to -2.99% YoY, the first negative weekly print in that streak. One week doesn't break a trend, but the direction of deceleration is real and worth tracking. Inventory remains the constraint: Altos total active supply was up just +1.17% YoY, and new listings turned negative on a spot basis (-7.14% YoY) even as the 13-week average held modestly positive (+0.71%). Pending days on market stood at 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are taking longer to go under contract, which is consistent with softening demand at the margin rather than a supply-driven slowdown. NAR's July existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR, beating expectations by roughly 210K units and up 1.25% YoY, though the SAAR reading itself ticked down for a second month from a recent high. The regional NSA data tells a cleaner story: the Northeast rose 3.9% YoY for a fifth consecutive month of gains, while the South was flat YoY. New home sales diverged sharply — July printed 607K SAAR, down 10.5% from June and -5.0% YoY, with months of supply jumping to 9.6 — a meaningful contrast to the relative resilience in existing sales. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 6.91% as of today, up modestly over the past week, with the spread to the 10-year Treasury at 2.12 points — still well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points, meaning the affordability lever of spread compression has not been pulled. The 4-week spread average of 2.08 is 19 basis points tighter than a year ago, which is progress, but slowly. MBA purchase applications ticked up in the most recent week (+2.2%), though the 4-week average is still -2.81% YoY and the 13-week average has now been negative for eight consecutive weeks — a more durable signal of demand hesitation. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA applications by two to four weeks, showed a spot bounce to +3.53% in late June but its 13-week average was still falling at -2.38% YoY; importantly, the MII has been running weaker than MBA all summer, which suggests the recent MBA uptick may not have follow-through. Watch whether the MII 4-week average can sustain its recovery as the fall buying season opens. On the builder side, the starts/permits divergence is the most consequential supply signal of the month: July single-family starts fell 9.9% to 808K SAAR and were -15% YoY, missing expectations, while single-family permits rose to 894K SAAR — the highest since May 2024. Builders are pulling permits but not breaking ground, which implies they are waiting for rate or demand conditions to improve before committing to new pipeline. That reluctance matters more for 2027 supply than for the current selling season.

The macro picture is more unsettled than the headline unemployment rate suggests. July payrolls came in at roughly -23K against a consensus of +59K — a meaningful miss — and average hourly earnings rose only 0.1% month-over-month against an expected 0.3%, which cools the wage-inflation story but also signals softer labor income for potential buyers. The unemployment rate itself fell to 4.1%, beating the 4.4% consensus, but the JOLTS data released this week complicates the picture: the hires rate dropped 0.2 points to 3.2% and the quits rate fell to 1.9%, both moving in the direction of a labor market where workers have less confidence and mobility — the conditions that historically suppress household formation and move-up demand. The long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) totaled 1.771 million in July, down from June, which is a constructive near-term read; still, the YoY count remains elevated relative to 2024 levels and the median unemployment duration is running 2.9% above a year ago. That cohort is not yet large enough to drive distressed-sale volume, but it is worth monitoring as it tends to presage mortgage delinquency with a 6-to-12-month lag. On inflation, core PCE rose 0.25% in July and is running at 3.34% YoY — above the Fed's target — which keeps the policy rate on hold and anchors the 10-year yield near 4.79%.
