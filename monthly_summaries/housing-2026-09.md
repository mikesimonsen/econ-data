**September 2026 Housing Summary**

New pending sales turned negative year-over-year on a spot basis in late June, but the four-week average held at +4.4% YoY and the 13-week average — which has now posted positive YoY readings for 55 consecutive weeks — sat at +4.8% YoY as of June 26. That streak is the number to hold onto: one soft spot week does not break a trend that durable. Altos inventory rose for eight consecutive weeks to roughly 1.07 million active listings, up about 1.2% YoY — growth that is real but decelerating, and new listings actually turned negative YoY in the spot week (-7.1%), though the four-week average remained barely positive (+0.4%). The inventory story is not one of supply overwhelming demand; it is one of both sides of the ledger softening in parallel. Pending days on market ticked to 70.9, up 6.7% YoY — not alarming, but worth noting as a sign that buyers have slightly more time to decide than a year ago. NAR existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR for July, down fractionally from June but up 1.25% YoY on the seasonally adjusted series and up 2.8% YoY on the NSA count — a meaningful beat against the 3.85 million consensus expectation. New home sales told a different story: 607,000 SAAR in July, down 10.5% from June and off 5% YoY, with months supply expanding to 9.6 — builders are sitting on finished inventory in a way that existing-home sellers are not. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.89% as of today, roughly 61 basis points above a year ago, which is the affordability headwind agents feel every day at the listing table. The more important number is the spread to the 10-year Treasury: 2.10 points currently, against a long-run norm of about 1.70. That 40-basis-point excess spread means rates can fall meaningfully without any Fed action — purely from bond-market normalization. The spread has compressed 13 basis points YoY and ticked down again today, so that process is underway, if slowly. MBA purchase applications turned up in the most recent week (+2.2%) after several weeks of softness, though the four-week average is still -2.8% YoY and the 13-week average has turned slightly negative — the first such reading after months of positive YoY territory. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks, bounced off its lows in late June (the most recent read), with the four-week average also reversing higher — a tentative sign that the August softness in applications may not deepen further into September. On the supply side, the starts/permits divergence is now unambiguous: total starts fell 12.4% in July, missing the 1,350,000 consensus by a wide margin and turning negative YoY for the first time in the cycle, while total permits rose 4.3%, beating expectations and turning positive YoY. Builders are authorizing future projects while pulling back sharply on current construction — a pattern that clears the near-term pipeline but constrains supply in 2027 and beyond. Watch the MBA four-week average over the next four weeks: if it stabilizes near flat YoY, demand is holding; if the 13-week average continues lower, the fall selling season will be softer than the existing-sales beat in July implied.

The macro backdrop is genuinely two-sided this month, which is unusual. August payrolls came in at +162,000 — nearly three times the +59,000 consensus estimate — and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%, also well below the 4.4% expected. That combination keeps the labor-market foundation under housing intact: employed households do not list distressed, and confident workers are more willing to commit to a purchase. Core PCE, however, printed at +3.34% YoY for July, and core CPI came in at +2.47% YoY — both above the Fed's 2% target, with shelter CPI still rising at +3.16% YoY. The tension is that a strong labor market removes urgency for the Fed to cut, while elevated inflation actively prevents it; the 10-year Treasury at 4.79% today reflects exactly that bind, and it is the direct input into the mortgage rate agents see on their rate sheets. The signal worth watching underneath the headline unemployment rate is the long-term unemployed cohort: workers unemployed 27 weeks or longer rose to 1.93 million in August, up nearly 9% in a single month, with median unemployment duration jumping to 11.4 weeks — up 15% YoY. This cohort does not affect the current selling season, but it is where future mortgage distress accumulates; an agent pricing a listing today does not need to worry about it, but an agent advising an investor on rental cash flow should.
