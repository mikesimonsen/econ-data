# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The weekly Altos demand read through late June showed new pending sales down 3.0% year-over-year on the spot reading, but the four-week average held at +4.4% YoY and the 13-week average remained at +4.8% YoY — positive for 55 consecutive weeks. That streak is still intact, though it is decelerating. The most recent weekly spot print turning negative is worth watching; if the four-week average follows, the streak ends. Active inventory continued rising, up about 1.2% YoY with eight consecutive weeks of gains, though new listings growth decelerated sharply — the spot week fell 7.1% YoY after running positive. The four-week average for new listings held just barely positive at +0.4% YoY. Pending days on market ticked up to 70.9 days, now running 6.7% above year-ago levels for 103 consecutive weeks, signaling that the pace of absorption has not tightened. August existing home sales came in at 3.98 million SAAR — down 0.5% YoY, technically negative, but it beat expectations of 3.85 million by a meaningful margin. The NSA read of 363,000 was down 3.5% YoY, a more sober signal. Inventory on the NAR side grew to 1.62 million units, up 5.9% YoY, with months supply at 4.9 — the market is not tight. New home sales for July fell to 607,000 SAAR, down 5.0% YoY, and months supply for new homes rose to 9.6, its highest reading in recent months — builders are sitting on completed product. Starts for July fell sharply, missing expectations at 1,239,000 SAAR and down 12.8% YoY, with single-family starts off 15% YoY; notably, South starts hit their lowest level since April 2023. Permits, however, rose to 1,433,000, beating expectations and turning positive YoY for the first time recently — the starts-permits divergence suggests builders are authorizing future projects while working down existing pipeline, a supply story that plays out over 18-24 months, not this season. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.07% today, up 78 basis points year-over-year and ticking higher in recent days. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.15 percentage points — well above the historical norm of roughly 1.7 points. The four-week spread average of 2.09 points has ticked back up after a brief compression, meaning the affordability relief that compression would deliver has not arrived. MBA purchase applications were down 4.2% YoY on the four-week average and the 13-week average has fallen for nine consecutive weeks — a sustained weakening. The Xactus MII, which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks (it measures mortgage credit inquiry intent, an earlier point in the buyer funnel than an application), showed a spot uptick in late June but its 13-week average was still falling at -2.4% YoY. That divergence — spot uptick in MII while MBA continues declining — is not yet a clear reversal signal; watch whether the MII four-week average sustains its uptick through September. The number to watch over the next four weeks is the Altos four-week pending average: if it slips below flat YoY, the 55-week streak ends and the demand read changes materially.

The macro backdrop is sending mixed signals that, taken together, point to a buyer who is employed but cautious and increasingly rate-sensitive. August payrolls came in at +162,000, beating a consensus of +59,000 by a wide margin — a result that removed near-term recession anxiety but also reduced the probability of an imminent Fed cut, which is the mechanism that would push the 10-year Treasury lower and, eventually, pull mortgage rates down. The unemployment rate held at 4.1%, also beating a consensus of 4.4%. However, JOLTS data for July showed the hires rate falling back to 3.2% and quits dropping to 1.9% — both declining YoY — which means the labor market is stable but less dynamic. Workers are staying put, which is historically associated with suppressed household formation and reduced move-up demand; people who feel uncertain about their next job do not list their house. The long-term unemployed (27+ weeks) rose to 1.93 million in August, up nearly 9% month-over-month and reversing a recent decline. That cohort does not threaten the current selling season, but if it continues accumulating, it is the future distressed-inventory story — watch this number quarterly.
