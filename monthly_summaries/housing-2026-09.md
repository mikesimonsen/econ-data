# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Altos weekly demand held positive through mid-summer but showed the first cracks in recent weeks: the single-week new pending sales reading for the week of June 26 fell 3.0% YoY — ending what had been a 55-week streak of positive YoY readings on the 13-week average — though that smoothed average still sits at +4.8% YoY and the 4-week average held at +4.4% YoY, suggesting the spot dip is noise at the edge of the trend rather than a clean reversal. Inventory continued rising, up 1.2% YoY on Altos total active supply and running eight consecutive weeks higher, while new listings growth decelerated sharply: the single-week read flipped to -7.1% YoY after running positive, though the 4-week average held a slim +0.4% YoY and the 13-week average — up 14 consecutive weeks — sat at +0.7% YoY, indicating the new-listings growth impulse is fading into seasonal norms rather than accelerating. Pending days on market stretched to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY, a meaningful signal that homes are sitting longer even as inventory remains below pre-2019 levels in most markets. NAR existing home sales came in at 3.98 million SAAR for August — above the 3.85 million consensus, but down 0.5% YoY on the SAAR and -3.5% YoY on the raw NSA count, which is the cleaner directional read; the YoY comparison turned negative for the first time after several positive months. New home sales told a softer story: July printed 607K SAAR, -5.0% YoY, with months supply at 9.6 — well into builder-uncomfortable territory. The 30-year fixed rate sits at 7.08% as of September 11, up 81 basis points YoY and rising three consecutive days. The spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.15 percentage points — against a historical norm near 1.7 points — meaning roughly 45 basis points of rate premium sit in lender margin and secondary-market risk appetite rather than underlying benchmark moves; the 4-week average spread has ticked up slightly after compressing earlier this year, so the hidden affordability relief that spread compression could provide has stalled. MBA purchase applications averaged -4.2% YoY on the 4-week basis through September 4, with the 13-week average down nine consecutive weeks at -1.3% YoY — a steady deterioration. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index (which leads MBA by roughly 2-4 weeks by capturing early credit-pull activity before applications are filed) bounced in late June — the 4-week MII average rose to -2.3% YoY from a deeper hole — a mild positive divergence from the MBA trend worth watching to see if it translates into application improvement through September. Builder permits firmed in July: total permits rose to 1,433K SAAR (+5.2% YoY), single-family permits reached their highest since May 2024, yet starts collapsed to 1,239K SAAR (-12.7% YoY), missing expectations by a wide margin. The starts-permits divergence suggests builders are pulling future authorizations while working down a stalled completion pipeline — a dynamic that constrains new supply in 2027 more than 2026. Watch over the next four weeks whether the Xactus uptick flows through into MBA purchase applications and whether inventory growth reaccelerates as new listings seasonally thin into fall.

The macro backdrop entering September is genuinely two-sided, which is why the rate and demand picture is muddier than the headlines suggest. August payrolls printed +162K — more than double the +59K consensus — and unemployment held at 4.1%, well below the 4.4% expected, which removes near-term pressure on the Fed to cut aggressively and keeps the 10-year yield anchored near 4.93%. That yield level, combined with the still-wide mortgage spread, is the primary reason rates remain above 7% despite earlier-year optimism about a glide path lower. August CPI came in cooler than feared at +0.4% MoM headline against a +0.9% consensus, with core CPI at +2.45% YoY — a meaningful deceleration — but core PCE for July held at +3.34% YoY, still above the Fed's target, and shelter CPI ticked up 0.26% for the month and is running +3.0% YoY, continuing to slow but not yet releasing the inflation pressure that would give the Fed room to move rates with conviction. The labor market reading that most directly feeds housing distress risk is not the headline unemployment rate but the long-term unemployed cohort: those out of work 27 or more weeks jumped 9.0% in a single month to 1.93 million, and median unemployment duration rose to 11.4 weeks, up 15% YoY — both reversing recent improving trends. This cohort does not affect the current selling season; it accumulates quietly until it shows up in delinquency data 6-12 months
