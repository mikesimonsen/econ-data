# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The Altos 13-week average for new pending sales held at +4.81% YoY through late June — positive for 55 consecutive weeks — but the spot reading dipped to -2.99% YoY that same week, and the 4-week average decelerated to +4.37%, down from stronger readings earlier in the summer. That spot dip coincided with inventory continuing to build: total active listings rose for eight straight weeks and sat roughly 1% above year-ago levels, while Realtor.com's active count was up 3.76% YoY through August. New listings turned slightly negative YoY on the spot week but the 13-week average remained +0.71% — supply growth is decelerating rather than reversing. Pending days on market edged to 70.9 days, up 6.71% YoY, a quiet signal that buyer urgency has not returned. NAR's August existing home sales printed at 3.98 million SAAR, beating the 3.85 million consensus but still down 0.5% YoY on a seasonally adjusted basis; the NSA read was -3.46% YoY, and the YoY trend has now turned negative after a brief positive stretch. New home sales (July, most recent available) told a softer story — 607K SAAR, down 5% YoY, with months of supply jumping to 9.6. The 30-year fixed sits at 7.20% today, up 83 basis points from a year ago, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is running about 2.20 points — well above the historical norm near 1.7 points. The 4-week average spread is 2.14, having ticked up two consecutive weeks. That gap is the hidden affordability lever: if spread compression of 30-40 basis points materializes — which requires no Fed action, only less stress in the MBS market — effective rates move meaningfully without any change in the 10-year. MBA purchase applications were -5.97% YoY on the 4-week average as of September 11, the weakest reading since September 2024, and the 13-week average has fallen for ten consecutive weeks. The Xactus MII (which tends to lead MBA by two to four weeks, capturing mortgage intent before it reaches the application stage) was -5.59% YoY on the spot read in late June, with the 13-week average down 2.38% — the MII has been softer than MBA all summer, which suggests the application weakness we are seeing now was visible in the Xactus data earlier. Watch whether the MII 4-week average, which ticked up to -2.30% YoY from worse levels, continues to recover. On the supply side, single-family permits slipped to 878K SAAR in August but are still +2.33% YoY and at their highest since May 2024; single-family starts bounced to 918K, reversing July's decline. The divergence to watch is completions: total completions fell to 1,128K SAAR, the lowest since June 2011, down 27.5% YoY. Builders have permits and units under construction but completions are evaporating — a pipeline squeeze that will constrain resale competition from new construction into 2027.

The macro backdrop explains why demand is soft without requiring a recession narrative. Payrolls came in at +162K for August, well above the 59K consensus, and unemployment held at 4.1% against a 4.4% expectation — on the headline, the labor market looks fine. But underneath, the JOLTS hires rate fell to 3.2% in July and quits dropped to 1.9%, both declining and both historically associated with reduced household mobility and therefore fewer voluntary move-up transactions. Fewer people quitting jobs means fewer people moving for new opportunities, and that is a direct suppressor of existing home sales. The long-term unemployed cohort (27+ weeks) jumped 8.98% in a single month to 1.93 million — that reversal is worth watching, not because it threatens this selling season, but because this group is the leading edge of future mortgage distress if it keeps building. On inflation: Core PCE was 3.34% YoY through July, and the August CPI print came in cooler than expected (headline +3.35% YoY, core +2.45%), with shelter CPI at +3.03% — still sticky but decelerating. The Fed will not be moved by one cool print when core PCE is still running a third above its 2% target, and the 10-year Treasury ticked back to 5.00% today after a brief pullback, keeping the affordability math difficult. Consumer confidence sat at 90.8 in July, below year-ago levels, which is consistent with buyer hesitation despite a functioning labor market.
