# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

Demand held positive through the summer, though the Altos data through late June shows the streak narrowing at the edges. The 13-week average for new pending sales sat at 89,672 — up 4.81% year-over-year and positive for 55 consecutive weeks — which is the durable signal worth anchoring to. The spot week printed -2.99% YoY, and the 4-week average came in at +4.37% YoY, decelerating from recent highs; the spot negative reading bears watching but does not yet break the trend. Inventory on the Altos side rose to roughly 1.07 million active listings, up just over 1% YoY — supply growth has essentially stalled relative to the 20-30% YoY gains we saw in 2024, and new listings on a 4-week basis are barely positive YoY (+0.41%), with the single-week print turning negative (-7.14% YoY). Pending days on market extended to 70.9 days, up 6.7% YoY — homes are taking longer to go under contract, which is consistent with where rates are. NAR's July existing home sales came in at 4.06 million SAAR, beating expectations of 3.85 million and running +1.25% YoY, though the headline SA print slipped modestly from June; the NSA YoY of +2.83% is the cleaner read and marks six consecutive months of YoY gains. New home sales told a different story: July's 607K SAAR missed and fell 10.5% from June, with months supply jumping to 9.6 — builders are sitting on elevated finished inventory, which is why median new home prices are down 0.88% YoY while existing home prices rose 1.97% YoY. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.89% today, up 61 basis points YoY, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is 2.10 percentage points — still roughly 40 basis points wide of the historical norm near 1.7pp, meaning a meaningful portion of today's rate is excess spread rather than Treasury movement. The spread has compressed 13bp YoY and ticked down again this week; if it continues toward 1.85-1.90pp, rates could soften to the mid-6s without any Fed action. MBA purchase applications recovered slightly the week of August 28 (+2.2% week-over-week) but the 4-week average is still -2.81% YoY and the 13-week average has been negative for eight consecutive weeks — that is a soft undercurrent beneath the positive pending streak. The Xactus MII (a credit-pull-based demand gauge that leads MBA by 2-4 weeks) showed a spot uptick in late June but its 13-week average has been falling for seven weeks and sits -2.38% YoY, diverging unfavorably from the Altos pending data. That divergence is the key tension to watch: if the MII does not firm over the next two to three weeks, MBA applications and then pending sales could soften into October. Housing starts fell hard in July — total starts missed at 1,239K SAAR against a 1,350K expectation, down 12.75% YoY — with single-family starts off 15% YoY; but permits rose to 1,433K, beating expectations, with single-family permits at their highest since May 2024. Builders are clearing the existing pipeline but pulling permits for future work, which matters more for 2027 supply than this selling season.

The macro backdrop delivered a genuine positive surprise in August: payrolls printed +162K against a +59K consensus estimate, and the unemployment rate held at 4.1% versus a 4.4% expectation — the labor market is not deteriorating at the pace many anticipated, and a stable job market is the first condition for stable housing demand. Wage growth at 3.09% YoY continues to outpace the Case-Shiller national price appreciation of 1.53% YoY (through June), which is the slow-moving affordability repair that rarely makes headlines. The inflation picture is more complicated for housing: core PCE rose to 3.34% YoY in July — above the Fed's 2% target — while CPI shelter held at 3.16% YoY and has risen seven consecutive months. Shelter CPI feeds directly into Fed comfort on the last-mile inflation problem; as long as it holds above 3%, the Fed has less room to cut, which keeps the 10-year anchored near its current 4.79% level and the mortgage rate elevated accordingly. On pricing, the geographic divergence in Case-Shiller's June data (released August 27) is striking and actionable: Chicago is up 6.94% YoY and rising for seven consecutive months; New York up 4.49% YoY; Boston up 2.73%. Meanwhile Dallas is -0.58% YoY, Phoenix -0.67%, Las Vegas -1.84%, Seattle -1.80%, and Denver -1.18%. Atlanta just turned positive YoY after a period of negative readings.
