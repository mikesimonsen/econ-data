# Monthly Housing Summary — September 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The four weeks through September 23 present a market pulling in two directions at once. On the demand side, the Altos 13-week average for new pending sales has held positive year-over-year for 55 consecutive weeks, but the single-week read turned negative in late June (down 3.0% YoY) and the 4-week average, while still positive at +4.4% YoY, has been decelerating. That gap between the durable trend and the freshest weekly signal is worth watching. Inventory continued to build — active listings are up roughly 2-6% YoY across the major trackers, with NAR reporting 1.62 million units and 4.9 months of supply, both up meaningfully year-over-year. New listings, though, have softened: the Altos 4-week average flipped negative YoY in late June, and Realtor.com's new listing count was essentially flat YoY in August, suggesting the inventory build is more a function of homes sitting longer than of fresh supply entering. Pending days on market at 70.9 days YoY is up 6.7%, confirming that pace is softening. NAR's August existing home sales came in at 3.98 million SAAR — beating the 3.85M consensus but still down 0.5% YoY, with the YoY comparison flipping negative for the first time in several months; the NSA read was -3.5% YoY, and regional weakness was broad. New home sales (July, 607K SAAR) fell more sharply, down 5% YoY, while new home months of supply rose to 9.6 — a level that historically pressures builder pricing and incentive behavior. Single-family starts recovered in August (+7.6% MoM, +5.6% YoY) but the broader starts print of 1,275K missed expectations, and completions fell to their lowest YoY level since June 2011 — a supply pipeline that is thinning faster than the permit data alone would suggest. The 30-year fixed rate ticked back up to 7.26% as of today, reversing a recent decline, with the 10-year Treasury also reversing to 5.12%. The 30-10 spread sits at 2.14 basis points above the Treasury — still roughly 44 basis points above the historical norm of around 1.70 points — meaning a spread compression story remains intact but has not yet delivered meaningful rate relief. MBA purchase applications (4-week average) are -7.4% YoY and at their lowest since August 2024; the 13-week average has fallen for 11 consecutive weeks. The Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA by two to four weeks, showed a tentative uptick from a declining base as of late June but remains -2.3% YoY on a 4-week basis — so it is not yet signaling a near-term recovery in application volume. Over the next four weeks, the key question is whether the rate reversal is a one-week noise event or the beginning of a new range, and whether the Xactus MII follow-through materializes into MBA applications as fall buying activity typically firms.

The macro picture is more mixed than the labor market headline suggests. August payrolls came in at 162K — well above the 59K consensus — and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%, also beating expectations of 4.4%. That jobs strength supports household formation and reduces near-term default risk, which is why the market has not corrected more aggressively. But look beneath the surface: the JOLTS hires rate fell back to 3.2% in July, the quits rate slipped to 1.9% (both down YoY), and the long-term unemployed cohort — those out of work 27 weeks or more — jumped nearly 9% in a single month to 1.93 million. That cohort does not affect the current selling season, but its accumulation is the early signal of future mortgage stress; watch it over the next two quarters. On inflation, the core CPI print of +0.29% MoM and +2.45% YoY was relatively contained, and shelter CPI at +0.26% MoM and +3.03% YoY continues its slow deceleration — the mechanism being that real-time rents (Zillow ZORI down 0.72% MoM in August) are finally pulling the lagged shelter component lower, which over the next two to three prints should modestly reduce upward pressure on core inflation readings and give the Fed more room to stay on hold rather than tighten. Core PCE for July was +3.34% YoY, still above target, which is why the 10-year Treasury at 5.12% is the real affordability ceiling right now — not Fed funds. The two-speed regional price story from Case-Shiller (June data, released August 27) remains the most actionable frame for agents: Chicago is up 6.9% YoY and New York up 4.5%, while Dallas is -0.6%, Phoenix -0.7%, Las Vegas -1.8%, and Seattle -1.8% — all still negative YoY.
