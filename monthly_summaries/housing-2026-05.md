# Monthly Housing Summary — May 2026

*For Compass International Holdings. Prompt source: `prompts/monthly_housing_summary.txt`.*

---

The past four weeks tell a spring-market story that is mostly intact, with one early-warning signal worth tracking. Altos new pending sales (contracts signed) finished the month at +10.5% year-over-year on the latest weekly read and +6.5% on the four-week average — the 47th consecutive week of positive year-over-year contract activity. Pending days on market sit at 76.2 days, with the year-over-year deceleration in DOM growth easing, meaning the market is not getting slower at an accelerating pace. Active inventory closed April at 985,828 single-family homes, +1.8% year-over-year, but inventory growth is decelerating and new listings dropped 12% week-over-week through May 1 — a single-week wobble against a backdrop of single-family building permits down year-over-year for 22 consecutive months. The summer inventory build is unlikely to exceed seasonal norms. NAR's most recent existing home sales print, revised, sits at 4.09 million SAAR; new home sales beat at 682K SAAR (+7.4% month-over-month), but builders are moving volume by cutting prices — the median new home price is down 6.2% year-over-year. The 30-year fixed sits at 6.42% with a spread of 2.07 percentage points over the 10-year Treasury — about 40 basis points above the historical norm of ~1.7pp. That spread compression alone, with no Fed action, could deliver meaningful rate relief. The signal to watch: the Xactus Mortgage Intent Index, which leads MBA purchase applications by two to four weeks, just turned negative year-over-year for the first time this cycle. If that does not reverse over the next two weekly prints, expect purchase applications to soften by late May, which would be the first real demand crack since last fall. The four-week window over Memorial Day weekend is the read on whether the spring carries into summer.

The April jobs report came in better than expected on the headline — +115K payrolls against a +59K consensus, unemployment holding at 4.3% — but the internals were softer than the print suggested. U-6 underemployment rose 20 basis points to 8.2% (its second consecutive monthly worsening), short-term unemployment jumped 16.7% on the month, and people unemployed 27 weeks or longer are now up 9.6% year-over-year. That long-duration cohort is the one that becomes mortgage delinquency risk six to twelve months out — not a current selling-season issue, but a real source of distressed-borrower volume building underneath. On the positive side, the JOLTS hires rate jumped to 3.5% in March, the first meaningful uptick in months; more job-changers means more relocations and more household formation, which is a direct tailwind for transaction volume. Core PCE at +3.2% year-over-year and shelter CPI at +3.0% keep the Fed on hold, and inflation expectations — both the Cleveland Fed survey and Treasury breakevens — reversed their declining trend last week, which is what is holding the 10-year Treasury (and therefore mortgage rates) from drifting lower. Regionally, Case-Shiller's two-speed market is widening, not converging: Chicago (+4.9%), New York (+5.0%), and Cleveland (+4.1%) continue to post healthy year-over-year appreciation, while Seattle (-1.8%), Denver (-2.2%), Tampa (-2.0%), Phoenix (-1.8%), and Dallas (-1.7%) remain in price correction. The national index at +0.7% year-over-year masks the divergence entirely. Agents in the Midwest and Northeast are pricing into a different market than agents in the Sun Belt, and the data does not yet show those gaps closing. The macro signal to watch next month: whether the long-term unemployed line keeps climbing, and whether inflation expectations continue their reversal — both feed directly back into mortgage rates and into the affordability math agents are walking buyers through right now.
